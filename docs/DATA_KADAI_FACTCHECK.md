# Fact-check: "Strategic Growth and Monetization Playbook for Data Kadai" (18 Nov 2026)

AK shared a deep-research playbook for Data Kadai (@DataKadai) on 18 Nov 2026. As with the Gemini report of 10 Nov
([`SHORTS_FACTCHECK.md`](SHORTS_FACTCHECK.md)), each claim the plan depends on was checked before anything became a rule.
The plan built from what survived is [`DATA_KADAI_PLAN.md`](DATA_KADAI_PLAN.md).

**Summary.**
- **Right, and useful:**
  - the diagnosis that our older Data Kadai Shorts bury the finding under survey names;
  - the advice to drop the "5 rounds, 1 point each" template;
  - the YPP numbers for new channels from 1 Feb 2027;
  - the data figures in the audit, where we could re-derive them.
- **Wrong:**
  - "existing partners face no doubled hurdle as long as they stay active": from 1 Feb 2027 they also need 10M qualified
    Shorts views in 90 days to earn any Shorts revenue;
  - all five "upgraded" titles are over YouTube's 100-character limit (113–147 characters).
- **Conflicts with our own measured rules:**
  - "two Shorts cut directly from each episode" is the cut-down that our study of 5,538 Shorts ranks worst;
  - "end every Short on the statistical cliffhanger" breaks rule O6, which says a Short pays off its own hook;
  - "titles are the CTR problem": in our study title wording moves views at most 1.3×, and topic about 25×.
- **Unverified:**
  - the India in Pixels ₹30 lakh Coca-Cola licence, which no source we found mentions;
  - every RPM, CPM and rate-card figure (vendor blog estimates);
  - most third-party subscriber and income figures for competitors.
- **Not realistic:** qualifying for YPP before 1 Feb 2027. That is 75 days away, and needs about 48,000 long-form views
  from a channel whose uploads get under 100 views.

Verdicts: **TRUE** · **PARTLY** · **FALSE** · **UNVERIFIED** (no source found) · **VENDOR** (an agency or tool estimate,
directional only) · **CONFLICTS** (contradicts a rule we measured or checked).

## Packaging

| Claim | Verdict | What is actually true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bureaucratic titles like "(ASI 2024-25)" suppress click-through | PARTLY | Plausible for long videos. For Shorts, our study found the topic moves views about 25× and title wording at most 1.3× (SHORTS_STUDY). The real fix is the topic and the first frame, not the title. |
| The five "upgraded" titles | FALSE as written | 113–147 characters each. YouTube's title limit is 100, and search and mobile cut at about 60–70. Shorts titles are capped at 50 by our rule K1. |
| "Use Shorts as trailers: two 35–45 s Shorts cut directly from each long episode" | CONFLICTS | Our study: Shorts clipped from long videos earn 0.1–1.1% of subscribers per Short, against 17–36% for native Shorts (SHORTS_STUDY finding 1). Rule: no cut-downs; each Short is its own loop script from the same verified numbers (SHORTS_RULES section 2). |
| "End every Short on the statistical cliffhanger, the 'Why?' question" | CONFLICTS | Rule O6: the hook is paid off inside the Short. A Short can end on the next question the long video answers, after its own reveal (SHORTS_RULES section 7, last row). |
| Retire "5 rounds, 1 point each" | TRUE | A templated storyline with numbers swapped is the pattern the inauthentic-content policy names. It also fails our topic test: one Short, one idea (T2). |
| Replace AI voice with a human voice | PARTLY | The only large study puts the cost of AI voice at about 7% of shares (SHORTS_FORMAT section 5). That is a real but modest effect, and it has not been tested on our channels. The plan already holds a 10-vs-10 test. AK's call. |
