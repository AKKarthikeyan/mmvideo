# Direction research: how the best explainers are directed (22 Nov 2026)

AK asked for research on direction before we write `docs/DIRECTOR.md`. The question: why do our films (Vox+, HyperFrames,
India Ledger) come out correct but mediocre, and how do the best explainer teams direct theirs? Each source below was
read at the link given, except where a line says "summary" (only a secondhand or partial read). Strength: **strong** =
the maker's own words; **medium** = interview or profile; **weak** = vendor blog or unverified.

## 1. What the best teams do

### Vox video (Joss Fong, Phil Edwards; the team's model)
- **One person owns a video from pitch to publish.** Fong: "We kind of pushed aside the typical assembly line model of
  making video." The head of video wanted work "as much yours as I can possibly make it." (medium: [Long Story][ls])
- **A topic goes to video only if it is "distinctly visual."** Stock footage over narration did not count. (medium: [ls])
- **Video producers write their own scripts; articles are not reused.** "Video requires a different kind of
  thinking." (medium: [ls])
- **Hold back the reveal.** In the black-hole video Fong opens on the telescopes, not the photo, so viewers understand
  "why this is so difficult before they saw the result." (strong: [Open Notebook][on])
- **Spoken writing is plain and repeats things.** "You can't really afford to be too clever or beautiful when you're
  doing voice-over." She keeps redundant lines ("That's right") because viewers can't glance back. (strong: [on])
- **Music is edited actively, not laid as a bed.** She changes the song about every 20 s, cuts tracks on the beat, and
  uses music starts and stops as "attention cues". A track that competes with the voice hurts comprehension. Music
  takes up to "a full day". The emotional peak (the photo) gets a choral track and "a reflective moment". (strong: [on])
- **The script editor's most valuable note is "I was confused at this part"**: "There's a really high risk of
  charging forward." (strong: [on])
- **Phil Edwards:** finds a "Rosetta stone" source per video. Research is done when facts start repeating. Visuals
  drive the script. Narrative devices on top of the facts: side quests, backstory, interruptions, visual gags, layered
  audio. Animation and pace stop archive material becoming "an archive dump". (medium: [Explainers][pe])

### Johnny Harris (ex-Vox)
- **Writes with the visual idea first**; the script supports pictures that already carry the story. Builds around
  **"visual anchors"**: one concrete thing on screen that makes an abstract idea graspable (he and his editor invented
  the map-explainer format this way). About four months per video, with a very deep research doc. (weak to medium:
  podcast summaries, [Medium][jh1], [Rosetta][jh2])

### Kurzgesagt
- Order: research (primary sources, experts, a source sheet) → script, "about a dozen drafts" → **sketch the visual
  metaphors and the transitions between ideas** → voice (the voice sets the timing) → animation → **an original score
  composed to the finished picture**. (strong: [kurzgesagt.org][kz]) Third-party accounts put a video at about 1,200 hours
  (summary).

### NYT Visual Investigations
- **The story is woven with the evidence, the questions around it, and the proof**; layers of corroborating evidence
  come before a claim; the method is shown to the viewer. (medium: [Reuters Institute][nyt])

### MrBeast production memo (leaked 2024; partly verbatim)
- "The first minute of each video is the most important minute." Minutes 1–3: "Stop telling people what they will be
  watching and start showing them." Planned **re-engagement points at about 3 and 6 minutes**. Minutes 3–6: "the most
  exciting and interesting content that is also very simple". Don't signal the end except to build toward the payoff.
  **The title and thumbnail set expectations; a mismatch makes viewers "feel like you were lied to and click off".**
  Work from the per-second retention graph. (medium: [memo repost][mb])

### Data animation in newsrooms
- **FT (Burn-Murdoch):** annotate; text is where the eye goes first; label lines directly, no legends; grey for the rest,
  colour for the point ("Minimize distraction. Maximize contrast."); iterate on communication, not on data. (medium:
  [iMEdD][ft])
- **IJNet (Liévano):** one narrow concept; storyboard every scene with the full voice-over; **a mock run (flip-book of
  sketches) before production**; animate only the key figures; lines and bars used "sparingly", or it becomes "a
  glorified slideshow"; "When there is nothing moving on the screen, the viewer unconsciously interprets it as a cue to
  stop paying attention"; but "subtlety is key". (medium: [IJNet][ij])

## 2. The craft principles behind them

- **Murch's Rule of Six** (*In the Blink of an Eye*): judge a cut by emotion (51%), story (23%), rhythm (10%),
  eye-trace (7%), 2D plane (5%), 3D space (4%). Higher items cover for lower ones, never the reverse. **Our checks
  cover only the bottom three** (overlaps, layout, readability). (strong: the book, via [StudioBinder][mu])
- **"But / therefore", not "and then"** (Parker and Stone): between every two beats there must be a causal link
  ("therefore") or a complication ("but"). A list of facts joined by "and then" is not a story. (medium: secondhand
  accounts of their NYU talks, [summary][bt])
- **Sound** (documentary practice): narration first, music below to guide emotion, effects quietest; ambience sets
  place, foley shows action; **silence creates tension and reflection** and is among the most powerful tools. (medium:
  [documentary.org][ds])
- **Retention evidence** is mostly vendor data: a clear hook cuts early drop-off; frequent visual change helps; the
  title must match the opening. Treat the numbers as directional. (weak: [Backlinko][rt] and similar)

## 3. What this means for us

| Finding | What we do now | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One owner per film (Vox) | Vox+ splits a film across three builders by scene | One director owns the treatment, shot list, sound and final review; builders follow the shot list |
| Visual first, then words (Harris, Edwards, Kurzgesagt sketches) | Narration is locked and voiced, then pictures are cut to it | Write the treatment and beat sheet with the visual anchor for each beat **before** the narration is final |
| Visual anchors and metaphors | A chart, document or card per sentence | Each act has **one** concrete visual anchor; charts serve it |
| Mock run or animatic before production (IJNet, Kurzgesagt sketching) | Stills checked only after the build | A rough animatic (key frames plus scratch voice) reviewed by AK **before** the full build |
| But / therefore | Scripts list checked facts in order | Every beat joined to the next by "but" or "therefore"; a beat that only says "and then" is cut or rewritten |
| Hold back the reveal; first minute (Fong, MrBeast) | Hook line first; the reveal often comes early | Open on the question and its stakes; plan the reveal and re-engagement points (about 3 and 6 min) |
| Music edited actively; silence (Fong, documentary practice) | Vox+: one bed ducked under the voice, one effect per 10 s; India Ledger: no music | Music changes with the story's sections and starts or stops as cues; two planned silences; one musical peak at the reveal (needs AK to revisit the 18 Nov no-music rule) |
| "I was confused here" review (Fong) | Fact checks and still frames | A first-time viewer note on the animatic and the final film, alongside the fact check |
| Murch: emotion and story first | Checks catch layout errors | The review asks first: what should the viewer feel here, and did the cut move the story? |
| Show the method (NYT) | Source chips on screen | Keep; this is our strength, plus a short "how we know" moment on big claims |
| Title and thumbnail match the opening (MrBeast) | Packaged after the film | Draft the title and thumbnail with the treatment; the opening pays them off within 30 s |

## 4. Gaps in this research

- No first-hand account of the Vox animatic or storyboard step, nor of Wendover, PolyMatter or Real Engineering's
  process: only generic guides came back.
- Nothing usable on how Indian business explainer channels (Think School and others) are produced.
- No primary study on retention for explainers; our own Shorts study (`docs/SHORTS_STUDY.md`) and channel analytics
  would be better evidence than the vendor numbers above.
