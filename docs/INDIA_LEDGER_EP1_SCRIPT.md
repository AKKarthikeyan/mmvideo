# India Ledger · Episode 1 · full script (draft for AK, 18 Nov 2026)

**Title:** How Do You Lose Money on Gold That Only Went Up?
**Length:** ≈ 10 min · ≈ 1,400 spoken words (≈ 9.3 min at 150 words a minute, ≈ 10 min with pauses)
**Plan and data table:** [`INDIA_LEDGER_WEEK1.md`](INDIA_LEDGER_WEEK1.md) (D-numbers below point to it). **Numbers:**
`python3 scripts/india_ledger_gold.py`. On the build day, re-run it with RBI's latest redemption price and update every
figure marked ⟳.

**Humour rules:** the joke is on the situation, never on a party, a minister or a viewer. Warm, family-flavoured, one
line, then straight back to the data. No joke ever changes a number. Every Act ends on a turn ("but", "so", "here's the
catch").

Format: **VO** is the voice · **SCREEN** is on-screen text (captions are separate) · **PICTURE** is the visual (HyperFrames
sample) · **SRC** is the source line shown small at the top.

## ACT 1 · The paradox (0:00–0:50)

**PICTURE:** frame 1, a gold line climbing across the screen, ending in a red stamp: LOSS. A bangle beside it.
**SCREEN:** GOLD 5× ↑ · LOST?

**VO:**
How do you lose money on gold that only went up?

The Government of India found a way.

**PICTURE:** a family locker opening; a bangle, a chain, a coin from someone's wedding.

In 2015, it made a bet against your mother.

It asked her to hand over her gold. She said no.
So it sold her paper gold instead.

**SCREEN:** ₹1.72 LAKH CRORE OWED ⟳
**SRC:** RBI tranche data · at ₹14,024/g (RBI, 13 Nov 2026) · our arithmetic

Today, it owes about one point seven lakh crore rupees on it.

Not because anyone stole it. Not because gold crashed.
Gold went up. That's exactly why it lost.

And this isn't history. Last week, one batch of these bonds paid back four point four times its price.
And holders can call in another eighty four thousand crore, at the next windows, whenever they choose.

**SCREEN:** +341% (13 NOV 2026) · ₹84,000 CR CALLABLE ⟳
**SRC:** RBI press release, 13 Nov 2026 · callable amount: our arithmetic

To see how this happened, start with a number almost nobody has seen.

## ACT 2 · The problem India couldn't dig its way out of (0:50–2:30)

**PICTURE:** a single gram of gold on one side of a scale; on the other, a pile that keeps growing.
**SCREEN:** 1 g DUG UP · 445 g BOUGHT
**SRC:** Ministry of Mines 2024–25 (as reported) · DGCIS FY 2025–26 · our arithmetic

**VO:**
For every one gram of gold India digs out of its own ground, it buys four hundred and forty five.

India mines about one and a half tonnes a year. Last year it imported seven hundred and twenty one.

Which makes gold India's second-biggest import. Right after crude oil.

**PICTURE:** bar chart of India's top imports, crude first, gold second, landing on the word "second".
**SCREEN:** ₹6.39 LAKH CRORE · 9.4% OF ALL IMPORTS

We run the country on oil. We run weddings on gold. Priorities.

And it's getting expensive. Four years ago India paid three point four lakh crore rupees for 879 tonnes.
Last year it paid six point four lakh crore. For less gold.

**SCREEN:** 86% MORE MONEY · 18% LESS GOLD
**SRC:** DGCIS, FY 2021–22 vs FY 2025–26 · our arithmetic

Eighty six percent more money. Eighteen percent less gold. Like your vegetable vendor, but in tonnes.

**PICTURE:** flow map (s07), lines arriving from Switzerland and the UAE into Indian airports; the dots swell by share.
**SCREEN:** DELHI AIRPORT 36% · MUMBAI 24% · CHENNAI 14% · GIFT CITY 7%

Most of it doesn't come by ship. It flies in. A third of India's gold lands at one airport: Delhi.
About half of it arrives from Switzerland and the UAE, which refine and trade it.

**SRC:** DGCIS principal commodity × port × country, FY 2025–26 · shares: our arithmetic

Every one of those grams is paid for in dollars. And dollars are what a country runs short of when it imports too much.

So in 2015, the government decided to do something about it.
On the same day, it launched two plans.

**SCREEN:** 5 NOV 2015 · PLAN A · PLAN B

Plan A: borrow the gold that's already here.
Plan B: if you can't get the gold, sell people something that feels like gold.

Both had the same target. Your mother.

## ACT 3 · Two plans, one catch (2:30–6:30)

### Plan A: the Gold Monetisation Scheme (2:30–3:50)

**PICTURE:** timeline (s11), a counter starting at 0 in November 2015.
**SCREEN:** PLAN A · LEND US YOUR GOLD

**VO:**
Plan A was simple. Indian homes hold an estimated thirty one thousand tonnes of gold.

**SRC:** World Gold Council, Aug 2026 (estimates range from 23,000 to 34,600 t)

If even a small part of it came out of lockers and into banks, India could import less.
You deposit your gold. You earn interest. The country saves dollars. Everybody wins.

So how much came out of the lockers?

Not a thousand tonnes. Not a hundred.

**PICTURE:** the counter crawls for nine years and stops.
**SCREEN:** 31 TONNES · 5,693 DEPOSITORS · 9 YEARS
**SRC:** Ministry of Finance, Mar 2025 (to Nov 2024)

Thirty one tonnes. In nine years. From fewer than six thousand depositors.

That's zero point one percent of the gold in Indian homes.

**PICTURE:** a second counter, India's imports, races to 31 tonnes.
**SCREEN:** INDIA IMPORTS THIS IN 16 DAYS

India imports that much in about sixteen days.

Nine years of persuasion. Sixteen days of shopping.

Why did it fail? Here's the line in the rules that nobody puts on the poster.

**PICTURE:** document close-up (s03), the RBI FAQ, one sentence highlighted.
**SRC:** RBI, FAQ on the Gold Monetisation Scheme

Your jewellery is tested, and with your consent, melted. After that, you can only get back gold. Never the piece.

**PICTURE:** a bangle sliding into a furnace; a plain bar coming out.

So the government wasn't asking for "idle gold".
It was asking for the bangle from her wedding. The chain her mother gave her. And it was offering to return it as a bar.

You can guess what Amma said. You can probably guess the tone, too.

In March 2025, the government shut the long-term parts of Plan A. Which brings us to Plan B.

### Plan B: paper gold (3:50–6:30)

**PICTURE:** a gold-bond certificate unrolls.
**SCREEN:** PLAN B · SOVEREIGN GOLD BONDS
**SRC:** RBI · Government Securities Act, 2006

**VO:**
Plan B was clever. If families won't give you their gold, sell them gold that doesn't exist.

A Sovereign Gold Bond. You pay rupees today, for, say, ten grams.
Eight years later, the government pays you whatever ten grams of gold is worth on that day.
Plus two and a half percent interest every year.

**SCREEN:** PAY ₹ TODAY → GET GOLD'S PRICE LATER + 2.5% A YEAR

No locker. No making charges. No uncle asking if it's real. People loved it.

**PICTURE:** 67 certificates stacking up, year by year.
**SCREEN:** 67 ISSUES · 147 TONNES · ₹72,274 CRORE · NOV 2015 – FEB 2024
**SRC:** RBI Annual Report 2023–24 · Ministry of Finance reply, Rajya Sabha

Sixty seven issues, from 2015 to 2024. A hundred and forty seven tonnes' worth. Seventy two thousand crore rupees.

And here's the catch.

**PICTURE:** the certificate flips over. The back is empty. No gold behind it.
**SCREEN:** THE GOVERNMENT SOLD GOLD IT NEVER OWNED

The government never bought the gold.

**VO:**
Legally, a gold bond is a government loan. The rupees came in and were spent like any other borrowing.
What the government kept was a promise: to repay at whatever gold costs on the day.

**PICTURE:** money breakdown (s04): your ₹ → the budget → a promise with a question mark.

Think about what that means. If gold stays flat, it's a cheap loan: two and a half percent, instead of the seven or so a normal government bond pays.
If gold shoots up, the government pays the difference.

It's a bet that gold won't rise much.

Against the one asset every Indian mother has been buying since before the government existed.

**PICTURE:** the price line from Nov 2015 to Nov 2026, tracing over the first certificate.
**SCREEN:** ₹26,840 → ₹1,40,240 PER 10 g · 5.2× ⟳
**SRC:** RBI issue price, SGB 2015-I · RBI redemption price, 13 Nov 2026

Ten grams cost twenty six thousand eight hundred rupees when the first bond was sold.
This month: one lakh forty thousand.

Five point two times.

**PICTURE:** document close-up of RBI's redemption notice; two bars, 12.9% and 7.75%.
**SCREEN:** FIRST BOND COST THE GOVT 12.9% A YEAR · A NORMAL LOAN: 7.75%
**SRC:** RBI press release 2023–24/1359 · 10-year G-sec, Nov 2015 (FRED)

The very first batch, the government's own number: it cost twelve point nine percent a year.
A normal loan that month cost seven point seven five.

**SCREEN:** MATURED BONDS: ≈ 16.5% A YEAR vs 7.1% ⟳
**SRC:** our bond-by-bond arithmetic, 24 matured issues

Across every bond that's been repaid so far, it works out to about sixteen and a half percent a year.
More than double a normal loan.

Somewhere, a moneylender is taking notes.

**PICTURE:** split screen: a bangle in a locker / a bond certificate.

And Amma? She held the real thing. She got the full rise, and she still has the bangle.
She won both ways. She also never had to log into a demat account.

## ACT 4 · Who pays, who gains, and what families do now (6:30–8:30)

**PICTURE:** scale (s12), a counter that ticks with the gold price.
**SCREEN:** ₹1.72 LAKH CRORE OWED · +₹12,277 CR FOR EVERY ₹1,000/g ⟳
**SRC:** RBI tranche data · our arithmetic at ₹14,024/g

**VO:**
So here's the bill.
About a hundred and twenty three tonnes of these bonds are still out there. At this month's price, the government owes about one point seven lakh crore rupees on them.

And it moves every day. Every time gold goes up by a thousand rupees a gram, the bill goes up by about twelve thousand crore.

Most of us check the gold price before a wedding. Someone in North Block checks it before breakfast.

**SCREEN:** ₹84,000 CR CASHABLE AT THE NEXT WINDOWS · FY 2028–29 ₹45,000 CR · FY 2031–32 ₹62,000 CR ⟳
**SRC:** RBI tranche data · our arithmetic at today's price

About eighty four thousand crore of it can be cashed in at the next redemption windows: those bonds are past five years, and the holders decide when.
The bigger waves come in 2028, and in 2031. The government has set up a fund to pay for it, and in 2025 that fund covered about half.

**SRC:** Business Today, Apr 2025

So who pays? The budget. Which means every taxpayer.
Who gains? The bondholders. Fair and square: they bought what was offered. Hold to maturity from issue, and the gain is tax-free.

**SCREEN:** WON THE BET: BONDHOLDERS · PAYS FOR IT: TAXPAYERS · BOTH: QUITE A FEW OF YOU
**SRC:** Union Budget 2026: tax-free only if bought at issue and held to maturity

If you hold a gold bond: congratulations, you won the bet.
If you pay income tax: congratulations, you also paid for it.

**PICTURE:** dot chart (s05) — the "idle gold" pile, with a chunk of dots peeling off toward a bank.
**SCREEN:** GOLD LOANS ≈ ₹5.1 LAKH CRORE · +105% IN A YEAR (MAY 2026)
**SRC:** RBI sectoral credit data, as reported · VERIFY on the RBI release

And families? They still won't deposit their gold. But they will pawn it.
Bank loans against gold jewellery roughly doubled in a year, to about five lakh crore rupees.

So the Indian family plan is: never sell the gold. Let the bank babysit it for a while.
Same bangle goes in. Same bangle comes out. That's the whole difference.

**SCREEN:** "FAIRLY HIGH-COST BORROWING" — ECONOMIC AFFAIRS SECRETARY, FEB 2025
**SRC:** ThePrint, Feb 2025

The government reached the same conclusion. In 2025 its own Economic Affairs Secretary called the bonds "fairly high-cost borrowing", and said gold imports hadn't come down the way they'd hoped.
No new bonds have been sold since February 2024.

But before you forward this video with "SCAM" in capital letters, stop. Because the numbers hide a few things.

## ACT 5 · What the numbers hide (8:30–9:50)

**PICTURE:** document close-up of our own assumptions, typed out.
**SCREEN:** WHAT THE NUMBERS HIDE

**VO:**
One. Owing isn't losing.
Part of that one point seven lakh crore is just the money the government borrowed. It would owe that on any loan. And for years it paid two and a half percent instead of seven.

So we went bond by bond, all sixty seven of them, and took that out.

**SCREEN:** REAL EXTRA COST ≈ ₹1.06 LAKH CRORE ≈ ₹730 PER INDIAN ⟳
**SRC:** our bond-by-bond estimate: 67 issues vs the 10-year G-sec yield of each issue month (FRED) · at ₹14,024/g · ₹82,000 crore at ₹12,024/g, ₹1.31 lakh crore at ₹16,024/g · population VERIFY

The real extra cost of the bet, compared with borrowing normally: about one lakh crore rupees, at today's price. Roughly seven hundred rupees for every Indian.
Smaller than the headline. Still the price of a lot of hospitals.

Two. It wasn't useless.
**SRC:** Business Standard, Mar 2024
One estimate says the bonds saved India three point three billion dollars of gold imports in a single year.

Three. It looked safe at the time.
**SCREEN:** ₹31,050 (2012) → ₹31,438 (2018) · VERIFY
Gold had gone almost nowhere for six years before the first bond was sold. Nobody knew it would quintuple.
Hindsight is twenty four carat.

Four. Gold doesn't only go up. It dipped on the way, including when import duty was cut in 2024.
The bill rises and falls with it, every single day.

**SCREEN:** EDUCATIONAL RESEARCH, NOT INVESTMENT ADVICE · NOT SEBI-REGISTERED

Now a new plan is being proposed. The World Gold Council wants India to bring ten to fifteen percent of household gold into the system, with a National Gold Board.

**SRC:** World Gold Council, "Swarnim Udaan 2047", Aug 2026

Attempt number three. Maybe this one asks Amma first. And doesn't bring a furnace.

**PICTURE:** the bangle, back in the locker. The locker closes. Cut to the climbing gold line from frame 1.

So who was right about gold?
The bondholders won. The taxpayers paid. And your mother kept her bangle.

Which leaves just one question.

**SCREEN:** GOLD 5× ↑ · LOST?  (cut straight back to frame 1)

How do you lose money on gold that only went up?
