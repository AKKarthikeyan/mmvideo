In 2015, the Government of India made a bet against gold.

So how do you lose money on gold that only went up?
It starts with a bangle.

## 2 · The pile India can't touch (0:30–1:25)

**SAY:**
Meet Amma. This bangle was her mother's. It has never left her locker.
Now multiply Amma by crores of homes. Indian families hold an estimated thirty one thousand tonnes of gold.
And almost none of it was dug up in India. For every gram India mines, it buys four hundred and forty three from abroad.
Gold is India's second-biggest import, after crude oil. Paid in dollars. Every single year.
So the government looked at Amma's locker, and saw a problem. And an opportunity.

## 4 · Plan A: give us the bangle (1:55–2:50)

**SAY:**
Plan A sounded simple. Lend your gold to a bank. Earn interest. Help the country.
But read the rules. Your jewellery is tested and melted. You get back gold. Never your piece.
So the government wasn't asking for "idle gold". It was asking for the bangle from her mother. And offering to return it as a bar.
Amma said no. So did almost everyone.
In nine years, India lent about thirty one tonnes. The country imports that much in about sixteen days.
Plan A was dead. Plan B was the real bet.

## 5 · Plan B: gold that doesn't exist (2:50–3:50)

**SAY:**
Ravi, next door, loved Plan B.
Pay rupees today for ten grams of gold. Eight years later, the government pays you whatever ten grams is worth. Plus two and a half percent interest, every year.
No locker. No making charges. No uncle asking if it's real.
Here's what Ravi didn't think about. The government never bought any gold.
It took his rupees and spent them, like any other loan. All it kept was a promise: to pay back at whatever gold costs on the day.
If gold stayed flat, that's a very cheap loan. If gold shot up, the government pays the difference.
That was the bet. That gold wouldn't rise much. In India.

## 6 · The bet goes wrong (3:50–4:45)

**SAY:**
From 2015 to 2024, the government sold this bet again and again. Seventy two thousand crore rupees of it.
Then gold did what gold does in India.
When the first bond was sold, ten grams cost under twenty seven thousand rupees. This month: more than one lakh thirty thousand. ⟳
Every time gold went up, the government's bill went up with it.
Today it owes about one point six lakh crore rupees on bonds still out there. ⟳ And that number moves every single day.
Most of us check the gold price before a wedding. Someone in the finance ministry checks it before breakfast.

## 7 · Who pays (4:45–5:40)

**SAY:**
So who pays? Not Ravi. Ravi won, fair and square. He bought what was offered. And if he held to the end, his gain is tax-free.
The bill goes to the budget. And the budget is you.
Now, to be fair, most of that one point six lakh crore is money the government borrowed. It would owe that on any loan.
So we went bond by bond, all sixty seven of them, and took that out.
What's left is the real price of the bet: close to one lakh crore rupees. ⟳ About six hundred and ninety rupees for every man, woman and child in India. ⟳
A bet you never placed. A bill you're paying.

## 9 · So, was it a scam? (6:15–6:45)

**SAY:**
So was it a scam? No. And this part matters.
In the three years before the first bond, gold actually fell. For three years after, it barely moved. Nobody knew it was about to go up five times.
It was a reasonable bet. It just lost. And a bet that loses still has to be paid.
The last new gold bond was sold in February 2024.
