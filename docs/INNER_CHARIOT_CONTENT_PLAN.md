## 3. Positioning

**One line:** the calm, sourced, illustrated guide to what the Indian texts actually say, for people who came looking for a way to think.

What every video does that the field does not:

1. **The verse with its source on screen.** Chapter, verse and the named public-domain translator ("AFTER SHRI PUROHIT SWAMI, 1935"), with the Devanagari. No competitor does this.
2. **Our reading is marked.** "OUR NOTE", in its own style.
3. **The school is named.** "Shankara reads this as…, Ramanuja as…". This takes the heat out of sectarian comment wars.
4. **Drawn in code.** No AI image of a deity, ever. Krishna only as a silhouette in light (rule 5).
5. **A narrator, never a god.** The voice tells you what the text says; it never speaks as Krishna.

The channel name gives the recurring image: **the chariot of the Katha Upanishad** (body the chariot, senses the horses, mind the reins, intellect the driver). It is the kit's signature scene, and it can close any episode on the same question: *who is driving?*

## 7. How each episode is built

The VoxPlus rules in `DHARMA_CHANNEL.md` still apply: a question by 0:05, a hook at least every 45 s, one spine sentence. Within that, a typical 10 to 12-minute episode runs:

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:05 | A concrete question. No logo, no "welcome" ("Why does the best archer alive put down his bow?") |
| 0:05 to 0:30 | The stake, the promise ("by the end you can answer it"), and one planted object that pays off at the end |
| 0:30 to 1:00 | The spine sentence; who is speaking, and from which text |
| 1:00 to 3:00 | Act one is a story (a scene from the text), with the first verse card about 2:00 and three seconds of silence on it |
| 3:00 to 6:00 | The model: the chariot board or the two birds, with its parts named one by one |
| about 6:00 | The turn: "But there is a problem with this reading." The other school or the objection, cued with a bell |
| 6:00 to 8:30 | The counter-reading, a second verse, the OUR NOTE marker, and one dry line of humour |
| 8:30 to 9:45 | The verdict; the planted object pays off |
| 9:45 to 10:35 | One thing to try today (OUR NOTE), and what would prove our reading wrong |
| 10:35 to 11:00 | The next episode's title said aloud; end screen; the disclosure in one spoken line |

Craft rules:
- **Pace and sound.** 140 to 155 words a minute, so 1,500 to 1,800 words for 11 minutes. Duck the tanpura under the voice (about −18 to −22 dB).
- **Sanskrit.** The narrator never reads it. It is shown on screen, or a human reciter records 5 to 10 seconds of it.
- **Write for the ear.** Short sentences, read aloud before the voice pass. Overly written scripts are what make synthetic voices sound robotic.
- **One recurring foil.** The blind king's "and then?" gives the episode a questioner without a talking-head host.
- **Vary the shape.** Some episodes run story-first, some problem-first, some as a mystery. A fixed skeleton with only the nouns changed is what the inauthentic-content policy demonetises.

**Shorts** (25 to 40 s):
- 0 to 2 s: the question or the number ("Gita 12.15 in 30 seconds").
- 2 to 9 s: the verse card, with the chapter and verse also in the title.
- 9 to 30 s: one plain-words gloss and one image.
- Last line: rhymes with the first frame, so the Short loops.

Pin a comment with the link to the long video. Treat Shorts as discovery. Nothing published shows that they convert viewers into long-form watchers.

**Titles:**
- Plain English plus the Sanskrit term, as a question or "what X actually says". Under 65 characters.
- Put the chapter and verse in the title where it fits.
- Never use "secret", "shocking", "hidden" or "they don't want you to know".

**Thumbnails:**
- One object from the kit (the bow, the diya, the chariot, the two birds), 3 to 5 words, no face and no deity.
- The same layout across a series, so the channel reads as a set.
- Run YouTube's Test & Compare (three thumbnails) on every long video.

## 10. How we will know it is working

Review at 30, 60 and 90 days, comparing each video against the channel's own typical performance for that length, not against big channels.

| Metric (YouTube Studio) | Act when |
| --- | --- |
| Intro retention (first 30 s) | Below 55% on two videos in a row: rewrite the first 30 seconds and check that the title, thumbnail and opening line make the same promise |
| Average percentage viewed | Aim for 35% or more on 10 to 12 min. A dip of 8 to 10 points inside 15 seconds: find the beat and fix the template |
| Click-through rate | More than 1.5 points under the channel's rolling average after 5,000 impressions: run Test & Compare |
| New vs returning viewers | New viewers leaving in the intro while returning viewers stay: the title is promising something the opening does not deliver |
| Shorts: viewed vs swiped away; engaged views | Judge Shorts on these, not on how many viewers they send to the long videos |
| Series: returning-viewer share; end-screen clicks | The test of whether a series builds a habit |
| Monetisation | Reach 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours early. A tighter Partner Program from February 2027 is reported but unconfirmed |

**Targets from the research doc:**
- 25K to 50K subscribers in six months.
- 100K or more in twelve months if two or three videos break out.

The likeliest breakouts are the Ramayana pair, the overthinking video and the Gita film.
