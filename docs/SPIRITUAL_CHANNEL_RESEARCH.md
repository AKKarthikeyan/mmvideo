# New YouTube channel for the spiritual Indian audience: research, gap, plan

Prepared 21 Oct 2026 in a Claude Code session from vidIQ data (keyword volumes, breakout videos, channel search, one transcript), web sources and a read of this repo's video engine. Numbers are as of 20 Oct 2026 and are vidIQ estimates unless a source is named. Nothing is uploaded or decided; this is the research the launch should be argued from.

---

## 1. The short version

**The market is large, India-heavy and in a land-grab.** Gita-specific YouTube searches alone run past 300K a month in India. Channels created in the last six months are reaching 20K to 270K subscribers, and videos on channels with 1K to 5K subscribers are routinely hitting 100K to 600K views. Browse and suggested traffic, not subscribers, decide reach here.

**Almost all of the new supply is the same thing:** AI image slideshows, an AI voice speaking *as* Krishna, no verse numbers, no sources, recycled "you are not late" scripts. The question "how to make hindu mythology video with AI" now gets about 12K searches a month and is growing 46%. That is the flood we should not join. YouTube's July 2026 inauthentic-content guidance targets exactly this (template content, AI personas giving advice), and judges channels as a whole.

**The gap:** nobody sits between the six-hour swami discourse and the AI-Krishna motivational clip. There is no Indian-rooted "School of Life" for the Gita, Upanishads and epics: plain words, 8 to 15 minutes, the actual verse on screen with chapter and verse, illustration drawn in code rather than generated, English original with a Hindi audio track. Demand for that register is visible: "the school of life" gets 84K searches a month from India, "psychology" 497K, "self improvement" 288K, and the English explainer channel The East Made Simple pulled 348K views on an Upanishads video from a 30K-subscriber channel based in Romania.

**It fits the engine we already have.** The Moat Files discipline (every word on screen comes from the script, verbatim quotes shown as source clips with the spoken phrase highlighted, "our note" visually distinct from the quote, code-drawn illustration kit, Hindi audio tracks, Shorts and carousels cut from the long video) is the exact production method this niche lacks. The new work is a new illustration kit (about 8 icons, 6 scene types), a code-composed tanpura bed, a public-domain scripture corpus, and a human reviewer for doctrine and Sanskrit.

**Recommendation:** launch with a Gita series anchored to Gita Jayanti (Mokshada Ekadashi, around 20 Dec 2026, verify with a panchang), run two long videos, one sleep reading and six to eight Shorts a week, and treat ad revenue as secondary. Realistic 6-month target: 25K to 50K subscribers; 12-month: 100K+ if two or three videos break out.

### 2.4 The calendar is the editorial plan

Breakout videos in the Hindi data cluster around festivals. During Pitru Paksha (Sept to Oct 2026), "Gita chapter 7 / 11 / 18 with mahatmya" videos did 565K views on a 1.2M channel and 112K to 118K on a 12.9K channel, 24K on a 742-subscriber channel. Sawan brought Shiva and Samudra Manthan stories (583K on a 2K channel). Janmashtami drove the "krishna" spike that is now unwinding. Navratri produced the Durga explainer podcast (86K in a week).

Each festival is an annual, predictable demand spike that a new channel can plan for and that the engine can refresh each year at near-zero cost.

### 3.4 What the dominant format actually does (transcript of the 662K-view video)

The Krishyukti_19 video "Stop Overthinking Before It Destroys You" runs: (1) a second-person hook, "does your mind get so tired you just want to sleep but can't?"; (2) a diagnosis of overthinking in everyday terms; (3) a parable of a girl who overreads a late reply; (4) the pivot: "I answered this question for Arjuna on the field of Kurukshetra", so the narrator *is* Krishna; (5) Gita 2.11 ("you grieve for what should not be grieved") and 2.47 (karmanye vadhikaraste), quoted without chapter or verse numbers; (6) the glass-of-water analogy; (7) "psychology also agrees, a research showed" with no citation; (8) a diary parable.

What to keep: the modern-problem hook, the Arjuna parallel, one concrete analogy, under ten minutes. What to drop: the AI voice impersonating God (a policy risk under the July 2026 guidance on AI personas giving advice, and a cultural risk), the uncited verses, the vague "research shows", and the recycled parables that a dozen channels share word for word.

## 4. The audience

- **Gen Z India is religious on its own terms.** A 2023 YouGov-Mint survey is widely cited as finding 53% of Gen Z Indians call religion important and 62% pray regularly (secondhand; the original release was not located). YouTube's 2025 review said 77% of Gen Z viewers in India watched content translated from another language, which is the case for multi-language audio tracks. Gen Z reportedly made 53% of spiritual-travel bookings in FY2026 (booking data via a travel site, unverified). Forbes India covers "bhajan clubbing" selling out a 10,000-seat show in Kolkata.
- **The English-speaking viewer wants psychology.** The search neighbourhood (section 2.2) says the entry point is anxiety, overthinking, purpose, comparison, grief, decisions. The Gita's own chapters 2, 3, 6 and 12 are literally about those.
- **The diaspora pays the bills.** US, UK, Canada, Australia and the Gulf are the second to fifth markets for "bhagavad gita" and "hinduism" and carry 3 to 5x the CPM. English original with a Hindi track serves both without re-rendering the picture, which is the rule this repo already works by.
- **Money is weak unless the audience is English.** India CPMs run about Rs 50 to 150 (upGrowth 2026 guide, an agency estimate). The comparable new channels above earn $0 to $1,040 a month at 20K to 200K subscribers. Ads alone do not justify the channel; the audience, the search positions on evergreen questions, and the engine as an asset do.

## 6. How to present differently (the house rules)

1. **Narrator, never deity.** The voice is a guide. Krishna's words appear as quotes with a source line (translation, chapter.verse), in the italic quote style; our interpretation appears as "our note" in the roman style. The Letters engine already draws this distinction (red verbatim quote, blue our-note).
2. **The verse is on screen.** Sanskrit in Devanagari, IAST transliteration, English line, source. Use the existing "clip" scene (a crop of the real page with the spoken phrase highlighted) on a public-domain edition. This is the Moat Files "filing quote" rule applied to scripture.
3. **Draw the idea, not the god.** Code-drawn scenes of the models the texts teach. No AI-generated images of deities at all (cultural sensitivity, the AI-slop look, and YouTube's channel-level inauthentic-content review all point the same way). MiniMax image plates only for mood backgrounds: landscapes, rivers, a battlefield at dawn, with the existing no-text, no-faces style string.
4. **Hook on a modern problem, land on a practice.** Overthinking, jealousy, grief, a decision, comparison, anger. Verse, model, then one thing to try today. Never "research shows" without the paper on screen.
5. **State the school.** When interpreting, say whether this is Shankara's Advaita, Ramanuja's reading, or the plain-sense reading. It disarms sectarian comment wars and signals seriousness.
6. **Plain words, as for a viewer who has never opened the Gita.** Same brief as the DMart story remake.
7. **Titles that are questions or counts, in plain English, with the Hindi version in the description.** From the data: "Every X explained in N minutes", "What the Gita actually says about Y", "Is X history or myth?", "When you feel Y: Gita a.b".
8. **One illustrated object per thumbnail** from the kit, three to five words, no deity face. Score with vidIQ before upload.
9. **Disclosure.** A standing line in the description: narration is synthetic, illustrations are drawn in code from the script, translations are named. This is the honest version of the "AI-labelled" tag the breakout channels carry.
10. **A human reviewer signs off on doctrine and Sanskrit** before upload, the same `reviewed_by` rule the Hindi tracks use. The engine cannot judge whether a reading is defensible.

### 90-day build

**Weeks 0 to 2, foundation.**
- Name, handle, palette (keep paper and ink; add one accent, say deep indigo or saffron), a 25-second channel intro with the existing intro engine.
- Corpus: download the public-domain texts into `sources/` (Project Gutenberg #2388 for the Song Celestial; Ganguli's Mahabharata; Max Muller's Upanishads; GRETIL for Sanskrit e-text). Confirm copyright status for India yourself; Gita Press and ISKCON translations are copyrighted and must not be quoted at length.
- Kit: 8 icons (diya, lotus, chariot, bow, flute, peacock feather, banyan, conch) and 6 scene types (chariot of the self, two birds, inverted tree, three-guna meter, rope-and-snake, salt-in-water). Export to the illustration library like the DMart kit.
- Audio: tanpura drone and a soft bell composed in code (extend `intro_music.py`). Narrator ear-test: the Indian-accent English male and the Hindi native voices exist; decide the rotation. Sanskrit shlokas: MiniMax will mispronounce them, so either show the Sanskrit on screen while the narrator reads only the English, or record 5 to 10 second shloka clips with a human reciter (one human element, and a strong authenticity signal).
- Reviewer: one person who can check doctrine and Sanskrit, named in `reviewed_by`.

**Weeks 3 to 4, pilot.** Three long videos through the full pipeline: "Gita chapter 2: the chapter that answers overthinking" (pillar A, competes directly with the 662K format but sourced), "Is the Mahabharata history? What the text itself says" (pillar C, 5K-a-month question, competition 22), and the Diwali explainer (pillar D). Six Shorts cut from them. Hindi tracks on all three. Score titles and thumbnails with vidIQ, run `hook_check.py` and `qa_vx.py`, reviewer sign-off, upload.

**Weeks 5 to 12, cadence.** Two longs, one sleep reading, six to eight Shorts, two carousels a week; Hindi tracks on every long; festival videos two weeks early. Review vidIQ comment insights monthly and let the questions in the comments pick the pillar B topics.

## 9. Risks and how the plan handles them

- **YouTube inauthentic-content policy (July 2026 guidance; channel-level review).** Handled by original scripts, code-drawn visuals, sources on screen, a named disclosure, and no AI persona giving advice. Note: one timeline (AIR Media-Tech) reports a Partner Program overhaul effective Feb 2027 with higher entry thresholds; unconfirmed, so plan to reach 1K subs and 4K watch hours well before then.
- **Copyright.** Quote only public-domain translations or our own paraphrase; never Gita Press, ISKCON "As It Is", or modern commentaries at length. Sanskrit source text is free.
- **Religious sensitivity and sectarian comment wars.** State the school; "what the texts say" rather than "debunked"; a reviewer; no deity imagery generated by a model.
- **Hindi monetisation is weak.** English original, Hindi as a track; diaspora reach through the English layer.
- **Seasonality.** Pillars A, B and E are evergreen; D captures the spikes.
- **Sanskrit pronunciation by TTS.** Human reciter for shlokas or on-screen Sanskrit only.
- **Sameness with the Moat & Margin look.** Same engine, different palette, kit and typographic voice, so the two channels do not read as one brand.
