# IC-01 script, draft 1: The Lakshman Rekha Isn't in Valmiki's Ramayana

Draft 25 Oct 2026, from `IC01_VALMIKI_BEFORE_THE_FILM.md`.
- **Quotes:** every on-screen quote is Ralph T. H. Griffith's wording, pulled from the corpus (`corpus.py canto`, Gutenberg #24869), cited by Griffith's book and canto.
- **Pictures:** Rama, Sita and the epic's other figures appear as backlit silhouettes in light (rule 5 as extended on 25 Oct).
- **Narration:** 761 words, about 5.2 min at 146 words a minute (counted by script). With the 12 quote cards held on screen, the runtime is about 7.5 min.
- **Proposed: voice the quotes in character**, as the Gita film voiced Krishna: Valmiki, Gautama, Janaka's men, Dasharatha, Sita, Lakshmana. Each quote is heard once, never re-read. That brings the runtime to about 9 min, without padding.
- **Not yet done:** a human reviewer's read. Lines marked **(check)** rest on the catalogue's memory notes, not on a text read this session.

Two passages in Griffith are **not quoted**: Lakshmana's lines on "womankind" (3.45), and Sita's words about ending her life (3.45). They are paraphrased with care, or left out.

### 0:00 · Cold open (Mystery)
**Picture.** Night. A forest hut. A line of light glows in the dust before the door, and hums.
**Voice.**
> In the oldest Ramayana we have, where is the line Sita must not cross?
> You know the one. A line in the dust, outside a hut in the forest.
> Cross it, and you're on your own.
> Millions of us grew up with that line.

*(Bell. The line flickers.)*

### 0:12 · Stake and promise
**Picture.** The line fades. Title card: **THE INNER CHARIOT · VALMIKI, NOT THE FILM**.
**Voice.**
> On the fifth of November, a film tells this story to millions.
> Before it does, let's open the source.
> I'll show you five places where the oldest telling says something different.
> You'll see the words on screen, with the book and the canto.
> And by the end, you'll know what that line in the dust really was.

### 0:35 · Who Valmiki is
**Picture.** A riverbank at dusk. Two curlews. An arrow: one bird falls. A palm leaf, and on it the first lines writing themselves in.
**Voice.**
> The story begins with a poet watching two birds.
> A hunter shoots one.
> Its mate cries out.
> And the poet, Valmiki, speaks a curse, in a rhythm no one had used before.

**Card (Griffith, Bala 1.2):**
> "The measured form of words I spoke / In shock of grief be termed a śloke."

*(3 s silence.)*
> Grief, shaped with care, became the first verse.
> That verse grew into some twenty-four thousand, in seven books.
> Every quote you'll see today is from the English of Ralph Griffith, made in the eighteen-seventies.

### 1:30 · Difference one: Ahalya (Two Versions)
**Picture.** Split frame. Left, warm paper, "the version you know": a stone, a foot, light. Right, lamplight: a grove, a woman's silhouette, half there, like mist.
**Voice.**
> You may know Ahalya as the woman turned to stone.
> Rama's foot touches the stone, and she's free.
> Turn the leaf.
*(The palm leaf turns; the frame goes to lamplight.)*
> In Valmiki, there's no stone.
> Her husband's curse is solitude.

**Card (Griffith, Bala 1.48):**
> "Thy bed the ashes, air thy food, / Here shalt thou live in solitude. … And not an eye thy form shall see."

*(3 s silence.)*
> She lives unseen, in penance, for years.
> And when the brothers find her, look who bows to whom.

**Card (Griffith, Bala 1.49):**
> "Then, with great joy that dame to meet, / The sons of Raghu clapped her feet"

> It's not a stone woken by a foot.
> It's a person, welcomed back.

### 3:00 · Difference two: the bow
**Picture.** Scale reveal: a great iron chest on eight wheels, hauled by a line of men that runs out of frame.
**Voice.**
> But surely you know how Rama won Sita.
> A great assembly of kings. Each one tries the bow. Each one fails.
> In Valmiki, the kings had come before, and failed before.
> On this day, it's a quiet court. A sage. Two boys.
> And a bow so heavy it arrives on wheels.

**Card (Griffith, Bala 1.67):**
> "Five thousand youths in number, all / Of manly strength and stature tall, / The ponderous eight-wheeled chest that held / The heavenly bow, with toil propelled."

*(3 s silence.)*
> Rama lifts it, "as if in sport," and draws the string,
> until the bow breaks in two.
> Then the whole court faints.
> The text says only the king, the sage and the two princes stayed on their feet.
> No contest. No garland. A father keeping a promise to his daughter.

### 4:15 · Difference three: the oath
**Picture.** Kaikeyi's chamber, lamplit. The king's silhouette, kneeling. A ring of light closes around his words.
**Voice.**
> But the happiness doesn't last.
> So why does a king who loves his son send him away?
> Later tellings soften it. In some, the gods themselves turn Manthara's mind **(check)**.
> Valmiki keeps it human. Fear in one room, a careless promise in another.
> Kaikeyi asks the king to swear first, and only then to hear.
> And he does.

**Card (Griffith, Ayodhya 2.11):**
> "I swear by Ráma my dear son, / Speak, and thy bidding shall be done."

*(3 s silence.)*
> He swears by the very son the wish will send away.
> That's not fate. That's a promise made blind.

### 5:15 · Difference four: Sita insists
**Picture.** A doorway, and a road leading out of it. Close on two hands. Then Sita's silhouette steps into the light.
**Voice.**
> So Rama must go. But he tells Sita to stay home, safe.
> You might expect her to agree quietly.
> She doesn't.

**Card (Griffith, Ayodhya 2.27):**
> "What words are these that thou hast said? / … I dismiss / With bitter scorn a speech like this"

*(2 s silence.)*
> And then the line she wins with:

**Card (Griffith, Ayodhya 2.27):**
> "My foot before thine own shall pass / Through tangled thorn and matted grass."

> In Valmiki, Sita argues. And she wins.

### 6:00 · The turn
**Picture.** Back to the hut. The line of light tries to draw itself. It stalls.
**Voice.**
> So, the line in the dust.
> If you grew up with the TV serial, so did we.
> But here is the scene in Valmiki.
*(Bell.)*

### 6:15 · Difference five: the deer, and the missing line
**Picture.** A golden deer between the trees. Close on its eye. Lakshmana's silhouette, bow lowered, half turned.
**Voice.**
> A golden deer appears near the hut.
> Sita wants it. Rama goes after it.
> And it's Lakshmana, not Rama, who sees through it.

**Card (Griffith, Aranya 3.43):**
> "'Tis magic, for the world has ne'er, / Lord of the world, shown aught so fair."

*(3 s silence.)*
> Far away, a voice cries out in Rama's voice.
> It's a trick. Sita is terrified.
> She begs Lakshmana to go.
> He refuses. He was told to guard her.
> And in her fear, she accuses him of the worst motives a man could have.
> It's the hardest moment in the scene. It's grief, speaking as anger.
*(The flame dims. Deepest night.)*
> So Lakshmana goes.
> But listen to how he leaves.

**Card (Griffith, Aranya 3.45):**
> "May all attendant wood-gods screen / Thy head from harm, O large-eyed Queen!"

*(3 s silence.)*

**Card (Griffith, Aranya 3.45):**
> "His reverent hands once more he raised, / His head he slightly bent, / Upon her face he sadly gazed, / And then toward Ráma went."

> No line.
> No circle in the dust.
> His protection was a prayer.

### 8:30 · What each version needed (no verdict)
**Picture.** Three lamps on a ledge. Each lights in turn as it's named.
**Voice.**
> So where does the line come from?
> It appears in later retellings. Scholars disagree about where it first appears.
> Here's what we think each version was doing.

**Kicker: OUR NOTE**
> Later tellers gave Sita a boundary drawn by someone who loved her.
> It's simple, and it's sacred, and it lets her be protected even when no one's there.
> Valmiki gives her something harder.
> A warning she couldn't hear in her fear. A brother's prayer. And a choice.
> Neither version is wrong to tell.
> They're answering different questions.

### 9:45 · Take it into the cinema
**Picture.** The five scenes as five small icons around the line of light, which is now steady and gold.
**Voice.**
> So when you watch the film this week, try this.
> Watch for five moments.
> Ahalya. The bow. The oath. Sita's answer. And last, the line.
> And notice which version the film chooses.
> What would prove us wrong? A manuscript of Valmiki with the line in it.
> We don't know of one. If you do, tell us, with the source.

### 10:35 · Close
**Picture.** The hut at dawn. The doorway is open. End card.
**Voice.**
> Next time: a woman alone in a grove of Ashoka trees, a demon king at the gate,
> and a single blade of grass between them.
> This was The Inner Chariot.
> Every verse here was shown with its book and canto. Our reading was marked as our note.
> The pictures are drawn in code, the voice is synthetic, and nobody here speaks for God.
