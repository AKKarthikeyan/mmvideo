## The five differences (all checked in Griffith; exact wording pulled at scripting)

| # | The version most people know | What Valmiki says | Where (Valmiki; Griffith) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ahalya was turned to stone, and the touch of Rama's foot freed her | No stone and no foot: she lives unseen, in penance, until Rama comes, and the brothers clasp *her* feet | 1.48–49; G I.XLVIII–XLIX |
| 2 | Sita chose Rama at a swayamvara of kings | No contest of suitors: Janaka sets a feat, the great bow is dragged in on a cart by thousands of men, and Rama breaks it | 1.66–67, 73; G I.LXVI–LXVII |
| 3 | Kaikeyi was turned by a goddess (Tulsidas) | A human story: Manthara's fear, and a king who swears his oath **before he hears** what the boons will be | 2.7–12; G II.XI |
| 4 | Sita follows Rama quietly into exile | She argues, angrily, that a wife shares her husband's fate, and wins | 2.26–30; G II.XXVI–XXX |
| 5 | Lakshmana draws a line Sita must not cross | **No line.** Lakshmana warns that the deer is magic ("'tis magic"); Sita, afraid for Rama, accuses him; he bows and leaves. Ravana comes as a mendicant | 3.43–49; G III.XLIII, XLV, XLIX |

The Shabari berries (no tasting in Valmiki, G III.LXXV) and "Hatred dies" (G VI.CXI) are later Shorts; they fall outside Part 1.

## Pictures and production

**Rule 5 decides how Rama and Sita are shown.** The house rule allows Krishna as a silhouette in light and says "no other deity is drawn". Rama and Sita are revered as divine, so as the rule stands they cannot be drawn. Two routes:

| Route | What it looks like | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Objects and places only** (works under the rule as it stands) | The bow, the hut, the line, the deer, footprints, a doorway, the palm leaf, hands seen only to the wrist, empty thrones. The story is told through what the characters touch and leave behind | Lower; fits the phad-scroll and Kaavad transitions |
| **B. Extend rule 5 to Rama and Sita as silhouettes in light** (your decision, as you made for Krishna) | Backlit silhouettes like the Gita film's Krishna and Arjuna; no faces, never colour-filled, never made by an image model | Higher: new figures (Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, Ravana as mendicant, Kaikeyi) |

Route A also suits the first video of a series, because the objects become the series' visual signature.

**New kit pieces** (whichever route):
- the forest hut at Panchavati;

- the great bow in its coffer;
- the palm-leaf manuscript leaf-turn;
- the split-screen "two versions" frame: warm paper on the left for "the version you know", lamplight on the right for Valmiki.
