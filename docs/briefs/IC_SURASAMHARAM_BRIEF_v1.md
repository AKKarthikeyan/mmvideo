## 2. Rule 5: how Murugan is shown

**If AK extends the rule (path A).** Each figure is a rim-lit silhouette known by one attribute:

| Figure | Known by | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Murugan | the vel, upright in his hand | One head, youthful build, faceless. The six faces are never drawn; the six appear only as six flames on the lake at his birth. |
| His mount and banner | the peacock; the rooster on the banner | Birds, drawn in code like the kit's other birds. They appear only after the tree is split. |
| Surapadman | a tall crowned silhouette, then the mango tree in the sea | No demon caricature: no fangs, horns or dark-skin coding. He is a king. Faceless like everyone else. |
| The cosmic form shown to Surapadman | light only | Same treatment as Krishna's form in Gita 11. |

**If AK says no (path B).** Murugan is the vel and nothing else: a leaf-bladed spear of light, standing or in flight,
never a hand on it. This is not a workaround; the vel is worshipped on its own as his form. His birth is six flames
on the water. Surapadman is a shadow across the sky, then the tree. The peacock and rooster are still drawn (they are
birds). Every beat below works on either path; where they differ the scene list says so.

**The translation for the screen (rule 3)**

- **Proposed: J. V. Chelliah, *Pattupattu: Ten Tamil Idylls* (Colombo, 1946 or 1947).** A library authority record
  (VIAF) gives his death as 1947, which would make it public domain in India since 2008 (life plus 60 years). That is
  one catalogue record, not a biography. **Before any card is built: confirm the death year from a second source, and
  copy the lines from a scan of the book, not from a website.** The 1985 Tamil University reprint may carry new
  front matter; use only Chelliah's text.
- **Kanda Puranam in English: nothing quotable found.** The Akila Sivaraman English edition (Giri) is modern:
  reference only. Layne Little's and the murugan.org translations are modern: reference only.
- **Option for AK:** show the Kanda Puranam verse in Tamil on the manuscript card with a line-by-line rendering of
  ours, headed `OUR RENDERING`, checked by the reviewer. This goes beyond rule 3 as written, so it needs his ruling.
  Without it, the film's only verse cards are the two Sangam passages, and the Kanda Puranam is named in narration.
- **Kit work either way:** the manuscript card is built for Devanagari. Tamil needs a Tamil face (Noto Serif Tamil, OFL)
  and the corpus needs a Tamil source entry so nobody types a verse by hand (rule 2).

## 5. The film

- **Title:** Why Murugan Rides His Enemy
- **Alternates:** The Enemy Became the Peacock · What the Vel Did Not Destroy
- **Spine:** The spear ended the war, and the enemy was kept.
- **Length:** about 6:30. **Format:** VoxPlus: a real question by 0:05, a hook at most every 45 seconds.
- **Looks:** Lamplight (L) for the text's world. Line art on warm paper (P) for the retelling and the present.
  The manuscript card (M) for every verse.

**Structure, against the draft's A/B/C.** The draft recommends C (open on the modern problem). This brief opens on
the peacock instead, and brings Arun in at the midpoint. Reason: the channel's difference is the sourcing, and the
draft's first 30 seconds ("imagine someone who humiliated you") could open any motivational video, while "the bird
he rides was his enemy" is a fact most viewers half-know and have never had explained.

**What changed from the draft, and why**

- The modern story is one continuous middle (beats 12 to 20) instead of two scenes split by the lesson, so the
  apology lands as the ending of Arun's story.
- The list of four transformations (anger, jealousy, fear, pride) is cut to two with concrete wording. The three
  "practical lessons" are dropped from the film; they can be the pinned comment.
- "Nothing magical has happened" is kept; the film does not promise that the story fixes a life.
- Every interpretive beat sits behind the `OUR NOTE` turn at 3:35 (rule 4). The draft's own closing note says the
  lessons are not in the Kanda Puranam; the film now says so on screen.
- Muruga never speaks, and nothing is narrated in his voice (rule 1).
