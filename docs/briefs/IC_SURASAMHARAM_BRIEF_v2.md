## 2. How the figures are shown

Each is a rim-lit silhouette known by one attribute:

| Figure | Known by | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Murugan | the vel, upright in his hand | One head, youthful build, faceless. The six faces are never drawn; the six appear only as six flames on the lake at his birth. |
| His mount and banner | the peacock; the rooster on the banner | Birds, drawn in code like the kit's other birds. |
| Surapadman | a tall crowned silhouette, then the mango tree in the sea | No demon caricature: no fangs, horns or dark-skin coding. He is a king. Faceless like everyone else. |
| Parvati | the hand that gives the vel | One beat only (beat 7). A standing silhouette, taller than the child, faceless; no other attribute. |
| The cosmic form shown to Surapadman | light only | Same treatment as Krishna's form in Gita 11. |

**The translation for the screen (rule 3)**

- **Proposed: J. V. Chelliah, *Pattupattu: Ten Tamil Idylls* (Colombo, 1946 or 1947).** A library authority record
  (VIAF) gives his death as 1947, which would make it public domain in India since 2008 (life plus 60 years). That is
  one catalogue record, not a biography. **Before any card is built: confirm the death year from a second source, and
  copy the lines from a scan of the book, not from a website.** The 1985 Tamil University reprint may carry new
  front matter; use only Chelliah's text.
- **Kanda Puranam in English: nothing quotable found.** The Akila Sivaraman English edition (Giri) is modern:
  reference only. Layne Little's and the murugan.org translations are modern: reference only.
- **Ruled by AK:** the Kanda Puranam verse is shown in Tamil on the manuscript card with a line-by-line rendering of
  ours, headed `OUR RENDERING`, checked by the reviewer. The verse is chosen once the padalam has been read (section 6).
- **Kit work:** the manuscript card is built for Devanagari. Tamil needs a Tamil face (Noto Serif Tamil, OFL)
  and the corpus needs a Tamil source entry so nobody types a verse by hand (rule 2).

**Claims in the draft, checked**

| Draft says | Finding | Script now says |
| --- | --- | --- |

No century is named for Kachiyappa. |
| "His mother, Goddess Parvati, bestowed the Vel" | Sources split. Those citing the Kanda Puranam say Shiva gives the vel; the Thai Poosam tradition says Parvati gives it. **Not settled against the Tamil text.** | "In the tradition kept at Thai Poosam, his mother places the vel in his hand." The giver is tied to the tradition, not the text. |
| "For six days, Muruga confronted the armies" | The festival is six days. A note on the shaivam.org edition places Surapadman's fall on days seven to ten of the war, so the text's war looks longer. **Not verified.** | "The festival remembers the war across six days." |
| The tree is split; one half a peacock, one a rooster; mount and banner | Consistent across every source found, citing the Kanda Puranam. | Kept. |
| "The Vel represents the sharpness of wisdom" | Devotional reading (the vel as jnana shakti). | Kept, as `OUR NOTE`, attributed to the devotional tradition. |
| Surapadman "represents" ego, anger, jealousy | The Saiva Siddhanta reading names the three brothers as the three bonds (Surapadman as anava, the sense of "I"). Anger and jealousy are our extension. **School attribution from memory; reviewer to confirm.** | Stated as the school's reading for ego; the rest marked `OUR NOTE`. |
| "At Tiruchendur" | Soorasamharam was enacted on the Tiruchendur shore on Sun 15 Nov 2026, about 3.50 pm (Deccan Chronicle; DT Next for the schedule). One report gives "more than three lakh" present; the department had forecast six lakh. | Used in the present-day beat with the source on screen. |

Two things in the text worth adding, **both from memory and to be checked against the Tamil before use**: before the
last strike Murugan shows Surapadman his cosmic form and for a moment Surapadman understands, then the pride returns;
and the text may give Murugan a peacock earlier in the war (Indra as the bird), which would change how we phrase
"became his mount". The script is worded so it survives either answer.

## 5. The film

- **Title:** Why Murugan Rides His Enemy
- **Alternates:** The Enemy You Must Defeat Is Not Outside You (the draft's) · The Enemy Became the Peacock
- **Spine:** The spear ended the war, and the enemy was kept.
- **Length:** about 6:30. **Channel and format:** The Inner Chariot, in the mixed look the North Star sets and
  `IC01_SCRIPT_v2.md` models. Pacing rule carried over from the channel's older VoxPlus format (`DHARMA_CHANNEL.md`,
  21 Oct): a real question by 0:05, a hook at most every 45 seconds. To be checked against the North Star on the Air.
- **Languages:** English original; Tamil and Hindi audio tracks. The Tamil lines on the verse cards are shown, not read
  by the TTS (rule 10); if they must be heard, a human reciter records them. The Tamil and Hindi narration scripts are
  written from the approved English and go to the reviewer with it. Dialogue ("Appa, look") stays natural in each language.
- **Looks:** Lamplight (L) for the text's world. Line art on warm paper (P) for the retelling and the present.
  The manuscript card (M) for every verse.
- **Structure (AK):** Arun first. His evening is the cold open; the story answers the question his night raises; his
  morning closes it.

**What changed from the draft, and why**

- The draft's Scene 1 ("imagine someone who has humiliated you") and Scene 4 (Arun) are merged: the film opens on
  Arun himself, so the opening is a person and not an instruction to imagine one.
- The four transformations (anger, jealousy, fear, pride) are cut to two with concrete wording. The three "practical
  lessons" are dropped from the film; they can be the pinned comment.
- "Nothing magical has happened" is kept; the film does not promise that the story fixes a life.
- Arun's evening is plain storytelling and needs no label. Every beat that reads the text into his life sits behind
  the `OUR NOTE` turn at 4:05 (rule 4). The draft's own closing note says the lessons are not in the Kanda Puranam;
  the film now says so on screen.
- Murugan never speaks, and nothing is narrated in his voice (rule 1).
