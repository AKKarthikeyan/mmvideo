## 5. The film

- **Title:** Why Murugan Rides His Enemy
- **Alternates:** The Enemy You Must Defeat Is Not Outside You (the draft's) · The Enemy Became the Peacock
- **Spine:** The spear ended the war, and the enemy was kept.
- **Length:** about 6:30. **Format:** VoxPlus: a real question by 0:05, a hook at most every 45 seconds.
- **Languages:** English original; Tamil and Hindi audio tracks. The Tamil lines on the verse cards are shown, not read
  by the TTS (rule 10); if they must be heard, a human reciter records them. The Tamil and Hindi narration scripts are
  written from the approved English and go to the reviewer with it. Dialogue ("Appa, look") stays natural in each language.
- **Looks:** Lamplight (L) for the text's world. Line art on warm paper (P) for the retelling and the present.
  The manuscript card (M) for every verse.
- **Structure (AK):** Arun first. His evening is the cold open; the story answers the question his night raises; his
  morning closes it.

**What changed from the draft, and why**

- The draft's Scene 1 ("imagine someone who has humiliated you") and Scene 4 (Arun) are merged: the film opens on
  Arun himself, so the opening is a person and not an instruction to imagine one.
- The four transformations (anger, jealousy, fear, pride) are cut to two with concrete wording. The three "practical
  lessons" are dropped from the film; they can be the pinned comment.
- "Nothing magical has happened" is kept; the film does not promise that the story fixes a life.
- Arun's evening is plain storytelling and needs no label. Every beat that reads the text into his life sits behind
  the `OUR NOTE` turn at 4:05 (rule 4). The draft's own closing note says the lessons are not in the Kanda Puranam;
  the film now says so on screen.
- Murugan never speaks, and nothing is narrated in his voice (rule 1).
