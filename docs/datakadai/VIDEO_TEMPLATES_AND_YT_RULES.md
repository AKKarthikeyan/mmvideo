# Data Kadai: video templates that work, and YouTube's rules (11 Oct 2026)

Two questions answered here: which video templates succeed for India data and map content, and what each one must do
to stay inside YouTube's rules.

- The performance numbers are measured on other channels (view counts, titles, a sample of frames), pulled on
  11 Oct 2026. They are reference points, not forecasts for a new channel. The videos themselves were not watched.
- The rules are paraphrased from YouTube's own help pages, read on 11 Oct 2026. YouTube changes them, and only
  YouTube decides a monetisation review. Links are at the end.

## The short answer

- **Shorts:** a hook-led story about a place, told on one map, one thing on screen at a time, 30 to 45 seconds.
  Story-led India map Shorts had a median of 73,000 views; numbers-and-chart Shorts had 6,200.
- **Long-form:** a 12 to 20 minute narrated map-and-chart video built on a place question (North vs South, a
  big-state duel, "Is X the next Y?"). These reached 3x their channel's median 37% of the time; other topics 13%.
- **The rule that matters most:** YouTube does not pay channels whose videos look template-made and mass-produced
  with little commentary. Code-made templates are allowed, but only when every video has its own story, its own
  script and something the maker adds beyond the numbers. That one rule shapes the template below.

## Part 1. Templates that succeed

### Shorts

Base rate for comparison: 12.4% of 4,103 India-titled Shorts reached 1 million views.

| Template | What it is | Evidence | Do we have it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Place story | How a state or UT joined India, or got its name. One dated event per beat on the map | State formation and merger Shorts: 26.7% reached 1M (60 Shorts). "How X became part of India": 15 Shorts on 9 channels, median 383,000 | No. To build |
| One state, one fact (series) | Fixed layout, a different state and a different fact each time | One state series: 24 Shorts, median 982,000, lowest 212,000 | Partly (Mapped and Beat scenes) |
| District ranking | Districts of one state, lowest to highest, on the state map | Six Tamil Nadu district Shorts on one channel: 684,000 to 2.3M | Engine yes (Tamil Nadu map). District data not collected |
| Data paradox or myth-buster | Two official numbers that seem to disagree, or a belief the table overturns | Curiosity-gap titles: 21.4% reached 1M (103 Shorts). One paradox Short: 4.6M in 9 days | Yes (Myth-buster) |
| Then vs now | One number, two dates | 18.5% reached 1M (81 Shorts) | Yes (Change) |
| "Your state" close | End on every state's number: where is yours? | Comment rates 7 to 12 times the median on personalised hooks | Yes (Mapped) |
| If this state were a country | Size comparison with countries | Hits of 8.4M to 12M on one channel, but flat versions on another sit at 15,000 to 41,000 | No. To build |

What the winners have in common (33 Shorts measured, 42 frames viewed):

- One map fills the frame, one state is highlighted, labels are one to four words or a single big number.
- Median length 44 seconds. Something new appears every one to two seconds.
- The hook is about a place and a surprise, not about a dataset.

What is weak, so not the core:

| Format | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Charts shown as video | Median 6,200 views; 5.7% reach 100,000 |
| Quiz or "guess the state" | 5.9% reached 1M (51 Shorts). Indian state quiz Shorts found in search: median 63 views |
| Plain top-N ranking | 15.5% reached 1M, no better than the same channels' other Shorts |
| State vs state as a Short | 70 Shorts, median 5,100 |
| Standalone GK lists (richest states, capitals, literacy, population) | 79% to 91% of such Shorts are under 1,000 views |

Language is the largest unknown. Of the top 125 Shorts checked, 93 were tagged Hindi. English-narrated chart
Shorts were the weakest group. Text-led maps with no narration reached 5.8M to 11M on one channel, so every Short
should be readable with the sound off.

### Long-form

| Template | Length | Evidence | Do we have it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Place duel or verdict question (North vs South, state vs state, "Is X the next Y?") | 12 to 20 min | 235 place videos: 37% reached 3x channel median vs 13% for other topics. North vs South: 63% (19 videos, 8 channels). Channels of 5,000 to 28,000 subscribers got 380,000 to 1.1M views | No. To build. Data in hand |
| Geography explainer on a map ("Where do Indians live?") | 8 to 15 min | One "why so few people live here" series: 68 videos, median 1.15M, lowest 139,000 | No. Needs Census data |
| "Every year" animated state map with a year counter | 6 to 15 min | Broad subjects: median 587,000 on one channel; narrow subjects about 200,000 | No. Data in hand (14 to 22 years) |
| India vs China, everything compared | 8 to 12 min | India-plus-China titles: median 2.6x channel median (20 videos) | No. Needs World Bank, IMF and UN data |
| One definitive ranking chart per topic | 4 to 8 min | "Richest states of India": 3.0M and 3.3M on small channels. A lottery: repeats of the same chart fell from 62,000 to 2,300 | No |

Weak in long-form: bar-chart races (last-year median 60,000; 73% under 100,000), anything under 3 minutes, anything
over 60 minutes, and "the truth about" titles (5.5% reach 3x, against 14.7% for all titles).

Two cautions on the long-form numbers. Fourteen of the 35 channels were found by searching for state topics, so
their hit rates are an upper bound; the unbiased check is 18% against 9.6% on only 22 videos. And the North vs South
hits came from channels with a presenter on screen, so a faceless version is unproven.

## Part 2. YouTube's rules that apply to this channel

| Rule | What it says (paraphrased) | What it means for Data Kadai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inauthentic content (monetisation; renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 Jul 2025) | No ads revenue for content that looks made from a template or feels repetitive after several videos in a row: minimal variation, slideshows or scrolling text with little narrative or educational value, AI content on generic templates without the creator's own insight. Allowed: a series in one format where each video has a distinct focus; automated tools and templates when the result shows the maker's creative input and educational value | The main risk for a code-made channel. Every video needs its own story, its own script, and a narrated "why this matters" |
| Reused content (monetisation) | No ads revenue for repurposing other people's material without significant commentary. Reading out someone else's text, such as a website or news feed, is named as not allowed | Never read out a press release or a table. Compute, compare, visualise and explain. Hot-scan drafts need an added analysis before they become a script |
| Spam (Community Guidelines, so strikes apply) | Bans using automation or AI to produce large volumes of similar content with minimal changes; bans titles and thumbnails that promise what the video does not deliver; bans rewards for likes or subscriptions. Asking viewers to comment or subscribe is allowed | Do not bulk-upload variants. Every number in a title or thumbnail must appear in the video. "Comment your state" is fine |
| Altered or synthetic content | Disclosure is required for realistic altered content: a real person made to say or do something, altered footage of a real event, a realistic scene that did not happen. Not required for clearly unrealistic or animated content, or AI help with scripts, thumbnails and infographics. Disclosing does not reduce reach or earnings | Animated maps and charts do not need the label. YouTube's pages do not mention text-to-speech narration either way (see "Not settled") |
| Misinformation | Bans misleading people about how, when or where to take part in a census; bans doctored content and old footage shown as new | Census 2027 videos: dates and steps only from the gazette notification. Our notes do not yet confirm the main-phase dates |
| Advertiser-friendly guidelines | Titles, thumbnails, descriptions and tags are all judged. Educational treatment of domestic abuse, sexual abuse, suicide and similar topics can get limited ads; child abuse as the main topic gets limited or no ads. Hate or mockery of a group gets no ads. Alcohol shown without glorifying it is fine | Statistics on violence and abuse may earn less; keep them out of thumbnails and titles. No mocking any state or community. The alcohol and tobacco maps are fine as statistics |
| Made for kids | General-audience content is not "made for kids". Simple games, songs and early-learning language push a video toward it. The creator is legally responsible for the setting | Set the channel to "not made for kids". Keep quizzes adult in tone |
| Shorts monetisation | Unedited clips, re-uploads and bot views earn nothing. Shorts over one minute that contain claimed third-party content are blocked. Shorts can be up to three minutes | Own music and own visuals keep this clean |
| Partner Programme | 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts-feed watch time does not count toward the 4,000 hours. A lower tier (India is listed) at 500 subscribers, 3 uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 hours or 3 million Shorts views gives fan funding but no ads. Reviewers look at the main theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, biggest share of watch time, titles, thumbnails, descriptions and the About section. Monetisation can be switched off after 6 months without uploads | Watch hours come from long-form only. The most-viewed and the newest videos must both pass the "inauthentic content" test on the day of review |

Outside YouTube's rules, but the same risk family:

- **Map file.** The state boundaries come from a public GitHub repository under the MIT licence. Its README does not
  name the original source. Compare the outline against the Survey of India map once before monetisation.
- **Voice.** MiniMax's terms for commercial use of the generated voice have not been checked.
- **Fonts and music.** Oswald and Inter are open-licensed fonts; the music is composed by our own code.

## Part 4. The recommended template, with compliance built in

### Short (30 to 45 seconds)

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 2 s | Hook on the map: a place plus a surprise or a question. Readable with no sound |
| 2 to 25 s | Three to five beats, one thing on screen at a time. Each adds one number with its unit and period. The narration says why, not only what |
| 25 to 38 s | Every state's number on the map: where is yours? |
| Last 3 s | Source line (publisher, table, period) and one question to comment on |

### Long-form (12 to 18 minutes)

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Cold open, 30 s | The question and why people argue about it |
| Scoreboard | The two sides, the measures, the sources |
| 6 to 10 chapters | One measure each: map, then chart, then what it means, then the caveat, then the running tally |
| Verdict | Who leads on what, with each side's strong and weak numbers. No "better" or "worse" |
| Sources | Every table, period and unit |

### Checklist for every video

1. The narration contains at least one sentence of our own reasoning: why the number is what it is, or why it matters.
2. The story and script are new. No two uploads in a row use the same template and the same hook type.
3. The title and thumbnail claim only what the video shows.
4. Source, period and unit are on screen and in the description.
5. The description says how it was made: data analysed and animated by Data Kadai from the named source; narration by an AI voice.
6. Audience is set to "not made for kids".
7. Sensitive topics are neutral and non-graphic, and never in the thumbnail.
8. Census, election and health claims come only from the official notification, or are left out.
9. Music and visuals are our own; no third-party clips.
10. No "#1" claim on a thin lead (our own rule; it also keeps titles honest).

### Checklist for the channel

- The About section says who makes the channel and how the videos are produced.
- The upload mix shows more than one format in any ten videos, and includes long-form.
- No bulk uploads of near-identical videos. One a day is fine when each differs.

## Not settled

- **AI voice.** YouTube's pages do not say whether a generic text-to-speech narrator needs the altered-content label.
  My reading is that it does not, because the label is for realistic altered content and for real people's voices.
  Saying "AI voice" in the description costs nothing and is the safer habit.
- **How strictly the template rule is enforced.** Channels that repeat one template dozens of times are live and
  large in our sample, but enforcement cannot be seen from outside, and those channels are survivors.
- **English narration.** No measured case of an English-narrated, faceless India map Short breaking out.
- **A threshold change.** One third-party article claims the Partner Programme thresholds rise in February 2027.
  YouTube's own page, read today, still shows 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 hours or 10 million Shorts views. Treat
  the claim as unconfirmed.
