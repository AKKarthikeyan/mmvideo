# Data Kadai: YouTube strategy

Prepared 11 October 2026 for AK. Plan window: Monday 12 October to Thursday 10 December 2026 (60 days). The first 30 days (to Tuesday 10 November) are dated.

## How to read this document

- Virality comes first. Each idea carries a virality score out of 10. **These scores are the editors' judgment of how likely people are to send the video to someone. They are not predictions of views.** No surviving idea scored above 7.
- "Measured" means it comes from the three research reports (Shorts India, long-form India, long-form global), pulled on 11 October 2026 with yt-dlp from public channel pages. Those numbers describe other, mostly established channels. They are not forecasts for ours. We have zero videos and zero subscribers.
- "Proposal" means my suggestion for AK to accept or change. "Assumption" means nobody measured it.
- 32 ideas were shortlisted and then reviewed by three red-team reviewers (growth, accuracy, viewer). Every idea with two or more KILL votes was dropped. Eight survived, each with at most one KILL vote: six Shorts and two long-form. The compact idea list holds 63 further ideas that were not red-teamed (most have "data not checked"). By my count the supplied lists hold 95 ideas in total; the critic counted 81, so the pool grew after the critic's pass.
- The full record of all 95 ideas (outline, numbers, scores, data check, every reviewer's vote) is in [YOUTUBE_IDEAS_CATALOGUE.md](YOUTUBE_IDEAS_CATALOGUE.md).
- **Added after review (11 Oct):** the strategy writer worked from shortened notes, so a few outlines and reviewer comments reached it cut off. Those gaps have been filled here from the full records. Templates are built in Claude sessions; AK's own hours go to checking scripts, approving and posting. YouTube's rules for these formats are in [VIDEO_TEMPLATES_AND_YT_RULES.md](VIDEO_TEMPLATES_AND_YT_RULES.md).

## 1. The short version

1. **The slate is thin, and honest about it.** Eight ideas survived the red team. Virality scores are 7 or below. The six surviving Shorts fill about two and a half weeks of posting (to 29 October); after that the plan needs a refill (see section 10).
2. **Lead with the hook, not the data.** Measured on other channels: story-led India map Shorts have a median of 73,000 views; chart-style number Shorts have a median of 6,200. Every pick below was chosen for a hook (a paradox, scale made literal, an origin story). The official number is the proof, not the point.
3. **Shorts order:** S003 Bihar 5G first (highest score, 7.4; the only idea all three reviewers kept; low effort; closest copy of a measured breakout). Then SG08 (Uttar Pradesh sugarcane, low effort), S032 (Andhra Pradesh fish), S008 (Higher or Lower, a test), S015 (Goa, highest virality alongside S003 but the Act must be read first), and S006 (Punjab groundwater) last because it is the most sensitive.
4. **Two long-forms.** L057 Karnataka vs Tamil Nadu first (target Sunday 8 November, slips a week if the kit is not ready), then L070 as the annual "richest states per person" table, timed to the RBI Handbook in December. Both need a new 16:9 kit. L057's "low effort" label is only true once that kit exists, because the idea that was meant to build it (L056) was killed.
5. **Run a 60-day test before committing to volume.** Compare template families (all-states, beat, place-story) and English narration against text-led maps. The research flags language as the biggest reach risk: 93 of the top 125 Shorts checked were Hindi. Section 9 gives proposed stop-or-continue thresholds.
6. **Trust is the differentiator.** Every frame shows publisher, table, period and unit. Pin a comment with the source on each video and keep a visible corrections note. Say "level" instead of "#1" when a lead is thin.
7. **What we will not do:** politics, religion or caste frames, secession-style what-ifs, quiz series before the test, standalone top-state GK lists, and 36-state config runs that look like a template factory (section 2.4).
8. **Expectation.** We cannot say what our hit rate will be. The only honest planning numbers are two bands: the chart-Short band (median 6,200) and the map-story band (median 73,000). The Partner Programme needs 10 million Shorts views in 90 days or 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, plus 1,000 subscribers. At a 73,000 median it would take about 137 Shorts to reach 10 million, so the Shorts route needs hits, not averages. Long-form is the steadier route.

### 2.3 What hit rate to expect

- **We do not know.** Every number above comes from channels that already had an audience or survived to be found. Treat the map-story band (median 73,000; 44.4% reach 100K) as the prize and the chart band (median 6,200; 5.7% reach 100K) as the floor to plan on for any Short whose hook is only a number.
- **What 10 Shorts can tell us (simple arithmetic, assuming independence):** if zero of the first 10 reach 100K, that is unremarkable in the chart band (about 56% chance) and very unlikely in the map-story band (about 0.3%). So zero of 10 would say we are in the chart band, and the fix is the hook, not volume.
- **Long-form:** on small channels, a place video cleared 10K views 80% to 83% of the time and 100K views 39% to 46% of the time. Those channels were found because they had hits, so read these as upper bounds. Among 16 channels with full histories, 8 had a 100K+ video within their first 14 uploads.
- **Watch hours (assumption, retention not measured):** a 15-minute video holding 40% of viewers is 6 minutes per view, so 4,000 hours needs about 40,000 views. At 30% it needs about 53,000.
- **Early reads are not final.** The viral Shorts sampled had a median age of 215 days; many compound over months. A 14-day read is an early signal only.

### 2.4 Mechanisms we will not use

| Skipped | Why |
|---|---|
| Ruling party by state, surname by state, religion or caste maps (35M, 11M, 4.8M, 780K views) | Hard rule: no politics, caste or communal ranking. The research shows politics earns no premium inside long-form channels either (6.9% of outliers against 7.2% of videos). |
| "Independent country" or "never existed" what-ifs (8.4M, 12M) | Needs neutral wording. We use "contribution", never secession. A flat version also fails (Data Duck 15K to 41K). |
| "Truth about", "reality", "secret", "exposed" titles | Measured below base (5.5% and 11.2% against 14.7%). |
| "Failed state" or "100 times better" framings | Mockery of a state breaks the hard rule. |
| Historical-boundary "Every Year" maps (Ollie Bye's India video, 9.4M) | Breaks the official-boundary rule; historical shapes not in hand. |
| Genetics and appearance of regions; war "Every Day" videos | Neutrality and violence rules. |
| Real leaders' faces, copyrighted clips | Hard rule. |
| Stock tips, price targets, personal-finance explainers | SEBI finfluencer rules. Also below base on top-decile rate (8.7%). |
| Single-policy explainers ("Make in India": 0.3x and 0.4x of channel median) | Poor performance. |
| Bar-chart races as a core format | Low odds (last-year median 60K). |
| Standalone top-state lists (richest, population, literacy, capitals) | 79% to 91% of Shorts under 1,000 views. Use only as a supporting beat. |
| 36-state config runs and near-duplicate chart variants | The "template factory" risk, and repeats of the richest-states video decayed from 62K to 2K. |

## 3. Content pillars and series

Five pillars. Every series below comes from a surviving idea. Cadences are proposals.

### Pillar 1: Origins (how a place became part of India)

- **Series: Became India** (from S015).
- **Format:** 35 to 45 second Short. Official-boundary map, one dated event per beat on a timeline ribbon, zoom to the place, one "today" number, a closing question. Neutral dated wording, the Act or notification named on screen, no weapons or flags.
- **Template:** new Place Story. Config = place, dated events (date, Act or notification, one neutral line), today number (table, value, period).
- **Cadence (proposal):** one Short a fortnight once the Goa Short is verified. Candidates the engine can run later (not shortlisted, need AK's call): Puducherry, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Lakshadweep, Sikkim.
- **Why it is the lead pillar:** it is the most open proven lane in the research (26.7% at 1M+). Only one origin idea survived, which is the weak spot (section 10, question 2).

### Pillar 2: Paradoxes and belief flips

- **Series: Data Paradox** (from S003).
- **Format:** a Short built on two numbers that should not go together, then the map, then a guardrail line, then "where does your state stand?".
- **Template:** existing Myth-buster.
- **Cadence:** one episode exists. Do not promise a weekly series until a second paradox is found and checked. Candidates must clear the same bar: a clean contrast with a built-in argument.

### Pillar 3: Scale made literal

- **Series: Bigger Than Two** (S032). Template: new Bigger-than-two bar (one bar against two stacked bars, a variant of the countdown beat). Cadence: one episode, plus its stated follow-up (Maharashtra's financial services against Tamil Nadu plus Delhi) only if the first does better than a chart Short.
- **Series: India Without [State]** (SG08, to be retitled around the state's contribution). Template: existing Breakdown (100-square grid), state greyed inside the whole official outline. Cadence: two episodes at most in the first 60 days. The idea file lists a slate of further states (West Bengal jute and mesta 79.8%, Rajasthan wool 47.5%, Andhra Pradesh fish 28.0%, Tamil Nadu factory workers 16.0%); running all of them would look like a template factory.
- **One-off: S006** (Punjab groundwater). Template: existing Breakdown with a small change (extra squares spill past 100). No series yet. If it works, "100 squares" is a candidate series.

### Pillar 4: Games

- **Series: Higher or Lower** (S008).
- **Format:** five head-to-head rounds, a 2-second think beat, then the real numbers and a running score; close on "your score in the comments".
- **Template:** the existing state-vs-state-over-five-rounds template, re-skinned as a higher-or-lower duel.
- **Cadence:** three episodes at most as a test, one a fortnight. The research found no Indian precedent for quiz Shorts, so no series decision until three have run.

### Pillar 5: Long-form anchors

- **Series: Rivals** (L057). Format: 14-minute narrated scoreboard between two named states, each round card showing both values, unit, period and source, with a separate "level" count for thin leads. Template: new Scoreboard Duel 16:9. Cadence: one every four to six weeks once the kit exists; the shortlist has no second surviving pair, so the next pair needs a new idea.
- **Series: Handbook Day** (L070). Format: 12 to 18 minute narrated map explainer with a pause-and-guess beat. Template: new DK Long Map Explainer, the flagship that defines the long-form engine. Cadence: once a year, refreshed each December when the RBI Handbook is published (usually December; not re-verified).

### On the bench (not red-teamed, data mostly unchecked)

- Eight ideas need World Bank, IMF or UN data we have not collected (scores 3.5 to 5.4): S009, S024, S040, L054, L055, L066, L074, L075. The research's strongest global hook is India-plus-China (2.6x), which lives here.
- District ideas S010 and S021 (4.2 and 5.4) need district data not collected. L067 and L071 need Census. L014 and L027 need historical boundary shapes, which conflict with the official-boundary rule.
- No idea covers Assam or the wider Northeast as a subject. The nearest bench item is LG04, "Assam Economy" (4.7, data not checked).

## 8. What the red team said

### Overall criticisms

**Growth reviewer** (kept 7 of 32: six Shorts, one long-form; judged from the idea text only, measured nothing):
1. About a dozen ideas are top-state-for-a-crop-or-economy lists relabelled as myth-busters or quizzes. That is the lane where 79% to 91% of Shorts are under 1,000 views, and the "myth" is often a straw man or just a size effect.
2. The proven breakouts win on pride, oddity or conflict. Our neutrality rules remove the conflict, so the replacement must be a sharper oddity or identity flip, not more numbers. Too many scripts put caveat, definition or "today" cards before the payoff, or pack five or six beats into one Short (S002, S017, S032, SG01, S015).
3. The list tells one story several times: roughly 15 distinct stories in 32 ideas (Bihar 5G, women in work, North vs South, richest states, state splits, crop rankings). The 36-state config run in S019 is exactly the repetitive-template pattern YouTube penalises.
4. Four ideas (S008, S031, S007, S039) bet on an Indian quiz format with no precedent (35 Shorts, median 63 views). The supporting evidence is global.
5. The long-form half is mostly 12-number scoreboards that withhold a verdict or a "why", built for a faceless synthetic-voice channel with unproven retention. Long-form is the watch-hours route, but it will not go viral from a cold start.

What the growth reviewer would change: run a 10-video hook test before building any new template (two origin stories, one paradox, two scale or identity flips, one time-locked Short); one idea per Short, with the oddity in the first two seconds and caveats after the payoff or in a pinned comment; add one Tamil Nadu-anchored Short from data in hand; build the duel engine once, for Karnataka vs Tamil Nadu, with a verdict per round; hold North vs South until the channel has an audience; drop any hook type whose first three tests all land at or below 6,200 views.

**Accuracy reviewer** (kept 15 of 32: 11 Shorts, 4 long-form; read only the two named files, could not open the PDFs, Acts or RBI notes):
1. Winner-takes-all scoreboards on charged pairings (S005, L056, L012, LG13, L063, L026) rest on hand-picked rounds, thin leads, zone definitions or a chosen year. L012's own recompute shows the women's labour-force winner flips between 2023-24 and 2025, and that South's median 5G share (61.3%) beats North plus Central (54.0%). The safe version of North vs South is a state-level dot plot with no zone crown and no tally.
2. Incomplete tables read as national ranks. The 2024-25 income table has 25 of 34 entries and lacks Goa, Sikkim and Gujarat, so "Delhi 1st", "Uttar Pradesh 24th" and "India's No. 2 richest" are false as national claims. Use 2023-24 and say "of the 33 reporting", or use 2022-23, the only year with all 34.
3. Nominal against real. L063 compares parent and child states in current prices. Chhattisgarh against Madhya Pradesh is 1.07x nominal but 1.32x at constant prices; Jharkhand against Bihar is 1.90x to 1.68x nominal but 1.90x to 1.88x at constant prices. Trend claims must use T020.
4. One hand-typed DoT table underpins five ideas. The data editor's "verified" only recomputes from the same file; it does not check the file against the source. Cheap fix: sum the typed state rows and compare with the printed India total (367,156 of 644,131), then check Bihar and Telangana against the PDF. Add "villages, not people" and "operator-reported".
5. The women's labour-force trio (S022, S004, S039): the measure counts job-seekers and unpaid family work, small-state samples are noisy, and the periods are mismatched. If one such Short is wanted, make it a single-year snapshot with the definition in the hook.
6. History Shorts (SG01, S015, S017): the facts are from memory and not in the source map. Unrelated "today" numbers tacked onto origin stories imply causation. Do these only after reading the Acts, with no "today" card.
7. The thin-lead rule is applied inconsistently. Define it once, for example under 1 point of share or under about 3% relative means "level", and let the builder enforce it.
8. Wording that overstates: "keeps" for net value added, "produces" for per-person income, "phones per 100 people" for connections, "highest in India" for a 20-state table, "disappears" and "India Without", and "where do most women work".

What the accuracy reviewer would change: one Bihar 5G Short (S003) and no near-duplicate; one richest-states video (L070); confirm the estimate stage in the RBI note for every 2024-25 crop figure; a one-line check before publishing each idea (source row re-checked, period and unit on screen, "among states with data", no cause stated).

**Viewer reviewer** (watching as a person in Uttar Pradesh, Assam or Punjab, not a Tamil Nadu data reader):

By the reviewer's count, 11 of 32 ideas centre on a South Indian hero state, and the rest sit in thin lanes: crop tables (seven), women in work (three), richest states (three), North vs South (three), Kerala (three), Bihar 5G (three), state splits (two).

- Comments per 10,000 views, against the measured benchmarks: median about 1.3 per 10,000; "your state" and argue-about-it hooks about 9 to 15 per 10,000.
- Likes against views (measured median 1.63%).
- Shares, if Studio reports them for your account (public share counts are not available).

**Long-form (per video, read at 48 hours, 30 days and 60 days):**
- Impressions, click-through rate, average view duration and average percentage viewed, plus the retention curve (find the beat where viewers leave).
- Views at 30 days, new against returning viewers, subscribers gained, watch hours, views per subscriber.
- Ratio to our own channel median, but only after about 8 uploads.

**Channel:** subscribers (1,000), public Shorts views in the last 90 days (10 million) and watch hours in the last 12 months (4,000).

### 9.2 The 60-day test (12 October to 10 December 2026)

Two comparisons, run together.

**Test A: template family.** Tag every Short with its family and compare medians.
- *All-states family* (existing): S003 (Myth-buster), S006 and SG08 (Breakdown).
- *Beat family*: S032 (Bigger-than-two bar), S008 (re-skinned five-round duel).
- *Place-story*: S015 (Goa) and any further Place Story episodes AK approves.

**Test B: English narration against text-led maps.** Assign styles by the alternation in section 6 (S003, S032 and S015 narrated; SG08, S008 and S006 text-led, then alternate for follow-ups). Never upload the same story twice.

**Limits you should accept up front:**
- With arms of about 3 to 8 Shorts and different topics in each arm, only a several-fold gap is believable. Treat the result as a direction, not a verdict.
- Only one matched pair exists from survivors (S032 against SG08, both "scale made literal"). A second pair for origin stories (Goa plus Puducherry) and a third for paradoxes need ideas that did not survive.
- The place-story arm has one episode, which cannot be judged. It needs at least three.

## 10. Open questions for AK

1. **Language.** The research's biggest reach risk: 93 of the top 125 Shorts checked were Hindi, English chart Shorts perform worst, and the hard rule says English only. Text-led maps with no speech reached 5.8M and 11M on one channel. For long-form, English is not a risk (English titles hit 3x+ at 15.0% against 12.0% for Hinglish), and 297 search suggestions carry "tamil", so a Tamil track has real demand later. **My recommendation:** keep English-only, run the 60-day narrated-against-text-led test, and decide on the Hindi or Tamil track on the day-60 result. Do you agree, or do you want the track opened earlier?
2. **Re-open the origin ideas?** The Place Story arm of the test needs at least three episodes and only Goa survived. Puducherry (SG01) was killed by accuracy and viewer, not growth. The accuracy objection (dates from memory) is fixable by reading the Acts and treaty text; the viewer objection is a script fix. Do you want to override and re-open it, with Lakshadweep, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and Sikkim behind it?
3. **Long-form order and kit.** Do you accept L057 first (the kit is built for a Karnataka against Tamil Nadu duel that already has two winning versions on other channels), or would you rather open with L070 on the Handbook?
4. **L070 timing.** Should L070 wait for the December RBI Handbook (which may fill Gujarat's gap; that is an assumption) or ship earlier on 2023-24 data? My proposal: wait for the Handbook, but publish by mid-December either way.
5. **Quiz formats.** S008 has no Indian precedent (35 Shorts, median 63 views). Keep it as a three-episode test? The batch-001 script file also holds 22 Guess-the-State scripts. Hold them until the test reads out?
6. **Sensitive survivors.** S006 (Punjab; farm-policy territory; the viewer reviewer killed it) and S015 (the 1961 military operation). Do you want to review the final scripts before they publish, and do you want S006 held?
7. **State balance.** The killed ideas skewed to the South; the survivors are more mixed (Bihar, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Goa, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu) but Assam and the Northeast appear in none. Do you want a Northeast or Assam story in the first 60 days? The only bench candidate is LG04, "Assam Economy" (score 4.7, data not checked).
8. **Refilling the Shorts pipeline.** The six surviving Shorts run out around 29 October. Options: (a) red-team the bench ideas that have data in hand; (b) re-open killed ideas whose objection is fixable (SG01 is the clearest); (c) cut Shorts from the two long-forms; (d) all three. Which?
9. **Hours.** The build list is long (the 16:9 kit, two new Short templates, a re-skin, two Breakdown variants and the verification pass). The templates are built in Claude sessions, so the limit is your review time: checking scripts, reading the Goa Act text, approving and posting. If that is short in weeks 1 to 4, drop S008 and S032 first and keep S003, SG08, S015 and the kit.
10. **Request-your-state loop.** Do you want a pinned comment inviting state requests, with a few answered as future episodes? It is the critic's cheap trust and engagement idea, and it does not require a 36-state run. The risk is the template-factory look, so requests would be answered one at a time.
