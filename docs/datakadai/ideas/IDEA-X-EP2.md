# Idea X, Episode 2 script: "The Emergency Pot: How Much Should Your Family Keep Aside?"

Draft 1, 17 Oct 2026. About 6 minutes. Same voice as Episode 1: no grown-up words without a plain explanation, every number turned into "what it means for your family".

**Sources (official, read 17 Oct 2026):**
- MoSPI's household spending survey (HCES 2023-24, 2,61,953 households): money spent per person per month, every state, villages and towns separately, without the value of free items.
- NCFE (the government's financial-education body), "Financial Wellbeing" page: "Aim for 3-6 months' worth of living expenses in your fund."
- SEBI's investor-education pages: "Saving = Income - Expenses" and "Don't save what is left after spending, spend what is left after saving."

**Our arithmetic:** a family of four (a stated example; the survey counts per person), × 3 or 6 months. These are 2023-24 spending figures. The video says so, and says prices have risen since.

## 1. The question (cold open)

*Screen: a calendar with a few pages torn off; a small pot of coins.*

> Imagine your family's earnings stopped for a while.
> Someone falls ill, a job ends, the roof leaks in the rain.
> The bills don't stop. Food, rent, school fees all keep coming.
> That's why grown-ups keep a small pot of money just for bad days. It's called an emergency pot.
> How big should it be in your state? Let's work it out.

## 2. The rule

*Screen: NCFE's sentence, word for word.*

> The government's financial-education body, NCFE, gives a simple rule:
> keep three to six months of your family's living costs aside.
> Living costs means everything a family spends in a month: food, rent, bills, travel, school.
> So first we need to know: what does a family spend in a month?

## 3. What a family spends

*Screen: a survey worker with a notebook at a door; then two numbers.*

> To find out, the government visited about 2.6 lakh homes across India and asked what they spend.
> In villages, one person spends about ₹4,122 a month on average. In towns, about ₹6,996.
> Families are different sizes. Let's take a family of four.
> A village family of four: about ₹16,488 a month. A town family of four: about ₹27,984.

## 4. India's emergency pot

*Screen: two pots, each filling to "3 months" and "6 months".*

> Now the rule. Three months to six months.
> A village family: about ₹49,000 for three months, and about ₹99,000 for six months, close to one lakh.
> A town family: about ₹84,000 for three months, and about ₹1.68 lakh for six.

## 5. Your state

*Screen: two maps, villages then towns, each state showing its six-month pot.*

> Here is every state. The pot is bigger where families spend more.
> The smallest: a village family in Chhattisgarh, about ₹66,000 for six months.
> The biggest: a town family in Sikkim, about ₹3.34 lakh.
> In Tamil Nadu: about ₹1.37 lakh in a village, about ₹1.96 lakh in a town.
> In Bihar: about ₹88,000 in a village, about ₹1.22 lakh in a town.
> *(A tile lets every viewer find their own state; the map shows all 36 states and territories.)*

## 6. Two things to remember

*Screen: two short notes.*

> One: these numbers are what families spend, not what they need. A family with little money may spend less because it has less.
> Two: this survey is from 2023-24. Prices across India have gone up about 9% since 2024, so today's pot would be roughly a tenth bigger.

## 7. How a pot gets filled

*Screen: a coin dropping into the pot every month; a counter.*

> A pot of ₹99,000 sounds huge. Nobody fills it in one go.
> SEBI, which looks after people's investments, puts it simply: saving is what is left when you take your spending away from your earnings.
> And it gives one rule worth remembering: don't save what is left after spending. Spend what is left after saving.
> If a village family keeps aside ₹2,000 every month, the six-month pot fills in about four years. The three-month pot, in about two.

## 8. Where the pot sits

*Screen: the pot next to a small key.*

> In the last episode, we saw that money kept in a tin shrinks as prices rise.
> But an emergency pot has a different job. It must be ready on the day trouble comes, not locked away.
> That is why it usually sits somewhere you can reach quickly.
> *(Screen note: this is not advice on where to keep money; NCFE and SEBI's pages are linked in the description.)*

## 9. Next

> So now we know the small pot. Next episode: why a town family needs more than a village family, state by state.
> Tell us in the comments: does your family have an emergency pot?

## Notes for AK

2. **The rule is NCFE's, quoted word for word.** The "₹2,000 a month" example is our arithmetic, with no interest added, which keeps it simple for children.
3. **The "about 9% since 2024" price line** comes from MoSPI's new index: September 2026 stood at 109.49 against 2024 = 100. That's a national figure, so the video says "roughly".
4. **Figures are rounded for speaking** ("about ₹99,000"). The screen shows the exact rupees.
5. **Chapter 8 is deliberately vague** about where to keep the pot. Naming a product would be advice.
