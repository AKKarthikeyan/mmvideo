# Episode 1 script: "Why Your ₹100 Shrinks: Ten Years of Prices in Every State"

Draft 1, 17 Oct 2026. About 6 minutes. Written for a 12-year-old: no word a child would not know, and every number turned into "what it means for you".

**Sources, read today:**
- **Prices:** MoSPI's price index, every month of 2015 and 2025, every state, from MoSPI's own data service. The old 2012-base series, which runs to December 2025.
- **Interest rates:** the National Savings Institute's rate history pages (post office savings account, PPF).

**Our arithmetic:**
- Price rise: each state's 2025 average set against its 2015 average.
- Interest: ₹100 grown at each period's printed rate.

These are past figures, not a forecast or advice.

## 1. The question (cold open)

*Screen: a ₹100 note, then a shopping bag.*

> In 2015, someone in your family kept a hundred-rupee note in a tin.
> Today, it is still a hundred-rupee note. But can it buy the same things?
> Let's find out, with the government's own numbers, for every state.

## 2. What "prices going up" means

*Screen: a basket with rice, milk, vegetables, a bus ticket, a school fee.*

> Every month, the government checks the prices of the things families buy: rice, milk, vegetables, bus fares, school fees, and many more.
> It puts all of them in one imaginary shopping basket.
> Then it asks a simple question: how much does this basket cost now, compared with before?
> That number is called the price index. Think of it as the price tag on the whole basket.

## 3. India's answer

*Screen: two baskets side by side: 2015 ₹100, 2025 ₹158.50.*

> Across India, a basket that cost ₹100 in 2015 cost ₹158.50 in 2025.
> So, prices went up by more than half in ten years.
> Turn it around: the ₹100 in the tin now buys only about ₹63 worth of shopping.
> Nobody stole the money. It just buys less. That is what grown-ups call inflation.

## 4. Your state

*Screen: map of every state, coloured by what ₹100 of 2015 buys in 2025.*

> It was not the same everywhere.
> In Manipur, ₹100 of 2015 buys only about ₹54 worth today: prices rose the most there.
> In Kerala, about ₹59. In Tamil Nadu and West Bengal, about ₹61.
> At the other end, Meghalaya: about ₹71. Delhi: about ₹70.
> So the same note shrank faster in some states than in others.
> *(Arunachal Pradesh has no complete figure for these years, so it is left out and the map says so.)*

## 5. Villages and towns

*Screen: two bars per state, village and town.*

> Across India, villages and towns ended up almost level: ₹100 of 2015 buys about ₹63 worth in both.
> Inside the states, it was different.
> In Manipur's villages, ₹100 now buys only about ₹50 worth. The note lost half its power in ten years.
> In Meghalaya's villages, it still buys about ₹75 worth.
> In Bihar, the town note shrank more: about ₹61 in towns, ₹67 in villages.
> In 20 states and territories, the town note shrank more. In 15, the village note did.

## 6. Three places to keep ₹100

*Screen: three jars: a tin, a post office savings account, PPF.*

> Now, a question a child can answer: where should the ₹100 have been kept?
> **The tin:** ₹100 stays ₹100. It buys about ₹63 worth now.
> **A post office savings account:** it has paid 4% a year since 2011. ₹100 grew to about ₹148. That sounds good, but the basket grew to ₹158.50, so it still fell a little behind. It buys about ₹93 worth.
> **The Public Provident Fund, PPF:** its rate moved from 8.7% down to 7.1% over these years. ₹100 grew to about ₹207, more than the basket. It buys about ₹131 worth.
> The lesson is not "put your money here". The lesson is: money that earns less than prices rise is slowly shrinking, even when the number goes up.

*Screen note: these are past rates; rates change every three months; PPF has a 15-year lock and a ₹1.5 lakh yearly limit; not advice.*

## 7. The idea to remember

*Screen: a race: "prices" against "your savings".*

> Think of it as a race.
> Prices are always running, about four to five steps a year across India.
> Your savings must run at least that fast just to stay in the same place.
> The RBI tries to keep prices running at 4 steps a year. That is its target.

## 8. Next

> So money left alone gets smaller. How much should a family keep aside for bad days, in your state?
> Next episode: the emergency pot.
> Tell us in the comments: what did ₹100 buy in your childhood?

## Notes for AK

1. **"Inflation", "price index" and "PPF"** are the only grown-up words. Each is explained the first time and then used sparingly.
2. **The tin, savings and PPF chapter** is the strongest part, but it names a government scheme. I've written it as "what happened", with the limits on screen, so it doesn't read as a recommendation.
3. **The figures are our arithmetic** from official tables. The video says so once in chapter 2 and once on the end card.
4. **The price story stops in December 2025**, where the old index ends. The new 2024-base index can't go back ten years by state.
5. **Length:** about 6 minutes.
