## What the official data can and cannot do

**Can:**
- **What a family spends, every state.** MoSPI's household spending survey (HCES 2023-24) gives money spent per person per month for all 36 states and union territories, village and town separately. All India: ₹4,122 (village), ₹6,996 (town). Lowest: Chhattisgarh villages, ₹2,739. Highest: Sikkim towns, ₹13,927. A full state map is possible.
- **What saved money earns.** The government's own savings rates: PPF 7.1%, Senior Citizens scheme 8.2%, post office monthly income 7.4% (NSI pages to 30 Sep 2026; the Oct-Dec notice is a PDF). These are the same everywhere.
- **How fast prices rise.** All India averaged about 4.7% to 5.0% a year over ten years (our arithmetic from MoSPI's price index). The RBI's target is 4%, renewed to 2031. A state-by-state ten-year average can be worked out from the old index, ranging from about 3.1% (Meghalaya) to 6.3% (Manipur).
- **What people earn, every state.** MoSPI's jobs survey (PLFS 2025) gives earnings per worker: salaried ₹22,699 a month, self-employed ₹14,861 a month, casual work ₹453 a day (All India).
- **Official plain-language rules to quote, not invent.** NCFE (the government's financial-education body) prints the emergency-fund rule: keep 3 to 6 months of living expenses. SEBI prints: "Saving = Income − Expenses" and "Inflation reduces the value of money".

**Cannot:**
- **No official "magic number".** No official page gives a rule like "25 times your yearly spending". So any "how much is enough" figure has to be our own arithmetic, with its assumptions on screen.
- **No family size by state on a web page.** The survey counts spending per person. State family sizes are in a PDF. Without it, the video uses "a family of four" as a clearly stated example.
- **No state figure that mixes village and town.** The video would show village and town separately, which is clearer for viewers anyway.
- **Spending is not need.** A poor family may spend little because it cannot afford more. Prices also differ between states. The video must say both.
- **The data is from 2023-24.** It needs one stated step to bring it to today's prices.

## Example of what the video could say (our arithmetic, family of four, 2023-24 prices)

| Place | Family spends a month | Emergency pot (6 months) | Pot that pays for 25 years* | "Forever" pot* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bihar, village | ₹14,680 | ₹0.88 lakh | ₹34 lakh | ₹0.84 crore |
| All India, village | ₹16,488 | ₹0.99 lakh | ₹39 lakh | ₹0.94 crore |
| All India, town | ₹27,984 | ₹1.68 lakh | ₹66 lakh | ₹1.60 crore |
| Tamil Nadu, town | ₹32,660 | ₹1.96 lakh | ₹77 lakh | ₹1.87 crore |
| Sikkim, town | ₹55,708 | ₹3.34 lakh | ₹1.31 crore | ₹3.18 crore |

\*If the money earns 7.1% a year (the PPF rate) and prices rise 5% a year. The "forever" pot is very sensitive to that gap: with prices rising at the RBI's 4% target, it drops by about a third. The video would show this as a slider, not one hard number.

## Topic options

**X1. "How much money would your family need to stop working? Every state."** (the core idea, about 7 minutes)
- **Flow:** what one family spends in a month (with a mango-tree picture: the pot is the tree, the interest is the fruit you eat) → why prices rising makes the tree need to grow → the two answers (25 years, and forever) → every state on the map, village and town.
- **Strength:** answers your question directly.
- **Risk:** depends most on assumptions. It needs careful words so it doesn't sound like advice.

**X2. "Step one: the emergency pot. How much should sit aside in your state?"** (about 5 minutes)
- **Flow:** uses the official NCFE rule (3 to 6 months of living expenses) on each state's spending.
- **Strength:** the fewest assumptions and an official rule, so it is safest and most practical. It would make a good Short too.

**X3. "Why your ₹100 shrinks: prices in your state over ten years."** (about 6 minutes)
- **Flow:** what ₹100 of 2015 buys now in each state, and what interest a savings account must earn just to stand still.
- **Strength:** the most school-friendly. It sets up X1.

**X4. "How long would your state's typical worker take to fill the pot?"** (about 6 minutes)
- **Flow:** set PLFS earnings against HCES spending, state by state.
- **Strength:** relatable.
- **Risk:** the weakest data fit. Earnings are per worker and spending is per person, so it needs several assumptions. I would not lead with it.

**X5. "City or village: how much more does a town family need?"** (about 5 minutes)
- **Flow:** the town-to-village gap in each state (Meghalaya's town families spend twice as much as its villages, Kerala's only 18% more).
- **Strength:** a clean map with no money assumptions.

**My suggestion:** make X1 the long-form, with X2 and X3 folded in as its first two chapters. The flow becomes "the emergency pot first, then why prices shrink money, then the big pot". That gives one story told in the order a child would learn it. X5 can be a Short.
