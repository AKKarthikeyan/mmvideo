# LF02 script: Dussehra to Diwali: India's Festival Season in Numbers

Draft 1, 17 Oct 2026, for AK to read before anything is built. About 7 minutes (LF01's 811 words ran 7:28; this draft has about 820). Post Sat 24 or Sun 25 Oct.

Every figure below was read at its publisher and confirmed by a second checker on 17 Oct 2026, except lines marked **[CHECK]**, which are read before voicing or cut. Figures in rupees are what the screen shows; the voice says them in words.

---

## 1. Cold open

*Screen: a calendar page, "Diwali · Sunday 8 November 2026".*

> This year, Diwali falls on a Sunday.
> Of the fourteen all-India holidays on India Post's list for 2026, it is the only one that does.
> Before the lamps go up, here is the festival season in official numbers.
> The dates, the kitchen, the sweets, the vehicles, the journeys home, and the cash.
> Every figure is from the government office that publishes it.

## 2. The calendar

*Screen: a strip of five dates.*

> Dussehra is on Tuesday, 20 October.
> Diwali, on Sunday, 8 November. Chhath, on Bihar's list, on Monday, 16 November.
> Guru Nanak's birthday falls on Tuesday, 24 November, and Christmas on Friday, 25 December.

*Screen: the 13 circles named — Arunachal Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Kerala, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Odisha, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Tripura, West Bengal.*

> Some places get a longer Dussehra.
> In thirteen of India Post's thirty-one circles, post offices also close on Monday, 19 October.

*Screen: RBI office list, October 2026.*

> Banks follow the Reserve Bank's holiday list, office by office.
> This October it lists six holidays for Agartala and five for Kolkata.
> Mumbai and New Delhi get two.

Sources: India Post holidays list 2026; RBI Holiday Matrix, October 2026.

## 3. The kitchen

*Screen: one row per item; three columns: 16 Oct 2024, 16 Oct 2025, 16 Oct 2026.*

> Now the shopping list.
> Every day, the Consumer Affairs department publishes the average retail price of essentials across India.
> On 16 October, a kilo of onion cost ₹52.70.
> On the same date last year, ₹26.88. That is ninety-six per cent more in one year.
> But two years ago it was ₹54.73. Onion is back near its 2024 price.
> Sugar: ₹57.82 a kilo, against ₹46.60 a year ago. Up twenty-four per cent.
> Jaggery costs more, at ₹66.41, but it rose less: fifteen per cent.
> Desi ghee: ₹704.51 a kilo, up from ₹666.98.
> Mustard oil: ₹205.30, up nine per cent. Tur dal: ₹131.06, up fourteen per cent.
> Some things cost less.
> Potato, at ₹22.65, is cheaper than a year ago, and cheaper than two years ago.
> Tomato, at ₹40.74, is a little dearer than last year, but far below 2024's ₹67.96.
> And milk: ₹61.44 a litre, up three and a half per cent.

Source: Department of Consumer Affairs, Price Monitoring Division, daily retail prices, 16 Oct 2026 (the page also gives one and two years back). **Re-read on the day of voicing**, and change the date on screen if the prices move.

## 4. Sweets and milk

*Screen: two bars.*

> The festival runs on sweets.
> In the official price index, milk-based sweets cost 9.36 per cent more this September than last.
> Sugar in the same index: 29.8 per cent more.

*Screen: state map, milk per person per day, 2023-24.*

> Most of those sweets begin with milk. So which state has the most milk per person?
> In 2023-24, India produced 471 grams of milk per person per day.
> Punjab: 1,245 grams. Rajasthan: 1,171. Haryana: 1,105.
> At the other end, Delhi with 62 grams, and Mizoram with 56.
> This is milk produced in a state, divided by its people. It is not what people drink.

Sources: MoSPI CPI (base 2024), September 2026, provisional (items "Prepared sweets, milk based" and sugar, the same reads as LF01); NDDB per capita availability of milk, 2023-24 (from the Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics; unit grams per day printed on NDDB's milk production page).

## 5. Vehicles

*Screen: four bars: Oct 2024, Sep 2025, Oct 2025, Oct 2026 so far (shaded as unfinished).*

> Then the big purchase.
> In September 2025, 19.31 lakh vehicles were registered across India.
> In October 2025, 41.70 lakh. More than double in a month.
> And about forty-two per cent more than in October 2024, when 29.32 lakh were registered.
> **[CHECK]** Last year, Dussehra and Diwali both fell in October. This year, Diwali falls in November.
> When we read the dashboard on 17 October, this October stood at 11.71 lakh, with two weeks still to go.
> We will come back with November's count.

Source: Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, VAHAN public dashboard, all vehicle classes, read 17 Oct 2026. The 42 per cent is our arithmetic on the two printed counts. The dashboard's own "growth" column is month on month; it is not used. The 2025 festival dates (Dussehra 2 Oct, Diwali 20 Oct 2025) still need reading at an official page; if not found, the line goes.

## 6. Journeys home

*Screen: three bars, CPI September 2026.*

> Then comes the journey home.
> In September, domestic air fares in the price index were 29.9 per cent higher than a year before.
> Train fares: 2.22 per cent. Bus and coach: 2.71 per cent.

*Screen: Kolkata Metro, Puja 2025.*

> In Kolkata, Durga Puja fills the Metro.
> On Maha Panchami last year, it carried more than 9.82 lakh passengers, its highest ever for a single day.
> From Panchami to Dashami, 46.56 lakh rode the Metro, against 41.15 lakh the year before.
> **[CHECK]** *Slot for Puja 2026: Metro Railway Kolkata issues a release after each Puja day; if 2026's figures are out by 22 Oct, one line compares them. If not, the line goes.*

Sources: MoSPI CPI, September 2026, provisional; Metro Railway Kolkata press releases of 28 Sep and 3 Oct 2025.

## 7. The cash

*Screen: two figures, RBI.*

> And the cash.
> The RBI tracks the currency in circulation: the notes and coins out in the economy.
> On 15 September, it was 13.2 per cent higher than a year before, up from 12.4 per cent a month earlier.
> In mid-October last year, the growth rate was 8.8 per cent.
> The RBI does not say why it has risen, so we won't guess.
> **[CHECK]** *Slot: the railway staff bonus (78 days' wages, over 11.40 lakh staff, approved 14 Oct). Read the PIB release first; with the exact amount, one line goes here. If not, it goes.*

Source: RBI Bulletin, September 2026 (as on 15 Sep and 15 Aug 2026) and November 2025 (as on 17 Oct 2025).

## 8. Close

*Screen: recap tiles, then end card.*

> So: a Sunday Diwali, onion at nearly double last year's price, sugar up almost a quarter, potato cheaper.
> And last October, more than twice as many new vehicles as the month before.
> Next, we look at gold before Dhanteras.
> Which price are you watching this festival season? Tell us in the comments.
