## Rounding and the 0.3 rule (decision rule d)

- The rule is applied in integer tenths on the printed figures. In floating point 2.0 - 1.7 is 0.30000000000000004 and 1.4 - 1.1 is 0.2999999999999998, so a naive test miscounts.
- 16 up, 12 down, 7 same. Of the 16 up: 10 by exactly 0.1, none by 0.2, 6 by 0.3 or more. Of the 12 down: 5 by 0.1, 1 by 0.2, 6 by 0.3 or more.
- Printed rise of 0.3 or more: Lakshadweep +0.8, Chandigarh +0.4, Jammu and Kashmir +0.4, Goa +0.3, Ladakh +0.3, Nagaland +0.3. Still 0.3 or more once the NFHS-6 figure is allowed to be off by 0.05 against the NFHS-5 report's two-decimal figure: Lakshadweep, Chandigarh, Jammu and Kashmir only.
- Printed fall of 0.3 or more: Meghalaya -0.7, Andaman and Nicobar -0.4, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Mizoram -0.3 each. After the same allowance only Meghalaya and Andaman and Nicobar stay at 0.3 or more; the four at -0.3 are "about 0.3".
- Claims by place against the rule: Jammu and Kashmir passes; Nagaland passes on printed figures only; Goa and Ladakh pass on printed figures only and have small samples; Kerala is level (1.8 to 1.8); Tamil Nadu is -0.1, "about the same".
- The 0.3 threshold is our editorial rule, not a statistical test (no state-level errors are published).

## How to recompute the counts and correlations (run from the repo root)

```python
import json, math
from decimal import Decimal as D
S = json.load(open("public/datayt/nfhs6/nfhs6_states.json"))["states"]
P = [k for k in S if k != "India"]                       # 35 places, Manipur absent
v = lambda k, i, c: D(S[k][i][c].strip("()"))            # c: 2 = NFHS-6 total, 3 = NFHS-5 total
t6 = {k: v(k, "18", 2) for k in P}; t5 = {k: v(k, "18", 3) for k in P}
d = {k: t6[k] - t5[k] for k in P}                        # Decimal, so 2.0 - 1.7 is exactly 0.3
print(sum(t6[k] <= D("2.1") for k in P),
      sum(d[k] > 0 for k in P), sum(d[k] < 0 for k in P), sum(d[k] == 0 for k in P),
      sum(d[k] == D("0.1") for k in P), sum(d[k] >= D("0.3") for k in P), sum(d[k] <= D("-0.3") for k in P))
def r(ind, c):                                           # Pearson r of indicator ind vs TFR across the 35 places
    x = [float(v(k, ind, c)) for k in P]; y = [float(v(k, "18", c)) for k in P]
    mx, my = sum(x) / 35, sum(y) / 35
    return sum((a - mx) * (b - my) for a, b in zip(x, y)) / math.sqrt(sum((a - mx) ** 2 for a in x) * sum((b - my) ** 2 for b in y))
print(round(r("12", 2), 2), round(r("16", 2), 2), round(r("12", 3), 2), round(r("16", 3), 2))
# expected: 30 16 12 7 10 6 6 / -0.36 0.29 -0.52 0.46

# NFHS-4 to NFHS-5 (C38): 33 comparable places, Manipur included
F = json.load(open("public/datayt/long/babies/figures.json"))
r1 = lambda x: D(str(x)).quantize(D("0.1"), rounding="ROUND_HALF_UP")
pa = {k: (r1(v["nfhs4"]), r1(v["nfhs5"])) for k, v in F["tfr_report_2dp"].items() if k != "India" and "nfhs4" in v and "nfhs5" in v}
pa["Manipur"] = (r1(2.61), r1(2.17))                     # NFHS-5 report Table 4.3; Manipur is not in NFHS-6
dd = {k: b - a for k, (a, b) in pa.items()}
print(len(pa), sum(x <= D("-0.3") for x in dd.values()), sum(x >= D("0.3") for x in dd.values()),
      sum(D("-0.3") < x < 0 for x in dd.values()), sum(x == 0 for x in dd.values()), sum(0 < x < D("0.3") for x in dd.values()))
# expected: 33 14 0 14 3 2   (the same counts come from the 2017 ministry release S12 against the Lok Sabha annexure S11)
```

Indicator ids were checked against the fact-sheet wording on 28 Oct 2026 and `scripts/datayt/nfhs6_meta.py`: 12 = women with 10 or more years of schooling (age 15-49), 16 = women age 20-24 married before 18, 18 = total fertility rate (children per woman), 2 = population below age 15, 3 = population age 60 and above. (The NFHS-5 fact sheets number these differently, 16, 20 and 22, so never mix the two numberings.)
