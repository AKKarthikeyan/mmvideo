## 2. The data contract

`LookStory` turns the built `data.json` (a `BeatData`, unchanged) into a `LookData` and hands the look `{L, B}`
(`LookProps`). Every string is data.json's own; the look fields are additions.

`LookData`: `D` (the original), `id`, `type`, `look`, `brand`, `lookOpts`, `seed` (FNV-1a of the id, 1 or more), `plate`
(`datayt/covers/<id>-gemini.png` when it exists, else null; `look_opts.plate` can name another or `false`), `frames`,
`barF` (58.06) and `beatF` (14.52), `textLed` (vo is empty: the text-led cut), `chip`, `sourceShort`, `sourceLines`,
`sourceLabel`, `timing` ("measured" or "none") and `timingMethod`, the story's own `accent`, `accent2`, `bands` (for the
closing map), and `beats`.

Each `LookBeat` is the original beat plus:

| field | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `i`, `f0`, `f1` | index; first frame and first frame after (Math.round(t × 30), as BeatStory) |
| `settleF` | round((t1 − 0.4) × 30): the beat must be still from here (scene_frames.py stills) |
| `lockF`, `lockHow`, `lockWord`, `late` | the frame the beat's number may land on, why, the spoken words; `late` if it falls in the last 12 frames |
| `hero` | fact: its `big`; spot/say: the first \*starred\* token holding a digit ("3×", "over 25×", "₹1.13 lakh crore") |
| `big` | fact `big` |
| `tokens` | every \*starred\* segment of the lines, with its number if it has one |
| `lineNums`, `textNums`, `qNums` | other printed numbers in lines, in `text`, in the closing question |
| `barsL` | `bars` with each label parsed (`bars` itself untouched) |
| `src` | the per-beat short source line (`look_opts.beatSources[name or kind]`, else `sourceShort`) |
| `subShown` | the sub a look prints (`look_opts.beatSubs`, else `sub`) |
| `voice`, `words` | the beat's narration line and its words with measured absolute times |

Every printed number is a `LookNum`: `text` exactly as printed = `qual + sign + prefix + digits + unit`; `sign` is the
printed character (U+2212 for minus); `prefix` (₹); `digits` with their grouping kept ("2,00,000", `grouping`: none,
thousands, lakh, western); `unit` as printed (" t", "%", "×", " lakh cr", " of 11", " years"); `value` (signed, unit
scale not applied); `scale` (1e7 crore/cr, 1e5 lakh, 1e12 lakh crore); `decimals`; `glyphs`; plus `role`, `lockF`,
`spokenF` and `lockHow`. The parser (`shared/numbers.ts`) and `word_times.py` use the same token rule, so a number joins
its measured lock by its exact text.

**The lock rule in code:** a digit may be drawn only from its `lockF`. Use `shown(n, f)` to choose between the number
and a glyph-free shape (blank tile, streak reel, empty card), and `assertLocked(n, f)` where digits are drawn: a frame
before the lock throws, so the harness fails instead of a Short shipping a number early. No count-up tweens: the only
values ever on screen are the printed ones.

`look_opts` (config `look_opts`, copied into data.json as `lookOpts`; until a Short is rebuilt the preview reads it from
the config through `registry.ts`): `sourceLabel`, `sourceLabelMatch`, `sourceShort`, `beatSources`, `beatSubs`,
`sourceLines`, `plate`, and anything a look reads (`variant`, `surface`, `lead`, `unit_object`...).

Run the `LOOK-data` still to see a story's contract: `npx remotion still src/index.ts LOOK-data out/looks/gd01-data.png
--props='{"id":"gd01"}'`. `LOOK-kit` shows the shared pieces (grain, vignette, all seven editor's marks, the bug on dark
and light, every font role on ₹2,00,000 · −0.63% · 3× · 25,000 t) in a brand and look:
`npx remotion still src/index.ts LOOK-kit out/looks/kit.png --frame=50 --props='{"look":"counter"}'`.

## 3. Brand tokens: how a look reads them

A look never types a colour, a font or a logo. It receives `B: LookBrand` (the brand resolved for that look):

- `B.c.<role>`: palette roles `ground ink paper accent accent2 deep muted` and derived tones `ray line empty softInk
  accent2Text accent2Light newsprint newsprint2` (tones of the same palette, named so nobody types a hex).
- `B.f.<role>`: `display hero mono serif body wordmark`, each `{family, weight, stretch, tnum, css}`; spread `B.f.hero.css`
  into a style, or pass `fitFont(B.f.hero)` to the fit functions. Fonts can differ per look (`lookFonts`): for Data Kadai,
  Slab uses Anybody + IBM Plex Mono, Counter Bricolage Grotesque + Space Mono, Dispatch Newsreader + Schibsted Grotesk;
  the wordmark is always Oswald 700. `hero` has tabular figures on (`'tnum' 1`) wherever the face's digits are proportional.
- `B.chip`, `B.safe` (x 95-850, y 210-1480 at 1080x1920: clear of both YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels overlays),
  `B.mapRamp` (null keeps the story's own bands), `B.Mark`, `B.Wordmark`, `B.wordmark.stacked`.
- `B.pairOk(fg, bg, px)`: true when that text-on-ground pair is allowed at that size. A pair listed in the tokens passes at
  4.5:1 at any size, at 3:1 only at its `minPx` (and at least 40 px), never below 3:1 or when it is on the `never` list
  (amber on cream for Data Kadai).

Data Kadai: maroon #7A2340 (accent), amber #C9822F (accent2), cream #F7F4EE (paper), ink #1F1A17 (ground, ink),
#4A1226 (deep), #7A6F66 (muted); Mark = the tea glass of `src/datayt/Brand.tsx`. Indica Atlas: deep #06202C (ground,
ink, deep), teal #0C3D52 (accent), amber #FFB020 (accent2), cream #F7F1E3 (paper), sea #11597A (muted); map ramp india
#3DBE8C to deep; Mark = the India-facing globe with the monsoon arrow, reproduced here from `MMVideo/src/brand/Brand.tsx`
(coastlines baked to JSON, so no cross-repo import and no d3-geo). Try any preview in the other brand:
`npx remotion still src/index.ts DATA-fm01-look-dispatch out.png --frame=980 --props='{"brand":"indicaatlas"}'`.

### Adding a channel brand

1. Copy `src/looks/brands/indicaatlas.tokens.json` to `<id>.tokens.json` and fill every palette role and derived tone,
   the faces (vendored under `public/fonts/looks/<family>/` with their OFL.txt), the font roles, `lookFonts`, chip, safe
   box, contrast pairs and `never` list, wordmark parts.
2. Write `<id>.tsx` exporting a `Brand` (`{...tokens, Mark, Wordmark}`); draw the mark in code.
3. Add it to `BRANDS` in `brands/index.ts`, `BrandId` in `brands/types.ts` and `LOOK_BRANDS` in `build_beats.py`.
4. `.venv/bin/python scripts/datayt/look_tokens.py` (contrast and fonts) and `look_fonts.py` (glyphs) must pass; then
   `look_stills.py <look> --brand <id>` and look at the sheets.

## 4. Fonts

Vendored from github.com/google/fonts (SIL OFL 1.1, each with its OFL.txt) and checked by `look_fonts.py` for ₹ U+20B9,
− U+2212, × U+00D7, %, +, ·, and digits that line up:

| face | file | axes | digits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anybody | anybody/Anybody-VF.ttf | wdth 50-150, wght 100-900 | proportional by default; tnum 900 units (tnum on in `hero`) |
| IBM Plex Mono Bold | ibmplexmono/IBMPlexMono-Bold.ttf | static | 600 units |
| Bricolage Grotesque | bricolagegrotesque/BricolageGrotesque-VF.ttf | opsz 12-96, wght 200-800, wdth 75-100 | tnum 580 |
| Space Mono Bold | spacemono/SpaceMono-Bold.ttf | static | 612 units |
| Newsreader | newsreader/Newsreader-VF.ttf | opsz 6-72, wght 200-800 | tabular by default, 0.55 em |
| Schibsted Grotesk | schibstedgrotesk/SchibstedGrotesk-VF.ttf | wght 400-900 | proportional; tnum 1300 (wide commas: labels stay proportional) |

**Noto Serif Display is replaced by Newsreader** for Dispatch: the critic was right, it has no U+2212 (`look_fonts.py`
on the google/fonts file: "MISSING minus U+2212"), so "−0.63%" and "−28%" would fall back to another face. Newsreader is
a news serif with optical sizes up to 72, all the glyphs, and tabular digits 0.55 em wide (Noto at wdth 66 was 0.559 em).
Its heaviest weight is 800 (the spec asked for 860). Oswald stays the wordmark only (`public/fonts/Oswald-700.woff2`, a
latin subset: letters only). At render time `LookFonts` loads each face with the FontFace API under `delayRender`, then
probes its characters against two unlike fallbacks: a glyph the face lacks fails the render.

`fit.ts` measures with the loaded face in a hidden span attached to `document.body` (outside any `scale()`, so a look
inside BeatPosts' scaled frame measures the same) and throws if a look face is measured before it loaded: measure only in
components rendered inside `LookFonts` (LookStory already does this for the look component).

- `BeatStory`: `D.look ? <LookStory D={D}/> : <BeatStoryBase D={D}/>`. Composition ids, durations, size and fps are

  post_assets.py keep working.
- `BeatPosts`: `DK-bp-x`, `DK-bp-ig` and `DK-bp-end` take `{id, look?, brand?}`; with a look (from data.json or the props)
  they render `LookXPost`, `LookIgBeat`, `LookIgEnd`. `DK-bp-map` and `DK-bp-list` keep the existing map drawing.
- Previews need no config edit: `DATA-<id>-look-<look>` for the 11 stories × 3 looks.
- Audio: `LookAudio` plays the same music.wav and vo_XX.mp3 at the same times with the same duck (0.14 under the voice,
  0.45 within 0.2 s, 0.5 in the text-led cut). Looks may add SFX `cues` (ducked to 40% under the voice); remember
  music_shorts already puts a hit on every beat t0.
- Timing: data.json t0/t1 are the truth; `grid.ts` gives bar downbeats and `snapAfter()` (snap a cue forward, never back).

## 6. Word timings (blocker 3)

`scripts/datayt/word_times.py` measures the narration that is already voiced; it never voices anything and never writes
data.json.

1. Each line is transcribed by faster-whisper 1.2.1 (model: the cached `medium`; `$WT_MODEL` picks another; nothing is
   downloaded unless `WT_ALLOW_DOWNLOAD=1`), with word timestamps and the line's own text as the prompt so numbers come
   back as words. Audio is decoded by Remotion's own ffmpeg (the venv's PyAV 19 is too new for faster-whisper's decoder).
   Transcripts are cached in `out/looks/word_cache/<id>/` by the mp3's hash, so a re-run takes seconds.
2. The script's words are aligned to the transcript (Needleman-Wunsch; digits whisper writes are spelled out, years as
   years), so every word of the line gets a time.
3. Number phrases are found in the script ("twenty-five thousand", "one point one three lakh crore", "five thousand
   seven hundred and seventy-seven crore", "twenty twenty-two" as a year, "six and a half", "twenty-five and twenty-eight
   thousand" giving 25,000). **Each onset is measured on the audio, not taken from whisper.** Whisper's word start is
   wrong in three ways: it sits on the first vowel of a word that opens with a fricative (it missed the 170 ms /f/ of "to
   fifteen"), it puts a word that follows a pause at the pause's start ("value: twenty-eight" 450 ms early) and it can sit
   inside the first vowel after a pause ("less; two" 130 ms late). The audio is cut into 2.5 ms frames (10 ms window,
   centred) with three levels: the pre-emphasised total (so a weak f / th / s still shows), the 80-1000 Hz band (voicing)
   and the 2.5-8 kHz band (hiss); each frame is silence, sonorant or obstruent. The number's first word picks the rule:
   - fricative (four, five, six, seven, three, thirteen, thirty, fourteen, fifteen, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, zero):
     the frication run that ends at the word's first vowel. The previous word's voiced /z/ ("rose four") or louder hiss
     ("cars four") is cut off, a closure ends the run ("cost | four"), a voiced blip after a stop release puts the onset at
     the blip ("cost[ə] four"), and a hiss two words share with no voicing change ("was zero", "rose zero") is split in
     the middle.
   - stop (two, ten, twelve, twenty): the burst, half-way up the rise out of the closure.
   - n / w (nine, nineteen, ninety, one): the fall into the murmur after a vowel ("higher nine"), the release of the
     previous word's closure ("moved one", "held ninety"), the rise out of its nasal ("from one"), the edge out of a
     silence, or the middle of a nasal two words share ("on nine").
   - vowel (eight, eighteen, eighty, eleven): the edge out of a silence or of the previous word's last consonant, or the
     rise out of the dip between two vowels ("to eighty"), never the voicing after a stop release when the previous word
     ends in a vowel (that is the "-ty" of "eighty").
   Whisper's start, moved to the end of a pause it falls in (or back to the end of a pause when it sits inside the first
   vowel after it), only picks the nearest candidate inside a window per rule.
4. The lock is on the rendered timeline: the narration line plays from frame round(at × 30) (BeatStory and LookAudio),
   so `lockF = ceil((round(at × 30) / 30 + onset + margin) × 30)`, margin 10 ms after a stop burst (a sharp event) and 25 ms
   after any other boundary. A number therefore lands 0.3 to 1.75 frames after its measured onset.
5. Each printed number is matched to a spoken one by value (within 3%; "about eight thousand" matches 8,134 t; years only
   match years). Lock rules per beat: the beat's own number spoken in its line -> that onset ("spoken"); already said in an
   earlier line -> beat start + 6 ("said-earlier"); never said -> the first number the narrator says in the beat
   ("first-number"); no number or no voice -> beat start + 6. Bar labels and other numbers: their own onset, else beat start
   + 6 if said earlier, else the beat's lock.
6. A word_times.json is used only if its lines match data.json's narration exactly (text and start time); a re-voiced
   Short falls back to "no-timing" until the script runs again.

Result for the 11 stories (77 beats): 41 locks on the beat's own spoken number, 7 said earlier, 9 on the first number
spoken, 20 beats with no number (say lines without one, maps, end cards); no lock falls in a beat's last 12 frames. The
critic's colon-pause case is real: gd01 "Homes and temples: about twenty-five thousand" locks at f183 (the burst of
"twenty" at 2.085 s), not the f174 of the character estimate.

**Measured error.** Every one of the 80 spoken numbers was read by eye off a 1600 px/s spectrogram of the existing
narration with the three levels drawn under it (sheets in `out/looks/word_onsets/z_00.png` .. `z_19.png`, not in git;
magenta = the reading, red = the measured onset, yellow = the lock frame's start, blue = whisper). The readings are in
`scripts/datayt/word_onsets_checked.json`, with an alternate where another boundary is defensible (a /t/ release before
the frication, a shared sound's ends, the critic's own reading). `word_times.py --check` compares every lock with them and
exits 1 if any lock is not 0 to 3 frames after its reading. The readings are not blind: they were made with the
detector's marker on the sheet (four earlier versions of the detector were wrong on 3 to 20 numbers each and were fixed
until every marker sat on the boundary), so checks (a) to (c) below are the independent ones.

| | all 80 numbers | the 41 beat locks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lock minus onset, frames | min 0.26, median 1.10, max 2.29 | min 0.44, max 2.29 |
| locks before the reading | 0 | 0 |
| locks more than 3 frames after | 0 | 0 |

The critic's five sampled locks, against the critic's own onsets: cw01 "ten" f260 (+0.6), fs01 "nine point four" f486
(+1.3), fm02 "zero point eight eight" f460 (unchanged: +1.35 after the middle of the hiss "was zero" shares), cw01
"fifteen" f428 (+0.6 after 2.5125 s; was f432, +4.6), fm05 "fifteen thousand" f567 (+1.55 after the /f/ at 1.315 s; was
f572). For fm05 the critic read the /f/ at 1.26 s; that is the /d/ release of "under", whose /ər/ formants run from 1.263
to 1.31 s, so against 1.26 s the lock would be +3.2.

The old rule (the later of whisper's start and an energy valley within 120 ms) was not only late. Against the same
readings it put 9 of the 41 beat locks early: fm03 "twenty-eight" 12.6 frames early (whisper put it at the start of the
pause after "value:"), fm04 "one point one three lakh crore" 7 frames early (same, after "fund:"), fs01 "twenty-nine
point eight", fm05 "two lakh" 3.2, fm01 "fifty-nine" 0.7, and four more 0.2 to 0.4 frames early (the energy window was
labelled by its start, and the line's start was taken as `at`, not the rendered frame). 9 more were over 3 frames late.

Independent checks. (a) The critic's own measure (no-prompt whisper start, pre-emphasised envelope, deepest valley in
-200/+80 ms, 25% of the rise) agrees with the lock (0 to 3 frames after) on 30 of the 41 beat locks; each of the other 11
was looked at on the spectrogram and the valley is in the wrong place: in the previous word ("itself", "up", "was",
"rose"), inside the nasal before the vowel ("higher nine", "on nine"), later inside the number (the vowel after the /f/ of
"rose four", the second syllable of "all fifty-three"), late inside the first vowel after a pause ("fund: one", "value:
twenty-eight"), or the /d/ release of "under". (b) Whisper (medium, no prompt) on the narration cut at each lock frame:
from the lock on it hears the number in 38 of 41 (it mishears "two lakh" as "do like" and "ten years" as "then yours",
and drops the "eight" of "eight thousand nine hundred and seventy-four", whose lock is 1.3 frames after a breathy onset);
before the lock it hears the word before the number, or the number's first sound only ("held no-", "at 10" from 35 ms of
"ten", "rose to" from the burst of "two"). (c) The rendered audio of DATA-gd01-look-slab and DATA-fm02-look-slab,
cross-correlated with the line's mp3, puts the narration at 133.0 ms (at = 120.0 ms) and 600.0 ms after frame 160
(at - 5.333 s = 592.7 ms): the line plays from round(at × 30) / 30, as the lock formula assumes.

## 7. The harness

```
.venv/bin/python scripts/datayt/look_stills.py <slab|counter|dispatch|all|a,b> [ids...] [--brand indicaatlas] [--concurrency N] [--no-sheets]
```

For every beat of every story it renders `DATA-<id>-look-<look>` at **lock + 8** (just after the number lands) and at
the **settle** frame (t1 − 0.4 s) into `out/looks/<look>/<id>/` (`<look>-<brand>/` for another brand), writes
`index.json` (frames, lock method, lock word), and a contact sheet `out/looks/<look>/<id>-sheet.jpg` (top row lock + 8,
bottom row settle). One bundle with `public/` symlinked (not copied: it is 1.1 GB) and one browser for the whole run, via
`scripts/datayt/look_render.mjs`; it exits 1 if any still or sheet fails (a number drawn before its lock, a missing glyph,
a fit that cannot fit, a source text without gd01's label all throw). `--safe-check` also fails any still with ink outside
x 95-850, y 210-1480 (every pixel outside must be the ground colour; for flat grounds only, so a look whose texture or
plate bleeds should not use it). Checks without rendering: `look_fonts.py`, `look_tokens.py`. The whole run (462 stills,
