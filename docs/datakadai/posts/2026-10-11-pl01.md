## YouTube Shorts
**Title** (42 characters): How Goa Became Part of India: 1947 to 1987

**Description:**
The dates, each with the document it comes from.

15 August 1947: India becomes independent (Indian Independence Act, 1947). Goa is under Portuguese rule.
19 December 1961: India's armed forces carry out Operation Vijay in Goa, Daman and Diu (Ministry of External Affairs; Press Information Bureau gives 18-19 December). 19 December is a public holiday in Goa.
20 December 1961: Goa, Daman and Diu are a Union territory under the Constitution from this date (Constitution (Twelfth Amendment) Act, dated 27 March 1962).
16 January 1967: opinion poll in Goa. To continue as a Union territory: 1,72,191. To merge with Maharashtra: 1,38,170. Invalid: 7,272. That is 55.5% and 44.5% of valid votes, on a turnout of 81.8% of 3,88,392 electors (Government Gazette, 21 January 1967; shares calculated by Data Kadai).
31 December 1974: India and Portugal sign a treaty at New Delhi. Portugal recognises India's sovereignty over Goa, Daman, Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli from the dates they became parts of India under the Constitution (UN Treaty Series, vol. 982). In force 3 June 1975.
30 May 1987: Goa becomes a state; Daman and Diu remain a Union territory (Goa, Daman and Diu Reorganisation Act, 1987).

Also on the record: on 18 December 1961 the UN Security Council met on Portugal's complaint and adopted no resolution (Yearbook of the United Nations, 1961).
