## Script

### Act 1 · The magic number: 2.1 (0:30 to 2:00)

**VO:** "Demographers watch one number: the total fertility rate. It's the average number of children a woman would
have in her lifetime at today's rates.
The magic line is 2.1. Two children replace the two parents. The extra 0.1 covers children who don't survive to
adulthood. Stay below 2.1 for long enough, and a population eventually stops growing, then shrinks."

**ON SCREEN:** two parent icons → two child icons + a small "0.1"; the 2.1 line drawn across the screen.

### Act 2 · India crosses the line (2:00 to 3:30)

**VO:** "In NFHS-4, in 2015-16, India's fertility rate was 2.2. Just above the line.
In NFHS-5, in 2019-21, it fell to 2.0. For the first time in the survey's history, India was below replacement.
And NFHS-6, from 2023-24, released this June: [VERIFY: 2.0]."

**ON SCREEN:** line chart NFHS-3 `[VERIFY: 2.7]` → NFHS-4 2.2 → NFHS-5 2.0 → NFHS-6 `[VERIFY]`, the 2.1 line
crossing.

**VO:** "But India isn't one number. It's 36 states and union territories, and they are living in different
demographic worlds."

### Act 3 · The map: two Indias (3:30 to 5:30)

**VO:** "Here is NFHS-6, state by state. [VERIFY: count] of 36 are now at or below 2.1.
The lowest: [VERIFY: Andaman and Nicobar, 0.9], the only part of India below one child per woman, then

### Act 4 · The fastest falls (5:30 to 7:00)

**VO:** "Some states changed astonishingly fast. Between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5, in about five years, Nagaland fell from

### Act 5 · What goes with fewer babies? Our analysis (7:00 to 9:00)

**VO:** "So what do low-fertility states have in common? We ran the numbers on all 36 states and UTs in NFHS-5.
Where more women finished at least ten years of school, fertility is lower. Where more girls marry before 18,
fertility is higher. In Kerala, 77 % of women have ten or more years of schooling, and 6 % of young women married
before 18. In Bihar, it's 29 % and 41 %.
A word of caution: this is a pattern, not proof that one causes the other. Income, cities, health care and jobs
all move together. But the pattern is strong and consistent."

**ON SCREEN:** scatter plot, each dot a state: x = women with 10+ years of schooling, y = fertility rate; trend line;
Kerala and Bihar labelled; "Correlation: −0.50" and "Pattern, not proof" caption. Second scatter for child marriage
(+0.45).

**VO:** "Here's what almost nobody expected. Between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5, only two states saw fertility go up:

In NFHS-6: Kerala [VERIFY: 1.8], Tamil Nadu [VERIFY: 1.7]. [FILL: one line on whether the rise held.]"
