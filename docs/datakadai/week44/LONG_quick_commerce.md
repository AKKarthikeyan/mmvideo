## Script

### Act 1 · The old kings (0:30 to 2:30)

**VO:** "For decades, Indian groceries had two kings.
The first is the kirana. In every lane, open early, closes late, knows your name, sends a boy with your order and
writes it in a notebook till the end of the month.
The second is DMart. No ads you'd remember, no fancy stores. Just the lowest prices, every day. It owns most of its
stores instead of renting them, so it can sell cheaper and still make money."

**ON SCREEN:** kirana notebook illustration; DMart "everyday low price" tag; DMart revenue bar
FY25 ≈ ₹57,790 crore, up ~16.7 % `[VERIFY against the FY25 annual report]` 🟢.

**VO:** "And DMart kept growing, year after year. Until something changed."

### Act 2 · The challenger (2:30 to 4:30)

**VO:** "The challenger doesn't have a shop you can walk into. It has dark stores: small warehouses that only
delivery riders can enter, packed close to where you live, so an order can reach you in about ten minutes.
Three companies built this almost overnight: Blinkit, owned by Zomato's parent Eternal; Zepto; and Swiggy's
Instamart."

**ON SCREEN:** map pins multiplying around a city; three logos as labels (text only, no brand artwork without rights).

**VO:** "How big did it get? According to Zepto's own IPO filing, in the year to March 2025 Blinkit handled about
49 crore orders, Zepto about 34 crore and Instamart about 27 crore. Together, about 110 crore orders. That's
roughly 30 lakh orders every day."

**ON SCREEN:** order counters 48.99 cr · 34.03 cr · 27.02 cr → 110 cr; 🔴 CLAIM (company filing).

**VO:** "Inside that race there's a race. Blinkit had about 44 % of the orders, Zepto about 31 %. Instamart, the
early mover, slipped from about a third of orders two years earlier to under a quarter."

**ON SCREEN:** share bars; Instamart 34 % → 24.5 % `[VERIFY: one headline says 21 %; use the filing figure]`.

### Act 3 · The battle (4:30 to 7:30)

**VO:** "Here's where it hit DMart. In the July to September 2024 quarter, sales at DMart stores open two years or
more grew just 5.5 %. Bernstein called it the slowest revenue growth in four years. The stock fell more than 8 % in a
single day, and brokers said the same thing: quick commerce is making a dent."

**ON SCREEN:** DMart same-store growth line bending down to 5.5 %; red "−8 % in a day" stamp; 🟢 (company results),
🟡 (broker commentary).

**VO:** "DMart didn't sit still. Its own delivery arm, DMart Ready, grew about 21.5 % in the first nine months of
that year, faster than the stores. But it promises a delivery slot, not ten minutes.
And the pressure didn't go away. In April to June 2025, revenue grew 16 %, but operating profit grew only 8 % and
net profit was flat. Margins were getting squeezed."

**ON SCREEN:** DMart Ready +21.5 %; Q1 FY26 revenue +16 %, EBITDA +8 %, profit flat 🟢.
`[FILL: latest quarter, Q2 FY27, with one line on whether the squeeze eased or deepened]`

which represents FMCG distributors, said about 2 lakh kirana shops had shut in a year. It blamed quick commerce and
asked the Competition Commission to investigate what it called predatory pricing.
Important: that's the federation's own estimate, not an official count. No regulator has confirmed it."

**VO:** "So who's winning?
