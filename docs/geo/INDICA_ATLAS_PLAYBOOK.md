# Indica Atlas: playbook for super engaging geography videos (v1, 27 Oct 2026)

**AK's question (27 Oct 2026):** "For the geography channel Indica Atlas, how do we create super engaging and interesting videos?"

**The answer in one paragraph.** A geography video grips when it makes the viewer's **own mental map** visibly wrong or incomplete, then fixes it with one clear "why", using the map as the story's stage. Science explains why this works:
- people's world maps are built from biased subregions that a few true "seed" facts can redraw (E26, E27);
- predicting before a reveal fuels curiosity and memory (E4, E31);
- material tied to the viewer's own place sticks (E30);
- vastness that doesn't fit what we know produces awe (E32).

Our own numbers agree: our best Shorts so far were questions about India itself (D1). The formats with fresh 1M+ hits on other channels are "Why (almost) nobody lives here", "Your map is lying" and map quizzes (D2).

This playbook sits on top of:
- the **Story Engine** (`docs/storytelling/STORY_ENGINE.md`: 3 engines, toolbox T1–T16, formats F1–F13, story gates);
- the **Shorts Engine** (`SHORTS_ENGINE.md`: moves M1–M5, compression rules C1–C13);
- the **research rules** (`docs/geo/RESEARCH_RULES.md`).

E-numbers point to `docs/storytelling/EVIDENCE.md`; D1–D2 are our own data (`docs/geo/research/CONTENT_REFRESH_2026-10-25.md`). **[house]** marks existing Indica Atlas rules; **[test]** marks bets to check against analytics.

## 2. Eight levers: why geography grips

| # | Lever | Why it works (evidence) | How we pull it |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **The mental-map error** | People store the world as subregions with systematic biases; one accurate "seed" shifts their whole region (E26, E27). Memory straightens and aligns maps (E28) | Find the place where everyone's map is predictably wrong ("further south than Kerala?", "closer to the Maldives") and give the single seed fact that redraws it |
| L2 | **Predict, then reveal** | Predicting raises curiosity and memory (E4); the gap between expected and real drives learning (E5); quizzing improves long-term memory more than re-reading (E31) | "Guess first." Hold 1–1.5 s. Then reveal. Quiz Shorts invite comment answers too (D2: quiz Shorts are the highest raw numbers) |
| L3 | **Your place** | Linking information to oneself improves memory (E30) | For Indian viewers: name the state, the city, the train, the monsoon they live with. For the world: tie it to the viewer's own map ("as far north as Cairo") |
| L4 | **Awe at scale** | Awe needs perceived vastness *and* something that doesn't fit what you know (E32) | A globe-to-street zoom (our engine) **plus** a fact that breaks the frame. Vastness alone is just a pretty shot |
| L5 | **One clear "why"** | Curiosity is stronger when one insight closes the whole gap (E1); viewers infer causes and ask why each thing is shown (E21) | Every video answers one "why" with one mechanism (a wind, a ridge, a treaty, a river shift), shown on the map |
| L6 | **A person in the place** | Absorption comes from imagery and attention (E7) | One real person per story (Nain Singh's bead count, a Minicoy sailor), drawn from records. Clarity, not guilt (E17) |
| L7 | **Distances you can feel** | Perspective sentences improve recall and estimation (E13) | "398 km: about the distance from [a city pair the viewer knows]" (both distances computed and sourced). Areas vs a country the viewer knows |
| L8 | **The expected answer, ruled out** | Saying the expectation, then beating it, creates the learning gap (E5) | The proven title shape "Why… (It's Not the X)" (D2): name the obvious cause and remove it in the first line |

### The Mercator trap (honesty rule)
"True size" videos are huge (D2), but research finds **no Mercator effect on how big people remember regions to be** (E29). So we never claim "your map fooled you about size". We say what is true: "India is bigger than [countries], combined", computed from official areas.

The Mercator effect **is** real for **directions** (E29). That gives us honest surprises: the real great-circle route between two cities the viewer knows, or which way a famous city really lies from Mumbai on a globe. (These are example angles; compute each route before use.) Compute great-circle directions ourselves and show them on a globe, never on a flat map.

## 3. The 12 Indica Atlas story engines

Each engine is a geography version of a Story Engine format (F…), with its Short moves (M1–M5, SHORTS_ENGINE).

### G1 · Why (Almost) Nobody Lives Here (F10 Paradox + L8)
- **Promise:** a huge or beautiful area that is nearly empty, and the real reason.
- **Research must contain:** population or density from the Census or official sources, the real limiting factor from a primary source, plus the obvious-but-wrong cause, which is ruled out *with evidence*.
- **Short:**
  - M1: "This area is the size of [country], and almost nobody lives there. It's not the [heat/cold]."
  - M2: Where it is, on the globe.
  - M3: The obvious cause, and why it isn't enough.
  - M4: The real factor (water, soil, law, terrain, history).
  - M5: Back to the empty map, now explained.
- **Long:** rule out three obvious causes as chapters → the real cause at about 2/3 → the people who *do* live there → what is changing.
- **Trap:** single-cause "geography decides everything". Places have several causes, and people make choices. Name the main factor and say "together with…".

### G2 · Your Mental Map Is Wrong (F1 Myth Flip + L1)
- **Promise:** "X is closer to / further south of / bigger than you think."
- **Research must contain:** distances and positions computed by us from official coordinates (RESEARCH_RULES 2.2), plus the comparison place.
- **Short:**
  - M1: The surprising comparison ("3× closer to the Maldives than to Kerala").
  - M2: Locate it on the globe.
  - M3: The ruler animation, showing the number.
  - M4: Why the map surprises (the seed fact that redraws the region).
  - M5: Back to the rulers.
- **Variants:** latitude twins ("Kanyakumari is further south than [city]"), direction surprises (E29), longest and shortest routes.
- **Trap:** distances copied from articles. Compute them, and state the method in the source table.

### G3 · Guess the Place: a quiz (F8 / F4 + L2)
- **Promise:** a game. Guess the state from its shape, three clues, or a satellite view.
- **Research must contain:** each clue verified; official outlines (Survey of India).
- **Short:**
  - M1: "Only 1 in 10 get this" (only if measured; otherwise "Can you get this?").
  - M2: Clue 1.
  - M3: Clue 2.
  - M4: Hold 1.5 s, then the reveal.
  - M5: "Comment your score" and the loop.
- **Why:** the testing effect (E31), prediction (E4), and comments (D2).
- **Trap:** clues that are trivia without a "why". Give the answer a one-line reason it's true.

### G4 · Why Here? (F10 / F2 + L5)
- **Promise:** why a city, port, pass or line sits exactly where it does.
- **Research must contain:** the site's physical facts (elevation, river and coast data from SRTM, Survey of India, Bhuvan), the founding record (period sources), and the human decision.
- **Short:**
  - M1: "Why is [city] right here?"
  - M2: Zoom from the globe.
  - M3: The terrain clue (river bend, gap in the hills).
  - M4: The human decision that used it.
  - M5: The city lights at night (NASA), the loop.

### G5 · The Line: borders, enclaves and odd boundaries (F4 Cold Case)
- **Promise:** "Why does the line bend here?"
- **Research must contain:** treaty or gazette text, coordinates (RESEARCH_RULES section 2), the official Indian map, and official wording for any disputed area.
- **Short:**
  - M1: The strange shape.
  - M2: Where it is.
  - M3: Clue: the treaty line or the river it follows.
  - M4: The document that explains it.
  - M5: The shape again, now obvious.
- **Must:** ⚑ sensitive by default; AK checks the wording; no side-taking (RESEARCH_RULES 4.4).

### G6 · From Space (F6 Zoom + L4)
- **Promise:** something you can only understand from above.
- **Research must contain:** dated satellite imagery (NASA, ESA, ISRO, public domain or properly licensed), and measurements.
- **Short:**
  - M1: The view from orbit.
  - M2: Zoom level 1.
  - M3: Zoom level 2.
  - M4: The thing that doesn't fit (accommodation, E32).
  - M5: Pull back out to the planet.

### G7 · The Journey (F7 Follow the Thing / F2 / F5)
- **Promise:** follow a route: a surveyor, a monsoon wind, a spice, a river, a train.
- **Research must contain:** the route from primary records (an explorer's own report, IMD data, historical sources), plus the person's own words where they exist (F5 layer).
- **Short:**
  - M1: The traveller and the impossible task.
  - M2: Where the journey starts.
  - M3: Stage 1 and the obstacle.
  - M4: Arrival, or the turn.
  - M5: The route drawn complete.

### G8 · Then vs Now (F8)
- **Promise:** the map changed: a river moved, a coast grew, states were redrawn.
- **Research must contain:** both maps or datasets with dates, and the same definitions.
- **Short:** M1 "Guess what this looked like in [year]" → M2 the old map → M3 the force of change → M4 the new map → M5 the morph back.
- **Must:** **one** clear before-and-after only [house]. Old borders shown only in official or historical context, labelled with the year.

### G9 · India and the World: one link (F9 / F7)
- **Promise:** a surprising thread between India and a faraway place (Zanzibar's doors, Berenike's pepper, Sikh police in Shanghai).
- **Role:** the **bridge for the 30% global slot** [house: 70/30 mix]. Each one gives Indian and foreign viewers a reason to care at once.
- **Short:**
  - M1: The object or fact far away that looks Indian.
  - M2: Globe arc from India to there.
  - M3: The trade route or the people.
  - M4: The evidence (excavation, record).
  - M5: The arc back to India.

### G10 · Region in 60 Seconds (F12 Quiet Turn / F6)
- **Promise:** a whole region understood fast ("India's Northeast explained").
- **Research must contain:** official boundaries and three facts, each with its "why".
- **Short:**
  - M1: "[N] states, [one striking fact]." (verified)
  - M2: The region on the globe.
  - M3: Three quick facts, each with its why.
  - M4: The unexpected link that ties them together.
  - M5: The region glowing whole.
- **Why it works:** "explained" Shorts are reliable 1M+ on other channels (D2); Indians search to *learn their own map* (D2).

### G11 · Geography vs Engineering (F2 Long Road)
- **Promise:** a bridge, tunnel or railway against the terrain.
- **Research must contain:** official project data (ministry, Indian Railways, NHAI) and the terrain facts.
- **Short:** M1 "For 100 years nobody could [cross X]" (only if sourced) → M2 the place → M3 why it was hard → M4 how they did it → M5 the train or car crossing, the loop.

### G12 · Extremes and Superlatives (F13 Scale Shock)
- **Promise:** the wettest, highest, last or most isolated place.
- **Research must contain:** an **official** record for the superlative. Superlatives are the easiest claims to get wrong; with no official record, use "one of the…" or drop the claim.
- **Short:** M1 the number → M2 perspective 1 → M3 perspective 2 → M4 what living there means → M5 the number, felt.

**Rotation:** never the same engine twice in a row. The 25 Oct suggested week (A×3, B×2, D×2, E×2, C×2, G×1, I×1, J×1, from D2) maps to:
- G1 ×3, G2 ×2, G10 ×2, G3 ×2, G5 ×2;
- G11, G12 and G8 ×1 each.

## 4. Two audiences in every video

The mix is 70% India and 30% global [house, AK 25 Oct]. Most viewers of an India topic are Indian, but the international viewer must never be lost.

| Rule | Indian viewer | International viewer |
|---|---|---|
| **Two anchors** | One thing from their life (the clock, the monsoon, their state) (L3) | One thing from their map (a country of the same size, a city at the same latitude) (L3, L7) |
| **Prime with the globe first** | Quick (they know India) | Essential: show where India and the place sit before any question (T1, E1) **[house]** |
| **Words** | No over-explaining | Every Indian term explained in a few words ("Lakshadweep, India's coral atolls") **[house]** |
| **Names** | Say them as locals do | Say them slowly and clearly; pronunciation from a native or official source (research claim) |
| **Pride, not chest-thumping** | Pride from understanding, never "we're the best" | Wonder, never "exotic India" |

## 5. Map visual grammar

| Rule | Why | How |
|---|---|---|
| **One change at a time** | Signalling (E12: 0.74) and segmenting (1.36). Change blindness in animated maps is a strong lead (not read in full; see EVIDENCE) | Never animate two things at once. Ease each transition and hold the result 0.5 s |
| **Labels next to the feature** | Words near the graphic (E12: 0.48) | No legends to decode in a Short; label on the map |
| **Highlight one thing, mute the rest** | Signalling (E12) | One amber highlight per frame; everything else desaturated (brand: deep #06202C, amber #FFB020, cyan #3DD9FF) |
| **Globe → map hand-off** | Prime first (T1) | Our engine already does this (1.35 s hand-off) **[house]** |
| **Ruler and counter for distance, area and height** | Perspective (E13) | The ruler draws while the counter runs 0 → value; then the comparison appears |
| **Satellite for wonder, flat map for logic** | Awe needs real vastness (E32); logic needs clean shapes (E12 weeding) | Satellite (NASA Blue Marble etc.) for M1 and M5; flat stylised map for M3 and M4 |
| **Directions on a globe, never a flat map** | The Mercator effect is in directions (E29) | Great-circle arcs drawn on the orthographic globe |
| **Official map, always** | RESEARCH_RULES 4.4 **[house]** | Survey of India outline; Natural Earth with India's point of view |
| **Safe zones** | YouTube interface overlays **[house]** | No labels under the caption bar or the right-side buttons |
| **A new visual every 2–3 s** | Shorts formula **[house]** + E10 | Cut on story beats, not on a timer |

## 7. Long videos (8–12 min): the scale ladder

Geography has a natural long-form structure: **planet → region → place → people → planet**.
1. **Cold open (0–30 s):** the strongest question on a satellite view (T16).
2. **Planet (1–2 min):** where it sits; the force that matters (monsoon, plate, current).
3. **Region (2–3 min):** the pattern; first loop, which closes on a turn.
4. **Place (2–3 min):** zoom to ground level; the peak at about 2/3 (T9).
5. **People (2 min):** one real person from the records (L6); how life works there.
6. **Planet again (30–45 s):** zoom out with the answer; callback to the cold open.

For G1, chapters 2–4 are "ruled-out cause 1, 2, 3", and the real cause sits at the peak.

## 9. Worked example: Minicoy, three ways

Facts from the Week 2 source table (`Indica Atlas/Week 2 - Metadata/Week2_Scripts_and_Sources.md`), which cites:
- the UT Lakshadweep administration pages (Minicoy is the southernmost island; 398 km from Kochi; about 130 km from the northern Maldives; Mahl spoken, as in the Maldives);
- the 1976 India–Maldives maritime boundary agreement.

The ⚠ marks there still apply. "3× closer" is calculated (398 ÷ 130), and the distance to Kerala's nearest coast should be re-checked before voicing.

| Engine | M1 Hook | M4 Turn | Why this engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G2 Mental map** (the Week 2 script) | "This Indian island is three times closer to the Maldives than to Kerala." | Lakshadweep's islands run south, and Minicoy is the last, so it ends up nearer the Maldives | L1: everyone's map puts Lakshadweep "near Kerala" |
| **G4 Why here** | "Why does this Indian island speak the Maldives' language?" | It is closer to the Maldives, and the people speak Mahl, the Maldives' language; the 1976 sea border still keeps it on India's side | L5: one "why" with a surprising cultural answer |
| **G5 The line** | "In 1976, India and the Maldives drew a line through the sea. This island is why it bends." | Only if a source says the line bends because of Minicoy. The US study only says Minicoy is the closest Indian land to that part of the line, so this hook **fails the research check**, and we drop it | Shows the gate at work: an exciting hook the facts don't support |

The G5 row is deliberate: a catchy hook that isn't in the research is not used. That's the difference between this channel and the ones we compete with.
