## Verdict on the title
- **"~10,000" is right.** The official 1999 count is 9,887 (IMD's report of February 2000, figure from the Government of Orissa) [C002]; other official tellings give 9,885 and 9,893 [K1]. Plus 40 missing.
- **"Under 100" is right, but the comparison isn't like for like.** Every later Odisha count is under 100: Phailin 2013 21 in the State's cyclone column, 72 counting the floods after [C015, C016]; Titli 2018 72 [C031]; Fani 2019 64 [C023]; Yaas 2021 3 [C034]; Dana 2024 0 [C037]. The later storms were weaker on **pressure and surge**: 912 hPa and a 5-6 m surge in 1999 [C004, C005], against Phailin 940 hPa and 2-3.5 m [C014], Fani about 1.5 m [C028] (952 hPa an hour or two before landfall [C021]). **Not on wind:** IMD's own reports disagree on 1999 (140 kt in the 2000 report, 120 kt in the 2024 report: K15, C058), so Phailin's 115 kt was almost level. Phailin, the strongest since 1999 [C014], is still the fairest test: 21 deaths from the storm.
- **Leave out** Hudhud (landed at Visakhapatnam) and Amphan (landed West Bengal-Bangladesh) [C039, C035].
- Truest one-line framing: *"9,887 died in 1999. When the strongest storm since then hit in 2013, the storm killed 21."*

## The real story, in plain words
- **1999, two storms in 12 days.** A very severe cyclone hit near Gopalpur on 17-18 October and killed 197 [C007]. Eleven days later the super cyclone crossed near Paradip on 29 October, around midday [C001], with a central pressure of 912 hPa and winds of 120-140 knots, depending on which IMD report you read [C004, C058]: the strongest in Orissa since 1885.
- **The sea did the killing.** A surge of about 5-6 metres ran up to 35 km inland [C005]; most deaths were from the surge [C006]. Jagatsinghpur district alone lost 8,119 of the 9,887, about four in five [C003, C048]; IMD's later account puts about 7,000 of them in one block, Ersama (hedge) [C059].
- **The warning was there; the shelters weren't.** IMD says it flagged the storm three days ahead [C010, attribute to IMD]. About 150,000 people were moved [C008], but there were only 23 permanent cyclone shelters, built by the Red Cross, which sheltered about 30,000 [C009] (both from one IMD scientist's paper: hedge).
- **What changed.**
  - Two months later, on 28 December 1999, the State set up the Orissa State Disaster Mitigation Authority, now OSDMA [C011, C012].
  - Shelters: 35 multipurpose cyclone shelters done by 2001-02 [C047]; 135 cyclone + 49 flood shelters by 2012 [C040], sited so that no one has to run more than 2.25 km [C042]; 162 more by 2019 under the World Bank-funded NCRMP [C041]; 879 multipurpose shelters in use for Fani in 2019 [C026].
  - Warnings to the last village: sirens and radio links in 1,205 villages from 2015 [C043]; trained volunteers [C044].
  - The order: "zero casualty". Before Phailin, Collectors were told to make sure no one stayed in a kutcha house on the night of landfall [C020]; the same principle was repeated for Dana in 2024 [C057].
- **The results.** Phailin: about 9.84 lakh evacuated [C018], 21 deaths in the State's cyclone column [C015]. Fani: 15,57,170 evacuated from 19 districts to about 9,177 shelters [C025, C026], 64 dead [C023]. Dana: 8,10,896 evacuated to 6,210 shelters, no deaths [C038, C037].
- **What still kills.** After Phailin, the floods killed 51, more than the storm's 21 [C016]. Titli landed in Andhra Pradesh, yet 47 of Odisha's 72 dead were in one district, Gajapati, 18 of them in one landslide at Baraghara, where 13 families had stayed home despite repeated warnings [C030, C031, C032]. (Gajapati's terrain is not sourced: don't call it hilly or inland yet.)
- **What it still costs.** Fani killed 64, but the DLNA puts damage at INR 16,465 crore and loss at INR 7,712 crore: INR 24,176 crore together, about 5% of the State's 2018-19 GSDP [C029]. Its separate recovery-needs figure is INR 29,315 crore; don't mix the two (K16).

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
G7: PASS. Verified facts that the usual "Odisha model" retellings leave out or get wrong:
1. **Four in five of the 1999 dead came from one district** (Jagatsinghpur, 8,119 of 9,887) [C003, C048], and most were killed by the sea surge, not the wind [C005, C006]. Map hook: one district lights up.
2. **The later storms were weaker on pressure and surge, not on wind; the fair test is Phailin** (940 vs 912 hPa; surge 2-3.5 m vs 5-6 m; wind 115 vs 120-140 kt, because IMD's own reports differ, K15). It killed 21 in the State's cyclone column [C004, C014, C015, C058]. Most retellings compare 1999 with Fani without saying so.
3. **After Phailin, the floods killed more than the cyclone** (51 vs 21) [C016]; 47 of Titli's 72 Odisha dead were in one district, Gajapati, 18 of them in a single landslide where families stayed home despite warnings [C032]. The coast learned; floods and the places the warnings did not move people from are the risk that remains. Good "twist" or closing question.
4. **"Zero casualty" is an order, not a record**: Phailin and Fani still had deaths; Dana, a much weaker storm, is the one with zero [C020, C037, C057].
5. **1999 had two cyclones in 12 days** [C007] (context, not hook).
6. **The cost side**: lives saved, property not: Fani's 64 deaths vs INR 24,176 crore of damage and loss (16,465 + 7,712; about 5% of GSDP) [C029]. (29,315 crore is recovery needs.)
Hedged candidate (needs a second source before it can be the hook): **in 1999 the warning came three days early but there were only 23 shelters** [C009, C010].
