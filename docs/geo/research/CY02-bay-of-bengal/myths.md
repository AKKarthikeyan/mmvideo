# CY02 Bay of Bengal cyclones: the popular version, checked

The claims come from the calendar title itself and from popular explainers seen in search results (UPSC notes such as examday.ai / dalvoy, drishtiias, thesouthfirst, vajiramandravi, iasparliament; all tier X). They were skimmed only to list claims. Nothing here enters the script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Bay of Bengal cyclones are **stronger** than Arabian Sea cyclones (the calendar title) | Embellished | C001, C003, C004, C007, C046 | The Bay makes about twice as many cyclones (satellite era), so it has more strong ones by count, and it holds the north Indian Ocean intensity record (1999). But storm for storm, an Arabian Sea cyclone has been as likely or more likely to turn severe (62% vs 62% since 1982; 62% vs 46% over IMD's whole record), and since 2004 the Arabian Sea has matched the Bay in extremely severe storms by IMD's count. The honest words are "more" and "deadlier"; there is no sign of "stronger" per storm (JTWC agrees on the per-storm odds, though at the very top, Category 5, it favours the Bay 8 to 1: C007) |
| M2 | The Bay gets **four times** as many cyclones (4:1) | Embellished | C001, C002 | True of IMD's long record (1891-1995: 4.8:1). Since 1982 it is about 2:1 (IMD's own table 120 vs 51) |
| M3 | **80%** of north Indian Ocean cyclones form in the Bay | Embellished | C001, C043 | True of the long record (IMD annual table 1891-1995: 458 of 554 = 83%), but 71% in 1982-2024 and 66% in 2004-2024 (IMD winds); the same 4:1-to-2:1 change as M2 |
| M4 | The Arabian Sea has seen a **52% rise** in cyclones | Confirmed | C005 | Deshpande et al. 2021, 2001-2019 vs 1982-2000, JTWC data. Note: cyclonic storms, not "formation" in general |
| M5 | The Bay is **warmer**, so it makes more cyclones | Embellished | C025, C037 | It is warmer every month (by 0.2-1.7 C), but the gap is smallest in May, a peak month for both seas, and largest in July-September when almost no cyclones form (wind shear) |
| M6 | River fresh water forms a **lid** that stops storms cooling the sea | Confirmed | C026, C027, C030, C045 | Measured for the post-monsoon north Bay. A model study gives the fresh layer about 40% of the effect and deep warm water about 60% |
| M7 | The fresh layer keeps the Bay **above 28 C** | Unverifiable | C025 | No source opened gives that threshold. Our SST averages are above 28 C in Apr-Nov in the box, but that is not the same claim |
| M8 | The Arabian Sea has **stronger winds** that churn up cold water | Unverifiable | — | Not tested in a source this run. The pre-monsoon Arabian Sea was over 31 C before the June 1998 cyclone (S07). Don't use |
| M9 | The Bay gets **leftover Pacific typhoons** | Confirmed | C023, C024 | IMD says so; IBTrACS shows 10 of 122 Bay cyclonic storms (1982-2024) began in the western Pacific, and the 1999 super cyclone began as a South China Sea low |
| M10 | The Bay is a **funnel** that piles up storm surge | Confirmed | C033, C034 | As an analogy only. "Convergence of the Bay" (Dube 1997, citing Ali 1979), plus shallow water and tides. Measured: about 1,400 km wide at 16N, under 500 km at 21.5N. "Funnel" is our analogy; label it |
| M11 | The Bay is **surrounded by land on three sides**, which keeps it warm | Unverifiable | — | No source tested the mechanism. Geography is true on the map; the warming effect is not sourced |
| M12 | The **1737** Bengal cyclone killed **300,000** | Unverifiable | C019, C020 | No primary source gives 300,000: it traces to the London Magazine (1738). The East India Company report said "near 3000 Inhabitants were killed", but that is Calcutta only, and Bilham allows 300,000 might include coastal villages of what is now East Bengal and Bangladesh. An IMD-journal model estimates about 35,000 for the region. 300,000 is still in NDMA's 2008 table and Dube 1997. Not narratable as fact either way |
| M13 | **21 of the 23** deadliest cyclones in history hit India and Bangladesh | Embellished | C015 | It is NDMA's statement, but the table counts 1737 at 300,000, includes 1999 at 9,893 under a "10,000 or more" heading, and predates Nargis. The direction (the deadliest storms are overwhelmingly Bay storms) is right |
| M14 | The 1970 Bhola cyclone killed **500,000** | Embellished | C010, C011 | WMO's record is 300,000; WMO's own news gives 300,000 to 500,000 |
| M15 | Monsoon winds **shear** storms apart in summer | Confirmed | C037, C038 | IMD: strong vertical wind shear; the Bay makes 622 monsoon depressions to the Arabian Sea's 89, but only 2 Bay cyclonic storms formed in July-August 1982-2024 |
| M16 | Cyclones in India now kill **far fewer** people | Confirmed | C022 | Amphan 2020 (super cyclonic storm): 98 deaths in West Bengal (IMD), more than 3 million evacuated (WMO) |
