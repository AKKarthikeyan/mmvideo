# CY03 Galwan and the LAC: dossier (research as of 28 Oct 2026)

Working title tested: "Galwan Never Ended: Mapping the LAC Points Where India and China Still Face Off".
Research only. No script, voice or render exists. Claim IDs in square brackets point to `claims.jsonl`. Every statement about a dispute is the statement of one government and is written that way: "India says", "China says".

## 1. Verdict on the title

The title is not supported as written. Three of its words fail against the official record:
- **"Never ended"**: India's External Affairs Minister told the Lok Sabha on 3 Dec 2024 that "disengagement has now been achieved in full in Eastern Ladakh" [C056]; MEA repeated "disengagement from all the friction points of 2020" in Feb 2025 and in its July 2026 brief [C056, C065]. The Chinese Foreign Ministry confirmed on 22 Oct 2024 that the two sides "have reached resolutions" [C055]. In 2026 the official texts (WMCC 35 on 27 May, WMCC 36 on 6 Aug, 25th Special Representatives talks on 25 Aug) speak of peace and tranquillity "maintained", with no face-off named [C062]. One caution: WMCC 36 and the 25th SR outcomes also speak of using the channels "to resolve outstanding issues" and of addressing "any situation on the ground" promptly; neither text says what the outstanding issues are [C062, C016].
- **"Still face off"**: no official text of 2025-2026 that we read describes any named friction point as currently in a face-off. The only 2022 clash outside Ladakh (Yangtse, Arunachal Pradesh) is a separate episode [C064].
- **"The LAC points"**: there is no agreed line to put points on [C010, C011, C014, C018]. We can label places; we cannot draw the line.

What is still true in Oct 2026, in the official words: the face-offs at the 2020 friction points are said to be resolved; patrolling is restored at Depsang and Demchok only; at "a few other places" there are "steps of a temporary and limited nature" [C054, C057]; the next step, de-escalation of the massed forces, is only at the stage of discussing "principles and modalities" (Aug 2025) [C059]; and India's own defence review for 2025 says the PLA keeps "10 Combined Arms Brigade size forces each" in depth while the Indian deployment is "robust" [C060]. The supportable line for a Short is therefore: **India says the 2020 face-offs are resolved; India's own defence review for 2025 still describes deployments in depth on both sides; and India says the LAC has "no common understanding in some areas".** (C056, C060, C011)

Suggested replacement framings for AK to choose (taste is AK's): (a) "In 1996 India and China promised to swap maps of the LAC. Today India says the line still has 'no common understanding in some areas'." (b) "Galwan's initial disengagement came in July 2020; the last friction points, Depsang and Demchok, were settled in October 2024; India's defence review for 2025 still calls the Indian deployment 'robust'." (C038, C054, C060) (c) "Five places in Ladakh where India and China stood off, and what happened at each."

## 2. The LAC: what may be drawn

- The 1993 Agreement tells both sides to "strictly respect and observe the line of actual control" and says that where they "have different views as to its alignment" they shall "jointly check and determine" it [C001, C002]. Its references to the LAC "do not prejudice their respective positions on the boundary question" [C003].
- The 1996 Agreement (Article X(1)) says the two sides "agree to exchange maps indicating their respective perceptions of the entire alignment of the line of actual control as soon as possible" [C004]. India's record: the clarification exercise ran "upto 2003" and then "the Chinese side did not show a willingness to pursue" it [C013]. Whether any sector's maps were swapped is not stated in any text we read.
- India says there is "no commonly delineated" LAC (Lok Sabha 2012, Rajya Sabha 2017, Defence Minister 2020) and the EAM, on 3 Dec 2024, "it does not have a common understanding in some areas" [C010, C011, C012]. The 25th SR talks (25 Aug 2026) only "discussed ways to improve understanding of LAC in appropriate areas" [C016].
- China says the LAC in the western sector "is clear" [C014]; MEA records China as insisting on a single "1959" LAC, which India "has never accepted" [C015].
- The Survey of India's own Ladakh map (3rd edition 2026) prints India's claimed boundary and has no "LAC" entry in its legend [C018].
- **Consequence for the map:** pins and labels only. Use the Survey of India outline (or the house Natural Earth India file, which agrees for the Ladakh points but is too coarse for Yangtse [C085]). No line, no dotted "approximate LAC", no shading of "Indian" or "Chinese" sides at Galwan. The two governments each place Galwan on their own side of the LAC [C030, C032].

## 7. Fresh angles (G7)

Verified facts that popular versions leave out or get wrong:
1. **Disengagement and de-escalation are two different official steps.** The face-offs ended (EAM: "fully achieved"); de-escalation, which "would address the massing of troops", is "the next priority" and, by Aug 2025, only at the stage of discussing "principles and modalities"; India's defence review for 2025 says the PLA "maintained 10 Combined Arms Brigade size forces each in tactical/ operational depth" [C056, C058, C059, C060]. Usable as the "why" or the closing turn. (Hook/why candidate)
2. **Patrolling is officially restored at only two named places, Depsang and Demchok.** At the others the earlier arrangements "continue to hold": a "temporary moratorium on ... patrolling" on the Pangong north bank and "steps of a temporary and limited nature" elsewhere, which "can be revisited as the situation demands" [C051, C054, C057]. Usable as the map reveal: two places lit, the others not.
3. **The 1996 treaty promised an exchange of LAC maps "as soon as possible"; thirty years later the government still says the LAC has no common understanding in some areas, and the August 2026 talks only "discussed ways to improve understanding of LAC in appropriate areas".** [C004, C011, C016] Usable as the hook.
4. **The "no firearms" rule is a ban on opening fire within two kilometres of the line, plus a ban on force during a face-off; no text we read bars carrying weapons.** [C005, C007, C008] (Careful: the EAM post itself is unreachable; say only what the treaties say.)
5. **India's official Ladakh map has no LAC line.** [C018]
6. The first fatalities on the border "in 45 years" are the EAM's own words [C037] (not fresh for Indian viewers, new for a global audience).
