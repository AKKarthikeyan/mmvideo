# How the map positions were read (CY03)
Source: Survey of India, LADAKH State Map, 3rd edition 2026, scale 1:1,000,000, PDF 2702 x 2091 pt (`sources/soi_ladakh_map_3rd_2026.pdf`, downloaded 28 Oct 2026 from surveyofindia.gov.in/pages/state-maps).
Method: `pdftoppm -r 150/200/300` crops; graticule lines printed on the map used as the scale (about 1,075 px per degree of longitude and 1,304 px per degree of latitude at 300 dpi; the 78 E and 79 E lines and the 34 N line were identified in the crop). Each label was read by eye and interpolated. Reading error about +-0.03 degree; the quoted tolerance is +-0.05 degree (about 5 km) after the independent check found the Galwan-Shyok junction near 78.16 E (we read 78.19 E): FOR PLACEMENT ONLY.
Crops used: crop2 (300 dpi, x7900 y3900 1700x1300): Galwan River, Shyok River, Gogra (Umlung), Hot Spring, Kongka La. crop3 (200 dpi, x4600 y2000 1300x1000): Depsang Plains. crop4 (200 dpi, x5700 y4350 1300x1000): Demchok. crop5 (200 dpi, x5400 y3250 1300x900): Pangong Tso (Brackish), Chushul.
Cross-check with an independent finding aid (OSM Nominatim, not evidence): Galwan clash-site node and Demchok village agree within the error (see geo_points_output.txt).
What the map shows: the official boundary of India as claimed (Ladakh includes the Aksai Chin), drawn as a continuous boundary line with no separate "LAC" line on this sheet. Labels seen: "Galwan River" (spelled with a macron over a), "Shyok River", "Depsang Plains", "Dopsang La", "Gogra (Umlung)", "Hot Spring", "Kongka La", "Pangong Tso (Brackish)", "Chushul", "Demchok", "Khurnak Fort (in ruins)".

Hot Spring: the sheet carries at least four labels "Hot Spring" (Kugrang valley, Chang Chenmo valley, near Demchok, near Chushul; another in Nubra). Which one is PP-15 (Gogra-Hot Springs) is not stated by any text read, so no PP-15 coordinate is given.
