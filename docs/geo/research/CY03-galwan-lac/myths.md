# CY03 Galwan and the LAC: the popular version, checked

Where these claims come from: the working title itself ("Galwan Never Ended ... Points Where India and China Still Face Off"), and the headlines and snippets of explainers and news pieces that came up in search results (tier X: Hindustan Times reprints, The Quint, Deccan Herald, The Week, Tribune, Scroll, Factly, TBS News and others). They were skimmed only to list which claims circulate. Nothing here enters the script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Indian soldiers did not fire at Galwan because "a treaty bans firearms on the border" | Embellished | C005, C006, C007, C008, C040 | The 1996 Agreement bans opening fire (and blasting, hunting with guns) within two kilometres of the LAC; the 2005 Protocol bars force or the threat of force during a face-to-face situation; the 2013 agreement says to "prevent exchange of fire or armed conflict". No text we searched bars carrying weapons. A post attributed to the External Affairs Minister in news reports (carrying arms is routine; the long-standing practice is not to use firearms in face-offs; date and wording unverified, tier X) could not be opened: C040 is UNVERIFIED |
| M2 | The Galwan standoff "never ended" / the two armies still face off along the LAC (the working title) | Embellished | C056, C057, C058, C060, C062 | India's official position since Dec 2024 is that disengagement from all the 2020 friction points is complete (EAM, MEA, MoD). What has not been reported as done is de-escalation, the pulling back of the massed forces: the MoD review for 2025 says the Indian deployment is "robust" and that the PLA keeps "10 Combined Arms Brigade size forces" in depth. So "face-offs ended, armies did not leave" is the supportable version |
| M3 | 20 Indian soldiers died at Galwan on 15 June 2020 | Confirmed | C033 | PMO, Defence Minister and MoD all say 20. The owner of the number is the Government of India |
| M4 | China has never admitted any deaths at Galwan | Contradicted | C035, C036, C070, C071 | China's ministries said from June 2020 that there were "casualties" and from 19 Feb 2021 that there were "casualties on both sides"; the Chinese Foreign Ministry's own transcript of 19 Feb 2021 calls the Chinese dead "martyrs who made the ultimate sacrifice defending the country's border" (C071); the Chinese military published a count and honours that day (Indian note: CMC announced posthumous honours on 19 Feb 2021). The figure of four reaches us only through state media (hedged, SECONDARY-ONLY) |
| M5 | The LAC is a clearly marked line that both countries have agreed | Contradicted | C002, C010, C011, C012, C014, C018 | India: "no commonly delineated" LAC (2012, 2017, 2020) and "it does not have a common understanding in some areas" (Dec 2024). China (24 Jun 2020): the LAC in the western sector "is clear". The 1993 treaty itself says the sides "have different views as to its alignment" in places. The official Indian map has no LAC line |
| M6 | There are official "buffer zones" in eastern Ladakh where nobody may patrol | Embellished | C051, C057, C067 | The word "buffer" is in none of the official texts we read. The official words are "temporary moratorium on military activities ... including patrolling" (Pangong north bank, Feb 2021) and "steps of a temporary and limited nature" at "a few other places" (EAM, Dec 2024). Which places is not listed |
| M7 | After October 2024, patrolling resumed at all the friction points | Contradicted | C054, C057 | The 21 Oct 2024 agreement restores patrolling and grazing at Depsang and Demchok only; at the other places the earlier arrangements "continue to hold" |
| M8 | The October 2024 deal "pulled both armies back" (de-escalation) | Contradicted | C056, C058, C059, C060 | Disengagement and de-escalation are different steps in the official record; in Aug 2025 the two sides agreed only to "discuss de-escalation, beginning with the principles and modalities thereof", and the published outcomes of the 25th SR talks (Aug 2026) do not mention it |
| M9 | The Galwan valley is named after the Ladakhi explorer Ghulam Rasool Galwan | Unverifiable | C086 | His 1923 book (the period record) does not say a river or valley was named after him or his family; it says the family name "Galwan" meant "robber". The naming claim could not be traced to a primary source here |
| M10 | Galwan was the first deadly clash on the border in decades | Confirmed | C037 | The External Affairs Minister's own words: "fatalities for the first time in 45 years" |
| M11 | The Galwan valley lies clearly on one country's side of the line | Unverifiable | C030, C031, C032, C018 | India says Indian structures are "on its own side of the LAC" and that Chinese troops sought to build "just across the LAC"; China says Galwan "lies on the Chinese side of the LAC". With no common LAC and no shared map, a script must attribute each statement to its government |
| M12 | India and China signed a treaty to exchange maps of the LAC, and did | Embellished | C004, C013, C016, C017 | The 1996 Agreement (Article X(1)) says they "agree to exchange maps indicating their respective perceptions of the entire alignment ... as soon as possible". India says the clarification exercise ran to 2003 and then stopped; whether any maps were exchanged is not stated in the texts read; in Aug 2026 the sides "discussed ways to improve understanding of LAC in appropriate areas" |
| M13 | Not a single shot was fired at Galwan on 15 June 2020 | Unverifiable | C039 | No official text retrieved says what weapons were or were not used on 15 June. The only official firing statement is about 7 Sep 2020 (PLA "fired a few rounds in the air", Indian Army statement) |
