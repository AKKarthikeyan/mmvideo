# CY04 Why the Bay of Bengal breeds cyclones: dossier (3 Nov 2026)

Short, made for the day after Cyclone Murasu's landfall. Research under AK's Shorts rule (1 Nov 2026): two original publishers,
**IMD** (FAQ on tropical cyclones, the annual cyclone table, the Murasu National Bulletin, and IMD's journal *Mausam* for the surge
causes) and **Sengupta et al. 2008** (*Atmospheric Science Letters*, measured data) for the fresh-water lid. All copies were read
in CY02 (27 Oct 2026) and re-used here; the Murasu bulletin is new.

## Spine
- More than twice as many cyclones as the Arabian Sea since 1982: 120 vs 51 [C001]. (The 1891-2024 record is almost 4:1; the satellite era is used.)
- Murasu: a severe cyclonic storm in the west-central Bay [C002].
- Reason 1, warmth: IMD says the colder Arabian Sea holds back formation and strengthening [C003].
- Reason 2, the lid: post-monsoon north Bay, fresh water caps warm water, storms mix up warm water [C004].
- Reason 3, the shape: shallow coastal water and the narrowing Bay make surges disastrous [C005]; the surge is the greatest killer [C006].

## Honesty notes
- "Warm shallow water" in the brief is half right: the warm water is **deep**, under a **shallow fresh lid**; what is shallow is the
  **coastal sea bed**, which makes surges worse. The script says it that way.
- Warmth is one reason, not the whole story (CY02 myth M5); the script says "warm water feeds storms", not that warmth explains the count.
- The lid is a post-monsoon, north-Bay effect; Murasu formed further south, so the script does not link the lid to Murasu.

G7: PASS. CY02 (posted look B) asked "are Bay cyclones stronger?" and answered no. This Short answers a different question,
"why so many, and why so deadly", with three causes (warmth, the fresh-water lid, the funnel), tied to Murasu. The only overlap
is the funnel, which CY02 drew as widths; here it is the third reason.
