## bowl

- `q011` A chip starts as rock. Ordinary quartz rock is turned into silicon, and the silicon is cleaned until it is almost perfectly pure. Then comes the hard part. [C-017]
- `q012` A chip needs its silicon as one single crystal. Every atom in line, like tiles on a floor, and not one out of place. [C-018]
- `q013` The trick for growing one goes back to a Polish chemist, Jan Czochralski. The story goes that one evening he dipped his pen into molten tin instead of ink. When he lifted it, a thin thread of metal came up with it: a single crystal. He published the method in nineteen eighteen. [A-C16, A-C15]
- `q014` Chip makers still use his idea. They fill a bowl with chunks of pure silicon, and heat it past fourteen hundred degrees Celsius, until it melts. [C-018, A-C03]
- `q015` They lower a tiny seed crystal until it just touches the surface. [C-018]
- `q016` Then they pull it up, very slowly, turning it as it rises. The liquid silicon freezes onto the seed, layer by layer, and a crystal grows. [C-018]
- `q017` The finished crystal is called an ingot. One quartz producer says a full ingot can weigh over five hundred kilograms and measure more than four metres. [A-C04]
- `q018` The ingot is sliced into thin discs called wafers, each about a millimetre thick. [C-019]
- `q019` And chips are built on the wafers. The same company says one ingot can become about two million chips. [A-C05]
- `q020` Now look again at the bowl. [no factual claim]
- `q021` It is called a crucible, and it has a terrible job. It must hold liquid silicon for days, without melting, cracking, or leaking anything into it. [A-C01, A-C04]
- `q022` Because the hot silicon slowly eats at the wall of the bowl. Whatever is in the wall ends up in the crystal. And a few stray atoms are enough to spoil the chips. [A-C01]
- `q023` So the bowl must take the heat, and be almost as pure as the silicon itself. The answer is quartz: melted, and shaped into a milky white bowl. [A-C01]
- `q024` And not just any quartz. In the top grade, fewer than ten parts in every million are anything else. [A-C02]
- `q025` And a bowl does not last. That producer says one crucible pulls about eight ingots, over four hundred hours. So the world needs new bowls, all the time. [A-C04]
- `q026` So here is the first surprise. The famous quartz is not in your phone. It is the bowl your phone's chips were born in. [A-C01]
