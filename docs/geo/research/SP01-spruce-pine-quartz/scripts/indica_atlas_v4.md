## How the strategy document is applied
| Rule in the document | Where it is used here |
|---|---|
| Visual evidence before context (Johnny Harris); start with the wow, then the how (Mark Rober) | The film opens on the storm, not on Taiwan. Chapter 1 opens on the finished four-metre crystal, then explains how it is grown. |
| Start from the viewer's misconception (Veritasium) | "Chips depend on Taiwan" is stated first and then extended. Three things people repeat about the town are listed in the first minute and tested one by one. |
| Proof, value, structure, stakes in the first 30-60 s (PVSS) | Proof: "we read the survey, the hurricane report and the railway's filings". Value and structure: three claims that did not hold up. Stakes: nearly $800 billion of chips. |
| "But / therefore", never "and then" | Every beat below starts with its link to the one before: BUT, SO, or a question it answers. |
| Open loops, stacked, all paid (Zeigarnik) | L1 "why would this storm matter to your phone?" (opened 0:10, paid in The Storm). L2 "three things that need a closer look" (opened 0:45; paid at the end of chapters 1, 3 and 4). L3 "where does the bowl's quartz come from?" (opened end of ch. 1, paid ch. 2). L4 "what happens when the speck takes a hit?" (opened end of ch. 3, paid ch. 4). A new loop opens before each old one closes. |
| Kishōtenketsu for explainers | Ki: the storm, Taiwan, the town. Shō: the bowl, the mountain, the two companies. Ten (the twist): the plants survived; what broke was everything around them, and the famous numbers have no source. Ketsu: the lesson. |
| Relatable characters; stage behaviour, do not declare emotion; plant an object and pay it off (Kurzgesagt, screenwriting section) | Kavin, a child with a phone (Kurz story kit cast, night-home set), appears four times. The phone is planted in the first 15 seconds and paid off in the last shot. He never speaks; he reacts. Tagged ILLUSTRATIVE. |
| Early micro-satisfactions; a re-engagement around minute 3 | The first myth is paid at about 2:50; the pen story sits at about 1:40. |
| Breathing room, no "overstim" | Holds after each big number; the death in the storm is given its own line and a pause. |
| End the moment the promise is paid | The film ends on the callback line. The closing question card is removed. |

## 1 · The bowl
10. `q-furnace full` (the wow first) · "This is a single crystal of silicon. One quartz producer, describing how wafers are made, says a full one can weigh over five hundred kilograms and measure more than four metres." [A-C04] **NEW order**

13. `q-dots` · BUT · "But pure is not enough. A chip needs its silicon as one single crystal. Every atom in line, like tiles on a floor, with almost none out of place." [C-018]
14. `q-pen` · "The trick goes back to a Polish chemist, Jan Czochralski. The story goes that one evening he dipped his pen into molten tin instead of ink. When he lifted it, a thin thread of metal came up with it: a single crystal. He published the method in nineteen eighteen." [A-C16, A-C15]
15. `q-furnace melt` · SO · "Chip makers still use his idea. So they fill a bowl with chunks of pure silicon, and heat it past fourteen hundred degrees Celsius, until it melts." [C-018, A-C03]
16. `q-furnace dip` · "They dip a tiny seed crystal into the melt." [C-018]
17. `q-furnace pull` · "Then they pull it up, very slowly, turning it as it rises. And a large crystal grows from the thin seed." [C-018]
18. `q-wafer slice` · "That crystal is sliced into thin discs called wafers, each about a millimetre thick. And chips are built on the wafers." [C-019]
19. `q-statement` · BUT · "But go back. Look again at the bowl." [no factual claim]
20. `q-furnace` crucible close-up · "It is called a crucible, and it has a terrible job. It must hold liquid silicon, at extreme heat, for hundreds of hours." [A-C01, A-C04]

22. `q-bowl clean` · SO · "So the bowl is made of quartz: melted, and shaped." [A-C01]
