# TP01 Tiruppur knitwear town: dossier (research as of 30 Oct 2026)

Every sentence carries claim IDs. Only claims marked VERIFIED-P, CORROBORATED, SECONDARY-ONLY (hedged) or DISPUTED (as a dispute) are used. Time-sensitive trade facts are left out because they are UNVERIFIED (see section 9).

## 1. Verdict on the title

1. Tiruppur is a real inland export town: the nearest port city, Kochi, is about 170 km away in a straight line, and Coimbatore is about 43 km away [C001, C200].
2. The district was inaugurated on 22 Feb 2009, carved from Coimbatore and Erode, and 61% of its 2.48 million people were urban in 2011 [C002, C003, C004, C005].
3. "90% of India's knitwear exports" is not supported: the read shares are 85% (1994, including indirect), about 75% (c. 2007), nearly 60% (2026 releases) and 54.74% (Sep 2026), each on its own definition [C066, C078, C079, C080].
4. The town's own export value is also unsettled: three different FY2025-26 figures are in print and no page explains the gap [C073, C074, C075, C076, C077].

## 2. Dated timeline

- **1930s:** a small hosiery unit opened by two friends to make banians (vests) for the local market, per a union leader's 2004 account [C022, C024, C025].
- **First factory, disputed:** 1935 (Textile Commissioner), 1937 (UNIDO: Gulam Kadar, Baby Knitting) and 1925 (a student paper, secondary) are in print; 1893 is an outlier [C028, C029, C027, C032, C033].
- **1942 and about 1960:** 34 firms in 1942 and 230 in 1960-61 (secondary, Chari via Esho); UNIDO says around 450 firms in 1960 [C034, C035].
- **Early 1960s:** cotton hosiery knitting was reserved for small-scale and cottage units [C100].
- **1962 onward:** factory-level strikes, some lasting six to nine months, per Krishnaswami (1989); who ended up owning the units is disputed [C106, C111].
- **1972:** an excise duty discouraged expansion of a unit, so firms opened new units, often in the same premises; the same year, a first export by Mohan Knits that "could not be sustained" [C105, C047].
- **Until 1980:** the industry stayed a cottage industry making innerwear for the local market [C044, C045].
- **From 1980:** exports begin, mostly indirectly through agents in Bombay and Delhi; until 1985 the only export item was the T-shirt [C048, C050].
- **1984:** a 127-day banian workers' strike over wage revision and dearness allowance (DA), led by AITUC [C116, C117, C122].
- **1987:** wages for banian workers negotiated through a joint committee [C134].
- **July 1990:** the Tiruppur Exporters' Association (TEA) is formed [C018].
- **1986 to 1993:** direct exports rise from Rs 37.48 crore to Rs 1,197.5 crore [C054, C055].
- **1994:** TEA asks for an inland container terminal, a sewage plant and a telephone exchange and says it "has received no reply from authorities"; the 157-unit Mudalipalayam complex is "self financed by the exporters" [C156, C161].
- **2000:** the water-company concession (30 years) is signed; commissioning is dated July 2005 or February 2006, which is disputed [C152, C153].
- **2003:** Noyyal farmers' association has fought polluters in court since this year [C169].
- **2006:** Madras High Court orders a fine until zero liquid discharge (ZLD), deadline 31 Jul 2007 [C170].
- **Oct 2009:** Supreme Court final orders, deadline 5 Jan 2010 [C172].
- **Early 2011:** High Court orders closure of more than 700 dyeing and bleaching units and plants [C173, C198].
- **June 2012:** eighteen CETPs (common effluent treatment plants) reopen with ZLD, at 15-30% of capacity at first, about 16-17 months after closure [C178, C179].
- **April 2016:** bipartite wage pact, 33% over four years [C136, C137].
- **17 Mar 2026:** the next pact, again 33% over four years [C138].
- **Sep 2026:** TEA reports 1,522 members [C020].

## 3. Who matters (three)

1. **Tiruppur Exporters' Association (TEA).** Formed 1990; 382 members in 1994, 1,522 in 2026 [C018, C019, C020]. It built the exporters' estate and was a promoter of the water company [C161, C152].
2. **New Tiruppur Area Development Corporation (NTADCL, the water company).** Promoters are a Tamil Nadu government vehicle (TWIC), IL&FS and TEA [C152]. Cost Rs 1,023 crore [C150].
3. **K. Subbarayan, banian workers' union leader.** Secretary or president since 1972, later an MP [C023]. His 2004 interview is the main account of the 1930s unit and of the 1984 strike, and is one side's recollection [C022, C118, C120].

## 4. How the town works

**Small units, by law and by habit.**
- The early-1960s reservation of cotton hosiery knitting for small units pushed anyone with more capital to split the work into two or three units [C100, C101].
- In 1994 the small-scale investment ceiling was Rs 75 lakh and one imported embroidery machine cost about Rs 40 lakh [C102].
- MIDS: "the easiest method adopted was to multiply the number of units" [C103].
- In 1984 Business India wrote that it was "unlikely that there are more than 500 families in the business" [C104].
- Counts of units differ by year and owner: 2,500 knitting units (1994 and 2007 notes), 6,250 in all (TEA, 2008) [C009, C010, C011].

**Worker to owner.**
- Kashtakkoottu: a skilled worker joins financiers as a partner and takes 10-25% of the profit [C097].
- A second-hand machine market passes machines from a failed unit to the next entrepreneur [C098].
- MIDS: "Almost all the big names ... have had very humble beginnings, having been workers themselves" [C099].
- The caste story (Chettiar owners leaving, Gounder owners rising) is one author's argument against "a different version" heard by MIDS; handle as disputed [C107, C108, C110, C111, C113].

**One wage bargain for the whole town.**
- Since 1987 banian wages go through a joint committee; since 2016 a bipartite pact between associations and unions [C134, C136].
- Agreements ran three years until 2007, then four [C135].
- 2016 and 2026 pacts share one shape: 33% as 18, 5, 5, 5; the 2026 pact lists six employer associations and nine unions [C137, C138, C139].
- After the 1984 strike the work "shifted rapidly toward contract work" and the workforce became "differentiated and insecure" (a review of Chari) [C125].
- A union official said weekly pay rose from a struggle to get Rs 1,000 in the 1980s to Rs 6,000-8,000 on piece rates [C132].

**Self-built water and cargo.**
- The town is dry and its rivers carry water only in the monsoon; there is no reliable piped supply; tankers travelled up to 50 km [C142, C143, C145, C146].
- The water company draws 185 million litres a day from the Cauvery near the Bhavani confluence through a main pipeline of about 53.5 km [C148, C149].
- Exporters funded the Mudalipalayam complex themselves in 10 months for Rs 14 crore [C161].
- Later a TEA-run container freight station sits about 10 km from town; goods go "through all southern ports and Mumbai"; exporters "often missed sailings" [C157, C159].

**Zero liquid discharge (ZLD).**
- Dyeing used 200 to 400 litres per kg against an international norm of 120 to 150 [C144].
- By Dec 2016, 450 dyeing units had set up 18 ZLD CETPs at Rs 1,013 crore [C180].
- The Dyers Association says 130 million litres a day are treated and 92% recovered (industry claim) [C186].
- The same source says more than 100,000 tonnes of mixed salt sit in sheds with "no proper disposal method" [C187].
- CPCB's D D Basu (2011): "That is why ZLD at Tirupur was a big mistake" [C188].
- Water-quality evidence is split: 2016 field report of black water at the floodgates, 2025 IIT Madras comment of "significant improvement" [C184, C185].

## 5. Export growth in numbers

- Direct exports grew about 32 times in value but only 6.45 times in pieces between 1986 and 1993 (calculated) [C056, C057].
- Value per piece rose from Rs 1.30 to Rs 6.43 [C058].
- Annual values: Rs 289.85 crore (1990), Rs 5,000 crore (2003), Rs 10,000 crore (about 2007), Rs 44,747 crore (FY25) [C061, C068, C070, C071].
- TEA targets Rs 1 lakh crore by 2030, which needs about 17-21% growth a year from Rs 46,000 crore (calculated) [C093, C094].

## 7. Hook and twist candidates (facts only; wording is for the script stage)

- **Hook A (from F1):** "In 1984 a business magazine wrote it was 'unlikely that there are more than 500 families in the business'; the town then counted about 2,500 knitting units in the 1990s and 2000s." [C104, C009, C010]. Use "about", attribute both.

- **Twist 1:** the water for a dry town comes by a 53.5 km pipeline from the Cauvery, promoted partly by the exporters themselves [C148, C149, C152].

- **Twist 3:** the same 33% wage deal, 18 then 5, 5, 5, was signed in 2016 and again in 2026 [C137, C138].
