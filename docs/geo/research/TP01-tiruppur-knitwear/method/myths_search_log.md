| F1 | Why so many tiny units: the early-1960s reservation of cotton hosiery knitting for small-scale units plus a capital ceiling (Rs 75 lakh in 1994) made splitting the easy route ("the easiest method adopted was to multiply the number of units"); a 1972 excise rule did the same; Business India (1984): "unlikely that there are more than 500 families in the business". Usable as the "why" answer for a dry inland town | Popular versions stop at the dollars or at the dyeing pollution; water supply and the exporters' own role are absent from the snippets | section 6; NTADCL pages, CIRC case study, MIDS p.16, UNIDO (2005) |
| F3 | The dyers' salt problem: ZLD works on paper but more than 100,000 tonnes of mixed salt sit in sheds with "no proper disposal method"; CPCB's Basu called ZLD at Tirupur "a big mistake" because RO-reject evaporation is costly; no independent compliance report exists for 2020-2026 | Popular versions present ZLD as a clean success (92-94% recovery) (M16) | section 7; Down To Earth 13 May 2011; Economic Times 25 Jun 2025 |
