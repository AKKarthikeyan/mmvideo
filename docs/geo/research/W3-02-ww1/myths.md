# W3-02-ww1 The popular version, checked

The claims come from the topic pitch and hints, and from headlines and snippets in search results (Tier X: news, explainers, UPSC notes, a Wikipedia page, Defence Journal, Arab News, Tribune). They were skimmed only to list claims. Nothing here enters the script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | "Over a million Indians fought in World War One" (the pitch) | Embellished | C002, C003, C004 | 1,096,013 Indians were sent overseas, but 43.3% (474,789) were non-combatants. Indian combatants sent overseas: 621,224. 877,068 combatants were recruited in all, not all of whom left India. The honest line: "over a million went overseas; about 620,000 of them as soldiers" |
| M2 | "1.3 million Indians served" | Embellished | C003 | 1,302,394 is the Government of India's count of "personnel" despatched from Indian ports to Nov 1918 and includes British ranks (S02 p.78). 1,381,050 is the all-told 1919 figure. Both are in the record; neither is "Indian soldiers" |
| M3 | "The Indian Army fought in France and Flanders" (the usual picture) | Embellished | C005, C007, C010 | True and moving, but only 12.6% of Indians sent overseas went to France; 61.6% went to Mesopotamia. In the Government's own table 54.8% of the dead died in Mesopotamia, 14.6% in France |
| M4 | "About 74,000 Indians died" | Embellished | C006, C022 | CWGC's figure, for 4 Aug 1914-31 Aug 1921. The War Office counted 62,056 to 31 Dec 1920; the Government of India 61,041 to 1920. CWGC itself prints 70,000 on another page. Different owners and windows |
| M5 | "Indian troops saved the British Expeditionary Force in 1914 / held the line at Ypres" | Unverifiable | C010 | Not tested in any source opened. Only the arrival (26 Sep 1914) and the Neuve-Chapelle share (C011) are in the record. Do not use |
| M6 | "India fought for freedom / in return for self-government" | Unverifiable | C020 | The 1917 declaration is real and was made mid-war. No document opened says soldiers or leaders fought "for" it. Gandhi's recruiting appeal and the Congress 1914 resolution were not read at source |
| M7 | "Indians were forced into the war" / "conscripted" | Unverifiable | C016 | The Government's book says recruitment was "purely voluntary". It is the Government's word about itself. No independent record of how recruitment worked in villages was opened (secondary historians say pressure was applied: not used here) |
| M8 | "Indians fought for the King / out of loyalty" | Embellished | C015, C021 | "Loyalty" is the Government's word (S02). Soldiers' letters (via the censor) speak of duty in one letter and of being helpless in another; none gives a family allowance as a reason. One word does not cover it |
| M9 | "India gave £100 million as a gift" | Embellished | C019 | The Government of India's own book calls it a "free gift"; it also says the sum was raised by loans and added over 30% to the national debt. Describe it as a contribution agreed by the Government with the Council's "general assent" |
| M10 | "Punjab supplied half the Indian army" | Embellished | C008, C009 | Punjab gave 51.2% of the combatants recruited in the provinces of British India, but 42.3% of all combatants once the Indian States' 115,891 recruits are counted (826,855 in all). Punjab had about 9% of the provincial population (1921). Say "about half of British India's provincial recruits". Hedge: the recruitment table may or may not include the Punjab States, and the Punjab registers (320,000 names, news only) were not opened |
| M11 | "The Indian Army was the largest all-volunteer force in history" (CWGC, a statement about WWII) | Unverifiable | | CWGC's page says this of the Second World War. Not a WWI claim; do not move it |
| M12 | "Indian soldiers got no credit / were forgotten" | Unverifiable | C012, C013 | Not a factual claim. CWGC commemorates them by name: 4,700+ at Neuve-Chapelle, 13,000+ at India Gate. Avoid the word "forgotten" (a framing, not a fact) |
