# W3-02-ww1: open questions

## Gaps that matter for the script
1. **Who decided India entered the war, in law (rule 2.3).** The Government's book (S02) shows the Viceroy reading the King-Emperor's message on 8 Sep 1914. No legal text (Government of India Act 1858/1915, the declaration of war) was opened. Do not say Indians "had no say" until a legal record is read. Draft request for AK or a library: the statute and a constitutional history citation.
2. **Independent record of how recruiting worked (C016).** "Purely voluntary" is the Government's claim about itself. Candidates not opened: the Punjab Government's own war reports and recruiting returns in the India Office Records (British Library), the Hunter Disorders Inquiry Committee report (1920), the Gandhi Collected Works vol. 14 (his April 1918 letter to the Viceroy and Kheda recruiting appeal), the Indian National Congress 29th session report (Madras, Dec 1914). None reached as text in this run.
3. **What Indian political leaders actually asked for in return (M6).** Only the British side of the 1917 declaration is in the dossier. The Congress and Muslim League resolutions of 1914-18 and Gandhi's own words are Tier P3 and should be read at source before any "why they fought" line about freedom.
4. **Reconciling the death counts (C006, K1).** Why the War Office (62,056) and the Government (61,041) differ, and what CWGC's 74,000 includes (post-war deaths, labour corps, disease, deaths in India?). Draft request to CWGC: "Please give counts of Indian Army First World War casualties by (a) date of death window, (b) rank group (officers, other ranks, followers/labourers), (c) cause where known, (d) country of commemoration." CWGC's search page returned 403 to scripts.
5. **France's share after 1915.** The Indian infantry's departure from France (Dec 1915) is *not* a verified claim here: the Indian Corps book and the Mesopotamia Commission mention Indian divisions moved from France to Mesopotamia (S08 lines ~1793, 2018-2075), but the dates and the number were not extracted. Check the Official History before saying "left France in 1915".
6. **CWGC's Delhi (India Gate) page** was blocked (bot check). The 13,000+ names and the inscription are as CWGC wrote them on another page [C013]. Re-check on the CWGC page or on the monument.
7. **Punjab States and the population base (C009).** Whether the war recruitment table's "Punjab" includes the Punjab States; the Census row does. A 1911 Census check was not done (the djvu text 404).
8. **Neuve-Chapelle "one third" (C011)** has no source behind CWGC's sentence. The Official History (Edmonds) or the Indian Corps book (S09) could give the numbers.
9. **Soldiers' letters:** the originals at the British Library (IOR/L/MIL/5/825-827) were not opened; S11 is a volunteer transcription. The independent checker should open at least the two letters in C021.
10. **Pronunciation for the voice:** Neuve-Chapelle, Marseilles, Mesopotamia, Dunsterforce: not verified.
11. **G6 independent check:** pending. ## Wording risks for AK (sensitive: colonial history; communities; neighbours)
- Do not say Indians "fought for Britain" or "for India" as a single reason: the record gives several and none is one cause. Say what the documents say, with the owner.
- "Voluntary" and "loyal" are the Raj Government's words about itself (S02): attribute or leave out. "Conscripted" and "forced" are not in any opened record: do not use.
- "Forgotten", "betrayed", "cannon fodder", "sacrificed for the Empire" and "the Raj's war": framings, not facts. Avoid.
- Say "the Indian Army of the British Indian Empire" or "Undivided India" (CWGC's term), not "India's army" as if today's republic. The dead and the recruiting provinces include the lands of today's Pakistan and Bangladesh. Do not credit "India" alone for them, and take no side on partition.
- Religion and region: Punjab's recruits were Muslims, Sikhs and Hindus; no source opened gives a verified split. A news snippet gave 156,000 Muslims, 62,000 Sikhs, 63,000 Hindus (Tier X, not used). Do not state a communal split.
- "Martial races", a British recruiting doctrine, was not opened as a record. Do not use the phrase or imply it.
- Nepal: the 58,904 recruits from Nepal are counted separately by the Government's book; the Gurkha regiments are not "Indian" soldiers in that table. Do not blur.
- Jallianwala Bagh, Rowlatt Act and the post-war aftermath are outside this research and were not read. Do not link the war to 1919 in the narration.
- Do not describe the CWGC memorials' inscription beyond the words quoted in [C013].
