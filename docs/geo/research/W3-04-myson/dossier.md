## Verdict on the premise
**The pitch is true as a description and weak as a question.** UNESCO and ICOMOS do call the tower temples of My Son "Hindu", dedicated "above all" to Shiva [C006]. But the records do not answer "why" with a migration or a conquest. The honest answer is shorter and more interesting:
- ICOMOS: the Cham came under the influence of the Hindu religion of the Indian sub-continent "early in their development, though the exact date is not known" [C007]. No source opened gives the date, the people or the route.
- The Committee that inscribed the site wrote that My Son shows "an indigenous society adapting to external cultural influences, notably the Hindu art and architecture of the Indian sub-continent" [C004]. ICOMOS's own draft had said the "introduction" of Hindu architecture [C005].
- What the Cham kings' own inscriptions show is **local kings, with their own names, putting a Shiva shrine at the centre of their kingdom and naming it after themselves** [C011, C013, C019], writing in Sanskrit and, from at least 991 CE, in their own language [C021].
- It was never only Shiva: Buddhism was strong in the north and by the later 10th century Hinduism at My Son "had achieved parity" with it [C016]; a king of 875 CE recorded a Buddhist monastery and the My Son lingam on one stele [C017].
- Suggested honest titles (AK decides): "How Vietnam got Shiva temples: its own kings chose them"; "The god at My Son was named after a king"; "India's archaeologists spent five years restoring Shiva temples in Vietnam. Who built them?" (the last hooks the Indian audience; see F4).

## The real story, in plain words (every line carries claim IDs)
- **Where.** A sanctuary in Duy Phu commune, Duy Xuyen district, Quang Nam province, central Vietnam [C001], at 15.774 N, 108.109 E by UNESCO's coordinate [C002], about 30 km inland [C032]. UNESCO dates the culture of My Son to the 4th to 13th centuries CE [C036]; inscribed in 1999 on criteria (ii) and (iii) [C003]. Today: eight groups, 71 standing monuments, property 142 ha [C033].
- **Who.** The Champa kingdom, dated by UNESCO to 192 CE [C008]. My Son was a royal sanctuary: Viet Nam's dossier says every Champa king came there at his accession to make offerings [C009]. Whether it was also "the capital" is disputed; do not say it [C010].
- **The first temple.** The oldest inscription at My Son is the stele of king Bhadravarman I founding the temple of Bhadresvara [C011], around the 4th-5th century CE [C012]. Finot, who published the stele, wrote that nothing is left of the earliest buildings, which were "no doubt of wood, since Cambhuvarman declares that they burned" [C014]. The temple burned in the reign of Rudravarman I; his son Cambhuvarman rebuilt it and gave the god the new name Cambhubhadresvara [C013].
- **The god carries the king's name.** Scholars read the god's name Bhadresvara as taken from its builder, king Bhadravarman, and say naming the deity after the reigning king became almost universal later [C037]. Later steles keep doing it: Isanabhadresvara in 991 [C021], Bhadresvara receiving a crown from Prakasadharman in 687 [C019].
- **A Cham voice.** A stele of 687 CE, in Sanskrit verse, records king Prakasadharman installing a silver sheath and giving a crown to the god [C019]; another pedestal, 7th century, has the same king building a shrine to Kubera, Shiva's companion [C020]; a four-line block of 991 CE is in Old Cam, the Cham language [C021]; Finot lists a stele of king Harivarman II at My Son with Sanskrit and Cham on the same face [C022].
- **The royal legend.** A stele of 657 CE gives the royal line a legend: the Brahmin Kaundinya planted a spear and married Soma, daughter of the serpent king, who founded the royal race [C023]. The same story is told of Funan in a Chinese history and of Cambodia's kings (secondary only) [C024].
- **Not only Shiva.** By one count (1927) 92 of 130 inscriptions are Shaiva and 7 Buddhist [C025]. The Cham king Jaya Indravarman's stele of 875 CE founds a Buddhist temple and monastery at Dong Duong and also recalls the My Son lingam, about 20 km away; it tells a legend instead of the history of the founders [C017, C018]. The best-known early Sanskrit text found in Champa (Vo-canh, not at My Son) is read by Finot as Buddhist in inspiration [C026].
- **After the kingdom.** The sanctuary was heavily bombed in August 1969 [C034]. India and Vietnam agreed a conservation MoU in 2014; India's ASI worked on groups A, H and K from 2017 to 2022 and handed them over on 20 December 2022 [C027, C029]; ASI reports finding a Shiva linga in A10 and a Shiva idol and yoni pitha in A13 [C028].

## People (at most three matter)
- **Bhadravarman I**, founder of the first My Son temple (4th-5th century CE; Chinese identification with Fan Hu-da only "probable but not certain") [C011, C012].
- **Cambhuvarman**, who rebuilt it and renamed the god [C013].
- **Prakasadharman (Vikrantavarman)**, whose steles of 657 and 687 CE survive at My Son [C019, C020, C023].

## Fresh angles (G7): verified facts that popular versions leave out or get wrong
- **F1. The Committee changed ICOMOS's wording.** ICOMOS recommended criterion (ii) as the "introduction" of Hindu architecture into South-East Asia; the Committee inscribed it as "an indigenous society adapting to external cultural influences" [C005, C004]. The official text says the Cham adapted; it does not say Hinduism was brought to them. (We do not know why it was changed.)
- **F2. A Buddhist monastery and the My Son Shiva lingam on one stele (875 CE).** A Cham king recorded both in a single inscription [C017]. A "Hindu kingdom" is too simple [C016].
- **F3. The first temple at My Son was probably wood, burned, and the god was renamed after the next king.** Nothing of it survives [C014, C013]. The Shiva at My Son is a royal lingam, named for kings [C011, C013, C021].
- **F4. India's own archaeologists restored three of My Son's temple groups (2017-2022) and report finding a Shiva linga in A10.** A Government of India account backed by the My Son board for the groups and years [C027, C028]. Strong Indian-audience hook; keep the attribution ("ASI reports").
- **F5. A Cham-language inscription at My Son (991 CE) and Cham on a Sanskrit stele.** The local language was written beside Sanskrit at the sanctuary [C021, C022].
- **F6. The Kaundinya-and-snake-princess royal myth on a My Son stele (657 CE).** The same legend is told of Funan and Cambodia's kings [C023, C024].
- Also: the nomination dossier by Viet Nam itself admits "a lot of vacuum" in Cham historical documents and dates "still open questions" [C035].

## Hook and twist candidates
- **Hook A (global):** "This god in Vietnam was named after a king." The twist: the first king founded the temple to Bhadresvara, and a later king rebuilt it and renamed the god with his own name added, Cambhubhadresvara [C011, C013].
- **Hook B (India):** "India's archaeologists spent five years restoring Shiva temples in Vietnam." Twist: the temples were built by Cham kings who wrote their own language beside Sanskrit [C027, C021].
- **Hook C (twist-only):** the official text says an "indigenous society adapting", not "introduction" [C004, C005].
- **Why answer** (careful): "Because Cham kings chose it; when and how the religion first arrived is not recorded" [C007, C004].
- Do not use: "colonised", "Indian kings", "the oldest", "the capital", "destroyed", a year for the first temple, a count of Cham Hindus today.
