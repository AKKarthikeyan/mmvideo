# W3-04-myson: the popular version, checked

The claims come from the pitch ("Why Vietnam's My Son has Hindu temples"), from the tone of search-result snippets and travel/tour pages (tier X, none opened beyond the snippet), and from older "Greater India" framing. They were listed only; nothing here enters the script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Indians (traders, Brahmins or kings) came and **colonised** Champa, and that is why it has Hindu temples | Unverifiable | C007, C004, C005, C024 | The Committee's own wording is "an indigenous society adapting to external cultural influences" (C004); ICOMOS first wrote "introduction" of Hindu architecture (C005). ICOMOS says the date and route are not known (C007). The 1927 "Indian colonies" reading (Majumdar) is an interpretation by an interested school (K10), not something the inscriptions say. The inscriptions opened name Cham kings with Cham royal names (-varman) building and renaming the shrine (C013). No source opened reports an Indian conquest of Champa |
| M2 | My Son was built by Indian dynasties (Cholas, Pallavas, Guptas) | Unverifiable | C011, C013, C019, C021 | The builders named in the inscriptions are Cham kings (Bhadravarman I, Cambhuvarman, Prakasadharman, Harivarmadeva, Jaya Indravarman). No source opened links a dynasty from India to the site. The nomination mentions Gupta-period inspiration in sculpture only (not used) |
| M3 | The Cham kingdom was a "Hindu kingdom" and everyone was Hindu | Embellished | C016, C017, C025, C026 | UNESCO/ICOMOS: Shiva cult was the state religion, but Mahayana Buddhism was strongly established in the north, and by the later 10th century Hinduism at My Son "had achieved parity with Buddhism". An 875 stele records a Buddhist monastery and the My Son lingam together. By Majumdar's 1927 count 92 of 130 inscriptions are Shaiva and 7 Buddhist |
| M4 | My Son is "the oldest Hindu temple in Southeast Asia" / "the largest" | Unverifiable | C011, C014, C015 | No source opened makes or tests a ranking. UNESCO says the monuments are "unique and without equal in Southeast Asia" (S02, not a measured claim). The oldest temple at My Son is lost; the standing towers are 7th century and later (C014, C015) |
| M5 | The temples at My Son date to the 4th century | Embellished | C012, C014, C015, C036 | The 4th-5th century is when the first, probably wooden, temple was founded (C012, C014); nothing of it survives. The towers ICOMOS dates are 7th to 13th century, most of the best from the later 10th |
| M6 | My Son was the capital of the Champa kingdom | Embellished | C010 | ICOMOS and UNESCO say so; Viet Nam's own dossier names Sinhapura (Tra Kieu) and Indrapura as capitals in Quang Nam. Safe word: royal sanctuary |
| M7 | The Champa kingdom began in 192 CE | Confirmed (secondary only) | C008 | UNESCO and ICOMOS give 192 CE; the underlying records are "sporadic Chinese records" (S01) that we did not read. Say "UNESCO dates it to 192 CE" if at all |
| M8 | My Son was "destroyed" by American bombing | Embellished | C033, C034 | ICOMOS: August 1969 bombing caused "great destruction"; UNESCO today lists 71 standing monuments in eight groups. Both are true; "destroyed" is not |
| M9 | Shiva is named at My Son as an Indian import ("the same Shiva") | Embellished | C013, C017, C018 | The god at My Son is a lingam named after the king (Bhadresvara, then Cambhubhadresvara). By 875 a stele tells of Bhrigu and Uroja sent by Maheshvara instead (C018). Shiva worship is Indian in origin; the royal naming is Cham |
| M10 | India restored all of My Son / India built it | Embellished | C027 | India's ASI restored three groups (A, H, K) in 2017-2022 (a Government of India account backed by the My Son board). Other groups are not part of that phase |
| M11 | The Cham are "Hindu" today / the religion died out | Unverifiable | | Not tested: no census table opened (open_questions.md) |
| M12 | The oldest Sanskrit inscription in Champa (Vo-canh) is a Hindu text | Contradicted | C026 | Finot (1915) reads it as of "too clearly Buddhist an inspiration". It is not from My Son. (Contradicted as a reading: scholars may differ; the point stands that it is not clearly Hindu) |
| M13 | The Kaundinya-and-snake-princess story is an Indian myth about Champa's origin | Embellished | C023, C024 | The story is on a My Son stele of 657 CE; the same story is told of Funan in a Chinese history and of Cambodia's kings (Majumdar, secondary). It is a royal legend, not a record of a migration |
