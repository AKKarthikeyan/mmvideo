## 0. The honest answer to the pitch

The pitch ("Why the Kumbh Mela moves between four cities") is a fair question but it has two answers that must not be mixed.

1. **The legend (told as a legend, attributed).** Government pages tell it differently: Uttar Pradesh's Prayagraj page says Mohini carried a pot of nectar, a scuffle broke out and four drops fell at Prayag, Haridwar, Nasik and Ujjain [C009]; India's UNESCO file has Vishnu as Mohini pass the nectar to Garuda and a few drops fall at the same four places [C010]; Maharashtra's Nashik page has Jayanta, Indra's son, run for twelve "days" (read as twelve years) and put the pot down at the four places [C010]. The Sanskrit Bhagavata Purana chapters on the churning (8.7-8.12), in the e-text we read, have the pot of nectar but name none of the four places [C026]. "Why four cities" is therefore answered by a legend, not by a record.
2. **The rule (stated by governments, checked by us).** Which city hosts a given Kumbh is fixed by where Jupiter, the Sun and the Moon stand in the zodiac [C004, C005, C006, C016]. Haridwar: Jupiter in Aquarius with the Sun entering Aries. Nashik and Ujjain: Jupiter in Leo (Simha), hence the name Simhastha [C005, C014]. Prayagraj: Jupiter in Taurus (or Aries) with the Sun in Capricorn [C005]. Counting with a NASA/JPL ephemeris, every recent event fits its rule [C012, C013]. So the festival moves because it follows Jupiter round the sky, taking about twelve years to return to a city [C007, C018].

The pitch's phrase "between four cities" is true as a list but not as a rhythm: the cities do not take turns every four years [C002, C015].

## 2. How the rule works (our own computation, method/ folder)

- Signs are the twelve sidereal zodiac signs (rashis), not the star constellations, and not the Western tropical signs. We used Lahiri's ayanamsa (23 deg 15' 00.658" at 1956-03-21, advanced by IAU 2006 precession). A different ayanamsa moves ingress dates by up to about three days (Hardwicke's 8 April vs our 11 April for the Sun entering Aries in 1796 [C021]). Say "about", never a dated minute.
- Jupiter in the sky (JPL Horizons geocentric longitudes, 1993-2041): mean return 11.87 years, so a fixed 12-year count drifts one year in about 95 years [C018].
- By the Haridwar rule (Jupiter in Aquarius on the day the Sun enters Aries) the qualifying years are 1998, 2010, 2021, 2033: 12, 11, 12 years apart [C017]. In 2022 Jupiter left Aquarius for Pisces on 14 April, about when the Sun entered Aries: a close call [C017]. Haridwar's only officially confirmed recent year is 2021 [C017]; 2010 is read only through a summariser [C031].
- Jupiter's sign at each recent event: Taurus (Prayagraj 2013), Leo (Nashik 2015, Ujjain 2016), Aquarius (Haridwar 2021), Taurus (Prayagraj 2025) [C013]. Taurus, Leo and Aquarius are 3, 6 and 3 signs apart round the 12-sign circle: four cities, three Jupiter positions.
- Jupiter in Leo: 15 Jul 2015 to 12 Aug 2016, containing both Nashik's Simhastha (2015-16) and Ujjain's (22 Apr 2016 onward) [C012].
- Next Nashik: Jupiter enters Leo around 1 Nov 2026, dips back to Cancer on 25 Jan 2027, returns to Leo on 26 Jun 2027 and stays to 27 Nov 2027; Maharashtra's authority is preparing for 2027 [C035].

## 3. Fresh angles (G7): verified facts the popular version leaves out or gets wrong

- **F1. Four cities, three Jupiter positions.** Nashik and Ujjain are tied to the same Jupiter sign (Leo) and share the name Simhastha; the festival really has three "slots" round the zodiac, Taurus, Leo and Aquarius, 3-6-3 signs apart [C013, C014, C012, C005]. Popular versions treat the four cities as four independent drops with four independent dates.
- **F2. The official summary is "every four years", the calendar is 2, 1, 5, 4.** UNESCO's description and the Prayagraj district page say four years; the real run of recent Kumbhs is 2013, 2015, 2016, 2021, 2025 [C002, C015, C016]. PIB (Ministry of Culture, Jan 2025) says it more truthfully: "four times over 12 years".
- **F3. The 1796 record and the 2025 site say the same thing about Haridwar.** An East India Company officer wrote down the Haridwar rule in April 1796 in nearly the words kumbh.gov.in uses now [C019, C005]. And in 1911 an official gazetteer gave that same Haridwar rule for Allahabad, where it does not fit (Jupiter was in Taurus, Sun in Capricorn in 1906) [C022]: records, too, mixed the sites up.
- **F4. India's own UNESCO file says "Nobody knows when the legend of the Kumbh was first crystallized"** and then says Hiuen Tsang was the first to mention it [C025]; the translation of Hiuen Tsang's Prayaga has a five-yearly giving-away of wealth and bathing, but no pitcher, no twelve-year count, no Jupiter and no four-city rotation [C024].
- **F5. Three of the four Kumbh rivers flow into the Ganga system; the Godavari does not.** Nashik's Godavari rises 80 km from the Arabian Sea and runs 1,465 km east to the Bay of Bengal; Ujjain district is wholly in the Ganga basin via the Kshipra and the Chambal; the nomination file's sentence "All the rivers ... are part of the mighty Ganges" is a statement of faith, which Nashik's own page puts as "Dakshin Ganga" [C027, C028, C029].
- **F6. The Kumbh site says the Bhagavata Purana links the alignments to the Kumbh; the churning chapters we read do not name the four places or give the rule** [C026, S04]. Soft angle (one e-text); keep out of the Short unless a Sanskritist confirms.
