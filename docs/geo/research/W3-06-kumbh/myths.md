# W3-06 Kumbh Mela, why it moves between four cities: the popular version, checked

The claims come from the pitch ("Why the Kumbh Mela moves between four cities"), from what India's own nomination to UNESCO and state-government pages say, and from search-result snippets of news and explainer pages (Tier X: logicallyfacts.com, counterview.in, news sites; none opened as evidence). Nothing here enters a script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | The Kumbh moves between four cities because drops of the nectar of immortality fell at those four places | Unverifiable | C009, C010, C011 | It is a legend, told in at least three different ways on official pages (Mohini spilling four drops; Mohini passing the pot to Garuda; Jayanta putting the pot down for twelve days). Tell it as the legend, attributed ("according to the legend, as the Uttar Pradesh government tells it"), never as the reason |
| M2 | The Kumbh is held "every four years by rotation" at the four cities (UNESCO's description; Prayagraj district page) | Embellished | C002, C015, C016 | It comes round four times in twelve years, but unevenly: Prayagraj 2013, Nashik 2015, Ujjain 2016, Haridwar 2021, Prayagraj 2025 are 2, 1, 5 and 4 years apart. Each city waits about twelve years |
| M3 | It is every twelve years because Jupiter takes twelve years to go round (or because twelve divine days equal twelve human years) | Embellished | C007, C017, C018 | Jupiter's measured return averaged 11.87 years (1993-2041), so the count is "about twelve"; by the Haridwar rule the qualifying years run 1998, 2010, 2021, 2033, a 12-11-12 pattern. The twelve-days-equal-twelve-years reading is the legend's own arithmetic (Nashik page) |
| M4 | The Bhagavata Purana says the sacred alignments of celestial bodies directly relate to the Kumbh (kumbh.gov.in) | Unverifiable | C026 | In the Sanskrit e-text of Bhagavata 8.7-8.12 (the churning) there is the pot of nectar but no four cities, no rivers and no Jupiter rule. One e-text only; other recensions and the Skanda Purana (cited by the Nashik page) were not opened |
| M5 | The 7th-century Chinese pilgrim Hiuen Tsang was the first to mention the Kumbh Mela (India's nomination file) | Contradicted | C024, C025 | His account of Prayaga, in Beal's 1884 translation, describes a quinquennial giving-away of wealth on the sands at the confluence and bathing to wash away sin; it names no pitcher, no twelve-year cycle, no Jupiter and no other city. Translation OCR is damaged in places but the relevant lines are intact |
| M6 | The Kumbh is ancient and has stayed alive "in its purest traditional form" | Embellished | C036, C025, C019, C022 | UNESCO's Committee asked India to stop using words like "intact" and "purest form". India's own file admits nobody knows when the legend crystallised. Our earliest dated English eyewitness of the Haridwar rule is 1796 (Hardwicke); the rule was misstated for Allahabad in an official 1911 gazetteer |
| M7 | All four Kumbh rivers are "part of the mighty Ganges" (nomination file) | Embellished | C029, C027, C028 | By drainage, the Ganga (Haridwar, Prayagraj) and the Kshipra (Ujjain, in the Ganga basin via the Chambal) are; the Godavari at Nashik runs its own 1,465 km to the Bay of Bengal. As tradition it is called Dakshin Ganga, the Ganga of the south |
| M8 | The nomination says the Kumbh is on "Saraswati in Haridwar" | Contradicted | C004, C008 | The Haridwar district's own page puts the nectar at Brahmkund on Har-Ki-Pauri on the Ganga ("Ganga emerges from the mountains"); UP and PIB pages name the Ganges for Haridwar. The nomination sentence reads "Ganga in Prayag, Saraswati in Haridwar" |
| M9 | A Kumbh at Allahabad is held when the Sun is in Aries and Jupiter in Aquarius (1911 Allahabad gazetteer) | Contradicted | C022, C005 | That is Haridwar's rule. On 15 Jan-1 Feb 1906 Jupiter was in Taurus and the Sun in Capricorn, the modern Prayagraj rule. The gazetteer's 1906 year is correct |
| M10 | Nashik and Ujjain are held under the same Jupiter sign | Confirmed | C013, C014, C012 | Both wait for Jupiter in Leo (Simha) and both are called Simhastha. Counted by us, Jupiter was in Leo 15 Jul 2015-12 Aug 2016, containing Nashik 2015-16 and Ujjain Apr-May 2016 |
| M11 | The 2021 Haridwar Kumbh was held a year early, only 11 years after 2010, for non-astronomical reasons | Unverifiable | C017, C031 | The claim appears in news and an opinion site (Tier X, not opened). Our count by the stated rule: Jupiter was in Aquarius when the Sun entered Aries in 2021 (Jupiter entered Aquarius 6 Apr 2021); in 2022 it left Aquarius for Pisces on 14 Apr 2022, about the same time the Sun entered Aries. 2010 as the previous Haridwar year is read only through a summariser (C031) |
| M12 | The big Maha Kumbh is held only at Prayagraj, once in 144 years | Unverifiable | | Seen only in a search summary of an official site; the page was not opened. Not in the dossier |
