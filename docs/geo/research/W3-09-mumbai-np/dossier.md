# W3-09 "Why Mumbai has a national park inside the city": research dossier (lite, 8 Nov 2026)

Research only. No script. Every sentence carries claim IDs. Sources: `sources.jsonl` (S01-S03); method in `method/`.

## Verdict on the pitch
**True, with care on "inside the city".** The Government of India's gazette calls the park "a unique habitat enclosed in the metropolis of Mumbai", in Thane and Mumbai districts [C001]. It spans two districts, so "inside the metropolis" or "on Mumbai's doorstep, within city limits in part" is safer than "inside the city" alone [C001].
The "why", per the park's own history: British-built Vihar and Tulsi lakes fed Mumbai's water, the city corporation protected their catchments, and that protected forest became a national park [C003, C004, C005].
Honest wording: "Mumbai kept a forest to guard its drinking water. That forest became a 104 sq km national park, with leopards in it."

## The real story, in plain words
- Two lakes, Vihar and Tulsi, were built by the British for Mumbai's water (park timeline: 1870) [C003].
- The Bombay Municipal Corporation acquired their catchments to protect them (timeline: 1942) [C004].
- Krishnagiri National Park, about 20 sq km, came under the Bombay National Park Act (timeline: 1950); 68.27 sq km became Borivali National Park (timeline: 1968) [C005].
- Renamed Sanjay Gandhi National Park in 1981, enlarged with Thane forests to 103.84 sq km [C002, C005].
- Leopards: 54 adults and 4 cubs photographed in 2024 [C007]; stray dogs are 24% of their diet in a 2015 study [C008].

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
1. **A water tank became a national park.** The forest survived because it was a drinking-water catchment, not because it was set aside for wildlife [C003, C004].
2. **The park's first name came from the caves.** "Krishnagiri" (black mountain) is the source of "Kanheri"; the national park's first name was Krishnagiri [C005, C006].
3. **Mumbai's leopards eat the city's stray dogs** (24% of diet) [C008], and at least 54 adults were photographed in 2024 [C007].
G7: PASS (angles 1-3 rest on VERIFIED-P claims absent from the popular "it's just a big forest" version).
