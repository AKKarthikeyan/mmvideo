# W3-11 Colachel 1741: dossier (Shorts lite, ⚑ sensitive)

Research run: 1 Nov 2026 (interrupted, sources saved) and 8 Nov 2026 (completed from saved copies). Sources: S01 Dutch records (Galletti 1911, Madras Govt), S02 Travancore State Manual (Nagam Aiya 1906), S03 Shungoonny Menon 1878.

## Pitch test
Pitch: "Colachel 1741: when an Indian kingdom beat the Dutch."
- **Year 1741: true** (all three sources; C004).
- **"Beat the Dutch": true for Colachel, overstated for the war.** Both sides' records agree Travancore took the Dutch post at Colachel (C003). The Dutch records call it a garrison surrender after a fire (C006); Travancore's histories call it a battle won. The peace treaty came years later (C009: S02 gives 18 Oct 1748 for a draft approved by Batavia and 15 Aug 1753 for final ratification).
- Honest wording: **"Colachel, 1741: when Travancore took a Dutch fort and the Dutch lost their grip on its coast."** Or: "In 1741 the kingdom of Travancore forced a Dutch garrison at Colachel to surrender."

## Who gains from each version (rule 6)
- S01 VOC letters: the losers; they blame a red-hot ball and a powder fire, and count their own small force. Internal, written within weeks.
- S02/S03 Travancore court histories: the winners, writing 137-165 years later; a pitched battle with a cavalry charge serves the dynasty. S02 copies S03.

## Hook and twist candidates
- Hook: "In 1741 the Dutch wanted to turn this coast into a Dutch province. They called it the most beautiful district in Malabar." (C001, C002)
- Twist 1 (fresh angle, G7): **The Dutch "army" was tiny.** Their whole Malabar field force was 350 Europeans and 490 Malays (C005); by their own letters the Colachel garrison surrendered after a red-hot ball fired their gunpowder and rice (C006).
- Twist 2 (fresh angle, G7): **The loser became the winner's general.** Captured Dutchman Eustachius De Lannoy went on to train and lead Travancore's army for about 37 years (C008).
- Twist 3: **It did not end the war.** The peace treaty came years later (C009, dates disputed); the two sides even disagree on the day (C010).

G7: PASS (two fresh angles: Dutch own force count and fire account C005/C006; De Lannoy switching sides C008; both missing from the popular "great victory" versions)

## What a 9-beat 85-100 word Short could safely say
1. In January 1741 the Dutch East India Company occupied Colachel, on the coast of the kingdom of Travancore. (C001)
2. Their letters planned to make the coast from Colachel to Cape Comorin a Dutch province. (C002)
3. Their whole field force in Malabar was 350 Europeans and 490 Malays. (C005)
4. In 1741 Travancore's forces attacked the Dutch post at Colachel, and it fell. (C003, C004)
5. Dutch letters say a red-hot ball fired a barrel of gunpowder and burned their rice, and the garrison surrendered. (C006)
6. Travancore's histories tell it as a battle won, with 24 Dutch prisoners taken. (C003, C007)
7. One prisoner, Eustachius De Lannoy, went on to train and lead Travancore's army. (C008)
8. His tomb says he served the king for about 37 years. (C008)
9. The war did not end there: the peace treaty came years later. (C009, hedged)
10. Even the date is disputed: the two sides' records differ by days. (C010)
