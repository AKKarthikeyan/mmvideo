# W3-12 "Why almost nobody lives in the western Thar Desert": research dossier (lite, 8 Nov 2026)

Sources: S01 Census DCHB Jaisalmer (P1), S02 CGWB Jaisalmer aquifer report 2017 (P2), S03 Census 2011 A-02 Rajasthan (P1). Computations: method/compute.py, method/compute_output.txt.

## Verdict on the premise
Embellished. The western Thar is sparse, not empty. Jaisalmer, the westernmost and largest district, had 6,69,919 people in 2011 (C004) at 17 per sq km against Rajasthan's 200 (C001): the least dense of the state's 33 districts (C002), with 11.22% of the state's land and 0.98% of its people (C003). Honest wording: "Why so few people live in the western Thar."

## Why (limiting factor, from the sources)
- Water, not land: about 185 mm of rain a year (C005), no perennial river (C007), groundwater down to about 124 m and often salty at depth (C006).
- Where people farm, they lean on the Indira Gandhi canal: about 61.6% of irrigated area in CGWB's table (C008); even there water tables are falling (C011).
- Ruled out with evidence: "the desert is emptying" (contradicted, C009/C010). Not tested and not to be implied: the border, military land or policy as a cause.

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
G7: PASS
1. The "empty" district is one of the fastest-growing: almost ninefold since 1901 (75,551 to 6,69,919), and 31.81% in 2001-11 vs the state's 21.31% (C009, C010).
2. The water is deep and salty: wells reach 124 m, and the deeper tube well is often saltier than the dug well in the same village (C006); the canal waters most irrigated land, yet levels fall even there (C008, C011).
