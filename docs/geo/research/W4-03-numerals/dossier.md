# W4-03-numerals "The 'Arabic' numerals on your phone are Indian": research dossier (lite, 14 Nov 2026)

Sources: S01 Bodleian Libraries 2017 release (holder of the Bakhshali manuscript); S02 Leonardo of Pisa, Liber abaci (1202); S03 Latin al-Khwarizmi, Cambridge MS Ii.6.5. ⚑ cultural-origin topic: see open_questions.md "Wording risks for AK".

## Verdict on the pitch
**True in substance, too strong as worded.** The two texts that carried the digits into Latin Europe both call them Indian: Leonardo's "nine figures of the Indians" (C001, C003) and the Latin al-Khwarizmi's "numbering of the Indians by nine letters", with place value and an empty-place circle credited to the Indians (C004, C005). But the route ran through Arabic-language arithmetic, and Leonardo names zero in Arabic (C002). Honest wording: **"Europe calls them Arabic numerals. The books that brought them to Europe call them Indian."**

What each source proves: S02 = digits named Indian, zero named in Arabic, place value taught; S03 = digits, place value and the placeholder circle credited to the Indians; S01 = a dot zero used hundreds of times in a manuscript from the subcontinent, carbon-dated by its holder to as early as the 3rd-4th century (some leaves).

## Fresh angles (G7)
G7: PASS. Verified facts the popular versions leave out or get wrong:
1. **The Arabic part is the name of zero, not the digits.** Leonardo: nine "figures of the Indians" plus "this sign 0, which in Arabic is called zephirum" (C001, C002).
2. **Al-Khwarizmi himself credits the Indians**, and even writes that if the Indians had another reason for their nine letters, his method still works (C004, C005).
3. **Europe's teacher learnt it as a boy at a customs house**, where his father was a scribe for Pisan merchants (C003); he rated the rival "algorismus" almost an error beside "the method of the Indians" (C010).
4. **The oldest "Indian" zero was found near Peshawar**, in today's Pakistan (C007).

## What an 8-beat 65-100 word Short could safely say (strongest verified one-sentence facts)
1. In 1202 Leonardo of Pisa began his book's first chapter: "The nine figures of the Indians are these." (C001)
2. He added a tenth sign, 0, "which in Arabic is called zephirum." (C002)
3. His father, a scribe at a customs house for Pisan merchants, sent him to learn the abacus as a boy. (C003)
4. A Latin version of al-Khwarizmi sets out "the numbering of the Indians by nine letters." (C004)
5. It says the Indians marked an empty place with "a little circle like the letter o." (C005)
6. A manuscript found near Peshawar in 1881 uses a dot for zero hundreds of times. (C006, C007)
7. Its holder, Oxford's Bodleian Library, carbon-dated some leaves to as early as the 3rd or 4th century. (C006)
8. That is about 800 to 1,000 years before Leonardo's book (our measurement). (C008)
9. Leonardo counted the rival "algorismus" almost an error beside "the method of the Indians." (C010)
10. The manuscript has been at the Bodleian since 1902. (C007)
