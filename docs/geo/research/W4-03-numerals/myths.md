# W4-03-numerals: popular claims, checked (lite: 3)

Nothing here enters the script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | "Arabic numerals" were invented by the Arabs | Contradicted | C001, C004, C005 | Both texts that carried them to Latin Europe call the nine signs Indian; al-Khwarizmi's Latin text credits the Indians with place value and the empty-place circle. The Arabic link is the route and zero's name (C002) |
| M2 | India invented zero | Embellished | C006, C009 | The Bodleian says placeholder zeros were also used by Babylonians and Maya; what it credits to India is the dot that became our 0 and zero as a number (Brahmagupta, 628) |
| M3 | The Bakhshali manuscript proves zero was used in the 3rd century | Embellished | C006 | The holder says "as early as the 3rd or 4th century" and that the leaves come from at least three periods; it does not say which leaves are oldest |
