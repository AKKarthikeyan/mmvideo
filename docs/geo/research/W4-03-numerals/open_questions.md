# W4-03-numerals: open questions and wording risks

## Open questions (not closed under the lite cap)
1. **Baghdad is not in our three sources.** None of S01-S03 names Baghdad, the House of Wisdom or al-Khwarizmi's dates. A Baghdad pin needs a 4th source (AK to authorise) or must be dropped. The verified route is: subcontinent -> (Arabic-language arithmetic, al-Khwarizmi) -> Latin (Cambridge MS) and -> Leonardo at a customs house -> Pisa.
2. **Bakhshali dating is the holder's statement.** The paper is "available on request"; which leaves carry which date is not in S01. Published scholarly objections after 2017 were not opened. Keep "as early as" and "some leaves".
3. **Bugia (C012).** The OCR reads "hugee"; Bugia/Béjaïa needs the page image and a gazetteer. Until then, no Algeria pin and no 896 km line.
4. Date of the Latin translation of al-Khwarizmi and the fate of the Arabic original: not in our sources; do not say.

## Wording risks for AK (⚑ cultural-origin claim)
a. **"Indian" means the subcontinent.** The Bakhshali manuscript was found near Peshawar, in today's Pakistan (C007). Do not say "found in India" or show an India outline around the pin. Say "ancient India (the subcontinent)" or "near Peshawar".
b. **Do not say "the digits on your phone were written in India" as if the shapes are the same.** The sources show the system and the names; S01 says today's 0 "evolved from a dot". Say "the system", "the nine figures", not "these exact shapes".
c. **"Not Arabic at all" is an overstatement.** Europe got them through Arabic-language arithmetic (S03 is a Latin al-Khwarizmi), and Leonardo gives zero an Arabic name (C002). Honest line: "Europe calls them Arabic; the men who brought them to Europe called them Indian."
d. **"India invented zero" is too strong** (M2). Placeholder zeros: Babylonians and Maya too (Bodleian, C009). Credit to India: the dot that became our 0, and zero as a number (Brahmagupta 628, "the Bodleian says").
e. **"Oldest zero, 1,800 years old"**: no. Use "carbon-dated as early as the 3rd or 4th century" with "some leaves" (C006, K1). Never "proved".
f. **Each source proves a different thing**: Liber abaci = nine digits called Indian + zero named in Arabic (C001-C003); Latin al-Khwarizmi = Indian digits, place value and the little circle (C004-C005); Bakhshali = dot zero in an Indian manuscript, dated by its holder (C006). Do not let one stand for another.
g. **Names**: "Fibonacci" is not in the text: he is "Leonardo son of Bonacci, of Pisa". "Algoritmi de numero Indorum" is the 1857 editor's title; the manuscript starts "Dixit algoritmi". Do not claim "algorithm comes from his name" (not in our sources).
h. **No nationalist framing**: avoid "stole", "erased", "the West hid", "India gave the world everything". Avoid any border on the map (house rule; Peshawar is in Pakistan).
i. The Liber abaci text we have is the revised version dedicated to Michael Scot; "1202" is the date its incipit gives.
