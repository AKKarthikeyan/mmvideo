# W4-05 "How a British plant hunter took tea from China to India": research dossier (lite, 14 Nov 2026)

Research only. No script. Every sentence carries claim IDs. Sources: `sources.jsonl` (S01-S03); method in `method/`. ⚑ colonial history: each record's author is named; no side taken.

## Verdict on the pitch
**True as worded, misleading as usually told.** Robert Fortune, sent by the East India Company, did take tea from China to India: in his own count upwards of 20,000 plants, eight tea makers and tools, which reached Calcutta on 15 March 1851 and went on to the Company's plantations in Kumaon and Garhwal [C001, C002, C004, C005]. But the Company's own Tea Committee had declared tea "beyond all doubt indigenous in Upper Assam" on 24 December 1834, 14 years before Fortune set out [C006], and local Singpho and Khamti people were already drinking it [C007]. Today Assam grows about half of India's tea (50.7% in 2024-25, our measurement) [C008].
Honest wording: "A British plant hunter did carry China's tea to India's Himalaya in 1851. But tea was already growing wild in Assam, and Assam now makes half of India's tea."
Not verified here: that Assam's bushes today are the native "Assam variety" (the topic hint's twist). Our sources show where India's tea is grown, not which plant it is [C008 note, C009].

## The real story, in plain words
- 1834: Company officers report a wild tea shrub in Upper Assam; the Tea Committee confirms it from the fruit, 24 Dec 1834 [C006]. A British officer writes that Singphos and Khamtis boil the leaves and dry them in balls [C007].
- The Committee thought it was "the identical tea of China" [C009].
- 1848: Fortune leaves Southampton for China, sent by the Court of Directors [C001]. He travels inland in Chinese dress, his queue dressed by a barber [C003].
- 1851: sixteen glazed cases leave Shanghai on 16 Feb, reach Calcutta 15 Mar [C004]; the plants are for the north-western Himalaya, e.g. Hawulbaugh near Almora [C005].
- 2024-25: Assam 666.82 of 1,315.77 million kg [C008]; Darjeeling 5.75 [C011].

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
1. **India's tea was already wild in India.** The Company declared Assam tea native 14 years before it sent Fortune to China [C006, C001; method/tea_numbers_output.txt].
2. **The British did not discover the drink.** Their own 1834 letter says Singphos and Khamtis already made it [C007].
3. Fortune's plants went to Kumaon and Garhwal, not to Assam, which now makes half of India's tea [C005, C008].
