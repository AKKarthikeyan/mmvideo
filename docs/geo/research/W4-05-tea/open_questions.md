# W4-05-tea: open questions

1. **The twist as hinted** ("much of India's tea is the native Assam variety, not Fortune's China plants") is NOT verified: no botanical or Tocklai/Tea Research Association source opened (lite cap). Our sources only show Assam grows half of India's tea. Early Assam gardens may also have used China seed; do not claim "Assam tea is 100% native plant".
2. Longitude of Tsong-gan-hien (Wuyi/Bohea) and exact Almora/Hawulbaugh coordinates: take from official geodata before the map is built.
3. The belief "India's tea came from China via a British smuggler" is not shown to be common (no textbook or quiz opened).
4. Fortune's 20,000 count is self-reported; how many survived is not checked against Company records.
5. Whether any Fortune plants reached Darjeeling: not in his 1852 book; not settled here.

## Wording risks for AK (⚑ colonial history)
- Do not call Fortune a "spy", "thief" or "smuggler" as fact; the 1852 book shows a disguise and a Company commission, not theft. Say "in disguise" and "sent by the East India Company".
- Do not say "the British discovered tea in Assam"; their own 1834 letters say local Singpho and Khamti people already drank it. Say "the Company recognised it as true tea in 1834".
- Do not say "India's tea came from Assam's own plants" or "is the Assam variety" (see Q1). Say "grown in Assam".
- Name the Singpho and Khamti as the 1834 letter does, but do not attribute "the first tea in India" to any one community; the letters also mention Manipur (1826) and the Bhutan hills.
- "Half of India's tea" is production by state (our measurement from Tea Board FINAL 2024-25), not by plant or by value.
- Avoid praise or blame language for the Company ("gift", "robbery", "plunder"); state what each record says and who wrote it.
- Dates: 1834 is official recognition, not first discovery; 1823 (popular date) is not in our sources, so do not say it.
