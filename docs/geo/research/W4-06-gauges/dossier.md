# W4-06 "Indian trains once ran on three track widths": research dossier (15 Nov 2026)

Indica Atlas Week 4, Shorts Lite mode (3 sources: S01 MoSPI Year Book ch.19, S02 PIB backgrounder 15 Apr 2026, S03 Ministry of Railways feature 21 Feb 2007). Research only: no script, voice or render. Status: pending independent check (verify.md).

## Verdict on the premise
**Not true as worded.** (1) Three gauge CLASSES, but FOUR widths: broad 1.676 m, metre 1.000 m, narrow 0.762 m and 0.610 m [C01]. (2) "Once ran" is the wrong tense: a Government of India document of 15 Apr 2026 still lists broad, metre and narrow gauge in the present tense [C02]. (3) What changed is the share: broad gauge went from about 72% (2002-03) to 88% (2013-14) [C07]; in 2010, 9,717 km was still metre or narrow gauge [C05].
**Honest wording:** "India built its railways on several track widths, and spent decades converting them to one. Broad gauge is now the backbone, but the others are not gone."

## The real story, in plain words
Broad gauge (5 ft 6 in) was used for the first lines; metre gauge was officially adopted as the second standard in 1871; narrower gauges served poor areas and fed goods to the main lines [C03]. Project Unigauge began on 1 April 1992; by Feb 2007 the Ministry said over 13,000 km had been converted, a step towards "one country, one gauge" [C06]. In 2010 the route was 63,974 km: 54,257 broad, 7,180 metre, 2,537 narrow [C04]. Broad gauge is 70,142 route km in March 2026 [C08]. No official 2026 share was opened [C11].

## Fresh angles (G7)
G7: PASS (three verified angles below; researcher judgement, checker to confirm)

1. **Four widths, not three** [C01]: the popular version miscounts; the broadest is 2.75 times the narrowest (our measurement).
2. **Still a live classification in 2026** [C02]: the pitch says "once"; the Government's own 2026 text says "has the following categories of track".
3. Narrow lines were built for "developing poor areas" [C03]: the width was a decision about who could afford a railway.
