# W4-08 "Mumbai was once seven islands": research dossier (lite, 15 Nov 2026)

Research only. No script. Sources: `sources.jsonl` (S01-S03); method in `method/`. Not sensitive (TOPICS.json), but colonial-era works are named by author.

## Verdict on the pitch
**True as worded, and officially stated** by the Gazetteer (1909) and by MCGM's own profile [C001]. The interesting corrections are underneath: the lists of the seven differ between records [C002, K1]; the Hornby Vellard (finished 1784) shut out the sea but did not make one island [C003, C004]; the last separate island, Colaba, was joined only in 1838 [C006].

## The real story, in plain words
- Seven islands, little changed until the 1700s [C001]: Colaba and Old Woman's Island in the south, the main Bombay island, Mazagon, Parel, Worli, Mahim [C002].
- At high tide the sea broke through a "great breach" between Malabar Hill and Worli, almost dividing the island in two (Grose, 1772) [C004].
- Governor William Hornby had the Vellard built, finished 1784, closing the breach [C003]. Reportedly he started without the Directors' sanction [C005, hedged].
- Colaba stayed a separate island until the causeway of 1838 [C006]. The 1845 Mahim-Bandra causeway links to Salsette, not an island of the seven [C008].
- Island area 22.48 sq mi (1909) vs Island City 67.79 km2 now: about 16-18% larger, definitions differ [C007].

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
1. The wall that closed the breach was built, reportedly, against the Company Directors' refusal; Hornby sat on his suspension order until he handed over [C005, hedged].
2. The last island was still an island 54 years after the Vellard: Colaba, 1838 [C003, C006].
3. The records do not agree on which seven [C002, K1].
G7: PASS (angles 1 and 2 rest on S02/S03 claims absent from the popular "seven islands joined" story).
