**Status: NOT CLEARED until verify.md says G6: PASS.** Sources: two documents, both tier P2: S01 US EIA, Today in Energy, 16 Jun 2025 (2024 data); S02 UNCTAD news release, 1 Apr 2026. Saved in `sources/` (local only). ⚑ Sensitive: war context and neighbours; see open_questions.md.

## Verdict on the premise
The pitch asks "who runs out of oil first, the West or Asia?" The data answer is narrower than the question.
- Direct physical exposure is Asia's: 84% of Hormuz crude and condensate went to Asia in 2024 [C004]; China, India, Japan and South Korea were the top four destinations [C005]; EIA says these markets "would likely be most affected" [C007]. The US imports through Hormuz were about 2% of its petroleum liquids consumption [C006].
- But "runs out of oil" is not what EIA says; prices are global, so the Short says "hit hardest", credits EIA, and never says "the West is safe".
- Wrong figures in the brief: "89%" and "74%" are not in EIA; EIA says 84% and 69% [myths M2]. Not used.
- Dropped: Qeshm flashes, the 34 attacks, "3-month high" crossings (news pages), Bab el-Mandeb (not sourced; separate Short).

## The real story, in plain words
In 2024 about 20 million barrels a day, about a fifth of the world's petroleum liquids consumption, moved through the Strait of Hormuz [C001], one of the world's key chokepoints, a narrow channel on a busy sea route [C010], between Oman and Iran [C002]. Very few routes go around it [C003], and bypass pipelines could take roughly an eighth of the flow [C008]. Most of that crude, 84%, went to Asia, mostly to China, India, Japan and South Korea [C004, C005]. The US took the equivalent of about 2% of its petroleum use [C006]. So EIA expects Asia to be hit hardest by a disruption [C007].

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
G7: PASS. Facts popular versions leave out or get wrong (myths.md):
1. **Hormuz oil mostly flows east, not west** [C004, C006]: the question "West or Asia?" has a number for each side: 84% to Asia against about 2% of US petroleum use.
2. **The commonly quoted shares are off**: 89% and 74% circulate; EIA's own figures are 84% and 69% [C004, C005].
3. **The pipeline "escape route" is about an eighth of the flow**: 2.6 million b/d against about 20 million b/d [C008]; and EIA notes the UAE pipeline already runs near its use.
4. **A UN body logged a 95% fall in transits in March 2026** (from about 130 a day to 6) [C009]: dated context, not today's status.
