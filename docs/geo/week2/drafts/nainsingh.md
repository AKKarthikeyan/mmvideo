### Fact table

Main source: T. G. Montgomerie, "Report of a Route-Survey made by Pundit —, from Nepal to Lhasa…", *Journal of the Royal Geographical Society* vol. 38 (1868), pp. 129–219. Read in full at JSTOR Early Journal Content, archive.org copy: https://archive.org/details/jstor-1798572 (text: https://archive.org/download/jstor-1798572/1798572_djvu.txt). Notes for AK:
- The report says "paces". The script says "steps" to keep it plain. One pace here is about 31.7 inches (80 cm), from "a pace of 31½ inches" on p. 145.
- I left out a line about his prayer wheel to stay within 95 words. If AK prefers it, it can replace beat 8: "His prayer wheel hid his notes, not a prayer."
- I did not say he was a schoolteacher or name his home valley (Johar, Kumaon), because I could not read either at a primary source.

### Map care
- Describe this as a 19th-century survey on the map of the time. Say nothing about today's China–India relations.
- Show India's boundaries as on the Survey of India's official map, and show the Nepal and China borders neutrally, with no labels or callouts on the boundary lines. Draw the route without crossing or highlighting any disputed sector.
- Label the region "Tibet" only as a place name for the journey (the 1868 report's own usage). Give it no political label.
- The surveyed route runs Kathmandu → Tadum → Shigatse → Gyantse → Lhasa (report pp. 131–136), and the 1,200-mile survey also covers the great road west towards Manasarovar. Keep the drawn line generic. Don't invent an exact track.
- Use a drawn portrait or a silhouette only. Don't use a photo of Nain Singh unless it is in the public domain and checked. No CC BY-SA images.
