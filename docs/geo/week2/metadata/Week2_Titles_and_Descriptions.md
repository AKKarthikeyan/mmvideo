TITLE:
This Indian Island Is 3x Closer to the Maldives 🏝️

DESCRIPTION:
Minicoy is the southernmost island of Lakshadweep, India's coral-atoll territory in the Arabian Sea. Kochi in Kerala is about 398 km away, while the Maldives begin roughly 130 km to the south.

The Nine Degree Channel separates it from the rest of Lakshadweep, and its people speak Mahl, the language of the Maldives. A 1976 India–Maldives sea boundary agreement keeps it on India's side.

## 02 · Day 1 · Evening · austria

FILE: 02_austria.mp4
SLOT: Day 1 · Evening

TITLE:
Landlocked Austria Once Had an Outpost in India 🏝️

DESCRIPTION:
In 1778 a ship sailing under the Habsburg flag reached the Nicobar Islands, which Denmark had claimed in 1756. Trader William Bolts planned to ship Indian and Chinese goods straight to Austrian ports.

The outpost lasted about three years, and Bolts's company failed in 1785. Today the Nicobars are part of India's Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

Sources: Imperial Gazetteer of India (1908); official account of the Novara expedition (1861); Haensel's letters (1812); Constitution of India, Art. 240.

## 03 · Day 2 · Morning · kuttanad

FILE: 03_kuttanad.mp4
SLOT: Day 2 · Morning

TITLE:
India Grows Rice BELOW Sea Level 🌾

DESCRIPTION:
In Kuttanad, Kerala, farmers grow rice below sea level. Many of its fields were reclaimed from Vembanad Lake from the 1800s, by building mud walls and pumping the water out, much like the polders of the Netherlands.

The UN's Food and Agriculture Organization lists the Kuttanad below-sea-level farming system as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System.

Sources: FAO GIAHS; Review of Agrarian Studies (2023); Kerala government.

## 05 · Day 3 · Morning · seazone

FILE: 05_seazone.mp4
SLOT: Day 3 · Morning

TITLE:
Why India's Sea Zone Is Over 2 Million km² 🌊

DESCRIPTION:
Under the UN Law of the Sea, a country's Exclusive Economic Zone reaches up to 200 nautical miles (about 370 km) from its coast. Inside it, India has rights to fish, oil and minerals, while ships of every country can still pass.

Islands get zones too. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep add a large share of India's sea zone, from a tiny share of its land.

Sources: UNCLOS (UN); PIB; Reserve Bank of India; UT Administration of Lakshadweep. The zone on screen is approximate.

FILE: 06_nainsingh.mp4
SLOT: Day 3 · Evening

TITLE:
His Prayer Beads Were a Secret Measuring Tape 📿

DESCRIPTION:
In 1866 the surveyor Nain Singh reached Lhasa in disguise, counting his steps on a rosary of 100 beads instead of the usual 108. Each bead marked 100 paces, and every tenth, larger bead marked 1,000.

His route survey of over 1,200 miles fixed Lhasa's position on the map for the Great Trigonometrical Survey.

Sources: Montgomerie, Journal of the Royal Geographical Society (1868); Royal Geographical Society gold medal list.

## 07 · Day 4 · Morning · thumba

FILE: 07_thumba.mp4
SLOT: Day 4 · Morning

TITLE:
India's First Rockets Were Assembled in a Church ⛪

DESCRIPTION:
Thumba, near Thiruvananthapuram in Kerala, lies close to the magnetic equator, where scientists wanted to study an electric current high in the sky. Balloons can't reach it and satellites fly too high, so rockets were the answer.

The parish gave St Mary Magdalene Church to science, and a new church was built for it. India's first sounding rocket lifted off from Thumba on 21 November 1963. The old church is now the space museum.

Sources: VSSC / ISRO; President of India (Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, 2005); Current Science (2020).

Why This African Town's Doors Look Indian 🚪

DESCRIPTION:

## 09 · Day 5 · Morning · barmer

FILE: 09_barmer.mp4
SLOT: Day 5 · Morning

TITLE:
Why India Found Oil Under a Desert 🛢️

DESCRIPTION:
In 2004, Barmer in Rajasthan's Thar Desert gave India its biggest onshore oil find in decades. About 60 million years ago a lake filled a sinking rift valley here, and algae and bacteria piled up in its mud.

Buried kilometres deep, that mud cooked into a waxy oil, the fingerprint of an old lake. Geologists have mapped over seven billion barrels in place (not all of it can be recovered).

Sources: Cairn India (2009); Central Ground Water Board; Dolson et al., AAPG Bulletin (2015); Farrimond et al., Petroleum Geoscience (2015).

7.5 kg of Indian Pepper, Buried in Roman Egypt

DESCRIPTION:
At Berenike, a Red Sea port of Roman Egypt, archaeologists found a jar holding over 7.5 kg of Indian black pepper. Monsoon winds carried ships here from India's Malabar coast, and caravans took the goods across the desert to the Nile and on to Rome.

Indians lived here too: a jar shard carries a name in Tamil-Brahmi letters.

Sources: Cappers, Roman Foodprints at Berenike (UCLA Cotsen Institute, 2006); Sidebotham et al., German Archaeological Institute (2025); University of Delaware.

## 11 · Day 6 · Morning · panipat

FILE: 11_panipat.mp4
SLOT: Day 6 · Morning

TITLE:
Why America's Old Clothes End Up in India 🧶

DESCRIPTION:
In 2024 India imported about 160,000 tonnes of used clothing, 58% of it from the USA. Many end up in Panipat, about 90 km north of Delhi, a town of weavers now called the "cast-off capital".

Customs makes sure the clothes are cut up first. They are sorted by colour, shredded into fibre and spun into new yarn, an old English method called shoddy.

Sources: UN Comtrade (HS 6309, 2024); Panipat district (Govt of Haryana); CESTAT order (2025); PIB; de Bercegol, Métropolitiques (2025).

## 12 · Day 6 · Evening · bhitarkanika

FILE: 12_bhitarkanika.mp4
SLOT: Day 6 · Evening

TITLE:
10 Crocodiles in 1973. Over 1,800 Now 🐊

DESCRIPTION:
In 1973 a survey of Bhitarkanika's creeks in Odisha saw only ten saltwater crocodiles, the biggest crocodile species alive. Poaching for skins had nearly wiped them out.

A crocodile project began in 1975, and over 2,300 young crocodiles were raised and released. Protection gave them the years they need to grow, and today over 1,800 live here.

Sources: Odisha Government; Journal of the Bombay Natural History Society (1973); IUCN Crocodile Specialist Group; Ramsar Sites Information Service.

TITLE:
Burma's Last King Died in a Small Indian Town

DESCRIPTION:
In November 1885 a British fleet reached Mandalay and King Thibaw of Burma (today's Myanmar) surrendered. The Viceroy's own telegram said his departure "removes all opposition".

He was sent to Madras and then to Ratnagiri in Maharashtra, about 2,400 km from Mandalay, where he lived under guard until his death in 1916.

Sources: Parliamentary papers on Burma (1886); Imperial Gazetteer of India; Bombay Presidency administration report (1907–08); Maharashtra Tourism.

FILE: 14_narcondam.mp4

TITLE:
This Bird Lives on One Tiny Volcano. Nowhere Else 🌋

DESCRIPTION:
The Narcondam hornbill lives only on Narcondam, a 6.8 km² old volcanic island alone in the Andaman Sea, over 100 km from North Andaman. Cut off by open sea, its hornbills became a species of their own.

No other Asian hornbill has a smaller range. About a thousand live there, and BirdLife lists the species as Vulnerable.

Sources: BirdLife International; UNESCO nomination text (2014); Naniwadekar et al. (IUCN Hornbill Specialist Group, 2020).
