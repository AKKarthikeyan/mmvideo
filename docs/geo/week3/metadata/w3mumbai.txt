TITLE:
Mumbai's Leopard Park Began With Two Drinking-Water Lakes 🐆

DESCRIPTION:
Camera traps photographed at least 54 adult leopards in Sanjay Gandhi National Park in 2024. A government draft notification calls the park a habitat "enclosed in the metropolis of Mumbai"; it spans the Mumbai and Thane districts of Maharashtra, in western India, and covers about 104 km² as administered (103.84 km²; 86.96 km² is the notified figure).

The park's own history starts with water: the British built Vihar and Tulsi lakes to supply Mumbai, and the Bombay Municipal Corporation acquired the lakes' catchments to protect them. In 1981 the park was renamed Sanjay Gandhi National Park. The outline in the video is a schematic drawn from 12 boundary points in the draft notification.

Sources: MoEFCC draft notification S.O. 229(E), 22 Jan 2016 (Eco-sensitive Zone, SGNP); Sanjay Gandhi National Park, Maharashtra Forest Department (History of SGNP); Wildlife Conservation Society-India with the Forest Department, "Living with Leopards" camera-trap report.
