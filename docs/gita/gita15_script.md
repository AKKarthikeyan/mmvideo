Draft 1, 21 Oct 2026. Narration: MiniMax English_Diligent_Man (Indian male). Krishna appears only as symbols.
Source text: sanskritdocuments.org (701-verse edition; 13.2 here = 13.1 in Gita Press). Translations are ours.
Lines marked **OUR NOTE** are our interpretation and appear in the roman 'OUR NOTE' style on screen; everything else is the guide's narration or a quote.
Each verse card: the reference is spoken, a bell, then a short silence while the Devanagari writes itself, then the IAST, then the English is read.

## I · Action

### Chapter 1 · अर्जुनविषादयोगः · arjunaviṣādayogaḥ · The yoga of Arjuna's despair

*On screen:* Chapter title card. Arjuna's view: teachers, grandfather, cousins drawn as banners and silhouettes on the other side. Bead 1 lights.

> Chapter one. The yoga of Arjuna's despair.

> Arjuna looks across the field and sees teachers, cousins, his own grandfather. And he breaks.

> **OUR NOTE** Even this collapse is called a yoga, because it is where the journey begins.

> Verse forty-seven.
>
> **Verse card 1.47** (Sanjaya)
>
> एवमुक्त्वार्जुनः सङ्ख्ये रथोपस्थ उपाविशत् ।  
> विसृज्य सशरं चापं शोकसंविग्नमानसः ॥ १-४७॥  
>
> *evam uktvārjunaḥ saṅkhye rathopastha upāviśat |*  
> *visṛjya sa-śaraṃ cāpaṃ śoka-saṃvigna-mānasaḥ ||*  
>
> “Having spoken thus on the battlefield, Arjuna sank down on the seat of the chariot, casting aside his bow and arrows, his mind overwhelmed by grief.” *(read aloud)*

*Citation chips:* 1.26

### Chapter 2 · साङ्ख्ययोगः · sāṅkhyayogaḥ · The yoga of understanding

*On screen:* Arjuna's words 2.7 as a quote; then a figure discarding a worn shawl for a new one (2.22); then an arrow leaving a bowstring with the target out of frame (2.47).

> Chapter two. The yoga of understanding.

> It begins with a surrender. Arjuna says: I am your disciple. Teach me.

> Krishna's first lesson is about who Arjuna really is. The self is never born and never dies. As we throw off worn-out clothes and put on new ones, the self moves from body to body.

> Then comes the most famous line in the Gita. Verse forty-seven.
>
> **Verse card 2.47** (Krishna)
>
> कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।  
> मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ २-४७॥  
>
> *karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana |*  
> *mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo’stv akarmaṇi ||*  
>
> “Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit of action be your motive, and do not cling to inaction.” *(read aloud)*

> **OUR NOTE** It does not say: don't care about results. It says: don't let results run you.

> And the chapter ends with a portrait of a steady mind: not shaken by sorrow, not hungry for pleasure, free of craving, fear and anger.

*Citation chips:* 2.7, 2.20, 2.22, 2.56, 2.55-72

### Chapter 3 · कर्मयोगः · karmayogaḥ · The yoga of action

*On screen:* A small sacred fire (yajna) for action as offering; then two lanes: a polished lane of other people's highlight cards and a rough lane lit from within.

> Chapter three. The yoga of action.

> Arjuna asks: if knowing is better than doing, why push me into this terrible act? Krishna answers: no one can stay without acting, even for a moment. So act, but act as an offering.

> And remember that people are watching. Whatever the best person does, Krishna says, others copy. Whatever standard they set, the world follows.

> And here is a verse for everyone who scrolls through other people's lives. Verse thirty-five.
>
> **Verse card 3.35** (Krishna)
>
> श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात् ।  
> स्वधर्मे निधनं श्रेयः परधर्मो भयावहः ॥ ३-३५॥  
>
> *śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt |*  
> *sva-dharme nidhanaṃ śreyaḥ para-dharmo bhayāvahaḥ ||*  
>
> “Better your own dharma, even done imperfectly, than another's dharma done well. Better to die in your own dharma; another's dharma is full of fear.” *(read aloud)*

*Citation chips:* 3.1, 3.5, 3.9, 3.21

### Chapter 4 · ज्ञानकर्मसंन्यासयोगः · jñānakarmasaṃnyāsayogaḥ · The yoga of knowledge and renouncing action

*On screen:* A flame passed down a chain: the sun (Vivasvan) to Manu to Ikshvaku (4.1); then the flame relit across ages; fire turning wood to ash (4.37).

> Chapter four. The yoga of knowledge.

> Krishna reveals who he is. He taught this yoga long ago to Vivasvan, the sun, who taught it to Manu, who taught it to Ikshvaku. And he makes a promise. Verses seven and eight.
>
> **Verse card 4.7** (Krishna)
>
> यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत ।  
> अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् ॥ ४-७॥  
>
> *yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata |*  
> *abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṃ sṛjāmy aham ||*  
>
> “Whenever dharma declines, O Bharata, and adharma rises, then I bring myself forth.” *(read aloud)*
>
> **Verse card 4.8** (Krishna)
>
> परित्राणाय साधूनां विनाशाय च दुष्कृताम् ।  
> धर्मसंस्थापनार्थाय सम्भवामि युगे युगे ॥ ४-८॥  
>
> *paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām |*  
> *dharma-saṃsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge ||*  
>
> “To protect the good, to destroy wrongdoing, and to establish dharma, I come into being, age after age.” *(read aloud)*

> How do you find this knowledge? Bow down, ask questions, and serve, Krishna says, and those who have seen the truth will teach you. And as a blazing fire turns wood to ash, the fire of knowledge burns all karma to ash.

*Citation chips:* 4.1, 4.34, 4.37

### Chapter 5 · संन्यासयोगः · saṃnyāsayogaḥ · The yoga of renunciation

*On screen:* A lotus leaf on dark water; a drop rolls across it and off, leaving the leaf dry.

> Chapter five. The yoga of renunciation.

> Arjuna asks again: which is better, giving up action, or acting well? Both lead to freedom, Krishna says, but acting without attachment is better. Picture a lotus leaf. Verse ten.
>
> **Verse card 5.10** (Krishna)
>
> ब्रह्मण्याधाय कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा करोति यः ।  
> लिप्यते न स पापेन पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा ॥ ५-१०॥  
>
> *brahmaṇy ādhāya karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā karoti yaḥ |*  
> *lipyate na sa pāpena padma-patram ivāmbhasā ||*  
>
> “One who acts, offering every action to Brahman and giving up attachment, is not stained by sin, as a lotus leaf is not wetted by water.” *(read aloud)*

> And the wise, he says, see the same self in a learned scholar, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and the person the world pushes away.

*Citation chips:* 5.1, 5.2, 5.18

### Chapter 6 · आत्मसंयमयोगः · ātmasaṃyamayogaḥ · The yoga of self-mastery

*On screen:* A diya in a windless room; wind lines arrive and the flame flickers, then steadies and stands still (6.19).

> Chapter six. The yoga of self-mastery.

> It opens with a line made for two in the morning. Verse five.
>
> **Verse card 6.5** (Krishna)
>
> उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् ।  
> आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः ॥ ६-५॥  
>
> *uddhared ātmanātmānaṃ nātmānam avasādayet |*  
> *ātmaiva hy ātmano bandhur ātmaiva ripur ātmanaḥ ||*  
>
> “Lift yourself up by yourself; do not let yourself sink. For the self alone is the friend of the self, and the self alone is its enemy.” *(read aloud)*

> Meditation here is not escape. Yoga is not for one who eats too much or too little, Krishna says, or who sleeps too much or too little. It is balance.

> Arjuna is honest. The mind is restless, he says, as hard to hold as the wind. Krishna does not deny it. By practice, and by letting go, it can be held. And a mind that is held is like a lamp in a windless place. It does not flicker.

*Citation chips:* 6.16, 6.17, 6.34, 6.35, 6.19

## II · Devotion

### Chapter 7 · ज्ञानविज्ञानयोगः · jñānavijñānayogaḥ · The yoga of knowledge and realisation

*On screen:* Movement card 'II · DEVOTION'. Eight elements as icons (earth, water, fire, air, space, mind, intellect, ego) strung like gems on one thread (7.7).

> The second movement turns to Krishna himself.

> Chapter seven. The yoga of knowledge and realisation.

> Earth, water, fire, air, space, mind, intellect and ego: this, Krishna says, is my lower nature. Life itself is my higher nature. Verse seven.
>
> **Verse card 7.7** (Krishna)
>
> मत्तः परतरं नान्यत्किञ्चिदस्ति धनञ्जय ।  
> मयि सर्वमिदं प्रोतं सूत्रे मणिगणा इव ॥ ७-७॥  
>
> *mattaḥ parataraṃ nānyat kiñcid asti dhanañjaya |*  
> *mayi sarvam idaṃ protaṃ sūtre maṇi-gaṇā iva ||*  
>
> “There is nothing higher than me, Dhananjaya. All this is strung on me, like gems on a thread.” *(read aloud)*

> I am the taste in water, he says, the light of the sun and the moon, the sacred syllable Om in all the Vedas.

*Citation chips:* 7.4, 7.5, 7.8

### Chapter 8 · अक्षरब्रह्मयोगः · akṣarabrahmayogaḥ · The yoga of the imperishable Brahman

*On screen:* A heartbeat line slowly becomes a flute melody line; a bow and a peacock feather side by side.

> Chapter eight. The yoga of the imperishable.

> Whatever a person remembers at the very end, Krishna says, that is what they reach. So he gives Arjuna the simplest practice in the Gita. Verse seven.
>
> **Verse card 8.7** (Krishna)
>
> तस्मात्सर्वेषु कालेषु मामनुस्मर युध्य च ।  
> मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्मामेवैष्यस्यसंशयः ॥ ८-७॥  
>
> *tasmāt sarveṣu kāleṣu mām anusmara yudhya ca |*  
> *mayy arpita-mano-buddhir mām evaiṣyasy asaṃśayaḥ ||*  
>
> “Therefore, at all times, remember me, and fight. With your mind and intellect offered to me, you will surely come to me.” *(read aloud)*

> **OUR NOTE** Not: leave the world, then remember. Remember, and do the work in front of you.

*Citation chips:* 8.5, 8.6

### Chapter 9 · राजविद्याराजगुह्ययोगः · rājavidyārājaguhyayogaḥ · The yoga of the royal knowledge and royal secret

*On screen:* A banana leaf; a tulsi leaf, a flower, a fruit and a small kalash of water are placed on it one by one. Chip: 'योगक्षेमं वहाम्यहम् · 9.22 · the motto on LIC's logo'.

> Chapter nine. The yoga of the royal secret.

> Those who worship me with nothing else in their minds, Krishna says, I carry what they need and protect what they have. That line is the motto on the logo of LIC.

> And the royal secret turns out to be gentle. Verse twenty-six.
>
> **Verse card 9.26** (Krishna)
>
> पत्रं पुष्पं फलं तोयं यो मे भक्त्या प्रयच्छति ।  
> तदहं भक्त्युपहृतमश्नामि प्रयतात्मनः ॥ ९-२६॥  
>
> *patraṃ puṣpaṃ phalaṃ toyaṃ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati |*  
> *tad ahaṃ bhakty-upahṛtam aśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ ||*  
>
> “Whoever offers me with devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, that offering, made with devotion by one whose heart is pure, I accept and partake of.” *(read aloud)*

> Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you give, he says, do it as an offering to me.

*Citation chips:* 9.22, 9.27

### Chapter 10 · विभूतियोगः · vibhūtiyogaḥ · The yoga of divine glories

*On screen:* Icons light up one by one like stars: the Himalaya, a peepal tree, the Ganga, spring blossom, and a calendar leaf for Margashirsha; then sparks fly from all of them.

> Chapter ten. The yoga of divine glories.

> Arjuna asks: where should I look for you? Among mountains, Krishna says, I am the Himalaya. Among trees, the sacred fig. Among rivers, the Ganga. Among months, Margashirsha, the month of Gita Jayanti. Among seasons, spring. Verse forty-one.
>
> **Verse card 10.41** (Krishna)
>
> यद्यद्विभूतिमत्सत्त्वं श्रीमदूर्जितमेव वा ।  
> तत्तदेवावगच्छ त्वं मम तेजोंऽशसम्भवम् ॥ १०-४१॥  
>
> *yad yad vibhūtimat sattvaṃ śrīmad ūrjitam eva vā |*  
> *tat tad evāvagaccha tvaṃ mama tejo’ṃśa-sambhavam ||*  
>
> “Whatever being is glorious, beautiful or mighty, know that it springs from a spark of my splendour.” *(read aloud)*

*Citation chips:* 10.17, 10.25, 10.26, 10.31, 10.35

### Chapter 11 · विश्वरूपदर्शनयोगः · viśvarūpadarśanayogaḥ · The yoga of the vision of the universal form

*On screen:* The summit. A tiny Arjuna silhouette before an expanding sacred-geometry mandala of light; rivers pouring into a sea (11.28); then everything folds back into the feather and flute (11.50).

> Chapter eleven. The vision of the universal form.

> This is the summit.

> Arjuna asks to see Krishna's cosmic form. You cannot see me with these eyes, Krishna says, so I give you divine sight. And Sanjaya, who is telling the story, tries to describe what Arjuna saw. Verse twelve.
>
> **Verse card 11.12** (Sanjaya)
>
> दिवि सूर्यसहस्रस्य भवेद्युगपदुत्थिता ।  
> यदि भाः सदृशी सा स्याद्भासस्तस्य महात्मनः ॥ ११-१२॥  
>
> *divi sūrya-sahasrasya bhaved yugapad utthitā |*  
> *yadi bhāḥ sadṛśī sā syād bhāsas tasya mahātmanaḥ ||*  
>
> “If the light of a thousand suns were to blaze forth in the sky at once, it might resemble the splendour of that great being.” *(read aloud)*

> Arjuna sees every world inside one form, with no beginning, no middle and no end. Warriors rush into it the way rivers rush into the sea. Then Krishna speaks a verse that the physicist J. Robert Oppenheimer said came to his mind at the first atomic test. Verse thirty-two.
>
> **Verse card 11.32** (Krishna)
>
> कालोऽस्मि लोकक्षयकृत्प्रवृद्धो  
> लोकान्समाहर्तुमिह प्रवृत्तः ।  
> ऋतेऽपि त्वां न भविष्यन्ति सर्वे  
> येऽवस्थिताः प्रत्यनीकेषु योधाः ॥ ११-३२॥  
>
> *kālo’smi loka-kṣaya-kṛt pravṛddho*  
> *lokān samāhartum iha pravṛttaḥ |*  
> *ṛte’pi tvāṃ na bhaviṣyanti sarve*  
> *ye’vasthitāḥ pratyanīkeṣu yodhāḥ ||*  
>
> “I am Time, grown mighty, the destroyer of worlds, come forth to consume the worlds. Even without you, none of the warriors arrayed in the opposing armies will remain.” *(read aloud)*

> Terrified, Arjuna begs to see the familiar form again. And Vasudeva, gently, becomes himself once more.

*Citation chips:* 11.8, 11.16, 11.28, 11.50

### Chapter 12 · भक्तियोगः · bhaktiyogaḥ · The yoga of devotion

*On screen:* Kinetic type: the qualities of the devotee appear one by one, each on a lit bead.

> Chapter twelve. The yoga of devotion.

> One of the two shortest chapters, just twenty verses.

> Who is the better devotee, Arjuna asks: the one who loves a form, or the one who seeks the formless? For people in bodies, Krishna says, the path of love is easier. Then he describes who is dear to him. Verse thirteen.
>
> **Verse card 12.13** (Krishna)
>
> अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च ।  
> निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी ॥ १२-१३॥  
>
> *adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṃ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca |*  
> *nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī ||*  
>
> “One who hates no being, who is friendly and compassionate, free from 'mine' and from ego, the same in pain and pleasure, forgiving ...” *(read aloud)*
> <small>the sentence completes in 12.14: '... that devotee is dear to me'</small>

*Citation chips:* 12.1, 12.5, 12.14

## III · Knowledge

### Chapter 13 · क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञविभागयोगः · kṣetrakṣetrajñavibhāgayogaḥ · The yoga of the field and its knower

*On screen:* Movement card 'III · KNOWLEDGE'. A field (kshetra) drawn as farmland seen from above; a small light above it that sees the whole field.

> The third movement asks: what is real?

> Chapter thirteen. The field and its knower.

> Verse two.
>
> **Verse card 13.2** (Krishna)
>
> इदं शरीरं कौन्तेय क्षेत्रमित्यभिधीयते ।  
> एतद्यो वेत्ति तं प्राहुः क्षेत्रज्ञ इति तद्विदः ॥ १३-२॥  
>
> *idaṃ śarīraṃ kaunteya kṣetram ity abhidhīyate |*  
> *etad yo vetti taṃ prāhuḥ kṣetrajña iti tad-vidaḥ ||*  
>
> “This body, Kaunteya, is called the field. Whoever knows it is called the knower of the field by those who know.” *(read aloud)*
> <small>13.1 in Gita Press numbering</small>

> **OUR NOTE** And Krishna says: know me as the knower in every field. You are not the field. You are the one who sees it.

*Citation chips:* 13.3

### Chapter 14 · गुणत्रयविभागयोगः · guṇatrayavibhāgayogaḥ · The yoga of the three gunas

*On screen:* A rope made of three strands twisting together: luminous white (sattva), red (rajas), dark (tamas), the traditional colours; then a figure stepping out of the knot (14.20).

> Chapter fourteen. The three gunas.

> Verse five.
>
> **Verse card 14.5** (Krishna)
>
> सत्त्वं रजस्तम इति गुणाः प्रकृतिसम्भवाः ।  
> निबध्नन्ति महाबाहो देहे देहिनमव्ययम् ॥ १४-५॥  
>
> *sattvaṃ rajas tama iti guṇāḥ prakṛti-sambhavāḥ |*  
> *nibadhnanti mahābāho dehe dehinam avyayam ||*  
>
> “Sattva, rajas and tamas, the gunas born of nature, bind the imperishable embodied self to the body, mighty-armed one.” *(read aloud)*

> Guna also means a strand of rope. Sattva binds with attachment to happiness and knowledge. Rajas with craving and restless action. Tamas with carelessness, laziness and sleep. Whoever rises above all three, Krishna says, is free.

### Chapter 18 · मोक्षसंन्यासयोगः · mokṣasaṃnyāsayogaḥ · The yoga of liberation through renunciation

*On screen:* All 18 beads lit. An open path splitting two ways; Krishna's reins held loosely, not pulled (18.63); then light gathers around the chariot (18.66).

> Chapter eighteen. The yoga of liberation.

> The longest chapter, seventy-eight verses, and it gathers everything.

> Renunciation, Krishna says, is not giving up action. It is giving up attachment to its fruit. He repeats his advice from chapter three: better your own path, done imperfectly. And he says the Lord lives in the heart of every being.

> And then, after seventeen chapters of teaching, he does something remarkable. He does not give Arjuna an order. Verse sixty-three.
>
> **Verse card 18.63** (Krishna)
>
> इति ते ज्ञानमाख्यातं गुह्याद्गुह्यतरं मया ।  
> विमृश्यैतदशेषेण यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु ॥ १८-६३॥  
>
> *iti te jñānam ākhyātaṃ guhyād guhyataraṃ mayā |*  
> *vimṛśyaitad aśeṣeṇa yathecchasi tathā kuru ||*  
>
> “Thus I have told you wisdom more secret than all secrets. Reflect on it fully, then do as you wish.” *(read aloud)*

> And then his last and most intimate word. Verse sixty-six.
>
> **Verse card 18.66** (Krishna)
>
> सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज ।  
> अहं त्वा सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ॥ १८-६६॥  
>
> *sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja |*  
> *ahaṃ tvā sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ ||*  
>
> “Giving up all dharmas, take refuge in me alone. I will free you from all sins. Do not grieve.” *(read aloud)*
> <small>schools read 'all dharmas' differently; every reading keeps the last words, mā śucaḥ: do not grieve</small>

*Citation chips:* 18.2, 18.11, 18.47, 18.61
