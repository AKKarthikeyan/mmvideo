## Episode 1: "TSMC keeps 51 cents of every dollar. How?" (about 7 min)

**Title options:**
- TSMC keeps 51¢ of every dollar it sells. Here's where the rest goes
- How a factory business keeps half of every dollar

**Thumbnail:** `GLOBAL-thumb` defaults: TSMC · 51¢ · of every dollar kept · Q3 2026 breakdown.

### 0:00 Cold open
**Scene:** `GLOBAL-split`: the 100 coins fill in, with green first.

**VO:** Between July and September, TSMC sold forty-six point seven billion dollars of chips. After paying for the factories, the engineers, the taxes, everything, it kept twenty-three point nine billion. That's fifty-one cents of every dollar. TSMC isn't a software company or a brand charging for a logo. It runs factories that cost well over ten billion dollars each. So how does a manufacturer keep half of everything it sells? And why, in a record quarter, did that number slip?

### 0:40 Where the revenue comes from
**Scene:** `GLOBAL-sankey`, step 1 (segments column).

**VO:** Start on the left. TSMC splits its sales into six kinds of customer. The big one is high-performance computing: the processors and AI accelerators in data centres. That's sixty-eight percent of revenue `[CHECK]`, up from fifty-seven percent a year ago `[CHECK]`. Smartphones, once the core of the business, are down to about a fifth.

**VO:** Add them up and you get forty-six point seven billion dollars. In Taiwan dollars, the currency TSMC actually reports in, that's one point four nine trillion, up fifty point nine percent on a year ago.

### 1:40 Why the newest chips matter
**Scene:** `GLOBAL-mix`: 2nm 8%, 3nm 33%, 5nm 28%, 7nm 10%, 16nm and older 21% of wafer revenue, with the bracket "7nm and newer: 79%".

**VO:** The second reason for the margin is which chips TSMC makes. Seventy-nine percent of wafer revenue came from seven-nanometre technology or smaller. Three nanometres alone was a third, and two nanometres, the newest process, is already eight percent. At that level, very few factories in the world can do the job at all. When customers have few alternatives, the price reflects it.

### 2:40 Following the dollar
**Scene:** `GLOBAL-waterfall`, one bar per line, then back to `GLOBAL-split` for the totals in cents.

**VO:** Now follow the dollar through the business. Making the wafers (materials, power, and above all the cost of the machines wearing out) takes thirty-three cents. That leaves about sixty-six cents of gross profit.

**VO:** Research and development, sales and administration take eight cents more. That's the part most manufacturers can't keep small, and TSMC spreads it over enormous volume. Operating profit: about fifty-nine cents.

**VO:** Interest and other income add about two cents. Tax, and the slice that belongs to minority shareholders in TSMC's subsidiaries, take about ten. Net of each other, that's eight cents. What's left is fifty-one cents of net profit for every dollar of sales.

### 4:10 Why the margin slipped
**Scene:** the gross and operating margin, last 5 quarters, as a line. `[CHECK]` the quarterly series in the management report.

**VO:** Here's the catch. Revenue grew seventeen point six percent on the previous quarter, but net profit grew only eight. Gross margin fell one point three points, to sixty-six point four percent. Operating margin fell one point six points, to fifty-eight point seven.

**VO:** `[CHECK: fill in the reasons TSMC gave on the earnings call. Common candidates are overseas fabs costing more to run, the two-nanometre ramp, and the exchange rate. Use only what management actually said, and attribute it.]`

### 5:20 What TSMC says comes next
**Scene:** `GLOBAL-guide`: the Q4 guidance bands against the Q3 actual dots, at NT$31.8 per US$. `[CHECK]` the Q4 revenue guidance.

**VO:** For the final quarter of the year, TSMC is guiding to a gross margin between sixty-five and sixty-seven percent, and an operating margin between fifty-six and a half and fifty-eight and a half. That assumes thirty-one point eight Taiwan dollars to the US dollar. In other words, it expects margins to stay roughly where they are, not to keep sliding.

### 6:00 What to watch
**Scene:** three cards: "2nm share of wafer revenue", "Overseas fab margins", "HPC share of revenue".

**VO:** Three numbers will tell you whether fifty-one cents holds. How fast two nanometres grows as a share of revenue. Whether the overseas factories start pulling margins down harder. And whether data-centre demand keeps pushing high-performance computing above two-thirds of sales. All three will be in the next report, and so will we.
