## 1. The giants, one by one

| Channel | Size | What they make | How they make it | The one thing people remember |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mark Rober** | ~57M (2026 list) | Engineering builds framed as stories (glitter bomb, squirrel maze, world's-largest builds) | Thriller pacing: "layering curiosity, setbacks, and reveals" (Opus analysis). Repeatable formats: "the format stayed familiar, but the story evolved". Outsources the building so he can focus on story and edit; cuts ~200 h of footage to 10 min. "We evolved to tell stories." "I can't teach you if I don't have your attention." | A big build plus a payoff, with a friendly face |
| **Kurzgesagt** | 25M+ (June 2026) | Big questions (space, life, existential), 8–15 min | ≥1,200 h per video. ~200 illustration panels; 2–3 illustrators for 8–12 weeks; 2–3 animators for 8–10 weeks; original music and sound design; 2–4 videos in parallel. Scripts checked by specialists at **two stages**; sources in every description (since 2019) | A recognisable visual universe (the birds, the flat neon style) and trust |
| **Veritasium** | 14M+ | 15–30 min investigations ("the thing everyone gets wrong about…") | Host on camera, experiments, expert interviews. Treats titles and thumbnails as part of the craft: "legit-bait" (type I, honest) vs "clicktraps" (type II). A "double barrel" title and thumbnail that create tension rather than repeat each other | A trusted face plus a surprising question |
| **NileRed** | 10.8M (19.4M across channels) | Chemistry transformations ("turning X into Y") | Long lab sessions filmed end to end; the slow process *is* the suspense | One repeatable promise: watch X become Y |

## 2. Patterns across all of them
1. **One promise you can say in a sentence**, repeated every episode. Turning X into Y (NileRed); build-and-payoff (Rober); what's inside (Action Lab); big questions in a bird universe (Kurzgesagt).
2. **A face or a world.** Every giant has a recognisable human (Rober, Muller) or a recognisable universe (Kurzgesagt). Viewers subscribe to *someone* or *somewhere*.
3. **The question is built into the format,** so retention is automatic: what's inside? will it work? what's the catch?
4. **Repeatable series formats** with a familiar shape and a new story each time.
5. **Title and first frame are part of the craft**: honest "legit-bait" that creates tension, never a trap.
6. **Trust machinery:** expert checks (Kurzgesagt, twice), sources in the description, open corrections.
7. **Long-form is the engine for most of them;** Shorts are clipped from it (Action Lab, Veritasium). The Shorts-native giant (Zack D.) wins on 3D spectacle and volume.
8. **Consistency beats perfection** (the Action Lab lesson). Only about 1% of videos go big.

## 3. What we already have that none of them combine
- **Story + mechanism + history in one Short.** Rober has the story, Branch has the mechanism, Kurzgesagt has the big picture. Nobody does all three for everyday objects in under two minutes. (Gap G1 from GAP_ANALYSIS.)
- **The claim-ladder stamp** (CLAIMED / PUBLISHED / REPEATED / ON SALE) on news episodes. I found no science channel that stamps how proven a claim is.
- **A source line under every fact** and a full fact table per episode, more visible than Kurzgesagt's description links.
- **Story craft from two masters:** Bhagyaraj's heart (ordinary heroes, a plant and its payoff, warmth) plus Nolan's skeleton (pledge/turn/prestige, the hairpin loop), now with AK's followable pacing.
- **A consistent hand-drawn look generated in code**: cheap per episode, unlike Kurzgesagt's 1,200 hours.
- **Credible global inventors,** including Indian and other non-Western ones where the history really happened (Bose, Kapany, Ajay Bhatt).
- **A ready tool for Tamil and Hindi audio tracks** (built 2 Oct).

## 4. What to add to make it unique (ranked by impact per effort)

**1. Give the channel a world: the Desk itself (high impact, low effort).** Every giant has a face or a world, and we have neither. Without a face on camera:
- Open every episode on the same desk, drawn in our style: lamp, blotter, a drawer.
- The drawer opens and the object rises out: a radio, a fridge, a chocolate bar. That one second becomes the brand in the feed.
- End on the desk too: the object goes back into the drawer. This also gives every Short a natural loop.
- Optional mascot: "the Inventor", a hand or a silhouette that writes the episode number on the blotter.

**2. The patent on the desk (high impact, medium effort).** Old patent drawings (US/GB before 1931) are public domain. Slide the real drawing onto the desk, stamp its number, then redraw it as our animated diagram. We already did this in Fridge (Perkins, Einstein) and Radio (Marconi). Make it a fixed beat in every Evergreen episode. No big science channel uses real patent pages as a signature.

**3. Two named, repeatable series (high impact, low effort).**
- **"Happy Accidents":** inventions born from a mistake (microwave/chocolate, Post-it, Velcro, penicillin, X-rays). It has a built-in question: what went wrong?
- **"Claim Check":** the news episodes with the stamp as the verdict (lab gold = CLAIMED, nuclear clock = PUBLISHED). Nobody does this. It builds trust and rides the news.

Put the series name in the title and on the first frame so viewers learn the shape.

**4. One "proof" moment per episode (medium impact, low effort).** The Action Lab and Rober win on visible proof. Add a try-it beat whenever it's safe, like the chocolate speed-of-light test. Invite viewers to film their result and put the best in a community post ("Content, Consistency & Community"). If AK is willing, a 2–3 s real clip of hands doing the test would add physical proof (a decision for AK).

**5. Honest double-barrel titles and first frames (medium impact, no cost).** The title and the first frame should create tension, not repeat each other. Examples:
- Title "He noticed his chocolate had melted" + first frame "1945 · RADAR LAB".
- Title "Your head is older than your feet" + first frame "TRUE. HERE'S WHY".

Always legit-bait, never a clicktrap (Muller's line).

**6. A trust page (medium impact, low effort).** Publish each episode's fact table: pinned comment → a page on theinventorsdesk.in (the domain was free on 22 Oct). That is Kurzgesagt's transparency, done better. Add a "Corrections" note when something changes (the lab-gold claim may move from CLAIMED to PUBLISHED).

**7. A long-form engine (high impact, medium effort, later).** Most giants grow through long-form. Once a month, stitch 4–5 Shorts plus new connecting beats into a 6–10 min episode ("Everything in your kitchen was an accident"). The Shorts then feed the long video and back again.

**8. Languages (medium impact, low effort).** Use the existing Tamil/Hindi audio-track tool on the long-form compilations. Check whether YouTube's multi-language audio is available for Shorts before relying on it there.

**9. Consistency (essential).** About 3 a week for 4 weeks before judging. That's the Action Lab lesson, and it's what lets YouTube learn the audience.

## 5. What not to copy
- Kurzgesagt-level production (1,200 h/video) or Rober-level builds. Our edge is speed plus a consistent drawn look.
- Type II clickbait ("9 in 10 people get this wrong").
- Copying Zack D.'s 3D spectacle. We'd be a weaker copy. Keep the hand-drawn identity.
- Changing the format after two uploads (AK: keep it).
