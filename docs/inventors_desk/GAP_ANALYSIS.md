Channel as it stands: hand-drawn Shorts, male voice, family-friendly tone. Each one tells an everyday gadget's origin story with an Indian thread and explains how it works. Episodes so far: 001 Radio (J C Bose), 002 Fridge (the 1833 ice ship to Calcutta), plus a news-style Mivi One test. Brand drafts are in `src/gadget/brand.tsx`.

**Method and limits.** The gaps below come from mapping who already covers what. Read the counts as rough sizes, and test the gaps with the first 10–15 uploads.

## 2. Gaps, ranked by fit with the format you already have

### G1. Story and mechanism together, with an Indian thread (the core gap)
History channels skip how the thing works. Mechanism channels (Branch, Lesics) skip the story. Indian channels skip both. No one at scale tells *who made it, how it works, and where India comes in* in under a minute. Episodes 001 and 002 already follow this formula, so the gap is the channel itself.

### G2. Credible Indian contributions to everyday tech
Many Indian videos claim "India invented everything". That lane is crowded and not trusted. A sourced version that admits what's disputed is close to empty. The radio script already does this ("Marconi *said* he heard…"), and Scroll notes the Bose-vs-Marconi priority story is simpler than the real history. Make that care part of the brand. Strong, well-documented hooks:
- **Fibre optics:** Narinder Singh Kapany, from Moga, did foundational work in the 1950s and named the field. Hook: the cable that carries your internet.
- **USB:** Ajay Bhatt, at Intel.
- **Pentium:** Vinod Dham.
- **Rockets:** Mysore's iron-cased rockets inspired Britain's Congreve rockets.
- **Why the sea is blue:** C V Raman's 1921 voyage, which led to the Raman effect.
- **Bose's other work:** J C Bose's plant-response experiments, the follow-up to 001.

### G3. The science behind Indian frugal inventions
The Better India and NIF cover grassroots innovators as human-interest stories. Nobody explains the physics. **Mitticool** (Mansukhbhai Prajapati's clay fridge, built after the 2001 Gujarat earthquake) runs on evaporative cooling, the same "wet your hand and blow" principle as episode 002. That makes it a natural sequel. NIF state books (Gujarat Innovates, Rajasthan Innovates) hold dozens more.

### G4. Things in Indian homes
Global object-history videos use Western objects: the zipper, the paperclip. Nobody covers the **pressure cooker** (Papin's 1679 steam digester, then Hawkins), the **mixer-grinder**, the **wet grinder** (Coimbatore), the **ceiling fan** or the **home inverter** (built for power cuts). These are high-recognition objects, unclaimed, and they work everywhere in India.

### G5. How India makes it and runs it
I found no channel explaining Indian tech systems simply:
- **UPI:** how a payment travels in two seconds.
- **FASTag:** RFID at the toll plaza.
- **Phone assembly:** how a phone is assembled in Tamil Nadu.
- **Chips:** the Tata Dholera chip fab, explained to a child.
- **Trains:** Vande Bharat and railway signalling.
- **First mobile call:** India's first call in 1995.

These take more research, but the hand-drawn kit can carry them without footage.

### G6. Ages 10–14 in an Indian context
Dr Binocs stops around age 10. SciShow Kids and Maddie Moate use UK/US references. Indian kids aged 10–14 who are curious about gadgets have no channel of their own.
⚠ Decision needed (§4): the current scripts read young ("Feels cold, right?").

### G7. Indian languages
Hindi and regional science leans heavily to facts and stunts. YouTube has said 37% of Indian viewers prefer a language other than Hindi or English. The regional-audio tool from 2 Oct (Tamil and Hindi tracks) makes this cheap. **Check first whether YouTube allows multi-language audio on Shorts.** If not, use it on long-form compilations.

## 3. Lanes to avoid (crowded, or off-brand)
- **Launch and spec news** (the Mivi One test). It's crowded, goes stale in a week, and fights Technical Guruji and others. If a launch is worth covering, turn it into an evergreen question ("Why do phones still have a headphone jack?").
- **Fact lists and stunts.** FactTechz and Crazy XYZ hold that ground with 20–37M subscribers.
- **Space news.** ISRO is heavily covered. Only do space as an invention story (how a rocket stays upright), not as news.
- **Big-concept physics and AI news.** Owned by Kurzgesagt, Veritasium and news channels.

## 4. Decision for AK: audience setting
The tone is pitched at children. Marking videos "made for kids" switches off comments, notifications and personalised ads, which usually means lower RPM and no comment feedback. Mislabelling them is a compliance risk. **Recommendation:** write for "a curious 12-year-old and their parent". Keep the tone warm, drop the toddler prompts, and the general-audience setting stays honest. This is your call; nothing has been changed.

## 5. Format idea that fits the name
**The patent on the desk.** Old patents and the original drawings in them are public. Examples: Perkins' 1834 patent, Marconi's 1896 patent. Redraw the original drawing on the desk as the hero visual, then animate it working. No science channel I found uses patent drawings as a signature look, and it ties directly to "The Inventor's Desk".
