## Verify first, per topic (leads from memory; open the documents; nothing here is evidence)
1. **USB:** the USB 1.0 specification (1996) and the USB-C specification (2014) at USB-IF; the connector standards (IEC 62680); Ajay Bhatt's own words (a recollection: label it as such); patents. The question is *why the first plug could only go in one way*, answered from the design documents, not from a retelling.
2. **Charger lump:** ferrite as a magnetic ceramic (J. L. Snoek, Philips Natuurkundig Laboratorium, 1930s–40s); the Philips and later TDK patents; the radio-interference rules that put them on cables (CISPR / FCC Part 15); a manufacturer's application note for the "how it works" beat.
3. **Jar lid button:** Google Patents search for "vacuum safety button" and "twist-off closure" (the 1950s–70s glass-jar closures); the food-packaging standards; a closure maker's own history (company claim: label it).
4. **Headphone jack:** the original switchboard plug patent (1870s, US), later Bell/Western Electric catalogues; the 3.5 mm standard (IEC 60603-11) and its first consumer use; Apple's 2016 removal as a **company claim**.
5. **Coin edges:** the Royal Mint's own history pages and records (the Great Recoinage of the 1690s, Newton at the Mint), the US Coinage Act of 1792 (edge lettering and reeding), a museum catalogue. Check the "anti-clipping" story carefully; it is the likeliest to be partly myth (the Claim Check angle).
6. **Feels like:** Siple and Passel (the 1940s Antarctic cooling measurements; *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society*); Steadman (heat index, 1979; *Journal of Applied Meteorology*); the 2001 US/Canada wind-chill revision (NWS and Environment Canada documents). The weather apps' own formulas differ: say which one we explain.
7. **Petrol pump:** US patents for automatic shut-off nozzles (1950s–60s; search "automatic shut-off nozzle" and "venturi"); fuel-dispensing standards; a nozzle maker's technical drawing. **Safety:** explain only; no "try it".

## Risks and honest flags
- **Saturation:** Jev flags USB (.75) and coins (.72) as covered by many channels, and car tyres (.77) is on the bench for that reason. Before scripting, search YouTube by hand for the same question to see how crowded it is; our edge is the **primary-source version with the patent or standard on screen**.
- **"Anti-clipping coin edges" and "USB design reasons"** are both stories that get retold loosely online: expect the research to correct a popular version. That is good material, not a problem.
- **Health, politics, danger:** none of the seven is health or political. The pump topic has a fuel hazard, so no try-it.
- **Length:** the 45–60 s items (feels-like) may need two "buts"; if it won't fit in 60 s, cut the heat-index half into its own Short.
