## The real story, in plain words
- **The inventor.** **George de Mestral** of Prangins, Vaud, Switzerland [C001]. He signed his patents "George"; newspapers and RTS wrote "Georges" [K8]. In 1984 he said he took a mechanic's diploma after his engineering degrees, and that being a mechanic was more useful to him in life than being an engineer (S07, 05:17). Before Velcro he filed a patent for a vine stake (1947) [C032].
- **The burrs, in his own words.** On Swiss TV in 1961 he said: in **1941**, on a **hunting** trip, little burdock burrs gripped "terribly hard" onto his clothes **and into his dog's ears** [C021, C023, C024]. In 1984 he told it again: coming home from hunting, tired, he stopped at the edge of a wood, took the burrs home and looked at them **under a microscope** [C022, C025]. He saw little hooks **"like chamois horns"**, "a perfection" [C044]. He thought: *if I can make hooks like these, I'll have made a fastener* [C045]. The 1961 film sets the hunt in the **Jura**, with a man, a child and two dogs [C022, C050]. Reuters' 1990 "walk in the woods outside Geneva" is contradicted by his own accounts [M17].
- **Ten years asleep.** He said the idea then **"slept" for about ten years**, until he could link it to the idea of a fastener [C038].
- **Lyon, three weeks, and a lucky mistake.** He went to the weaving school in Lyon, having no weaving training, and with an old professor made the first sample in **three weeks** [C039]. His luck: a **storeroom worker sent the wrong thread**, and he said the thread they had ordered would never have worked [C040]. (In 1984 he also credits a Professor Goldstein with pointing him to DuPont's nylon [C046].)
- **The patent.** On **22 October 1951** he applied for a Swiss patent [C002]. The US version (filed 1952, granted 1955) [C003] calls it a **"velvet type fabric"** [C005] and never mentions burrs [C012].
  - **How it was made:** nylon thread [C007] is looped over heated bars so it keeps its shape, then cut on one side so the loop becomes a hook [C008]. The heat came in the end from an infrared lamp at 2 cm (about 200 degrees), and the first hooks were cut with embroidery scissors [C047].
  - **The first design:** two pieces of the same hooked fabric, **hooks grabbing hooks** [C010].
- **The mountain hut.** To solve the cutting of the hooks he shut himself away for **15 days at the Trient mountain hut**, and said the solution he found "in the solitude of the mountains" was still in use [C041]. The cutter worked like a clipper ("une tondeuse") [C017].
- **It barely held.** His 1957 patent admits that with two hook layers (about 120 hooks per cm²) only **about 30%** of hooks caught [C014]. So he put **loops on one side**, about **1,000 per cm²** [C013, C015].
- **Industry.** After many failures, a ribbon-loom maker (heard as "M. Muller de Fric") joined him, and after **four years** they had the first machine and the first industrially made fabric [C043]. By the end of 1959 there were enough looms to weave 60,000 km of ribbon a year [C051]. His view: **an invention is 5% of the business; the other 95% is work and sweat** [C042].
- **Space and money.** By 1963 the US Air Force was testing zero-g walking on a Velcro-lined aircraft ceiling [C030]. NASA used it extensively on Apollo [C029]; he said NASA found it through a Reader's Digest article [C048]. In 1984 he said the patents ran out in 1971, the early licences were sold cheaply, and *"I didn't make a fortune, but I earned an honest living"* [C049].
- **The twist.** A 2015 study says Velcro works more like the plant *Circaea lutetiana* than like burdock [C028].

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
G7: PASS. Verified facts the popular version leaves out or gets wrong:
1. **The first Velcro was hooks on hooks, with no loops; only about 30% of the hooks caught** [C010, C014]. This is the hook.
2. **The idea slept for ten years before he began** [C038]. This corrects "ten years of work".
3. **A storeroom mistake supplied the thread that worked** [C040]. A true happy accident.
4. **The hook-cutting fix came from 15 days alone in a mountain hut** [C041].
5. **He saw "chamois horns" under the microscope** [C044], and the burrs were in **his dog's ears** [C024].
6. **A 2015 study: Velcro works more like a different plant than burdock** [C028]. This is the twist.
7. **People walked on an aircraft ceiling with it in 1963** [C030].
8. **"I didn't make a fortune"** [C049].

## Artistic licence notes (rule 4.8)
- **Dog:** real (his words), with **burrs in its ears**. The breed is unknown, so keep it a generic hunting dog. The 1984 film staged a black-and-white pointer type; the pilot's chestnut "Irish Pointer" follows the company claim only.
- **Scenery:** the Jura, as in the 1961 film: spruce forest, open meadow, dry-stone wall. **No Alps** at the burr moment. The Alps belong to the Trient hut scene.
- **George:** 1984 reconstruction: felt hunting hat, loden-style jacket, shotgun (fits the pilot's felt hat). 1961 interview: short dark hair, tweed jacket, tie [C052].
- **Hooks:** the 1961 film shows real Velcro hooks as open curls cut on one side (frame 1961_000758).
