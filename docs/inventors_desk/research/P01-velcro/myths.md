# P01 Velcro: the popular version, checked

The claims come from our own pilot script (written 27 Oct before the rules existed) and from the company page (S09). Nothing here enters the script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | A hunting trip **in the Swiss Alps** | Embellished | C021, C022, C041 | The hunt is confirmed in his own words; the 1961 film places it in the Jura, not the Alps. The Alps come later: the Trient mountain hut where he solved the hook cutting |
| M2 | His **dog** came back covered in burrs | Confirmed | C021, C024 | His own words (1961 and 1984): burrs in his dog's ears, and on his clothes |
| M3 | The dog was an **Irish Pointer** | Unverifiable | C024 | A company claim only. He never names the breed; the 1984 reconstruction used a black-and-white pointer-type dog |
| M4 | It happened in **1941** | Confirmed | C023 | He says "en 1941" in the 1961 film (his own account). Reuters' 1948 (C026) is still unexplained |
| M5 | He looked at a burr **under a microscope** | Confirmed | C025, C044 | His 1984 words; hooks "like chamois horns" |
| M6 | The burr had **hundreds** of tiny hooks (our pilot) | Unverifiable | — | The company says "thousands"; no count found in a primary source. Say "tiny hooks", no number |
| M7 | He designed **one strip of hooks and one of loops** from the start | Contradicted | C010, C014, C015 | His 1951 patent pressed hooks against hooks. Hooks + loops came in his 1957 patent, because only about 30% of hooks caught |
| M8 | It took **about ten years** to get right (our pilot) | Contradicted | C038, C043 | In his own words the idea *slept* for about ten years before he started; then came Lyon, the Trient hut and four years with the loom maker |
| M9 | He **patented it in 1955** | Embellished | C002, C003 | 1955 is the US grant. He filed in Switzerland in 1951 |
| M10 | The name is from French **velours + crochet** | Confirmed | C027 | 1961 film narration ("Vel comme velours, croc comme crochet") + HLS |
| M11 | **NASA invented** Velcro | Contradicted | C029 | NASA says no |
| M12 | It holds **astronauts' gear in space** | Confirmed | C029, C030 | Apollo use (NASA); 1963 Air Force zero-g walking tests |
| M13 | Burdock's hooks are **what Velcro copies** | Embellished | C028 | A 2015 study says burdock hooks are needle-like and single-use, and Velcro works more like *Circaea lutetiana* |
| M14 | He built the company **after** the 1955 patent (company page) | Contradicted | C006 | Velcro S.A. was the assignee on the October 1952 US filing |
| M15 | Commugny is **in the Swiss Alps** (company page) | Contradicted | C033, C041 | Commugny sits at about 420–444 m near Lake Geneva; the mountain retreat was the Trient hut |
| M16 | He patented a **toy aeroplane at 12** | Unverifiable | C036 | Reuters and the company say so; not found in the patent search |
| M17 | It began on **a walk in the woods outside Geneva** (Reuters 1990) | Contradicted | C021, C022 | His own accounts say he was coming home from hunting; the 1961 film says the Jura |
| M18 | He **got rich** from Velcro | Contradicted | C049 | His own words: the patents ran out in 1971, the early licences were cheap, "je n'ai pas fait fortune" |
