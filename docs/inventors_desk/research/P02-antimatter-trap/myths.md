# P02 Antimatter trap: the popular version, checked

The claims come from the October 2026 coverage (ScienceAlert 22 Oct, Smithsonian, Scientific American 21 Oct, saved in `popular/` and read only to list claims) and from AK's working title. Nothing here enters the script except through claims.jsonl.

| # | Popular claim | Verdict | Claims | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **"Physicists caged antimatter for months"** as if that were the new feat (working title; ScienceAlert headline) | Embellished | C003, C090, C072, C080, C081, C099 | The positron really was held for months, but even a SINGLE positron was caged for 3 months in Seattle in 1984 ("Priscilla"), and antiprotons for 2 months (1990) and 405 days (BASE, 2015/16). The new part is one positron controlled in its quantum ground state, one quantum at a time |
| M2 | **"For the first time"** scientists trapped and held a positron (Smithsonian headline) | Contradicted | C001, C058, C059, C090, C093 | Dehmelt's Seattle group trapped positrons by 1981, held one positron (Priscilla) for 3 months by Sept 1984, and isolated single positrons in a Penning trap by 1987. The paper's "first time" is the one-positron *quantum cyclotron* |
| M3 | It was held **"for a month"** (Scientific American headline and photo caption) | Contradicted | C003, C021 | The paper says "for months"; the Northwestern release says "for months at a time". SciAm's own body text also says "for months at a time" |
| M4 | The trap is at **0.3 degrees above absolute zero** | Confirmed | C004 | 300 mK in the paper |
| M5 | The positrons come from **radioactive sodium** and are slowed by a **tungsten crystal** | Confirmed | C006, C007 | Paper, p.1-3 |
| M6 | The trap is **"a container without walls"** | Confirmed | C010, C018 | Gabrielse's own phrase (SciAm); the paper's mechanism: fields, no contact |
| M7 | The team **measured** whether antimatter differs from matter | Contradicted | C015 | Not yet. The paper demonstrates the device; the positron magnetic-moment measurement is still to come |
| M8 | The electron was measured to **one part in 10 trillion** | Confirmed | C017 | 0.13 ppt (Fan et al. 2022/2023) |
| M9 | Positrons have been **observed since the 1930s** | Confirmed | C032 | Anderson's paper, September 1932 |
| M10 | **Penning invented the Penning trap** (common shorthand; implied by the name) | Contradicted | C043, C044, C052, C053, C060, C064 | Penning patented a vacuum *pressure gauge* in 1936. Dehmelt built the trap from the gauge's geometry in 1959 and, he wrote, named it after Penning; the Nobel committee says it was developed at the same time in Paul's lab and in Seattle |
| M11 | The Universe **"should not exist"**: the Big Bang made equal matter and antimatter | Confirmed (secondary only) | C033 | This is the theory as CERN states it, a puzzle, not a measured fact. Voice: "physicists think" |
| M12 | Antimatter could make a **bomb** / is dangerous (the science-fiction frame the release itself raises) | Contradicted | C019, C035 | Gabrielse: "it's not dangerous. We don't have enough antimatter to make an explosion." CERN: the largest traps hold about 10^-18 kg |
