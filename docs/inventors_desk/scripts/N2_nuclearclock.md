## Nolan design (Mode A, steps 1–8)

**Theme → shape (tool 1).** Knowing *when* is how we know *where*, and every leap in clocks moved the "ticker" deeper into matter. So the Short is **three parallel clocks** (as in Dunkirk) that converge on one rule, inside a **hairpin**: the final line answers the first.

**Diagram (tool 2):**
```
b01 HOOK  "your head ages faster than your feet"  (pledge, unexplained) ──────────────┐
b02 NEWS  first clocks that tick with an atom's centre                                │
b03 "follow three clocks"                                                             │
      ┌─ A · SEA 1707      b04–05  lost fleet: you need the exact time                │
      ├─ B · POCKET today  b06–07  GPS: a billionth of a second = 30 cm               │
      └─ C · LAB Oct 2026  b08     laser on a thorium crystal                          │
b09 RULE  "a clock is only as good as the thing that ticks inside it"                 │
      A pays → b10 Harrison: 5 s in 81 days      (cross-cut, faster)                  │
      B pays → b11 caesium: 9 billion ticks/s                                         │
      C pays → b12–13 electrons get jostled → hide the tick in the nucleus (turn)     │
b14 CATCH + stamp PUBLISHED; not yet better; ~10,000× steadier possible               │
b15 clocks feel gravity: 1 mm (prestige)                                              │
b16 HAIRPIN "your head is older than your feet" ──────────────────────── loops to b01 ┘
```

**POV (tool 3).** Second person, subjective, inside each strand: *you* are the navigator; it's *your* phone. Objective in b14 (the claim and the numbers). Visually, strand scenes use the hand-drawn colour look, and the claim beat uses the mono data look.

**Clocks (tools 4, 5).**
- **Strands:** sea (1707 → 1761), pocket (today), lab (October 2026). They're cross-cut twice: set up in b04–08, paid off faster in b10–13.
- **Audible clock:** a tick sound that speeds up with each era, plus an on-screen **tick-rate counter**. It climbs from 1/s (pendulum) to 9,192,631,770/s (caesium) to ~2,000,000,000,000,000/s (thorium) and blurs at the top.

**Rules (tools 6, 7).**
- **The rule:** "A clock is only as good as the thing that ticks inside it" (b09).
- **Exposition is the game:** the puzzle "how do you find where you are? You need the time" is posed in b04, and it's the heist the three strands solve.
- **Rule broken:** electrons get pushed around (b12), so hide the ticker in the nucleus (b13).

**Heart (tool 8).** More than 1,400 drowned sailors (b05) are the human cost that makes the time matter. *Your* dot on the map (b07, b11) carries it into today.

**Trick (tools 9–11).**
- **Pledge (b01):** an ordinary-sounding but strange claim, left unexplained.
- **Turn (b13):** the tick moves inside the nucleus.
- **Prestige (b15–16):** clocks that feel a millimetre of gravity, and the hook was literally true.
- **Fair-play plant:** the claim is in b01 and paid off in b16, with no information withheld.
- **Hairpin:** the last line rhymes with the first, so the Short loops.

**Finish (tools 12–14).** The cross-cut climax is b10–13: three payoffs in quick succession, cut faster. No moral dilemma: n/a for this topic, scored honestly below. Last image: two small clocks at head and feet, ticking very slightly out of step.
