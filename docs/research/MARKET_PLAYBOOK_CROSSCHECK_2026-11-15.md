# Cross-check of the pasted "Market Research and Strategic Playbook" (15 Nov 2026)

The input is kept verbatim in `INPUT_market_playbook_2026-11-15.md`. It was checked by three research passes on 15 Nov 2026: channel and video facts, economics and YouTube policy, and public-domain rights. The fourth pass, on visual format, is summarised in `INNER_CHARIOT_NORTH_STAR.md` section 5.

Verdicts:
- **CONFIRMED:** we found it in the source.
- **PARTLY:** the core is right but a detail is wrong or the method is missing.
- **WRONG**
- **UNVERIFIED:** we couldn't check it.
- **VENDOR:** it appears only on a marketing blog, with no method.

**Bottom line:** the document's direction holds up: English long-form, verse-cited, human-crafted visuals, no AI slideshows. About a third of its numbers are vendor marketing, or can't be traced at all. Three of its claims are wrong in ways that matter to us (section 4).

**What the breakout channels actually look like.** This matters more than their numbers.
- **udbhava** is code- or motion-made in the style of *tholu bommalata* (South Indian leather shadow puppetry), with the Sanskrit verse on screen and verse numbers cited (Katha 1.1.3, 1.2.18, 1.3.3). It names Shankara and Krishnananda as sources. Its credits say **"Narration voices: ElevenLabs"**. **It is the closest thing to our channel that exists, and it reached 456K views with a disclosed synthetic voice.**
- **Cosmic Insights** is an illustrated, dramatised slideshow, labelled "creative & dramatized interpretation", with invented dialogue.
- **Tattva Darshan** has glossy AI-style thumbnails and cites no verses.

So two of the three breakouts are the "AI slideshow" format the document calls a trap. It reaches views. What it doesn't build is trust, a series or a defensible channel. All three are small channels with a single outlier, not proven businesses.

**The synthetic narrator voice is not settled by YouTube's text.**
- The Help page requires disclosure of *realistic* altered or synthetic content. Its voice examples are about cloning a real person.
- YouTube's 2024 blog post lists "synthetically generating a person's voice to narrate a video" as a case to disclose.
- **Our rule stands:** tick the Studio box, and keep the spoken and written disclosure (house rule 7).

## 4. What the document gets wrong, and why it matters to us

1. **"Avoid AI slideshows, they're low-yield."** That's half true. They do get views (765K for Cosmic Insights). Their real cost is the inauthentic-content policy and trust. We avoid them because they can't carry a verse with its source, not because nobody watches them.
2. **"Human voice, or a custom-trained voice."** udbhava shows that a disclosed synthetic voice can carry a verse-cited essay to 456K views. A human reciter still matters for Sanskrit (house rule 10).
3. **"Curated classical art" as the main visual layer.** Most classical Indian paintings of the Gita and Ramayana show deities' faces, which conflicts with house rule 5. Doré engravings are European and off-tone for our channel. Museum open-access images (Met, Cleveland, LACMA) are usable for **places, courts, objects and sages**, never for deity faces unless AK changes rule 5.
4. **Its launch slate leans on texts with no public-domain English** (rights pass, 15 Nov):

   | Text | Usable English | Verdict |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Yoga Sutras | Charles Johnston 1912 (Gutenberg #2526), Woods 1914 | USE |
   | Vivekachudamani | Madhavananda 1921 (he died Oct 1965, so it just clears India's 60-year rule) | USE |
   | Vishnu Purana | H. H. Wilson 1840 | USE |
   | Tirukkural | G. U. Pope 1886 | USE |
   | Ashtavakra Gita | Lala Baij Nath (1904/1907; died 1914) | USE WITH CARE: check the title page |
   | Mandukya with Gaudapada's Karika | M. N. Dvivedi 1894 (died 1898). No scan found yet | USE WITH CARE |
   | Avadhuta Gita, Drg-Drshya Viveka, Kashmir Shaivism sutras, Tattva Bodha, Chitrakavya | None clean (Nikhilananda died 1973; modern translations only) | PARAPHRASE ONLY, marked OUR NOTE with the Sanskrit verse number |

5. **"Jung and the Shadow" pairings.** Jung's standard English translations, by R. F. C. Hull (who died in 1974), are still in copyright. We can name and explain his ideas, but we can't quote him at length on screen.
