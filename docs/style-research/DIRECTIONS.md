## 1. Lamplight: backlit silhouettes with a gold rim (shadow-theatre lineage)

**Look.** Deep indigo to violet to rose to saffron skies, or a dark room lit by a single diya. Every figure, horse, tree and bird is one flat near-black shape (#160C1E or a tint of it, never pure black) with NO outline. A thin warm-gold rim picks out the edge that faces the light. Light is the only colour: sun discs, diya flames, god rays, lamplight pooling on a floor, all drawn as gradients that fade to zero, not as blur filters. Three to five parallax planes are separated by haze, dust motes drift through the light, and there is fine film grain, a soft vignette and slow push-ins. Large close-ups of hero shapes can carry tholu-bommalata-style perforation: small lotus and vine holes that glow through. Verse and title cards are gold Tiro Devanagari over darkness, with one kolam line drawing itself behind them. Faces never exist.

**Why it fits.** THIS IS MY BET. (1) Light against darkness is the texts' own metaphor: the lamp of knowledge, the rope mistaken for a snake in dim light, 'lead me from darkness to light'. (2) It is faceless and aniconic by construction. (3) It hides our weakest skill, interior drawing, which is exactly what made the outlined kit read as '2nd grade', and it relies on our strongest: gradients, timing and code. (4) High contrast reads on a phone, so Shorts work. (5) About 60% is already built in src/katha (Sky, Dust, Haze, Rimmed, Frame, Finish, VerseCard, TitleCard). The verse-card reference docs/katha/ref-verse.png is already at a professional standard. (6) A rigged CC0 horse and a faceless CC0 mannequin now give real gaits and gestures. The current traced team in ref-chariot.png moves like a comb, and it comes from a NonCommercial source. To be honest about the risk: this is the most common look among faceless philosophy channels. The Indian specifics are what make it ours: the diya as the signature light, perforated shadow-puppet detail, Devanagari typography, kolam lines, and Mithila ornament from direction 2 on title cards and trees (the two share an indigo-and-gold palette, e.g. Mithila seed 014 'Midnight marigold'). The current ref-chariot frame is also too busy for a calm channel (armies, spears, flags), and its Krishna figure breaks the house rule. Both are fixable. If the owner wants one look that nobody else has and will accept slower production, pick direction 2 instead.

**Effort.** Low to medium. The chariot prototype is one sitting, because sky, dust, grain and cards already exist in src/katha. Then 2 to 3 days to build the skinning tracer, replace the NC horses and birds, and build the metaphor scenes, which are still stubs in src/katha/metaphors/.

**Risks.** Generic look: it is the most imitated faceless-channel aesthetic. Without the Indian signature (diya, perforation, Devanagari, kolam, Mithila ornament) it reads as 'stoic wallpaper'. Silhouettes are unforgiving of proportion, so all horses and people must come from the rigs, not be freehand. The UAL mannequin is muscular, so check that it reads as an ordinary person in silhouette; slim the chest scale if needed. A whole film at night gets monotonous, so a colour script is needed. Gradients band after YouTube compression without grain. Each live WebGL layer costs about 0.1 to 0.5 s per frame on this GPU-less container (SwiftShader), so bake the rig cycles to SVG for long films. Deity creep: the existing figures.ts gives Krishna a crown and a peacock feather, and a silhouette with those attributes still depicts him, so keep every figure unmarked unless the owner rules otherwise. (Rule 5 now allows Krishna's marked silhouette in the Gita film; see the note at the top.) The kit's own lesson stands: no large feGaussianBlur or feTurbulence (long renders dropped black tiles).

**Deterministic in Remotion.** Yes. SVG sky, haze, ridges, dust and grain are pure functions of frame plus seeded rnd(i)/noise2D, with no Math.random or Date. The 3D layer sets every mixer's time explicitly from frame (clipTime), never from a clock or delta, so out-of-order rendering is safe. The wheel, pennant and road are closed-form in f. Paper Shaders take speed 0 and frame = f*1000/fps, with delayRender for the noise texture. Production bakes the rig cycles to SVG pose JSON, which removes WebGL entirely.

## 2. Mithila fine-line: heritage textile line art on dark grounds

**Look.** Deep forest, indigo, soot or madder grounds with a faint cloth grain. Every form is drawn in cream, ochre and vermilion hairlines with a double contour and a hatched kachni channel between the two lines. Leaves are tiny and repeat by the hundred. A hatched block-print border with corner rosettes frames the field. Horses, chariot and people are flat profile shapes in the ground colour, carried by the double-line grammar and patterned inside like Madhubani animals. People have solid heads with no features. Motion is lines drawing on, trees growing, leaves stirring, and borders assembling from the corners; animal motion is stepped at 12 poses per second, so it feels hand-drawn.

**Why it fits.** It is the most distinctive option and the least like clip-art: thin lines instead of thick ink, very dense marks instead of flat fills, and restrained mineral palettes. The 24-seed contact sheet (viewed) reads as a museum textile, and it is unmistakably Indian without any deity. It also solves several scenes at once: the generator natively places two birds on one tree (compose() reserves a perch on each side), it can be flipped into the inverted ashvattha, and it does borders and title-card trees. The modules are pure, DOM-free functions of seed and time, so they suit Remotion well. It is second only because chariots, horses, people, conch and battlefield do not exist in the grammar yet, and building them is real design work. Our pose-trace pipeline and offset hatching make that procedural rather than freehand, but it is still the riskiest part. It also pairs with direction 1 as the ornamental layer, using the shared indigo-gold palette.

**Effort.** Medium to high. One day to vendor and test the generator; 1 to 2 days for the pose tracer (shared with direction 1) and the double-contour hatch renderer; then each new subject (conch, temple, river, battlefield) is a day or more of design in the grammar.

**Risks.** Chariot, horses, people, conch and battlefield have to be newly designed in the grammar, which is the part where our drawing has failed before. Every seed shares one template (centred tree, mound, peacock, fish register), so variety comes only from palette and branching, and it can feel repetitive across a long series. The isolated peacock and fish motifs are cruder than the trees. Traditional Madhubani faces (a large frontal eye) conflict with the faceless rule, so heads stay solid. Hairlines can alias or moire on phones and in Shorts. Narrative action and emotion (the 2 a.m. overthinker, battle) are harder to show in a decorative frieze than with light. Culturally, present it as 'inspired by Mithila painting', not as Madhubani.

**Deterministic in Remotion.** Yes. compose(seed) is a pure function of a seed string (Mulberry32/FNV-1a). completeSVG(model, {motionTime: f/fps}) samples the wind rig deterministically. nodeState(node, t) gives growth progress from time. Pose stepping and wheel rotation are closed-form in f. Draw-on uses evolvePath from frame. Never use Muggula's CSS keyframes; drive strokeDashoffset from frame instead. No WebGL.

## 3. Ink and lamplight wash: painted brush plates on warm paper (Bengal-School wash, Indian forms)

**Look.** Warm handmade paper with visible grain. Lamp-black ink in five tones with tapered, pressure-shaped brush lines that swell and thin. Soft graded washes with pooled edges, a lot of empty paper, and distant plains and Himalayan ridges dissolving into mist bands. Figures are small and gestural, faceless by the nature of the medium. There is one warm accent per scene only: the diya flame, a saffron pennant, the lotus, a gold Devanagari underline. Animals and people move in stepped poses at 12 per second, and each pose is re-washed, so edges 'boil' like hand-painted animation. Transitions are ink blooms; the camera makes slow lateral pans like unrolling a scroll.

**Why it fits.** This is the strongest 2D painterly answer to the owner's complaint. Tonal washes and tapered lines are the biggest perceived jump from 'school worksheet' to 'artwork', and ink restraint is the register of contemplative philosophy films. India has its own lineage for this look, the Bengal School wash of Abanindranath Tagore and Nandalal Bose, so it can be Indian rather than Zen if the forms are Indian. Empty paper forgives the lack of an illustrator. It ranks third because the defaults of every ink library are Chinese or Japanese (pines, pavilions, seals, kanji), the painted plates cost the most to render, and the result depends on composition skill more than the other directions.

**Effort.** High: about 2 days for the pipeline (bake compositions, ridge port, presets), the pose library shared with directions 1 and 2, then per-scene plate painting and a cultural audit.

**Risks.** It can be read as Chinese or Zen unless every motif is re-skinned to Indian forms, and monochrome can feel cold over a long video without the warm accent. p5.brush watercolour on this GPU-less container is far too slow to repaint per frame, so it must be 'bake plates, animate camera, light and stepped poses'; plates cannot morph. Consistency across many scenes needs strict presets. Watercolour amplifies weak composition rather than hiding it. tengubyte/sumi is pre-1.0, not on npm and wall-clock driven, so we own the vendored copy. It also suits landscapes, rivers and night better than the gunas or salt diagrams.

**Deterministic in Remotion.** Yes, with discipline. p5.brush seeds itself from Math.random at import, so call brush.seed() and brush.noiseSeed() before every bake and redraw from scratch; plates are baked once anyway. Watercolorizer takes an injected seeded random. perfect-freehand is a pure function. Stepped poses and parallax are closed-form in f. sumi effects are driven by painter.draw(f/fps), never by its clock.

## 4. Lamplit clay diorama: toon-shaded 3D miniature

**Look.** A small, quiet world seen from a slow, low dolly, like a stop-motion set. Matte objects with a two- or three-band toon ramp in a fixed palette: indigo night, terracotta earth, ivory horses, unbleached-cotton cloth, brass accents. Exponential fog and a dusk gradient sky, with one warm key light from a diya or the setting sun and a cool HDRI fill. Gentle bloom on flames only, plus grain and a vignette. Faceless mannequins wear code-made shawls and dhotis so they read as people, not dummies. Devanagari cards are 2D layers over a defocused 3D plate.

**Why it fits.** This is the honest 3D option. The CC0 rigged horse and mannequin give the most convincing motion of any direction, there is real perspective and parallax for long camera moves, and once the scene is modelled, new shots are cheap camera moves. It ranks last because the probe frame (its frame image was not kept; the code is in docs/style-research/probe/Probe.tsx.txt) currently looks like a mobile-game prototype. Every prop (chariot, diya, conch, lotus, temple) must be modelled in code because no CC0 Indian models are reachable. The flora is Western. And CPU-only rendering costs 1.5 to 3 hours per 10-minute video. Its best use is probably as the motion source for direction 1, not as the visible style.

**Effort.** High: several days to model the Indian props and cloth, plus slow CPU renders for every iteration.

**Risks.** Saturated defaults slide straight into a 'mobile game' look, so palette discipline is mandatory and success is not guaranteed. Without cloth the mannequin reads as a crash-test dummy. Every Indian prop must be modelled in code. The flora kit is Western, with no peepal, banyan or lotus. Rendering takes about 0.35 to 1 s per frame on CPU (1.5 to 3 hours per 10-minute video), and god rays or transmission are slower still. Drei components that animate from a clock (Sparkles, Float, Cloud) must be frame-driven or frozen. Environment presets and the Draco decoder fetch from blocked CDNs.

**Deterministic in Remotion.** Yes, if every animated value comes from frame: mixer.setTime(clipTime(f)), EZ-Tree tree.update(f/fps), uv offsets and the dolly in closed form, drei Stars and Sparkles with speed 0 and frame-driven, Noise effect seeded per frame, and no useFrame clock or delta. HDRIs are bundled from npm, not fetched.
