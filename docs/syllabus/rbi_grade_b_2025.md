# RBI Grade 'B' (DR) 2025: scheme and syllabus

Source: Reserve Bank of India Services Board, Advt. No. **RBISB/DA/03/2025-26**, "Direct Recruitment for the Posts of
Officers in Grade 'B' (DR) (General/DEPR/DSIM) Cadres, Panel Year 2025" (rbi.org.in). Syllabus text is from
Appendix-II (General) and Appendix-III (DEPR, DSIM), pages 32-46 of the notice. Machine-readable topic tree:
[`rbi_grade_b_2025.json`](rbi_grade_b_2025.json).

When a video uses this syllabus, cite it on screen as: *Source: RBI Services Board, Advt. RBISB/DA/03/2025-26*.

## At a glance

| Cadre | Vacancies | Phase I | Phase II | Interview |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General | 83 | 18 Oct 2025: one online objective paper, 200 marks, 120 min (General Awareness, English, Quant, Reasoning) | 6 Dec 2025: ESI 100 + English writing 100 + GFM 100 = 300 | 75 marks |
| DEPR (economics) | 17 | 19 Oct 2025: Economics objective 100 + English descriptive 100 | 7 Dec 2025: two Economics descriptive papers, 100 each (written on paper) | 75 marks |
| DSIM (statistics) | 20 | 19 Oct 2025: Statistics objective 100 | 7 Dec 2025: Statistics descriptive 100 (180 min, on paper) + English descriptive 100 (90 min) | 75 marks |

Total vacancies: 120. Applications ran 10-30 Sep 2025. A candidate can apply for DEPR or DSIM, not both.

Eligibility (as on 1 Sep 2025), in short: age 21-30 (born 2 Sep 1995 to 1 Sep 2004), with category, M.Phil./Ph.D. and
experience relaxations in the notice. General cadre needs a graduation with 60% (50% for SC/ST/PwBD) or a
post-graduation with 55% (pass marks for SC/ST/PwBD). DEPR needs an MA/MSc with economics or finance as the principal
constituent, 55% (50% for SC/ST/PwBD). DSIM needs a master's (55%) in statistics, maths, quantitative economics,
econometrics, informatics, data science, AI/ML or big data analytics, or a four-year bachelor's (60%) in those fields.
See the notice for the full lists.

All papers are set in Hindi and English except English papers. Every cadre has an unscored Personality Assessment before
the interview. Final merit = Phase II (or all written papers for DEPR/DSIM) + interview.

## 1. Grade 'B' (DR) General

### Phase I (objective, 200 marks, 120 min, sectional timing)

1. General Awareness
2. English Language
3. Quantitative Aptitude
4. Reasoning

Sectional and aggregate cut-offs apply; shortlisting for Phase II is on aggregate.

### Phase II

| Paper | Format | Time | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I. Economic and Social Issues | 50% objective (30 Qs) + 50% descriptive (attempt 4 of 6: 2 x 15, 2 x 10), typed | 30 + 90 min | 100 |
| II. English (Writing Skills) | Descriptive, typed, 3 questions | 90 min | 100 |
| III. General Finance and Management | 50% objective (30 Qs) + 50% descriptive (attempt 4 of 6), typed | 30 + 90 min | 100 |

If more than 4 descriptive answers are given, only the first 4 are evaluated.

#### Paper I: Economic and Social Issues (ESI)

- **Growth and Development**: measurement of growth (national income and per capita income); poverty alleviation and
  employment generation in India; sustainable development and environmental issues.
- **Indian Economy**: economic history of India; changes in industrial and labour policy, monetary and fiscal policy
  since the 1991 reforms; priorities and recommendations of the Economic Survey and Union Budget; Indian money and
  financial markets and their linkages with the economy; role of Indian banks and the RBI in the development process;
  public finance; political economy; industrial developments; Indian agriculture; services sector.
- **Globalization**: opening up of the Indian economy; balance of payments; export-import policy; international economic
  institutions (IMF, World Bank, WTO); regional economic co-operation; international economic issues.
- **Social Structure in India**: multiculturalism; demographic trends; urbanisation and migration; gender issues; social
  justice.

Suggested reading (indicative): Uma Kapila (Indian Economy series); Misra & Puri, *Indian Economy*; Debraj Ray,
*Development Economics*; C.N. Shankar Rao, *Sociology*; K.K. Andley and Sundaram, *Public Finance*; financial
newspapers; RBI Bulletin, EPW; World Development Report; Economic Survey; RBI Annual Report; Report on Trend and Progress
of Banking in India; Report on Currency and Finance; IMF World Economic Outlook; RBI and BIS websites.

#### Paper II: English (Writing Skills)

Assesses writing skills, including expression and understanding of the topic.

#### Paper III: General Finance and Management

**a) Financial System**

1. Structure and functions of financial institutions
2. Functions of the Reserve Bank of India
3. Banking system in India: structure and developments; financial institutions (SIDBI, EXIM Bank, NABARD, NHB, NaBFID)
4. Recent developments in the global financial system and their impact on India
5. Role of information technology in banking and finance
6. Non-banking system
7. Developments in digital payments

**b) Financial Markets**: primary and secondary markets (forex, money, bond, equity etc.): functions, instruments,
recent developments.

**c) General Topics**

1. Financial risk management
2. Basics of derivatives
3. Global financial markets and international banking: broad trends and latest developments
4. Financial inclusion
5. Alternate sources of finance; private and social cost-benefit; public-private partnership
6. Corporate governance in the banking sector
7. The Union Budget: concepts, approach and broad trends
8. Basics of accounting and financial statements: balance sheet, P&L, cash flow statement, ratio analysis (debt to
   equity, debtor days, creditor days, inventory turnover, ROA, ROE etc.)
9. Inflation: definition, trends, estimates, consequences and remedies; WPI and CPI components and trends; balancing
   inflation and growth through monetary and fiscal policy

Suggested reading, finance (indicative): Kurihara, *Monetary Theory and Public Policy*; Mishra & Puri, *Indian Economy*;
Meier and Baldwin, *Economic Growth and Development*; Prasanna Chandra, *Financial Management*; Hill and Jain,
*International Business*; RBI Annual Report; Trend and Progress of Banking; Report on Currency and Finance; Economic
Survey; RBI website.

**d) Management**

1. **Fundamentals of Management and Organizational Behaviour**
   - Introduction to management; evolution of management thought (scientific, administrative, human relations, systems);
     management functions and managerial roles; nudge theory.
   - Organizational behaviour: meaning and concept; personality (factors, Big Five model); reinforcement; perception and
     perceptual errors.
   - Motivation: content theories (Maslow, Alderfer ERG, McClelland, Herzberg two-factor) and process theories (Adams
     equity, Vroom expectancy).
   - Leadership: trait, behavioural, contingency, charismatic, transactional and transformational theories; emotional
     intelligence (concept, importance, dimensions).
   - Interpersonal relationships: transactional analysis, Johari window.
   - Conflict: concept, sources, types, management.
   - Organizational change (Kurt Lewin) and organizational development: strategies for change, theories of planned change
     (Lewin's change model, action research model, positive model).
2. **Ethics at the Workplace and Corporate Governance**
   - Meaning of ethics; why ethical problems occur in business.
   - Theories of ethics: utilitarianism (social costs and benefits), rights and duties, justice and fairness, ethics of
     care, integrating utility/rights/justice/care; virtue ethics; teleological theories; egoism; relativism.
   - Moral issues in business: ethics in compliance, finance, HR, marketing etc.
   - Ethical principles in business: organization structure and ethics, role of the board, best practices in ethics
     programmes, code of ethics, code of conduct.
   - Corporate governance: factors affecting it; mechanisms.
   - Communication: steps in the process; channels; oral vs written; verbal vs non-verbal; upward, downward and lateral;
     barriers; role of IT.

Suggested reading, management (indicative): Robbins & Coulter, *Management*; Robbins, Judge & Vohra, *Organisational
Behaviour*; Dessler & Varkkey, *Human Resource Management*; Decenzo & Robbins, *Fundamentals of HRM*; Velasquez,
*Business Ethics*; A.C. Fernando, *Business Ethics: An Indian Perspective*; Crane & Matten, *Business Ethics*;
B.N. Ghosh, *Business Ethics & Corporate Governance*.

## 2. Grade 'B' (DR) DEPR (Department of Economic and Policy Research)

Job: economic analysis and research, contributing to policy formulation, and compiling data for select sectors.

| Phase | Paper | Format | Time | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Paper I: Economics | Objective, online | 120 min | 100 |
| I | Paper II: English | Descriptive, typed | 120 min | 100 |
| II | Paper I: Economics | Descriptive, written on paper | 120 min | 100 |
| II | Paper II: Economics | Descriptive, written on paper | 120 min | 100 |

Total 400 + interview 75. Phase II shortlisting is on the Phase I aggregate.

### Phase I, Paper I: Economics (objective)

1. Microeconomics: theories of consumer demand; production; market structures and pricing; distribution; welfare
   economics.
2. Macroeconomics: theories of employment, output and inflation; monetary economics; IS-LM model; schools of economic
   thought.
3. International economics: theories of international trade; balance of payments; exchange rate models.
4. Theories of economic growth and development: classical and neo-classical approaches to growth; major theories of
   development.
5. Public finance: theories of taxation and public expenditure; public debt management.
6. Environmental economics: green GDP; environmental valuation; environmental policy instruments.
7. Quantitative methods in economics: mathematical and statistical methods; OLS regression.
8. Current developments in the Indian economy: growth, inflation, poverty, unemployment, financial sector, external
   sector, fiscal, agriculture, industry, infrastructure, services.

### Phase I, Paper II: English (descriptive)

Assesses writing skills, including expression and understanding of the topic.

### Phase II, Paper I: Economics (descriptive). Micro and macro carry equal weight.

**Microeconomics module**

- Consumer theory: cardinal and marginal utility analysis; consumer surplus; indifference curves; price, income and
  substitution effects; game theory.
- Production theory: forms of production function; laws of returns to scale; partial vs general equilibrium.
- Market theory: pricing under different market structures.
- Distribution theories: Ricardo, Marx, Kalecki, Kaldor.
- Welfare economics: Pareto optimality; schools of welfare thought including Arrow, Coase and Sen.

**Macroeconomics module**

- National income accounting: methods of measurement.
- Employment and output: classical and neo-classical approaches; Keynesian theory; post-Keynesian developments;
  business cycles.
- Inflation: types; Phillips curve; Taylor rule; Lucas critique.
- Money and banking: quantity theory; neutrality of money; IS-LM and AD-AS models; money multiplier; monetary policy
  scope, objectives and instruments; inflation targeting.
- Growth and development: theories of growth (classical, neo-classical); theories of development.
- International trade and BoP: trade theories; exchange rate determination; impossible trinity.
- Public finance: theories of taxation, public expenditure and public debt management.

### Phase II, Paper II: Economics (descriptive). Quant and Indian economy carry equal weight.

**Quantitative methods in economics**

- Mathematical methods: differentiation and integration; optimisation; sets; matrices; linear algebra; linear
  programming.
- Statistical methods: central tendency and dispersion; probability; time series; index numbers.
- Econometrics and advanced applications: regression analysis; panel data econometrics; time series econometrics;
  basics of Bayesian econometrics; basic applications of AI/ML.

**Indian economy: policy and trends**

- Fiscal policy in India: evolution, scope and limitations, current trends.
- Monetary policy in India: evolution; functions of the RBI; monetary-fiscal coordination; inflation targeting;
  operating framework; current trends.
- Banking and financial sector development: banks and other constituents of Indian financial markets; current trends.
- Inflation in India: trends and drivers.
- External sector: exchange rate management; external debt; balance of payments; current trends.
- Sectoral and other developments: agriculture, industry, services and social sector.

## 3. Grade 'B' (DR) DSIM (Department of Statistics and Information Management)

Job: collecting, compiling and analysing banking, corporate and external sector data; statistical and econometric
modelling and forecasting of inflation and growth (classical and Bayesian inference, high-dimensional regression, time
series, simulation); survey design; index numbers and inequality measurement; managing large structured and unstructured
datasets, queries, reporting systems and the data warehouse; applying data science, AI and ML.

| Paper | Format | Date | Time | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I: Statistics | Objective, online | 19 Oct 2025 | 120 min | 100 |
| II: Statistics | Descriptive, written on paper | 7 Dec 2025 | 180 min | 100 |
| III: English | Descriptive, typed | 7 Dec 2025 | 90 min | 100 |

Total 300 + interview 75. Papers II and III shortlisting is on Paper I. Standard: post-graduate level.

### Papers I and II: Statistics (detailed syllabus)

1. **Probability, probability distributions and sampling theory**
   - Classical and axiomatic probability; Bayes theorem; strong and weak laws of large numbers; characteristic
     functions; central limit theorem; probability inequalities.
   - Distributions: binomial, Poisson, geometric, negative binomial, uniform, normal, exponential, logistic, log-normal,
     beta, gamma, Weibull, bivariate normal etc.
   - Exact sampling distributions (chi-square, t, F, Z) and applications; asymptotic distributions and large-sample tests;
     association and contingency tables.
   - Sampling: simple random, stratified, systematic, cluster, two-stage, PPS; ratio and regression estimation;
     non-sampling errors and non-response.
2. **Linear models and economic statistics**
   - Linear algebra: vectors, matrices, spanning, partitioned inverses, g-inverse, orthogonal and idempotent matrices,
     characteristic roots and vectors, Cayley-Hamilton, quadratic forms and their definiteness, simultaneous reduction of
     two quadratic forms, similar matrices.
   - Regression: simple linear regression (assumptions, estimation, inference, diagnostics); polynomial regression;
     Box-Cox, square root and log transformations; weighted least squares; inverse regression; multiple regression under
     Gauss-Markov; correlated observations; simultaneous estimation; hypothesis tests, confidence intervals and regions;
     multicollinearity and shrinkage (ridge, LASSO, elastic net); model selection; residual diagnostics; dummy variables;
     outliers.
   - Index numbers: construction; standard indices; chain-base and fixed-base conversion; base shifting, splicing,
     deflating; inequality (Gini, Lorenz curve); basics of macroeconomics and national accounts.
3. **Statistical inference**
   - Estimation: unbiasedness, sufficiency, consistency, efficiency; factorization theorem; complete statistics; MVUE;
     Rao-Blackwell and Lehmann-Scheffe; Cramer-Rao inequality.
   - Methods: moments, maximum likelihood, least squares, minimum chi-square, basic Bayes estimators.
   - Testing: type I and II errors, critical region, size, p-value, power; most powerful and UMP tests; Neyman-Pearson;
     likelihood ratio tests; goodness of fit; Bartlett's test.
   - Non-parametric: Kolmogorov-Smirnov, sign, Wilcoxon signed-rank and rank-sum, Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis,
     Friedman, Kendall's tau, Spearman's rank correlation; order statistics; distribution fitting; kernel density
     estimation.
4. **Stochastic processes**
   - Poisson process: arrival, inter-arrival and conditional arrival distributions; non-homogeneous processes; law of
     rare events; compound Poisson.
   - Markov chains: transition matrix, Chapman-Kolmogorov, regular chains and stationary distributions, periodicity,
     limit theorems, recurrent events; Brownian motion as the limit of a random walk; martingales.
5. **Multivariate analysis**: multivariate normal; logit and probit; Mahalanobis D-squared; linear discriminant
   analysis; canonical correlation; principal components; factor analysis; cluster analysis.
6. **Econometrics and time series**
   - General linear model, OLS and GLS; heteroscedasticity; pure and mixed estimation; autocorrelation and tests; Theil
     BLUS; multicollinearity; ridge regression.
   - Stochastic regressors, instrumental variables, panel regression, autoregressive and distributed lag models;
     simultaneous equations (identification, rank and order conditions, estimation methods).
   - Time series: stationarity; AR, MA, ARMA; ACF/PACF identification; Box-Jenkins; ARCH/GARCH; ARIMA/SARIMA; order
     of integration; trend vs difference stationarity; unit-root tests.
7. **Optimization and statistical computing**
   - Unconstrained optimization (Taylor, convex and coercive functions; Newton, gradient, conjugate gradient,
     quasi-Newton); constrained optimization (penalty methods, Lagrange multipliers); convex sets; linear programming
     (formulation, vertex theorems, graphical solution, simplex).
   - Simulation; jack-knife, bootstrap, cross-validation; robust, nonlinear and generalized linear regression; trees;
     EM algorithm and imputation; Bayesian modelling; MCMC, simulated annealing, Gibbs sampling, Metropolis-Hastings;
     neural networks; association rules.
8. **Data science, AI and ML**: supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning; accuracy measures; linear, logistic
   and penalized regression; naive Bayes; nearest neighbour; decision trees; SVM; kernel methods; neural networks;
   random forests; k-means, k-medoids, linkage methods; Dunn index, gap statistic; bagging and boosting (AdaBoost,
   gradient boosting); RNN; CNN; NLP; recursive feature elimination; VIF; ensembling and stacking; elastic net;
   grid-search tuning; feature importance; cross-validation strategies.
9. **Database and data warehouse management**: data structures; RDBMS and NoSQL fundamentals; normalization; SQL
   (querying, updating, aggregating); inner, left, right and outer joins; document, key-value, wide-column and graph
   stores; data warehousing, star and snowflake schemas, ETL, OLAP vs OLTP; indexing and optimization; big data
   frameworks.

### Paper III: English (descriptive)

Assesses writing skills, including expression and understanding of the topic.
