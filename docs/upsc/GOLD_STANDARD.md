# UPSC series: the Gold Standard (v1, 1 Nov 2026)

**AK's rule:** "NCERT Books are the first primary resource for all subjects, in addition to this add other resource and produce GOLD standard. Each topics should in depth understanding and should be teach ELI5 format without reducing the deapth of the subjects."

In one line: **as simple as a child can follow, as deep as the exam demands.** An episode that is simple but thin fails. An episode that is deep but hard to follow also fails.

This page sits on top of `STORY_ENGINE.md`. An episode cannot go to voice until it passes every gate in section 5.

---

## 1. Where facts come from, in order

| Order | Source | Used for | Rule |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **NCERT, the current edition** | The base of every episode: what the topic is, how it is structured, what must be taught | Read first, always. Record book, class, chapter, edition and reprint year |
| 2 | **The publisher of record** (section 3) | Laws, judgments, schemes, every figure, the current position | Wins over NCERT when newer. Note the difference in the claim ledger |
| 3 | **Government teaching material and university-press scholars** (section 3) | Whatever goes beyond NCERT, mostly optional-paper topics, and debates | Named in the description, and on screen where it is an interpretation |
| Banned | Coaching notes, blogs, news, YouTube, AI summaries, Wikipedia | Nothing | May suggest where to look. Never evidence |

**Rules that follow from this:**
1. **Background knowledge is not a source,** including the agent's own. It can only suggest which page to open.
2. **Never show an NCERT figure as current.** Textbook figures age. Show it with its year, or replace it from the publisher of record.
3. **Two independent sources for anything important or surprising.** One must be from order 1 or 2.
4. **If sources disagree, say so.** Do not quietly pick one. If it cannot be resolved, it goes on screen as a disagreement or does not go on screen.
5. **Our own words and our own drawings.** NCERT text, figures and maps are never copied or shown. Source PDFs stay on the SSD (`LOCAL_ONLY.md`), never in the public repo.
6. **Say exactly who said it.** "The Court held" only for what the majority held. Otherwise name the judge and whether the opinion is separate or dissenting. Mark our own arithmetic and reading as ours.

## 2. NCERT traps to handle

- **Editions are changing.** Many books are 2026-27 reprints of 2022-23 revisions, and new-curriculum books are arriving. NCERT itself put the Class 8 Social Science Vol II issued in February 2026 on hold pending a revised version. Always record the edition.
- **Some content was removed.** NCERT publishes lists of rationalised content. A topic the UPSC syllabus asks for may no longer be in the current book. Check the list and, if needed, the older edition in NCERT's textbook archive, and record that it is an older edition.
- **NCERT is not always enough.** Economics theory at optional level and most of Agriculture will need order 3 sources. An episode with no NCERT chapter says so in its ledger, and nobody forces a weak match.
- **Contested history.** Editions differ on some topics. Compare them, and treat these under the contested-history guardrail in `PLAN.md`.

## 4. How to teach: ELI5 without losing depth

**Depth is decided before writing.** Before any script, build the **depth checklist**:
- every heading and concept in the NCERT chapters
- every word the UPSC syllabus uses for this topic
- the current position, and the main debates or exceptions
- how UPSC has asked it in past papers

Every item must map to a scene. Nothing is cut to save time. If it does not fit, it moves to Part 2.

**The exam answer sits inside the payoff** (AK, 2 Nov 2026). It is how the main question gets answered, not a recap after it, so the video still stops within 30 seconds of the payoff.

**Length follows the checklist.** There is no fixed length. A topic that is too big becomes a series.

**Every concept climbs the same five rungs:**

| Rung | What happens | Skipping it means |
|---:|---|---|
| 1. A picture from daily life | Something a child has seen: a shop, a queue, a cricket match, a kitchen | Nobody follows |
| 2. The real name | Say the real term straight away, and use only that term from then on | It fails in the exam |
| 3. The real mechanism | How it actually works, step by step, each step causing the next | It is shallow |
| 4. Where it gets hard | Exceptions, limits, what the analogy gets wrong, the debate | It is misleading |
| 5. The exam-grade statement | One precise sentence at NCERT level or above, plus recall | It does not last |

**Language rules:**
1. Never drop or replace a technical term. Teach it. Writ, repo rate and isostasy all stay.
2. One idea per sentence. One new term per scene. Define a term before using it.
3. Plain short words everywhere except the term being taught.
4. Answer "why" before "what". Every fact must be the answer to a question the viewer already has.
5. Link with "but" and "so", never "and then".
6. Make big numbers feel real with an honest comparison.
7. Every analogy is labelled as an analogy, and we say where it stops being true.
8. The drawing does the teaching. Words to the ear, keywords to the eye (`STORY_ENGINE.md` T10).
9. Never talk down. Simple words, adult respect. A viewer may be a 12-year-old or a 28-year-old aspirant.

**What it sounds like.** An example of the method only. Its facts are not yet checked against sources.

> **Fails, too hard:** "Isostasy is the state of gravitational equilibrium between the crust and the mantle."
>
> **Fails, too thin:** "Mountains float on rock like boats on water!"
>
> **Passes:** "Put a big block of wood and a small one in water. The big block stands taller, and it also sinks deeper. Mountains behave a little like this. Tall land has a deep root pushing down into denser rock below. That balance has a name: isostasy. But here the picture stops being true. The rock below is not water. It is mostly solid, and it moves over very long stretches of time. So when ice melts or a mountain wears down, the land rises back slowly. Scientists explain this balance in more than one way, and the two classic models are Airy's and Pratt's..."

## 5. The gates

Every episode gets a filled gate sheet. All must pass.

| Gate | Test | Pass means |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** NCERT first | NCERT chapters read and recorded, with edition | Listed, or "no NCERT chapter" stated with what replaced it |
| **S2** Claim ledger | Every claim in the script has a source and a status | 100% confirmed. Unverified claims are off screen |
| **S3** Freshness | Every figure and law checked at the publisher during this build | Dated. Living facts recorded in `docs/upsc/living/<id>.csv` |
| **S4** Second source | Key and surprising claims have two independent sources | None missing. A source that repeats the first does not count |
| **S5** Independent audit | Someone other than the researcher opens every claim used in the script, at the cited place, and checks value, who said it, in what capacity, how strongly, and whether it is our own inference | Every used claim passes. Finding the key phrase in the file is not a pass: the pilot audit failed 22 of 178 rows after the researchers had run their own phrase checks |
| **D1** Coverage | Depth checklist mapped to scenes | 100% taught, or moved to a named next part |
| **D2** Terms kept | Every syllabus and NCERT term appears and is defined | None missing |
| **D3** Rung 4 and 5 | Each main concept reaches the hard part and the exam-grade statement | None stops at the analogy |
| **E1** Child test | Someone with no background retells the idea after watching | They can explain it in their own words. Real people for the pilots |
| **E2** Language | Term defined before use, one new term per scene, sentence length checked | No breach |
| **E3** Analogy check | Each analogy is labelled, has its limit stated, and nothing false follows from it | No breach |
| **R1** Expert review | A qualified human reads script and final video | Nothing false, nothing important missing. Jev checks wording and cannot pass this |
| **R2** Recall | Practice question, written by us, can be answered from the episode | It can |

Keep the working files in `research/upsc/<id>/`: `ncert_map.md`, `checklist.csv`, `claims.csv`, `gates.md`, and `sources/` (local only). `docs/inventors_desk/scripts/research_check.py` is the model for an automatic gate script, which gets built with the pilots.

## 6. The reference standard (AK, 3 Nov 2026)

**Goal:** for Economics, Geography, Polity and History, the channel is the default place a student goes to learn and revise
the subject. Agriculture stays in the plan behind these four.

**Complete at the subtopic level.** A serial is complete only when every one of these maps to an episode, a part of one, or a
named reason it is left out:
- every heading and subheading of every current NCERT chapter on the subject (Classes 6 to 12);
- every phrase of the UPSC syllabus for the subject (Prelims line, GS papers, optional paper);
- every idea a UPSC past paper has tested, as far as UPSC's own site provides the papers.

The map lives in `research/upsc/_map/<subject>_subtopics.csv`. A topic's parts come from its share of the map, at about
15 minutes a part. No subtopic is skipped to keep a video short: it moves to the next part.

**What makes it a reference, not just a video:**
1. **Findable.** Titles carry the syllabus words a student searches for. Chapters (YouTube timestamps) at every subtopic.
   A public index lists every syllabus phrase with the episode and timestamp that teaches it.
2. **Revisable.** Each episode ships with a one-page note (the exam card and the key terms) and two or three practice
   questions written by us. Each season ends with a revision episode built from the exam cards.
3. **In order.** Playlists follow the serial order in `SERIES.md`, so a student can start at episode 1 and finish the subject.
4. **Trustworthy.** Every claim is sourced in the description. Errors are corrected in the open (pinned comment,
   description, an Update Short when needed) and logged.
5. **Current.** Living facts are checked on the schedule in section 3 of `PLAN.md`; each video's description carries the
   date its facts were last checked.

## 7. Understand it, enjoy it, and keep it current (AK, 3 Nov 2026)

**AK:** "Even if we are able to cover one subject it should be like thoroughly. Students should understand all topics
regardless of exam, also fun to learn." And: dated topics are updated to what students need today.

**Thorough beats wide.** One subject is finished properly before the next starts. No episode is published thin to keep a
schedule.

**For understanding, not only for the exam.** The viewer is anyone who wants to understand the subject: a student, a
parent, a curious 15-year-old, an aspirant. Every episode must make sense to someone who will never sit the exam. The exam
card stays, inside the payoff, but the episode is built around understanding, not around exam phrasing.
- Start from the viewer's own life: a price they paid, a bank message on their phone, a rain they waited for.
- Every idea gets a real example from India today, sourced, and one from everyday life.
- Test of success (gate E1): after one viewing, the viewer can explain the idea to a friend in their own words and use it
  on a new example.

**Fun to learn.** Fun comes from the story and the picture, never from cutting depth or bending a fact:
- a recurring cast with personalities (the kid and the people around the kid), and humour that moves the explanation
  (the Bhagyaraj rule: delete a joke and something should be lost);
- surprising real facts and "guess first" moments before a reveal;
- small games: predict the answer, spot the mistake, what would you do;
- real stories of people and decisions behind the idea, sourced;
- running jokes and callbacks across a season, so the serial feels like a show.

**Current, not dated.** Teach the situation as it is today, checked at the publisher on the day of research: the current
Finance Commission, today's payment systems, the latest data series. Older schemes, commissions and events (an earlier
Finance Commission, demonetisation in 2016, an old plan) appear as history, with their dates, and only where they explain
today. A topic list taken from an older source is always brought up to date before research.
