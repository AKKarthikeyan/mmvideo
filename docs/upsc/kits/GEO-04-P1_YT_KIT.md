On Hawaii's Mauna Loa, which USGS calls the largest active volcano on Earth, lava usually pours out gently. In 1991, Mount Pinatubo exploded, and USGS says global temperatures fell by about half a degree Celsius over 1991 to 1993. Both are openings where melted rock reaches the surface. So why do some volcanoes ooze and others explode?

Part 1 of 2 on volcanoes. We cover what a volcano is, magma and lava, what comes out of one (NCERT's list), the switch between runny and viscous lava, NCERT's five kinds of volcano plus the cinder cone, the shapes magma leaves underground (batholith, laccolith, lapolith, phacolith, sill, dyke), and where volcanoes sit: near plate boundaries, with more than half of active volcanoes ringing the Pacific. Hawaii does not fit that map, and a hotspot explains it. Part 2 goes to Barren Island, the Deccan Traps and eruptions that changed the world's weather. For UPSC Prelims (the 2024 paper asked how many of four things come out of a volcano), GS Paper I and the Geography optional.

3. The Hawaiian islands formed at a plate boundary.

## Reply under the pinned comment (post a day later)
```
Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only.
1 is correct: magma is melted rock underground; once it erupts, it is lava (NCERT Class XI ch.3, USGS).
2 is correct: USGS says thin, runny lava lets gas escape quietly, while thick, pasty lava traps it until it bursts out.
3 is wrong: Hawaii's islands formed more than 3,200 km from the nearest plate boundary. A hotspot, a fixed hot region in the mantle, explains them (USGS This Dynamic Earth; J. Tuzo Wilson, 1963).
```
