## Description (paste as is)

```
India's Constitution has a line about bathing ghats. It says no citizen can be kept from shops, public restaurants, or public wells, tanks, bathing ghats and roads. Why would a constitution need to name a bathing ghat? And the same Constitution lets the State reserve seats and jobs for some groups. Is that a break in the promise of equality, or part of it?

Part 1 of 3 on Fundamental Rights. We start with what a right is (NCERT: a justified claim), the bill of rights in Part III, who must respect rights (the State, Article 12) and Article 13, which makes a law that takes away a Fundamental Right void to that extent. Then who holds which right (person or citizen), the equality code of Articles 14 to 18, and Article 16(4). We trace the 1992 nine-judge judgment and the creamy layer, the 50% rule, the 2019 quota for economically weaker sections (upheld 3:2 in 2022) and the 2024 seven-judge ruling on sub-classification. Part 2 covers the freedoms, and life and liberty. For UPSC Prelims, GS Paper II and the Polity optional.
```

```
Consider these statements about Fundamental Rights:
1. Equality before law in Article 14 is available to every person, not only citizens.
2. For Article 13, the word "law" includes an ordinance, a rule or a notification.
3. NCERT reads Article 16(4) as a violation of equality.
Which are correct? (a) 1 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer and reasons in the replies. Sources are in the description.
```

## Reply under the pinned comment (post a day later)
```
Answer: (b) 1 and 2 only.
1 is correct: Article 14 says "any person". Article 21 is also for every person; the six freedoms of Article 19 and Articles 15 and 16 are for citizens.
2 is correct: Article 13 takes a wide view of "law", including an ordinance, a rule or a notification.
3 is wrong: NCERT reads Article 16(4) plainly and says reservation is not a violation of equality.
```
