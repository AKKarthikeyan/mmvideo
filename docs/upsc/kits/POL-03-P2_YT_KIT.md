NCERT opens its chapter on rights with two true stories. One is about a man named Machal Lalung, held in judicial custody for fifty-four years and released in 2005. Article 21 says no one can lose life or liberty, except by procedure established by law. So can any law take your liberty, as long as it follows its own procedure?

Part 2 of 3 on Fundamental Rights. Part 1 was equality; this part is freedom. We cover the six freedoms of Article 19 and their reasonable restrictions (including the 2015 Shreya Singhal judgment on Section 66A), Article 20 for the accused, and Article 21: the 1950 Gopalan reading, then Maneka Gandhi (1978), when procedure had to be fair, just and not arbitrary. Then how Article 21 grew (privacy in 2017, education in Article 21A, livelihood, clean water and air), Article 22 on arrest and preventive detention, rights in an Emergency, and Articles 23 and 24 on exploitation. Part 3 covers religion, culture and education, and the right to move the Court. For UPSC Prelims, GS Paper II and the Polity optional.

```
Consider these statements about Fundamental Rights:
1. The six freedoms of Article 19 are available to every person, citizen or not.
2. After Maneka Gandhi (1978), the procedure under Article 21 must be right, just and fair, not arbitrary.
3. During an Emergency, the right to move the courts cannot be suspended for Articles 20 and 21.
Which are correct? (a) 1 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer and reasons in the replies. Sources are in the description.
```

## Reply under the pinned comment (post a day later)
```
Answer: (c) 2 and 3 only.
1 is wrong: Article 19 gives its six freedoms to citizens. Article 21, by contrast, is for every person.
2 is correct: the lead opinion in Maneka Gandhi (seven judges) held that the procedure must be right, just and fair, and must also pass the test of equality in Article 14.
3 is correct: the President may suspend the right to go to court for most Fundamental Rights during an Emergency, but not for Articles 20 and 21 (exception added in 1978; Constitution as on 10 Sep 2026).
```
