## What the evidence shows (and what is judgement)
Evidence:
1. The same channel's short or animated explainer beats its long lecture by 10 to 20 times (OnlyIAS Extended monsoon; Prepp - IAS),
   and short Polity explainers beat long lectures by about 25 to 100 times.
2. Hindi reaches the largest audiences by far (Khan Sir's lectures at 13 to 18 million). English reaches millions only when the
   video is a short visual explainer or case study.
3. The highest-demand topics checked: RBI and the repo rate, GDP, the monsoon, Kesavananda / Basic Structure, the Preamble,
   demand and supply. The lowest: kinds of banks, NPAs and the insolvency code (exam niche).
4. Budget explainers lose their audience within about two weeks of 1 February.

Judgement (Claude):
- Our format (short, animated, sourced) is the format that wins; the bar is set by Khan Sir in Hindi and by Priya Jain and Think
  School in short form. A Hindi track from the first episode looks necessary, not optional.
- The best-known short explainers are years old: being current (today's RBI corridor, the 16th Finance Commission, new CPI base)
  is a real edge.
- Release order should open with high-demand episodes while the playlists keep the serial order (see `docs/upsc/PLAN.md`).
