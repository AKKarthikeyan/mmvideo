# Map India's money in three languages

There is real scope for an India-focused Economics and Geography channel, but the open lane is narrower than "economics and geography for everyone": demand is large and measurable, while supply is heavy but comes in the wrong shapes. India is the number-one YouTube market for "economics explained" (about **172K searches a month from India**), "geopolitics" and "what is inflation"; Class 10 economics chapter names alone draw **44K–117K Indian searches a month**; and the reachable pool runs from **about 1.85 crore Class 10 candidates a year** to **8.2 lakh UPSC applicants** inside a YouTube audience of roughly **500 million**. Against that, exam brands (StudyIQ about 20.5M subscribers, Drishti about 12.8M, Mrunal Patel about 2.05M) sell lectures and summaries that lead into paid batches, school mega-channels teach on whiteboards from textbooks NCERT replaced in 2026-27, and since mid-2026 brokerage-funded English channels have proved that place-based Indian economics breaks out (Angel One's "Karnataka vs Tamil Nadu" reached **1.24M views on a 30.5K-subscriber channel**). What remains open is Hindi and Tamil data-and-map explainers, economic geography that links physical causes to economic results, infrastructure measured from satellite imagery, the under-covered East and North-East, and the new NCERT syllabus, all told neutrally with sources on screen. AK's engine fits this lane closely: its satellite before/after, terrain flyover, flow-map and 3D state-prism templates, its Hindi and Tamil audio-track pipeline and its source-on-screen house rule answer exactly the credibility gap the political explainers leave. Money is the hard part, because at **₹40–160 per 1,000 long-form views** ads cannot pay for premium visuals, so sponsors (roughly ₹1–6 lakh per integration at 100K–500K subscribers, with SEBI limiting broker money) and owned products must carry the business, while the most serious legal exposure is India's map law rather than SEBI. The recommended launch is a separate channel from Sunday 15 Nov 2026 with one Sunday flagship and one Wednesday syllabus explainer a week, timed to RBI's December policy, the GDP back series, Budget 2027 and board exams; because vidIQ credits ran out, Reddit was unreachable and many pages could not be opened, much of the evidence rests on search summaries and third-party trackers, and the verification list at the end should be cleared before any spending.

## Exam and school giants own the lecture; nobody owns the map

### Mrunal's model teaches the syllabus, and the free part feeds the batch

Mrunal Patel is the benchmark for free Indian economy teaching, with about **2.05M subscribers, 243.4M views and 1,226 videos by April 2026**, or roughly 198K lifetime views per video ([youtubers.me](https://us.youtubers.me/mrunal-patel/youtuber-stats)). The mrunal.org/economy page itself could not be opened, so its structure was rebuilt from indexed sub-pages: a curriculum of numbered "Pillars", a yearly "Win" update series running from Win21 to Win26, slide-driven Hinglish classes and downloadable handouts. Win26 states plainly that it exists "for ex-learners… so they don't have to buy course again" ([mrunal.org/win26](https://mrunal.org/win26)), the free Budget 2026 videos are 13–16-minute summaries ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=730amx3umts)), and the full course sits in paid Unacademy batches such as PCB16, which runs 16 Jul–14 Dec 2026 with 63 lessons ([Unacademy](https://unacademy.com/course/mrunals-economy-for-upsc-prelims-20272829-pcb16-powerhouse/475XLPXS)) and was advertised at **₹16,999** around late 2025 ([Telegram](https://t.me/s/Mrunalorg)). Because the site labels its material for State PSC, SSC, IBPS, CAPF and MBA interviews, one economy curriculum serves many exams at once ([mrunal.org/download](https://mrunal.org/download)).

Three weaknesses mark out the space a newcomer can take. Learners call his lectures "sufficient for only prelims" and say the Economic Survey and Budget must be read separately for Mains ([Quora](https://www.quora.com/Are-Mrunal-lectures-sufficient-and-very-good-for-economics-for-UPSC-preparation)); his geography output is effectively dormant, with only an old G1/G2 series still cited ([NetMock](https://netmock.com/best-youtube-channels-for-upsc-aspirants/)); and his platform is in flux, since upGrad closed its takeover of Unacademy on 1 Sep 2026 at more than 90% below the 2021 peak valuation ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/upgrad-completes-unacademy-acquisition-at-206m-valuation/)). Mrunal himself wrote that his YouTube earnings "have declined greatly" when he moved teaching behind Unacademy in 2018 ([mrunal.org](https://mrunal.org/2018/12/economy-upsc-2019.html)), an early sign that free educator video pays poorly on its own. The lesson is to borrow his free, visibly structured curriculum, downloadable source sheets, yearly "data reset" series and multi-exam labelling, while dropping the 1.5-hour slide talk and the free fragments that only make sense alongside a paid course.

### General-audience explainers are political or business stories, and brokerages just moved in

The largest general explainers are built around a personality and cover politics or business, not India's economic geography: Dhruv Rathee has 33.2M subscribers ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@dhruvrathee/index.md)), Think School about 6.2–6.6M while still adding about 180K a month ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/thinkschool); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@thinkschool/)), and Abhi and Niyu 6.69M after losing 10K in 30 days ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@abhiandniyu/)). ## Demand arrives as chapter names, rivalries and "explained" searches

### Students search the textbook, common people search the concept

The vidIQ keyword pull for India shows three distinct demand shapes, measured as estimated monthly YouTube searches from India, a unit inferred rather than documented by vidIQ ([vidIQ, IN, 29 Oct 2026](https://app.vidiq.com/)). Students type the NCERT chapter name plus the class, so "money and credit class 10 economics" draws about **117K**, "globalisation and the indian economy class 10 cbse" 77K, "development class 10 economics" 63K, "manufacturing industries class 10 geography" 56K and "sectors of indian economy class 10" 44K; exact long phrases often fall below the floor, so "physical features of india" barely registers while "physiography of india" draws about 9K at a moderate competition score of 45. Common viewers type "explained" or "what is", giving "economics explained" about 172K, "geopolitics" about 105K and "inflation explained" about 20K at a competition score of only 47, with India the top market for several of these. News-pegged terms spike and fade, as "repo rate" rose 269% while "budget 2026" had fallen 24.5% by October. Map terms carry the lowest competition in the dataset ("india map" about 33K at 32, "bharat ka naksha" at 21), and creator brands dwarf topic searches ("pw" about 465K, "drishti ias" about 330K), so a new channel will be found through topics, chapters and recommendations rather than through people searching its name.

### Breakout videos show which topics win, at every channel size

Outlier videos, which far exceed their channel's usual views, point the same way regardless of channel size. Next Infra, with just 4.7K subscribers, drew 310K views on the Dedicated Freight Corridor ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rFXKTUvfV_g)); upGrad's Guwahati video reached 264.7K, 102 times its usual ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RQPkuDnodqs)); The Hindu's monsoon explainer drew 279K ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhaKvWls4DY)); a Vidarbha statehood video drew 365.8K in four days ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q1IrsvCE184)); and BBC Hindi's video on the Himalaya and the economy reached **2.9M** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uWYyxOmGlfg)). Rivalry pairs pull unusual engagement, as Telangana vs Andhra Pradesh drew 2,628 comments on 564K views, about 4.7 per 1,000 (derived) ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-YwZYLTmk_c)). Regional pride is a growth engine, and it maps directly onto Tamil and Hindi audio tracks.

The common-person case rests on a literacy gap as well as curiosity. Only about **24% of Indian adults were financially literate** in S&P's survey ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/76-percentage-indian-adults-financially-illiterate-survey/44508.html)) and about 27% in NCFE's 2019 round ([NCFE](https://old.ncfe.org.in/images/pdfs/reports/NCFE%202019_Final_Report.pdf)), while YouTube is the top online news source for **55%** of Indian respondents in the Reuters Digital News Report 2025 ([Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/07/01/ai-influencers-and-a-public-thats-losing-interest-the-big-challenges-for-indian-media)). Demand also follows a fixed calendar: the Economic Survey at the end of January and the Union Budget on 1 February ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/policy/monthly-policy-review/january-2026)), board exams from mid-February, UPSC Prelims in late May, and RBI policy every two months, with the next meetings on **2–4 Dec 2026 and 3–5 Feb 2027** ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=62422)). On top of that, 2026-27 is a reset year for Indian data. MoSPI moved the GDP base to 2022-23 on 27 Feb 2026 with a back series due by December 2026 ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/FaqDetails.aspx?NoteId=157582)), Census 2027 population counting begins in February 2027 ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/centre-notifies-april-september-2026-window-for-census-2027-houselisting/)), and NFHS-6 factsheets for 715 districts arrived in May 2026 ([AIR News](https://newsonair.gov.in/nfhs-6-fact-sheets-mark-significant-milestone-in-indias-evolving-health-and-statistical-ecosystem/)), so every incumbent's data-dependent back catalogue is going stale at the same time.

## Seven gaps, ranked by how well AK's engine fills them

The gap is no longer "nobody explains Indian state economics", because brokerages closed that in English during 2026; what is still open shows up across all four axes. On topic, the open ground is economic geography that connects physical causes to economic results, such as the monsoon to kharif output to rural demand, the Western Ghats to rainfall to plantations and hydropower, or the coal belt to poverty: the physical half exists as UPSC mapping videos and the economic half as brokerage videos, but nobody joins them visually. On format, the open slot is an 8–15-minute animated map-and-data explainer with sources on screen, since exam incumbents make 3–11-hour Hindi one-shots that reach 13.7K–165.6K views ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FwADikr7PWU)) while Base IAS's 6–11-minute map videos reach 119.7K–978K. On audience, the open viewer is the one not preparing for any exam, because almost every big player is optimised to sell a course or win board marks. On language, Hindi is the largest hole, as the outlier pool for "भारत की अर्थव्यवस्था" ("India's economy") is dominated by 2–7-minute TV news clips and exam marathons ([vidIQ outliers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GNmh8ePe3fQ)).

Gap 1 ranks first because the asset already exists and no competitor shows measured before/after imagery. Gap 3 is the largest audience prize but collides with the one structural limit in AK's pipeline, discussed in the next section. Gap 5 is the most time-bound, since explainers built on the new GDP base, the back series and the first Census 2027 figures will become reference assets for UPSC, State PSC, RBI-exam, undergraduate and school viewers together, and the window to publish them first runs roughly from December 2026 to 2028. Two alternatives deserve a hearing. A Shorts-first channel would grow fastest, as XploreMap Nitish shows, but as the money section shows it would earn almost nothing.

## Hindi carries the audience, English carries the rupees

### English master, human-checked Hindi and Tamil tracks, and pictures light on text

Language preference is lopsided toward Indian languages. A Google report found **54% of Indian viewers preferred Hindi videos against 16% for English** ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/1-in-3-indians-watch-online-videos-usually-in-hindi-says-google)), 93% prefer Indian-language content ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/93-youtube-viewers-watch-indian-language-content-mindshare-e4m-content-marketing-report-134060.html)), though both figures date from 2019–20, and 98% of internet users consume some Indic-language content ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/india-to-exceed-900mn-internet-users-by-2025-125011600669_1.html)). Money runs the other way, since premium ad rates go to English content with urban, high-income or overseas audiences ([Upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-india-guide-2026/)) and Indian-made content drew about **45 billion hours** of watch time from outside India in a year ([Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/youtubes-india-bet-inr-21000-crore-paid-out-to-indian-creators-commits-inr-850-crores-to-power-indias-creator-nation/)). AK's pipeline already sits between the two, keeping English as the original audio and adding Tamil and Hindi as extra tracks on the same video (`/home/user/mmvideo/docs/AUDIO_TRACKS.md`). YouTube says creators using multi-language audio get **more than 25% of watch time from non-primary languages**, a global claim with no India split ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/multi-language-audio/)). The feature is limited to "a subset of creators with access to Advanced features", and any auto-dub must be deleted before uploading your own track ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en)), while AK's native-speaker approval step avoids the backlash YouTube met over default AI dubs ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-youtube-ai-dubbing-backlash-default-setting/)).

The constraint is that the pipeline never re-renders the picture, so on-screen labels, sources and chapter cards stay in English and Hindi-medium or Tamil-medium students hear their language while reading English. The fix is to design this channel's visuals light on text, letting numerals, maps, arrows and colour carry the story and reserving English text for sources and proper nouns; to add Hindi and Tamil titles and descriptions in Studio's Languages tab so the tracks can be discovered; to favour topics where Tamil and Hindi audiences already have a stake, such as Tamil Nadu's November monsoon, Uttar Pradesh vs Bihar or the Ganga plain; and to write the Hindi track in a Hinglish register that keeps searched English terms such as "repo rate" and "GDP". At day 90, a Hindi track above about 30% of watch time would justify rendering Hindi-picture versions of the best flagships for a dedicated Hindi channel, following Dhruv Rathee's separate-channel model, while a share under 10% would keep tracks as an add-on. Expansion beyond these two languages needs new tooling, because Telugu and Malayalam are on hold in the pipeline after MiniMax returned unreliable timing (`AUDIO_TRACKS.md`); Sarvam's Indian-language dubbing ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/text-to-speech/dubbing)) or human voice-over would unlock Telugu, Kannada, Bengali and Marathi, where no data-driven economy or geography explainer of scale was found.

### Long-form is the product, Shorts are the doorway, and one-shots are a trap

The winning English state and city videos run 5–21 minutes, with Uplearn's at 5–14 and Angel One's at 11–21 ([Angel One](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC0NC2T0Tmb-wlPYzzVNgZpg); [Uplearn](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCeQ8yzZhjJ7svzXBqYzmyMA)), and longer documentaries still work when the topic is strong, as Purpose Over Noise's 23-minute megaprojects video passed **1.2M views** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HUQzGu-39U4)). Connected TV rewards this family-screen documentary format, with YouTube reaching **75M+ Indian adults on TV screens** in April 2025 ([Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/brandcast-2025-ctv-shorts-and-youtube-as-indias-new-tv/)). Shorts are the discovery surface, as short video reaches about **588M Indians** ([Tele.net on IAMAI-Kantar](https://tele.net.in/indias-internet-user-base-crosses-950-million-in-2025-as-per-iamai-report/)) and YouTube Shorts reportedly has 650M+ monthly logged-in viewers in India ([Revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/youtube-shorts-statistics)), and map "reveal" moments travel well alone, a 2-minute bar-chart race of state GDP drawing 81.4K views on a 13.5K-subscriber channel ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=29ZdnwFtETE)). Even Think School takes roughly two-thirds of its views from long-form ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/thinkschool)). Multi-hour one-shots belong to the exam incumbents, and podcast-style talks in this niche earn small multiples, as NSE's Sanjeev Sanyal talk drew 15.1K ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jbev0UYKwig)).

### A separate channel beats folding this into Moat & Margin

The channel should stand on its own, cross-promoted from Moat & Margin, and AK's template catalogue already assigns the terrain, wind-flow, satellite and 3D state-map samples to a brand called "Indica Atlas" (`/home/user/mmvideo/hyperframes/README.md`), which is the natural home. The audience of students and common people differs from Moat & Margin's business-and-markets viewers, and mixing them muddies recommendation signals; Uplearn's stock breakdowns drawing 1–8K next to its 124K–596K state videos shows how sharply company content and place content split audiences; and keeping company and stock analysis on Moat & Margin holds the new channel well clear of SEBI's securities zone. The counter-argument is that an existing channel already has monetisation and possibly multi-language audio access, so the new channel should be opened and verified now and its access to multi-language audio checked before launch, with English-only publishing and tracks added later as the fallback, since the pipeline is built to add tracks to existing videos.

## At ₹40–160 per thousand views, sponsors and products must pay

YouTube passes 55% of long-form ad revenue to creators and 45% of a pooled allocation for Shorts ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/were-proud-of-how-quickly-youtube-shorts-has-become-popular-in-india-123129.html)). Unaudited agency estimates put Indian education revenue per 1,000 views (RPM) at **₹40–160** and finance-adjacent content at **₹100–450**, with Q4 rates running 40–80% above Q1 ([Upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-india/)). India's median CPM, what advertisers pay per 1,000 impressions, is about $0.70–0.83 against $10.26 in the US ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/youtube-income-per-1-lakh-views/)), so the share of views from the US, UK, Canada and Australia moves revenue more than language does. Shorts estimates vary more than tenfold, from under ₹1 to about ₹30 per 1,000 views ([Boss Wallah](https://bosswallah.com/blog/creator-hub/youtube-shorts-vs-long-videos-which-earns-a-better-rpm-for-indian-creators-in-2026/)). In rupees (derived), 1M long-form views a month from a mostly Indian audience earns about **₹0.4–1.5 lakh**, the same views as Shorts earn ₹1,000–15,000, The Graphic Earth's lifetime of roughly 45.5M views would be worth only about ₹18–68 lakh at Indian rates, and every ₹1 lakh of production cost per video needs **0.67M–2.5M long-form views** to recover from ads alone.

Sponsorship is where money becomes meaningful, and SEBI shapes who can pay. India's influencer-marketing spend is about **₹3,000–3,500 crore** (Kofluence, 2025), with banking, financial services and insurance at 10–12% ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/creator-economy-in-india-hits-rs-3500-crore-fueled-by-ecomm-fmcg-sectors-kofluence-73457.htm)), yet only **8–10% of Indian creators monetise effectively** and brand money makes up about 90% of creator revenue ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-from-content-to-commerce-mapping-indias-creator-economy)). Agency rate cards price a 60–90-second integration on a 500K-subscriber finance or tech channel at **₹2.5–6 lakh** and a dedicated video at ₹8–20 lakh ([Upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/?p=48753)), while paid partnerships for small accounts historically started at about ₹25,000 per video ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/rs5-lakh-for-a-post-finance-influencers-aka-finfluencers-purses-swell-as-content-demand-rises-2973.htm)). Finance-influencer deal rates reportedly fell 40–60% after SEBI's crackdown ([Equentis](https://www.equentis.com/blog/financial-influencers-face-40-60-decline-in-brand-deals-amid-sebi-crackdown/)), and brokers and fund houses may now work only with creators "strictly involved in investor education" ([afaqs](https://afaqs.com/news/influencer-marketing/sebi-finfluencer-clampdown-likely-to-force-them-to-find-alternate-revenue-streams)). That leaves Zerodha, Upstox and Angel One as both competitors and possible sponsors with extra paperwork, while non-SEBI brands are easier to sign: atlas and guidebook publishers (Oswaal already sponsors Magnet Brains), edtech, software, travel and insurers, who answer to IRDAI rather than SEBI. Paid government-scheme promotion, such as a Bihar scheme reportedly paying ₹10,000 per lakh views ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/tags/bihar-creator-partnerships.html)), should be refused because it undercuts the neutrality that is the brand.

Products are where Indian education creators built real businesses. Finshots turned newsletter trust into Ditto Insurance, which drew about 70% of its buyers from Finshots ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2022/01/zerodha-backed-startup-finshots-ditto-insurance/amp)) and reached **₹97.1 crore revenue in FY25** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/ditto-insurance/financials/)), and Mrunal converts free video into ₹16,999 batches. Ankur Warikoo's courses passed ₹100 crore in sales before he shut them in May 2026, answering "Huge" when asked about AI's role ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/ankur-warikoo-shuts-down-rs-100-crore-courses-business-after-five-year-run-154706.html)), which marks generic recorded courses as fragile. For this channel the durable layers are a newsletter as an owned audience, exam-linked map and data packs (free source sheets first in Mrunal's handout style, then a paid "atlas" for UPSC, State PSC and Class 10), and licensing the engine's rendered maps to coaching institutes, schools and media.

## The map, not SEBI, is the legal risk that can sink this channel

Map law is the gravest exposure for a map-heavy channel. Publishing a map of India that does not conform to SoI maps is an offence under s.2(2) of the Criminal Law Amendment Act, 1961, punishable by up to 6 months in jail or a fine on a government complaint, and questioning India's frontiers by "visible representation" in a way that harms security carries up to 3 years under s.2(1) ([India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/6798?locale=hi)). Enforcement has been real: FIRs against Twitter India's head over a map showing J&K and Ladakh outside India ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/998764/fir-against-twitter-india-head-in-up-for-displaying-map-with-j-k-ladakh-outside-india)), an SoI notice over a Play Store quiz app ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2023/07/223-survey-of-india-google-playstore-app-inaccurate-map-india/)) and a reported SoI warning to Anthropic over dotted borders and omitted islands ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/india-objects-to-anthropic-over-map-depicting-indian-borders-incorrectly/tldr)). The practical problem in AK's engine is specific: the India samples (s06 wind, s15 state prisms) use `geojson/india.geojson` from github.com/udit-001/india-maps-data, which mixes 34 whole-state outlines with 726 district outlines (`hyperframes/README.md`) and has not been checked against SoI boundary data, which DST says "shall be used as standard for publishing political maps of India" ([DST](https://geospatial.dst.gov.in/IndianEntityTermsandCondition.aspx)). Until that check is done, every frame showing India's outline, including globe spins and thumbnails, is a liability.

SEBI is manageable as long as the channel stays in education. Since August 2024 SEBI-regulated firms may not associate with anyone giving securities advice or claiming performance without registration ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/09/223-sebi-regulated-firms-barred-from-collaborating-with-unregistered-finfluencers/)), and educators may use listed-company price data only with a **30-day lag from 1 Jul 2026** ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/amp/securities-law/sebi/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-revises-norms-sharing-and-usage-market-price-data-educational-purposes-533509)). The Common Advertisement Code notified on 8 Oct 2026 limits "celebrity" endorsements by regulated firms to brand-level promotion with prior approval ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/sebi-relaxes-advertising-norms-keeps-celebrity-endorsements-under-prior-approval-158597.html)); the June draft would have treated anyone with more than 5 lakh followers on one platform as a celebrity, and whether that threshold survived is unconfirmed ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/500000-followers-sebi-wants-to-classify-you-as-a-celebrity-for-financial-ads-539216-2026-06-25)). The ceiling for getting it wrong is high, as SEBI impounded **₹546 crore** from one trading academy ([Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/securities/1726258/sebis-crackdown-on-finfluencers-regulations-and-enforcement)).

## A 90-day launch built on one weekly flagship

### Positioning, four series and a fixed two-slot week

The positioning line is *India's economy, on the map: every number sourced on screen, no stock tips, no party politics, in English, Hindi and Tamil*, a sentence that is both the brand and the compliance policy and that fills the credibility gap left by political explainers and unsourced AI channels. Four series carry it. "Built from Space" shows satellite before/after of infrastructure; "Rival States" runs data-led head-to-heads under a name deliberately distinct from Angel One's "State of States"; "Why Here?" links physical geography to economic results; and "NCERT in Maps" takes syllabus chapters, titled with the exact chapter name and class. A fifth strand, "Data Day", publishes event explainers within 24–48 hours of RBI decisions, MoSPI releases, the Economic Survey and the Budget. The week has a 10–16-minute Sunday flagship on Angel One's proven slot, a 6–10-minute Wednesday syllabus or search explainer at a fixed 7 pm IST in Uplearn's pattern, and three to four Shorts cut from each long video's "reveal" moment, with every episode built as one master carrying three audio tracks and the mix held near Think School's reported 60% evergreen to 35% news-pegged ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/thinkschool)). Launch should fall on **Sunday 15 Nov 2026**, leaving 2.5 weeks for the SoI boundary fix, channel verification, a check of multi-language audio access and four videos finished in advance.

### Growth comes from exact titles, numbered series and regional pride

Sunday titles should pose a rivalry or a "why here" question, while Wednesday titles should use the exact searched phrase ("Money and Credit Class 10 Economics", "economy for upsc", "physical features of india class 9"), and abstract macro titles should wait until the channel has an audience, given that Angel One's AI, inflation and UPI videos stalled at 1.6K–9.7K. Every series should be numbered, playlists organised both by syllabus and by franchise, and every video given chapters, which require a first timestamp at 00:00, at least three timestamps and Advanced features ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579?hl=en-GB)), with each chapter card printing its data source. New channels draw early traffic mainly from search because recommendations need watch history ([ytgrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-traffic-sources)), which makes the Wednesday syllabus lane the channel's starter motor. Under-covered regions should come early, rivalry episodes should carry a pinned sources comment with a public invitation to correct, Moat & Margin should promote the launch through end screens and community posts, and teachers should get free map stills with attribution, which also helps the channel's videos surface in other creators' playlists.

## What this evidence can carry, and what to verify first

The research ran into four limits that cap its confidence. vidIQ ran out of credits after roughly 10 keyword calls and 8 outlier calls, so there are no Hindi or Tamil keyword volumes, Shorts outlier data or channel growth curves. Many pages could not be opened, including mrunal.org/economy itself, YouTube, Social Blade and regulators' primary texts, so most figures come from search-engine summaries or third-party trackers with differing snapshot dates; Think School's subscriber count, for instance, ranges from 5.3M to 6.6M across sources. Reddit was unreachable, leaving audience sentiment to rest on Quora answers six to nine years old, and the shared web-search budget ran out before Kannada, Bengali, Telugu and Gujarati exam supply, YouTube's advertiser guidelines for political topics and several licence pages could be checked.

The India boundary must be fixed against SoI data first, because it is the only item that can cause criminal exposure; multi-language audio on the new channel comes next, since the language strategy depends on it; then Hindi and Tamil keyword research, the final Common Advertisement Code text, a benchmark of Moat & Margin's own revenue per view and audience geography, and finally the size of Dhruv Rathee's dubbed channels, which is the only real-world test of separate-language channels in this genre.

## Conclusion

The opportunity is time-boxed in a way the incumbents have not acted on. The 2022-23 GDP base and its back series, NFHS-6 districts, Census 2027 and NCERT's integrated Class 9 book all land between February 2026 and 2028, so the existing lecture libraries go stale in the same window, and the first channel to publish clear, sourced, map-led versions will set the reference that students, aspirants and ordinary viewers return to. The brokerages that proved state economics breaks out look more like future sponsors than rivals: they publish only in English, their stock content underperforms, and SEBI's association rules steer their money toward exactly the education-only creator this channel would be.

The durable moat is discipline rather than design. SoI-conformant maps, a source on every frame, a public corrections log and declared synthetic audio are things that AI-generated map channels and partisan explainers cannot credibly copy, and they double as legal protection in maps, the one area where a slip costs most. The single structural mismatch is that AK's pipeline keeps the picture in English while the largest audience gap is Hindi, and text-light visuals plus a measured day-90 decision on Hindi-picture renders resolve it without rebuilding anything.
