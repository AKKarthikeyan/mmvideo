You are a market researcher for a new YouTube channel. The channel will teach Indian Economics, Geography and Polity
in depth, mapped to the UPSC Civil Services syllabus and NCERT, as short animated cartoon explainers (about 10-15 minutes,
plus Shorts), every claim sourced, kept up to date, for students and anyone curious. Find out what already exists and
where the real gaps are. Use web search and YouTube. Today's date is in your system; use it.

RULES
- Every channel, video and number you report must come from a page you opened: give the URL and the date you saw it.
- Subscriber and view counts change: give them as seen, with the date. Mark anything estimated as "estimate".
- Never invent a channel, video, number or quote. If you cannot find something, say NOT FOUND.
- Viewer complaints and requests must come from real comments, Reddit or Quora threads you opened (link them),
  quoted in 15 words or fewer.

QUESTIONS
1. The main YouTube channels that teach UPSC Economy, Geography and Polity (English and Hindi; also any Tamil, Telugu,
   Bengali, Marathi): channel name, URL, subscribers, language, format (live lecture, slides, whiteboard, animation,

   whether they cite sources on screen or in the description. At least 15 channels.
2. Animated or illustrated explainers on these subjects for Indian audiences (UPSC or general, for example channels

4. What students say they struggle with or wish existed: unclear topics, too-long lectures, outdated content, errors,
   no sources, no Hindi, no practice. Quote real comments and threads (with links).
5. Language: how big is Hindi demand compared with English for these subjects (for example comparable videos on the
   same topic in each language and their views)?
6. Formats that perform: long lectures versus 10-20 minute explainers versus Shorts. Give real examples with views,
   subscriber counts and upload dates.
7. Monetisation and policy risks for this kind of channel: YouTube's rules on AI voices and "repetitive or mass-produced"
   content, educational content guidelines. Link YouTube's own policy pages.
8. Your conclusion: the three to five biggest gaps a new channel could fill, and the three biggest risks. Separate
   what the evidence shows from your own judgement.
