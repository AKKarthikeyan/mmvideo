# Asian Paints and its dealers: still a moat, now that a rival is copying it?

**One-line answer:** Asian Paints says its dealer network is "one of our strongest competitive advantages" [C004], but the same report shows it sold to 2,920 fewer dealers and distributors than a year earlier [C001, C033], and a rival says it has built 50,000+ dealers in under two years [C012]. The documents we read show a contest over the dealer, not a verdict on who wins it.

*Draft for AK. Claim IDs in brackets are for checking; strip them in any publish copy. Every Birla Opus figure is Grasim describing itself. Nothing here is independent measurement.*

## Why it matters now

Asian Paints' chairman calls the year just ended one of "unprecedented competitive intensity" [C005]. Birla Opus, the paints business of Grasim, says it crossed 10% revenue market share in March 2026 and became the third-largest decorative paints brand in India in under two years [C016, C017]. Asian Paints' own risk table names the dealer as the thing it protects against competition [C008]. And a competition regulator has been asked to look at how dealers are treated [C028, C030]. So the question "is the dealer network still a moat?" is live, and the honest answer depends on what each side says, what a court recorded, and what nobody has yet measured.

## The story

### 1. A business that runs through shopkeepers
Asian Paints sold 99.0% of its goods in FY 2025-26 to dealers and distributors [C002]. The top ten of them together took 0.9% of that [C002]. Put plainly: less than one rupee in every hundred came from the ten biggest customers [C002]. Our reading is that this means there is no handful of big dealers to sign up (inference, not the report's statement). A rival has to win shop by shop.

But does the company think that wall is safe? Not quite, therefore its own words matter next.

### 2. "One of our strongest competitive advantages"
Asian Paints' MD and CEO writes that the company added nearly 6,000 new retail touchpoints in the year, and that "the overall network strength remains one of our strongest competitive advantages" [C003, C004]. The report also says channel partnerships are "one of the Company's enduring strengths" [C007]. Nearly 6,000 over a year is about 16 a day (our arithmetic on C003). The report does not say what a "touchpoint" is or give a total [C003].

But the same report holds a number that does not sit easily beside that confidence, therefore the next section.

### 3. The count that fell
In its sustainability table, Asian Paints reports selling to 72,873 dealers and distributors in FY 2025-26, against 75,793 the year before [C001]. That is 2,920 fewer, about 3.9% [C033], or roughly one in every 26 (our arithmetic on C033). The table is auditor-assured, which is stronger than a bare company claim [C001]. Two cautions. First, it counts customers who bought in the year, not shops that stock the paint, so it must not be set against "touchpoints" [C001, C003]. Second, the report gives no reason for the fall, and we cannot say these dealers went to a rival [C033].

But a falling count is only half the picture, because the company says it is also defending the counter, therefore the next section.

### 4. Defending the counter
The chairman lists "systematically enhance our counter share across all geographies" as a pillar of the year and says the company made "a concerted push" on it [C006]. "Counter share" is the report's phrase and is not defined or measured there [C006]. In the competition-risk row, the stated response is "prioritised long-term dealer partnerships, with a focus on enabling their growth and sustaining profitability" [C008]. Asian Paints says it also upgraded the tinting machines and software it offers dealers, with no count given [C009], and added over 500 Colourideas and Colour Cube stores [C010]. (Plain-words explainer, not a sourced claim: tinting is where the shop mixes colour into base paint to match the customer's pick.)

But a machine on the counter is something a rival can also supply, therefore the entrant's side.

### 5. The entrant builds its own counter
Grasim says Birla Opus now has over 50,000 dealers in more than 11,500 towns, supported by 146 depots [C012]. Its dealers are "armed with new-age compact tinting machines" [C013]. Grasim says it made them smaller because dealers needed more retail space, to "free up valuable floor space" [C014], and describes a machine with 2,320+ shades linked to central software [C015]. By its own chart, towns covered went from 6,679 to 11,500, up 72% [C020]; that is about 4,800 more towns (our arithmetic on C020). It says it has 1,200+ franchised stores [C022], about 4.5 lakh contractors and painters [C023], and a three-year dealer loyalty programme with trips abroad for top dealers [C021]. It says it "continues to invest aggressively behind consumer awareness and channel engagement" [C024].

Two limits. Grasim's report gives no count of tinting machines [C013], so any "45,000 machines" figure is unsupported. And "dealers" at Grasim and "dealers/distributors sold to" at Asian Paints are defined differently, so 50,000 against 72,873 is not a like-for-like ratio [C012, C001].

But even the entrant's own report concedes where the contest is decided, therefore the next section.

### 6. What the challenger says it needs
Grasim's outlook says the new competitive dynamics will be most beneficial for "a challenger with brand strength, capacity, and distribution depth" [C025]. It calls Birla Opus the second most widely distributed decorative paints brand in India [C018]. So the entrant itself lists distribution depth as a requirement [C025]. Our reading: the entrant presents it as built, the incumbent claims it as its strength [C012, C004], and no source we opened measures who is ahead.

But the fight has also gone to a regulator, therefore the last section.

### 7. The regulator, and what it has and has not said
The judgment records that Grasim made a representation to the CCI in December 2024 alleging that Asian Paints abused its dominance, including by "directing its dealers to return / not use or not install the tinting machines supplied by" Grasim [C028]. It also records an allegation of offering dealers foreign travel and other incentives for exclusivity, and of threatening dealers against stocking Grasim's paints [C029]. These are allegations. The CCI ordered an investigation, and on 11 September 2025 the Bombay High Court dismissed Asian Paints' petition against that order [C027, C030]. The court quotes the Supreme Court that such an order is "merely a prima facie opinion directing the Director General to carry the investigation" [C031]. No finding of wrongdoing appears in what we read [C031]. An earlier JSW complaint of the same kind was investigated and dismissed by the CCI in 2022, though the court notes the sections invoked by Grasim differ [C032]. That precedent does not predict this outcome.

Therefore, the supportable position is narrow: both companies treat the dealer's counter as the prize [C008, C013, C024], the incumbent's own count dipped [C033], and whether dealers actually moved is unmeasured.

## What we don't know

- **Is the network really shrinking?** The 72,873 vs 75,793 fall is real in the table [C001], but the report gives no reason [C033] and "touchpoints" rose by nearly 6,000 on a different, undefined measure [C003]. The two cannot be netted.
- **Whose network is bigger?** Different definitions of "dealer" [C012, C001]. Asian Paints gives no store total, so Grasim's "largest organised retail footprint" claim cannot be tested [C022, C010].
- **Tinting-machine counts:** neither report gives one [C009, C013]. The popular 45,000 figure is unsupported.
- **Dealer behaviour:** margins, credit, shelf space, and whether volume is moving are in none of the three sources we opened. Not a gap we can fill from the ledger.
- **Market shares:** only Grasim's own claim [C016]. Asian Paints' report gives none, and the competing popular figures are unverified.
- **The CCI case:** we have the court's account only [C030]. The CCI's own order, Asian Paints' reply and any Director General report were not opened.
- **The contractor figure:** the "nearly 4.5 lakh" count is confirmed in Grasim's report, but the chart beside it shows a growth badge that does not match its own two values [C023]. We state no growth rate.
- **Is Shorts-mode research enough?** AK to decide. Official earnings-call audio is the likeliest source of machine counts and dealer commentary.
