## Verdict on the premise
What the documents support is narrower than "moat" or "not a moat". Asian Paints says its network strength is "one of our strongest competitive advantages" [C004] and lists long-term dealer partnerships among its answers to competition [C008]. The same report shows the number of dealers/distributors it sold to fell from 75,793 to 72,873 [C001, C033]. The entrant says it built 50,000+ dealers in 11,500+ towns with compact tinting machines in under two years [C012, C013, C017], and its own outlook names "distribution depth" as what a challenger needs [C025]. Whether dealers actually move volume to the entrant is not in any source we opened (open_questions.md 3-4). "Moat" is our word; none of the three sources uses it about Asian Paints' network.

## The real story, in plain words
1. Asian Paints sells almost entirely through dealers and distributors: 99.0% of its sales in FY 2025-26 [C002]. The top 10 together took 0.9% of that [C002], which our reading takes to mean there is no handful of big dealers to buy (inference, not a source's statement).
2. Its management calls the network a strength and says it added nearly 6,000 retail touchpoints in the year [C003, C004, C007]. Its chairman says the year had "unprecedented competitive intensity" and that the company made "a concerted push to enhance our counter share" [C005, C006].
3. In its own risk table, the competition risk is answered with "Prioritised long-term dealer partnerships" [C008]. The dealer is the contested asset in the company's own words.
4. Tinting machines are part of the dealer offer on both sides. Asian Paints says it improved its tinting machines and software for dealers, with no count given [C009]. Grasim says Birla Opus dealers have "new-age compact tinting machines", a smaller machine built because dealers needed floor space, and a machine with 2,320+ shades linked to central software [C013, C014, C015].
5. The entrant's scale, by its own account: 50,000+ dealers, 146 depots, 11,500+ towns (up from 6,679 a year earlier), 1,200+ franchised stores, and about 4.5 lakh contractors [C012, C020, C022, C023]. It says it passed 10% revenue market share in March 2026 and is third-largest [C016, C017]. It says it "continues to invest aggressively behind consumer awareness and channel engagement" and runs a dealer loyalty programme with foreign trips for top dealers [C024, C021].
6. Grasim itself writes that the new competitive dynamics favour "a challenger with brand strength, capacity, and distribution depth" [C025].
7. The fight has reached a regulator. Grasim told the CCI in December 2024 that Asian Paints abused dominance, including by directing dealers to return or not use or not install the entrant's tinting machines, and by offering dealers foreign travel for exclusivity [C028, C029]. The CCI ordered an investigation on 1 July 2025; the Bombay High Court dismissed Asian Paints' petition against that order on 11 September 2025 [C027, C030]. The court quotes that such an order is "merely a prima facie opinion" [C031]. An earlier JSW complaint was investigated and dismissed in 2022 [C032].

## How the mechanism works (as the sources describe it)
Both companies describe tinting machines and software as something the dealer uses [C009, C013]. Grasim says dealers needed more retail space, which is why it built a smaller machine [C014]. Both sides therefore compete for the same dealer and counter: Asian Paints through dealer partnership, store formats and counter-share pushes [C006, C007, C008, C010], the entrant through machines, depots, loyalty trips and franchise stores [C013, C021, C022, C012]. The allegation before the CCI is that Asian Paints responded by pressing dealers to drop the entrant's machine and paints; that is an allegation only [C028, C029, C031].

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
G7: PASS. Verified facts the popular version leaves out or gets wrong (popular claims are in myths.md):
1. **Asian Paints' own dealer/distributor count fell** from 75,793 to 72,873 (about 3.9%) in the year it says it added nearly 6,000 touchpoints [C001, C033, C003]. The popular story says "70,000+ dealers" and stops. The two figures measure different things and must not be netted (conflicts K1). Strongest hook candidate.
2. **The top 10 dealers/distributors take 0.9% of Asian Paints' sales** [C002]. The network is fragmented, so an entrant must win dealer by dealer, not by signing a few big ones.
3. **The entrant's own report names "distribution depth" as a requirement for a challenger** [C025], and says its dealer machine was shrunk because dealers needed floor space [C014]. The popular story gives the machine as a giveaway with a count (45,000) that no document opened supports [myths M1].
4. **The CCI route has failed once before**: a JSW complaint of the same kind was investigated and dismissed in 2022; Grasim's complaint is different in its sections and is only at the investigation stage [C032, C030, C031]. Popular headlines say "prima facie abuse"; the court quotes that it is "merely a prima facie opinion" [C031].
5. **Towns covered by Birla Opus rose 72% in one year (6,679 to 11,500)** by the entrant's own chart [C020].
