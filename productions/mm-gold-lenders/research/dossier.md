## Verdict on the premise
The filings support the paradox: Muthoot's standalone gold-loan book is up 43% in a year while the tonnes pledged are down 2% [C1, C2]; its own deck puts the value of each pledged gram up 28% and the lending per rupee of gold up from 57 to 65 paise [C7]. They do not by themselves support "gold has fallen hard this year": the September price data show gold higher than a year earlier at 30 September (Muthoot's valuation price, WGC's domestic average) and easing from a January peak [C7, W3]. The November fall is sourced (IBJA, F1: about -14.5% from 19 Oct to 23 Nov, after the 30 Sep figures); a fall since the January peak is not (U1); its arithmetic about what a fall would do is sound [A1, R2, R3, R4]. The evidence that growth is already slowing is thin and mixed: banks slowed, NBFCs accelerated, Muthoot raised guidance [R6, R6b, C12].

## The story, in plain words (what the documents let the script say)
1. Muthoot says its standalone gold-loan book grew 43% to Rs 1,78,956 crore while pledged gold fell from 209 to 204 tonnes [C1, C2]. Our calculation: about Rs 6,000 a gram became about Rs 8,800, +47% [C1+C2].
2. Muthoot's deck splits that: gold price per gram Rs 10,566 to Rs 13,547 (+28%), and the "margin of safety" on loans 43% to 35%, i.e. about 57 to 65 rupees lent per 100 of gold [C7]. The filing leaves out accrued interest.
3. Manappuram says its consolidated gold loans rose 92.5% on tonnage up 22% [C6], and its standalone loan-to-value went from 57.3% (March) to 65.6% (June) and 64.5% (September) [C8]. IIFL says gold loans are up 90%, shows LTV 70%, and gold gross NPA rose from 0.12% to 0.90% [C9]. SBI says its personal gold loans are up 89.31% to Rs 1,37,349 crore with 0.05% gross NPA [C10], about 3.1% of its domestic advances (our calculation) [C11].
4. RBI's rules: loan-to-value caps of 85/80/75% for consumption loans [R1], kept throughout the loan [R2]; bullet loans counted at the total payable at maturity [R3]; gold valued at the lower of yesterday's close and the 30-day average [R4]; auction needs adequate notice, a reserve price of at least 90% of current value, and surplus back within seven working days of receiving the proceeds [R5]. Same Directions for banks and NBFCs, deadline 1 April 2026 [R7].
5. Arithmetic on that definition: a 70% loan is 87.5% after a 20% fall and 100% after 30% [A1], before interest.
6. Early signs in the filings: Muthoot credit losses Rs 99 to Rs 188 crore, still 0.10% of loans [C5]; IIFL GNPA up [C9]. RBI says bank gold-loan growth slowed but stayed strong [R6].
7. Jewellers: India's Q3 gold demand was 29% lower by weight [W2] but WGC says spending rose and the rise was price-led [W3]; Titan's India jewellery business income +21% and profit before tax +53% (+18% without a customs-duty gain of Rs 536 crore) [J1]; Kalyan's consolidated revenue +31% [J2].

## Fresh angles (gate G7)
G7: PASS. Verified facts that popular coverage of "gold-loan boom" leaves out:
1. **The boom was mostly value per gram, not gold**: Muthoot's tonnage fell 209 to 204 while the book grew 43%; per gram +47%, of which price +28% and the rest extra lending per rupee of gold [C1, C2, C1+C2, C7].
2. **The cushion thinned while the price was high**: the cushion fell from 43% to 35% in the same year Muthoot's own valuation price rose, and 30 September 2026 was the highest price of its five quarter-ends [C7].
3. **Gold at Manappuram is a story of LTV jumping, then easing**: 57.3% in March, 65.6% in June, 64.5% in September [C8].
4. **The rulebook turns a price fall into a faster LTV rise**: the LTV must hold throughout the loan, bullet loans count interest at maturity, and valuation takes the lower of two prices [R2, R3, R4]; the auction reserve price uses the same current value [R5].
5. **Banks and NBFCs diverged in RBI's September data**: banks decelerated, NBFCs accelerated in loans against gold jewellery [R6, R6b].
6. **Titan's +53% profit growth is +18% without a customs-duty gain**, a gain that ran over two quarters [J1].
Reason for PASS: facts 1, 2 and 4 are in the companies' own documents and the regulator's text, and most explainers on the topic cite loan-book growth and gold prices only.
