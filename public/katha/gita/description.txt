Kurukshetra, before sunrise. Two armies of one family, one chariot between them, and before a single arrow flies, the finest archer of his age lets his bow fall. What his charioteer, Krishna, tells him is the Bhagavad Gita, the Song of the Lord.

This film tells all eighteen chapters the way the Gita itself is framed: through Sanjaya, who by the sage Vyasa's grace can see the battlefield, describing it to the blind king Dhritarashtra. Every verse is on screen with its chapter and verse, in Devanagari, transliteration and English. The Sanskrit is shown, not recited. English verses are quoted from Shri Purohit Swami (1935, edited text); Gita 4.7 is quoted from Swami Sivananda (1949, edited text). Chapter 13 is numbered as in our source, which counts Arjuna's opening question as 13.1, so our 13.28 and 13.34 are 13.27 and 13.33 in 700-verse editions such as Gita Press.

A note on how we work. Every verse is shown with its chapter and verse, in a public-domain translation named on screen. Our own reading is marked OUR NOTE. The pictures are drawn in code, the voice is synthetic, and nothing here speaks for God.
