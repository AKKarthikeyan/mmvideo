# Narrated earnings video beats racing bars

Moat & Margin's global channel can succeed, but only part of the planned format mix has both demand and room. The strongest opening is the **narrated, animated earnings Sankey on YouTube**. Static Sankeys flood X, LinkedIn and Substack, and at least six free or "AI" generators now make one from a ticker. Yet this scan found no YouTube channel publishing animated, narrated results-day Sankeys at scale, and the category leader, App Economy Insights (about 300-345K newsletter subscribers), has no YouTube presence that surfaced. Demand for finance video is real: YouTube is the most-cited social channel for financial information in a 2026 FINRA Foundation survey, and quarterly earnings updates rank as the company content investors value most. Narrated economics and business explainers have proven demand, with long-form averages of about 230K to 1.1M views across established channels. But the incumbents there have mostly plateaued, and the ones still growing quickly have a credentialed human voice: Patrick Boyle added roughly 130K subscribers from May to October 2026. Pure bar chart races are the weakest part of the plan. Flagship race channels are flat, dormant or merged, the format takes 20 minutes in a free template, and it matches the "templates, with little variation from video to video" wording of YouTube's July 2026 inauthentic-content clarification almost exactly. That clarification also bars monetization of AI personas presented as human experts giving finance advice, and enforcement applies to the whole channel. The Remotion and MiniMax text-to-speech pipeline is therefore allowed but carries risk. It stays safe only if every video carries a distinct narrative and original analysis, the narrator never poses as an adviser, and the channel leans on long-form video, since entry thresholds for new partners double to 8,000 watch hours on 1 February 2027.

> **How to read the numbers.** Every subscriber, view, growth and revenue figure in this report is a third-party estimate (Social Blade, vidIQ, HypeAuditor, CreatorDB, Playboard, OutlierKit, SPEAKRJ and similar trackers) or a company's own claim. All of them were gathered from **search-engine snippets on 2026-10-18**: the research environment blocked direct page fetches, so no tracker page or YouTube policy page was read in full. Snapshot dates differ by tracker and are given where the snippet showed one. Format and voice traits marked "unverified" come from researchers' background knowledge, not 2026 sources. Policy wording attributed to YouTube help pages comes from search excerpts and has not been checked character by character. **Check every figure and quote live before using it in a decision or a published piece.**

## Finance video demand is real and concentrated around earnings

The demand case does not rest on any one channel's numbers. In a March 2026 Fyllo-KCSA survey of about 7,200 US investors, **about 56% of retail investors said they use YouTube for investment research**, and **quarterly earnings updates were the most valued type of company content (63%)**, although that question was not YouTube-specific ([Yahoo Finance / Fyllo-KCSA](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/more-half-u-investors-aged-130000626.html)). The FINRA Foundation's April 2026 brief found YouTube was the most-cited social channel for financial information: **30% of all respondents and 77% of people who follow finfluencers** ([FINRA Foundation](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf)). YouTube also led US TV viewing with **13.4% share in April 2026**, its sixth straight month at number one, according to Nielsen data reported by AdWave ([AdWave](https://adwave.com/resources/youtube-tv-viewing-share-q2-2026)). That matters for a chart channel, because large-screen viewing rewards dense, legible animation more than phone-first talking heads.

Earnings demand comes in spikes rather than a steady stream. Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 revenue of **$96.2B, up about 106% year on year**, was reported on 26 August 2026, and its next report is expected around 17-18 November 2026 ([Intellectia](https://intellectia.ai/blog/nvidia-q2-earnings-august-2026-analysis); [TrendSpider](https://trendspider.com/markets/symbols/NVDA/)). One aggregator put Nvidia's Google Trends score at **30/100 in June 2026, down about 51% over three months** ([AltIndex](https://altindex.com/ticker/nvda/google-trend)), which shows how quickly interest falls between reports. The channel needs a calendar built around reporting dates, plus evergreen work to fill the troughs. No Google Trends values were obtained for "bar chart race", "GDP comparison" or "earnings", so a manual Trends pull (Web vs. YouTube Search, five years, worldwide and US) is still needed.

Revenue depends on where viewers live more than on the niche. Third-party estimates put finance RPM at roughly **$8-$25 per 1,000 US views**, with top performers above $30 ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche); [InfluencerMarketingHub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/how-much-do-youtubers-make/)). Indian audiences sit around **$0.50-$1.60 RPM** in finance and about 8-20x below the US in every niche ([incomefromviews](https://incomefromviews.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-country/); [videodubbing.com](https://videodubbing.com/blog/post/youtube-rpm-earnings-per-1000-views-by-country-2026/)). Shorts earn about **$0.03-$0.15 per 1,000 views** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-rpm/)). A reasonable planning band for a global English macro-and-companies channel is $6-$15 RPM. That band is an inference from these estimates, and it shrinks quickly if the audience skews toward South Asia. "Global" therefore has to mean global subjects shown to high-RPM viewers, not a global average audience.

Static earnings Sankeys now cost almost nothing to make. SankeyArt, Sankify, Sankey Earnings Generator, Paperglass, SankeyMATIC, JV Invest and an open-source GitHub generator all turn SEC filings into Sankeys, several of them advertised as "AI" ([SankeyArt](https://www.sankeyart.com/sankeys/public/); [Sankify](https://www.sankify.ai/blog); [sankeyearnings.com](https://sankeyearnings.com/); [GitHub](https://github.com/anarion80/sankey-finance)). App Economy Insights' advantage is its brand, its early start and its written analysis. It makes its charts in G Suite ([X](https://twitter.com/EconomyApp/status/1574894687458013184)), and that workflow does not produce video. Audiences clearly like the form ("EVERY company should report earnings via a Sankey diagram" ([X](https://x.com/mrjain/status/1717484396125761983))). The documented complaints are about what a Sankey leaves out: a reader asked where capex fits ([AEI comments](https://www.appeconomyinsights.com/p/how-to-analyze-an-income-statement/comments)), SankeyMATIC's creator splits taxes because showing them as one lump "would be a bit misleading" ([SankeyMATIC](https://sankeymatic.com/data/apple/)), and fiscal-year labels clash across companies, with Nvidia's "Q1 FY27" posted next to Amazon's "Q1 FY26" ([X @SankeyArt](https://x.com/sankeyart?lang=en)). Narration can fix all three: it can explain the fiscal calendar, the currency (TSMC reports in NT$ but guides in USD) and the cash-flow items a P&L Sankey omits.

On YouTube, searches turned up only Sankey-building tutorials, earnings news clips and one-off narrated explainers. No channel was publishing animated, narrated earnings Sankeys as a series ([YouTube tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6XlCeQzgMv4); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oCzpnCwSuQE)). That is the clearest supply gap in the scan, with two caveats. First, YouTube could not be searched directly, so small channels or Shorts accounts may exist. Second, no view data exists for any animated Sankey video, so demand for this exact format is unproven. A skeptic would argue the gap exists because a single Sankey is a ten-second insight that does not hold a ten-minute video. The answer is to treat the Sankey as the anchor visual in a results story (guidance, segment shifts, peer comparison, what the market priced in), not as the whole video.

Bar chart races sit at the opposite end. Flourish has offered a free, no-code race template since 2019, one creator reports building a race in about 20 minutes, and generators such as ViralDataRace and DataToVid now ship ready-made GDP and "most subscribed" templates with 9:16 export ([Flourish](https://flourish.studio/visualisations/bar-chart-race/); [Medium](https://medium.com/@drewenton/i-made-a-bar-chart-race-in-flourish-in-20-minutes-30a97e31fcdb); [DataToVid](https://www.datatovid.com/templates/gdp-race)). GDP and debt races kept appearing through 2026, including a debt-to-GDP race uploaded on 9 February 2026 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXVHMYrG31E)). The format was being called the "fidget spinner of data viz" as early as 2020 ([Built In](https://builtin.com/data-science/bar-chart-races)), and r/dataisbeautiful banned it for a time in 2019 because of spam ([FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/tag/bar-chart-race/)). World Bank and IMF data races cannot carry a channel on their own. They can still work as segments inside narrated stories, particularly on finance-native rankings that template tools do not cover, such as company market caps or sector margins over time.

The narrated-explainer middle ground has demand but also crowding, and the documented criticism there is about trust. China-collapse videos reliably draw millions of views from creators who know "what their audience wants to hear" ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/01/04/2023/the-youtubers-going-viral-by-predicting-chinas-collapse)), and the incentive pushes creators to "cherry-pick data to fit a certain narrative" ([Economics Help](https://www.economicshelp.org/blog/171323/economics/videos-about-economic-collapse-on-youtube/)). Economics Explained faces "poorly researched" accusations on an economists' forum, although that thread is anonymous and about five years old ([EJMR](https://www.econjobrumors.com/topic/economics-explained-is-a-terrible-youtube-channel)). Searches for India-focused economics video surfaced only exam-prep channels and news outlets, with no global-audience data explainers ([UnboxCareer](https://unboxcareer.com/blog/best-economics-youtube-channels-india-2026)). None of the incumbents is known for fast turnaround after earnings.

Taken together, the gaps are specific. First, results-week narrated earnings video on global mega-caps (TSMC, Nvidia, Apple, Samsung, ASML), with careful handling of fiscal years and currencies. Second, Asian and Indian corporate stories told for Western, high-RPM viewers. Third, visible sourcing: on-screen citations and downloadable data in a genre accused of cherry-picking. Fourth, filings-based company deep dives as the opposite of collapse bait. These four are inferred from absences and critiques, not from audience surveys.

## YouTube's July 2026 rules decide the voice and format

YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to **"inauthentic content"** on 15 July 2025. It describes the category as "repetitive or mass-produced" content that "has always been ineligible for monetization" ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). In mid-July 2026 (dates reported as 13, 15 or 16 July) it split the category into three named types while saying there was "no change in our underlying policy at all" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)). Each type maps directly onto a choice this channel has to make.

**Generic or repetitive content** covers material "that can be easily made with AI, computer-generated imagery (CGI), or templates, with little variation from video to video", in the words of YouTube trust-and-safety head Matt Halprin. He added that tutorials can count "if they're reproducing content that's already prevalent on the platform" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). The pipeline is exposed here. Remotion renders from code, and the cheapest way to run an earnings series is one Sankey template with a new company's numbers dropped in each quarter. That is the profile the policy describes. GDP races fail on both counts: they are templated and they are already everywhere. The defence is real variation from one video to the next: a different narrative arc, a different argument, scenes built for that company's story, and analysis a generator could not produce. Templates themselves are allowed when the final video shows creative vision and educational value ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)). The separate Spam policy also bans using "automated tools or AI to churn out high volumes of similar content with minimal changes" ([YouTube Spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)), so volume itself is a warning sign.

**Unsatisfying or off-putting content** includes "channels that heavily rely on generic templates or emotionally manipulative themes", and coverage cites deceptive imagery as an example ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)). No official example mentions economics thumbnails. Still, a catalog of repeated "X is COLLAPSING" framings is the kind of repeated emotional formula the language points at (an inference), and it is also the cherry-picking trope that damages the trust a research brand depends on.

**AI personas as experts on sensitive topics** is the type that matters most for a finance channel. YouTube's wording covers "any content that presents itself as a human expert providing advice to viewers on topics such as health, legal issues, finances, or politics". One listed example is "AI-generated podcast hosts offering financial guidance, investment tips or wealth management advice" ([YouTube monetization policies, en-GB](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). The rule targets the impression of a human expert, not synthetic narration as such. Secondary guides read it the same way: AI narration over a well-researched explainer is fine, while an AI avatar presented as a credentialed adviser is not ([exemplary.ai](https://exemplary.ai/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy)). For MiniMax text-to-speech, that means three things. Do not create a named AI "analyst" character. Do not deliver buy, sell or allocation calls in a synthetic voice. Frame the content as information, and credit the human Moat & Margin research team on screen. Given that the channels still growing are the ones led by expert human voices, the stronger option is a human narrator for flagship and opinion videos. TTS then fits data-dense segments, dubs and fast-turnaround earnings explainers, with human analysis visibly behind them.

Two further rules raise the cost of a mistake. Reused content is assessed across the whole channel, including titles and descriptions. If YouTube "cannot clearly tell that you made the content", it can remove monetization from the entire channel, and it may remove channels from the Partner Program or terminate them ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). Payments can be held for up to 90 days during review, and appeals must be filed within 21 days ([en-GB](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB)). Permission does not help: the policy applies "even if you have permission from the original creator" ([YouTube FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/community-guide/271248162/%F0%9F%94%8E-faq-reused-content-youtube%E2%80%99s-partner-program?hl=en)). Charts the channel renders itself from World Bank or IMF data are arguably original work (an inference). Licensed or re-posted Sankeys from App Economy Insights or Visual Capitalist would not be. Enforcement is real: YouTube terminated the AI fake-trailer channels Screen Culture and KH Studio, which had over 2M combined subscribers, in December 2025 ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2025/12/youtube-terminates-screen-culture-kh-studio-fake-ai-trailer-1236652506/)). This scan found no verified case of a finance or economics explainer channel being demonetized under the inauthentic-content policy, and YouTube has published no numerical threshold for "too much" ([Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/youtube-ai-slop-inauthentic-content-monetization-rules)).

The disclosure rules are lighter than they first appear. Animated or unrealistic content and AI-written scripts are exempt from the "altered or synthetic" disclosure requirement, and "disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop); [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)). Animated charts with TTS are therefore probably not subject to mandatory disclosure. A cloned voice of a real person, however, sits close to the "makes a real person appear to say" trigger, and disclosing it costs nothing. From May 2026 YouTube also detects AI content with its own signals ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/youtube-will-now-automatically-label-ai-videos/)). No YouTube rule requires a financial-advice disclaimer ([Termly](https://termly.io/resources/articles/youtube-disclaimer/)). Securities regulators' rules on finfluencers were outside this scan and need a separate check.

The February 2027 Partner Program change shapes the launch plan. From **1 February 2027**, new creators need **8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days, plus 1,000 subscribers**. Channels already in the program keep their status but must accept updated terms by 31 January 2027 ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)). Shorts revenue also gets a reported **10M views per 90 days** floor ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/10/youtube-now-requires-creators-to-have-twice-as-many-watch-hours-to-start-earning-money/)). Reaching the old 4,000-hour bar in the roughly 15 weeks before the deadline would lock in partner status, but that is ambitious for a new channel. It is also unconfirmed whether applications submitted before the deadline but reviewed after it fall under the old rules. Plan for 8,000 hours. As a rough calculation, that equals about 120K views at four minutes watched per view. That favours longer, high-retention evergreen explainers that keep accumulating watch time, alongside earnings videos whose views fade after each reporting week. A Shorts-first plan suits neither the new floor nor the $0.03-$0.15 RPM. Auto dubbing is now available to all channels in 27 languages, and Expressive Speech in 8, including German and French ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-auto-dubbing-expressive-speech/)). A Remotion pipeline can also re-render chart labels per language cheaply, which helps tilt a global audience toward higher-RPM European viewers.

## Conclusion

The question is not whether data-driven finance video has an audience. It does. The question is which parts of the plan avoid two traps at once: commoditized supply and channel-level policy risk. Narrated earnings Sankeys for global mega-caps avoid both, as long as each video is a results story with its own argument and not a re-skinned template. Narrated explainers have proven demand but reward credibility, and the best way to earn it is equity-research rigour on Asian and Indian companies for a Western audience, with visible sourcing. Template bar chart races fail on both counts and belong inside stories, not as the product. The production engine is an advantage for speed, localization and visual consistency. It turns into a liability exactly where it is most efficient: identical structures, high volume and a synthetic voice that sounds like an adviser.

The voice decision carries the most weight. YouTube's rules leave room for AI narration, but the growth data and the policy both reward a real, credentialed human visibly behind the analysis. A hybrid setup (human-led flagship videos, credited TTS for data-dense and dubbed segments, no AI persona, no advice) keeps the channel on the safe side of the July 2026 line and lets it compete where the incumbents are weakest. Before committing, the team should check four things live that this scan could not: current subscriber and view figures for the table above, the full text of YouTube help page 1311392, Google Trends interest for the target queries, and whether any small Shorts or YouTube accounts already publish animated earnings Sankeys.
