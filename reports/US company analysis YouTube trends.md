# New filings and AI doubts steer demand

As of 19 Oct 2026, US interest in company stories is being pulled by four things at once: the 2026 mega-IPO wave (SpaceX listed in June, Anthropic is expected in November), a chip-led sell-off that has everyone asking whether the AI spending is real, an unusually dense earnings run (Netflix and SpaceX on 20 Oct, Meta and Tesla on 21 Oct, then Apple's first call under a new CEO on 2 Nov), and the ever-present "how does X make money" demand for household names like Costco, Walmart, Amazon and Netflix. For Moat Atlas, the best first picks are **SpaceX, Netflix and Nvidia**, followed by **Apple, Anthropic (once its public S-1 appears), Tesla, Costco and Walmart**. They fit the channel's real edge: explaining a fresh filing in plain words, which the filing-based SpaceX and Anthropic videos in this window show draws attention. The current slate is mostly sound, but it should change in five places. SpaceX goes in for Visa on 10 Nov. Nvidia becomes a results-week bonus tied to its confirmed date, not a fixed 17 Nov Tuesday. Ferrari vs Toyota becomes Tesla vs Toyota. Walmart takes the Black Friday week. JPMorgan moves to 19 Jan, because its Q4 results arrive on 14 Jan, two days after its planned slot. **One caveat governs everything below.** No Google Trends index, keyword-tool volume or per-video YouTube view count could be retrieved: every page fetch was blocked, and the research rests on search-result snippets. The ranking is a judgement built from news coverage, event timing and proxies (head-term search estimates, most-searched-ticker lists, and which videos drew follow-up coverage). It is not measured demand. Six checks AK can run himself in about an hour (listed at the end) would confirm it or reorder it.

## The ranking rests on proxies, not measured volumes

All three research streams hit the same wall: YouTube, vidIQ, Social Blade, OutlierKit, Ahrefs, Exploding Topics' full pages and the Google Trends RSS feed were blocked by DNS or proxy errors. Four kinds of evidence survived. The first is third-party estimates of head-term search volume: **Amazon at about 151M US searches a month** and ChatGPT at 68M ([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/blog/top-google-searches)), and **Walmart at about 45.5M, #8 in the US**, from roughly May 2026 data ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/google-searches)). These are navigational searches by shoppers and users, not explainer demand, so they show how familiar a name is, not whether people want "how it makes money" videos. The second is most-searched-ticker lists. On Benzinga Pro's 2025 list, **Tesla was #2, Nvidia #3, Palantir #4, Amazon #10 and Meta #11** ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/01/49699047/exclusive-top-20-most-searched-tickers-on-benzinga-pro-in-2025-where-do-tesla-nvidia-palantir-apple-stocks-rank)). A search-data estimate put Nvidia at **~1.83M searches, up 22% over three months** to July 2026 ([Rising Trends](https://www.risingtrends.co/trends/investing-trends-2026)). The third is the event calendar, confirmed on company investor-relations pages where possible. The fourth is which in-window videos drew secondary write-ups or rebuttals.

**No search volume was found for any "how does [company] make money", "[company] explained" or "[company] business model" query, and no view count was found for any video uploaded since mid-August.** The searches for those queries returned the explainer articles themselves (Fox Business, FourWeekMBA and others on Costco; Yahoo Finance and INDmoney on Nvidia). That shows publishers keep refreshing these pieces, which is indirect evidence of steady demand, but it does not size that demand ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/how-does-costco-make-money); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/how-does-nvidia-make-money-215014575.html)). Reviews of vidIQ make the structural case that search drives discovery most in educational and how-to niches, which is where Moat Atlas sits ([TubeAnalytics](https://www.tubeanalytics.net/blog/vidiq-review-2026)). Read every rank below as "most likely, given the calendar and the coverage", not as a measurement.

## Four storylines are pulling US attention this autumn

**The 2026 IPO wave is the freshest supply of new filings, and that is Moat Atlas's home ground.** SpaceX listed on Nasdaq on 12 Jun 2026 at $135 a share, raising a record ~$75B ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/spacex-raises-75-billion-in-record-setting-ipo-ahead-of-nasdaq-debut.html)). Its listing has reportedly slipped to November at a ~$2T target valuation. CNBC called it the AI trade's "last big hope for a major IPO this year" on 19 Oct, and Kalshi gave 75% odds of an announcement this year ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/19/anthropic-ipo-ai-trade.html); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-pushes-ipo-november-investors-212000869.html)). OpenAI is out until 2027 at the earliest ([FutureSearch](https://futuresearch.ai/anthropic-openai-ipo-dates-valuations/)). The video evidence matches. The two best-evidenced single-company uploads in the window are Patrick Boyle's **"Is Anthropic Worth Two Trillion Dollars?" (27 Sep)**, which drew Substack and Motley Fool write-ups ([Finance Labs](https://financelabssei.substack.com/p/patrick-boyles-2-trillion-ai-question)), and a ColdFusion video on what the SpaceX filing "really says", which drew a dedicated rebuttal video from another channel ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1eip93s1eA)). Neither view count could be confirmed. The format signal is still clear: "what the filing says" and "is it really worth $N?" are the angles getting engagement right now.

**AI-spending doubt is the theme that links the tech names.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell **11.4% in the week to 16 Oct**, led by Micron, AMD, Broadcom and Intel, and the S&P 500 had its worst week since June ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/15/business/tech-selloff-nasdaq-ai-intl); [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/markets/dow-jones/2026/10/16/us-markets-weighed-down-by-extended-sell-off-in-the-tech-sector/)). That turns Meta (21 Oct), Microsoft and Alphabet (28 Oct), Amazon (29 Oct) and Nvidia (mid or late November) into one connected story: the money the cloud giants spend becomes Nvidia's revenue, and its chips are made by TSMC. That is a ready-made Follow the Dollar episode. It also gives the TSMC launch episode a topical hook it lacks today. TSMC's Q3 revenue was about **$46.7B, with high-performance computing at about 68%** (roughly $68 of every $100), and it raised Q4 guidance to $52.3–54.3B ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/taiwan-semiconductor-manufacturing-company-limited-reports-earnings-results-for-the-third-quarter-en-ce785dd3df8bf227)). **Evergreen demand favours names a child would recognise, which suits the Moat 60 style.** The names with the deepest steady interest on these proxies are Amazon, Walmart, Apple, Tesla, Nvidia, Costco and Netflix. Company Man's biggest hits are still "The Decline of Pizza Hut" (~4.4M views) and "The Decline of KFC" (~3.92M), both older uploads ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@companyman114/)). A 2026 video titled "How Does Netflix Make Money in 2026? The Business Model Nobody Sees." shows that small channels keep making this format ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6MRJVzl8bRs)). The pattern for a castle-and-moat explainer is simple: Netflix, Costco, Walmart, Tesla and SpaceX rockets need no introduction to the viewer, while Visa, JPMorgan and LVMH need more set-up before the out-of-every-$100 picture means anything. One theme is fading: Bitcoin searches are at multi-year lows and XRP interest is down more than 90% from its peak ([CryptoTimes](https://cryptotimes.io/2026/10/17/bitcoin-price-holds-81k-amid-315m-liquidations-google-searches-hit-multi-year-low); [U.Today](https://u.today/xrp-searches-drop-90-on-yearly-scale-in-google-trends)), so crypto companies such as Coinbase should wait.

There is a tension with the channel's title rules. "Decline", "COLLAPSING" and "worse than you think" titles clearly travel, but the content plan bans hype words. The two formats with in-window traction, a **question with a big number** ("Is Anthropic Worth Two Trillion Dollars?") and a **filing reveal** ("SpaceX Secretly Filed as an AI Company", per a summary of the ColdFusion video ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/ce1b91076c7279b0))), both fit inside those rules. Valuation questions also need care, because "is it worth $2T?" slides towards a verdict. Moat Atlas should answer with "here is what the filing says, and here is what you'd have to believe", never yes or no.

## Conclusion

The research points to a timing edge more than a topic edge. For the next three months, US curiosity about companies is being fed by documents that have never been explained before: SpaceX's first public filings, Anthropic's coming S-1, and the AI-spending figures inside every big tech quarter. A channel whose whole method is "read the filing, draw the $100" is unusually well placed to catch that, provided it moves within days rather than weeks. That argues for running the slate with a fixed evergreen backbone (Netflix, Walmart, Costco, Starbucks, Tesla vs Toyota) plus two or three slots that open on dates the companies set. A rigid 12-Tuesday plan will keep landing just before or just after the moment, as Nvidia and JPMorgan already do.

The weakest part of this answer is also the cheapest to fix. Every rank here would survive or fall on an hour of Google Trends and vidIQ checks, and the biggest open question is whether YouTube searchers want the IPO-era names (SpaceX, Anthropic) more than the household explainers (Costco, Walmart). Running those checks before booking 10 Nov and 24 Nov matters more than any further desk research.
