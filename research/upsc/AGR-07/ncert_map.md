## Verdict: NCERT coverage of MSP is thin

There is no NCERT agriculture textbook. NCERT mentions MSP in **four of the twelve chapter files read**, and the treatment is one paragraph at most. No NCERT chapter read defines MSP, names the crops it covers, names the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, gives a price, a cost concept, a procurement figure, a scheme or a date. Zero text hits for: CACP, A2, C2, FAQ (Fair Average Quality), PM-AASHA, Price Support Scheme, open-ended procurement, Swaminathan, Shanta Kumar, "22 crops", "Fair and Remunerative". The thickest NCERT source (Class IX Economics, "Food Security in India") could not be fetched (codes return 404); see open item O03 in `claims.csv`.

So the Gold Standard order applies: NCERT gives the setting and two or three terms; everything else comes from order 2 sources (CACP, PIB, Parliament replies, MoSPI). The episode must not pretend NCERT covers it.

## Where two NCERT books differ

* **Depth.** Class XI (`keec105`) names MSP, buffer stock and PDS as a set of instruments; Class X Geography (`jess104`) names "minimum support price, remunerative and procurement prices" without explanation; Class X Economics (`jess201`) mentions only "support prices". No book says what "remunerative price" or "procurement price" is, and current Government and CACP texts read do not use them as separate prices (they speak only of MSP and, for sugarcane, FRP). Treat the two older terms as school vocabulary to be explained, not as current categories.
* **Tone on MSP.** Class XI ch.2/ch.5 describe procurement as part of the Green Revolution success and as protection of farmers; Class XI ch.3 describes a "low MSP" as one cause of farm distress after 1991. These are different periods and different judgements; neither carries a number.
* **Edition marks.** `keec1`, `jess1`, `jess2`, `legy2`, `lesy2` carry "Reprint 2026-27"; `hess4` (Class VIII) is marked 2024-25. All are reprints of 2022-23 revisions; none is a new-curriculum book.

## Rationalisation of content

The "Rationalisation of Content in the Textbooks" page in `keec1`, `jess1`, `legy2`, `lesy2` and `hess4` gives only the criteria (overlap, lower or higher class, difficulty, self-learning, irrelevance) and says the edition is "a reformatted version after carrying out the changes". **It does not list what was removed.** NCERT's own page (ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/Rationalised.pdf) says the same, in two sentences, and points to each textbook's preliminary pages. So whether MSP material was cut from older editions cannot be determined from NCERT's own documents; an older edition from the NCERT archive would have to be compared. Marked as open: the lead can decide whether it matters (the 2020 laws box in `keec105` cannot date from before 2020, so the revised edition appears to have added, not cut, material on this theme; not checked against the older edition).

## What the topic needs that NCERT does not cover

1. What MSP is (and is not) and its legal basis; the 22 crops; the sugarcane FRP. (`claims.csv` L02-L06, L08-L09)
2. The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, its 1965 origin and how a recommendation becomes a Cabinet decision. (L01-L04)
3. Cost concepts A2, A2+FL, C2; the Budget 2018-19 "1.5 times" principle and which cost it uses. (F01-F10)
4. Season-wise MSP numbers, margins and announcement timing. (M01-M08, living file)
5. How the Government actually buys: FCI, State agencies, DCP, FAQ, open-ended paddy and wheat procurement; Price Support Scheme, PM-AASHA, price deficit payment, market intervention. (P01-P11)
6. How much is bought, from which States, and how many farmers are reached; the 2015 "6%" figure, its age and what newer data say. (P12-P18, C01-C12)
7. Stocks, State bonuses and fees, crop choice, trade policy: the effects and CACP's and the Shanta Kumar committee's recommendations. (D01-D10)
8. The 2020 laws, the 2021 repeal and the committee announced on 19 Nov 2021. (L10-L13)
9. Positions on a legal guarantee for MSP, by named holders. (L05, L13, F11-F12, D01, D06, O05)
