Redistribution moves income through taxes and transfers. Stabilisation steadies the economy when demand rises or falls too fast.

### b03 · *THEREFORE* · rung 1 · new term: direct tax
**PICTURE:** Two doors: DIRECT, where a salary slip goes in; INDIRECT, where a price tag goes in.  
**VOICE:** Taxes come in two families. A direct tax is paid on your income or profits, like income tax. An indirect tax is added to the price of goods and services, like GST.  
**SCREEN:** direct: on income or profits (income tax) · indirect: added to the price (GST)  
**CLAIMS:** E12-A02 (CONFIRMED), E12-A07 (CONFIRMED)

### b04 · *BUT* · rung 2 · new term: incidence
**PICTURE:** The bill from the opening splits into two labels: IMPACT points at the shopkeeper, INCIDENCE at the customer's wallet.  
**VOICE:** But that line hides the real question. Economists separate impact from incidence. Impact is who hands the tax to the government. Incidence is who finally bears it. For a direct tax they are the same person. For an indirect tax they can be different people.  
**SCREEN:** IGNOU: impact = who pays the government · incidence = who bears the burden  
**CLAIMS:** E12-A03 (CONFIRMED)

### b05 · *THEREFORE* · rung 2-3
**PICTURE:** The TRUE OR FALSE card flips to FALSE. The shopkeeper hands an envelope to a government window; the same amount shows on the customer's price tag.  
**VOICE:** So back to the shop. The CGST Act says the tax shall be paid by the taxable person. In the usual case, that is the supplier. But CBIC's own FAQ says the burden of GST is to be borne by the final consumer. So the answer is false. The shop pays by law. In design, you bear it in the price.  
**SCREEN:** CGST Act: the taxable person pays · CBIC: the final consumer is to bear it · answer: false  
**CLAIMS:** E12-A05 (CONFIRMED), E12-A06 (CONFIRMED), E12-K05 (CONFIRMED)


## Chapter 3

### b06 · *BUT* · rung 3 · new term: elasticity
**PICTURE:** A supply-demand diagram drawn on a notebook: a tax wedge between two curves; the price rises by less than the wedge; the wedge is coloured in two parts, BUYER and SELLER.  
**VOICE:** But can the shop always pass on the whole tax? Not always. IGNOU shows that when a tax pushes the price up, buyers buy a little less. So the whole rise need not be passed on. The price rises by less than the tax, and the seller absorbs the rest. How the burden splits depends on how easily buyers and sellers can walk away.  
**SCREEN:** IGNOU: the burden is shared; the split depends on elasticity of demand and supply  
**CLAIMS:** E12-A04 (CONFIRMED), E12-A03 (CONFIRMED)

### b07 · *THEREFORE* · rung 2 · new term: progressive tax
**PICTURE:** Three staircases: one rising, one flat, one falling, each with a small figure climbing it.  
**VOICE:** Taxes also differ by who carries more of the weight. A progressive tax takes a higher rate as income rises. A proportional tax takes the same rate. A regressive tax takes a falling rate as income rises. India's personal income tax is progressive. People below a limit pay nothing.  
**SCREEN:** IGNOU: progressive · proportional · regressive · NCERT: income tax is progressive  
**CLAIMS:** E12-A09 (CONFIRMED), E12-A11 (CONFIRMED), E12-A08 (CONFIRMED)

### b08 · *BUT* · rung 3
**PICTURE:** Two family budgets as two jars, one small and one large; the same coin of tax drops into each. In the small jar it fills a much bigger slice. A label: OUR READING.  
**VOICE:** But what about GST? NCERT calls indirect taxes regressive, because they hit all income groups equally. IGNOU also calls them regressive. Here is our reading. The same tax in rupees is a bigger share of a smaller income. A poorer family spends a larger share of its income on everyday purchases. So a tax in the price tends to take a bigger share of it.  
**SCREEN:** NCERT and IGNOU: indirect taxes are regressive · our reading: same rupees, bigger share of a smaller income  
**CLAIMS:** E12-A10 (CONFLICT)
  
**NOTE:** A10 is CONFLICT: NCERT's reason ('equally') is named as NCERT's; the share-of-income reason is labelled ours.

### b09 · *THEREFORE* · rung 2 · new term: subsidy
**PICTURE:** A price tag with a slice cut away and paid by a government hand; a rupee coin cut into slices, 6 paise highlighted.  
**VOICE:** Money flows the other way too. In plain words, a subsidy is money the State puts in so that something costs less. NCERT says some are open, on food or fertiliser. Others are hidden, when public goods like education and health are priced low. In the twenty twenty-six to twenty-seven Budget's rupee chart, major subsidies are six paise of every rupee.  
**SCREEN:** NCERT: explicit and implicit subsidies · Budget 2026-27 'Rupee Goes To': major subsidies 6 paise (total includes States' share of taxes)  
**CLAIMS:** E12-B06 (CONFIRMED), E12-B08 (CONFIRMED)


## Chapter 4

### b10 · *THEREFORE* · rung 1
**PICTURE:** A pen on a factory desk; its parts arrive with a small tag 'Rs 1'; the finished pen gets a tag 'Rs 20 + Rs 2 = Rs 22'.  
**VOICE:** Now the old problem that GST was built to fix. Why did a pen once carry more tax than it should? Follow one pen, using CBIC's own example. The maker's inputs cost ten rupees and carry one rupee of excise. The pen sells for twenty rupees plus two rupees of excise. That is twenty-two.  
**SCREEN:** CBIC example: inputs ₹10 + ₹1 excise · pen ₹20 + ₹2 excise = ₹22  
**CLAIMS:** E12-E03 (CONFIRMED)

### b11 · *BUT* · rung 2 · new term: cascading
**PICTURE:** The Rs 22 tag gets a second tag stacked on top of it, VAT; the stack wobbles like a tower of boxes, labelled TAX ON TAX.  
**VOICE:** But then the State's VAT was charged on the whole twenty-two rupees. That includes the two rupees of central tax. So a tax was charged on a tax. NCERT calls this cascading. NCERT says the old system gave minimal credit for tax paid on inputs. In the pen example, excise could not be set off against State VAT.  
**SCREEN:** CBIC example: VAT on all ₹22 · NCERT: cascading, tax on tax  
**CLAIMS:** E12-E03 (CONFIRMED), E12-E02 (CONFIRMED)

### b12 · *THEREFORE* · rung 2 · new term: input tax credit
**PICTURE:** A chain of hands from factory to wholesaler to shop to customer; at each link a small coin passes and a receipt for the earlier coin is handed back as credit. The wobbly tower collapses into one neat tag.  
**VOICE:** GST fixes this with a credit chain. Tax is paid at every stage, but each seller gets credit for the tax already paid before. So only the value added at each stage is taxed. NCERT calls it effectively a tax on value addition.  
**SCREEN:** NCERT, CBIC: tax at every stage, credit for earlier tax · only value addition taxed  
**CLAIMS:** E12-E01 (CONFIRMED), E12-E02 (CONFIRMED), E12-A06 (CONFIRMED)

### b13 · *THEREFORE* · rung 2 · new term: destination principle
**PICTURE:** A map of India in plain outline: a truck moves within one State and the bill shows CGST + SGST; a second truck crosses a State line and the bill shows IGST. No disputed borders drawn.  
**VOICE:** GST is also a destination tax. The revenue goes to the place where the goods are consumed, CBIC says. So it is split two ways. Inside a State, the Centre levies CGST and the State levies SGST. That is why one bill can show two taxes. Across States, the Centre levies IGST and shares it.  
**SCREEN:** CBIC: destination principle · within a State: CGST + SGST · between States: IGST, shared  
**CLAIMS:** E12-E09 (CORRECTED), E12-E04 (CONFIRMED)
