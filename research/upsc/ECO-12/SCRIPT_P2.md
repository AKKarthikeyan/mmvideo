### b04 · *THEREFORE* · rung 2 · new term: concurrent power
**PICTURE:** The two locked boxes open into one shared table drawer marked 246A; a truck crossing a State line drops its tax into a separate drawer marked 269A, which then splits in two.  
**VOICE:** The key new article is two hundred forty-six A. It gives both Parliament and the State legislatures the power to make GST laws. But when a supply crosses a State border, only Parliament can make the law. That inter-State tax is collected by the Centre and shared, under Article two hundred sixty-nine A.  
**SCREEN:** Art. 246A: Parliament and States both legislate on GST · inter-State: Parliament only · Art. 269A: IGST collected and shared

**VOICE:** But who recommends the rates for this shared tax? Not Parliament alone, and not any one State. Article two hundred seventy-nine A creates the GST Council. The Union Finance Minister chairs it, with the Union Minister of State in charge of revenue or finance. Every State sends its finance or taxation minister, or another minister it nominates.  
**SCREEN:** Art. 279A: GST Council · Union Finance Minister (chair) · Union MoS (revenue or finance) · each State's minister

**VOICE:** Now your guess. Every decision needs three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting. The Centre's vote counts as one-third. All the States together count as two-thirds. **SCREEN:** Union of India v Mohit Minerals (19 May 2022): IGST on ocean freight in CIF imports struck down

### b14 · *THEREFORE* · rung 3
**PICTURE:** Two papers on a desk: UNION'S ARGUMENT: BINDING and the Court's order: PERSUASIVE. A torn draft page marked PROPOSED 279B: DISPUTE AUTHORITY (DROPPED).  
**VOICE:** On the way, the Court answered the bigger question. The Union argued the Council's recommendations must bind Parliament and the States, or there would be an impasse. The Court disagreed. It held the recommendations are persuasive, not binding. One reason: Parliament dropped a proposed Article two hundred seventy-nine B. It was an authority to rule on departures from the Council's recommendations. The Court read that as meant to keep recommendations persuasive.  
**SCREEN:** Union: recommendations should bind (else an impasse) · Court: persuasive, not binding · Art. 279B (dispute authority) proposed, dropped  
**CLAIMS:** E12-D05 (CORRECTED), E12-D02 (CONFIRMED), E12-D03 (CONFIRMED)

### b15 · *BUT* · rung 5
**PICTURE:** Two hands on one shared pen over a GST law page, neither hand above the other; a speech-bubble chain going back and forth.  
**VOICE:** But the Court drew a line. When the Government makes rules under the CGST and IGST Acts, it is bound by the Council's recommendations. Parliament and the State legislatures keep their own power to make laws. The Court described Indian federalism as a dialogue between cooperative and uncooperative federalism, from collaboration to contestation. It said the Union and the States have equal power to legislate on GST.  
**SCREEN:** Court: rule-making bound; legislation not · federalism as a dialogue between Union and States, equal power to legislate
