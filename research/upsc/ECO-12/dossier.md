## PART A. Dossier

**1. What it is, why it matters, the order to meet the ideas.**
Every purchase has a tax hidden in it. The question "who really pays?" has two answers: the person the law makes pay (the supplier files and pays GST: A05) and the person who finally bears it (the consumer: A06), and the split depends on how easily the price can move (A03, A04). Order for a learner:
(1) what a tax is and why a law must stand behind it (A01, B02); (2) direct vs indirect (A02, A07); (3) impact vs incidence and shifting (A03 to A06); (4) progressive, proportional, regressive, and the NCERT slip about "regressive" (A08 to A11); (5) subsidy as the other side of the ledger (B06 to B08); (6) the old mess: cascading, with the pen story (E02, E03); (7) GST: destination principle, dual structure, credit chain (E01, E04 to E07); (8) how the Constitution made it possible: 101st Amendment (C01 to C19); (9) the Council and federalism, Mohit Minerals (D01 to D08); (10) which taxes merged and what GST brings in (F01 to F10); (11) compensation to States, COVID, loans and the cess (G01 to G17); (12) rates today (H01 to H07); (13) agricultural income (J01 to J07).

**2. Hard parts and common misconceptions (correct position, source).**
- "The shopkeeper pays GST from his own pocket." He pays it to the government but is built to pass it on; CBIC says the final consumer bears it (A05, A06). Whether he can pass on all of it depends on demand (A04).
- "Indirect taxes are regressive because they hit everyone equally" (NCERT p.80). Equal rupees is not equal share of income; the correct reason is that a poorer household spends more of its income on taxed goods (inference, A10).
- "GST in the Budget is the GST collected." No: Budget 'GST' is the Centre's CGST/IGST line (Rs 10.19 lakh crore BE 2026-27); gross collection was Rs 22.27 lakh crore in 2025-26 and includes State GST (F06, F07).
- "The Council's decision binds the Union and States." Supreme Court, Mohit Minerals, 19 May 2022: recommendations are persuasive (D02), though rule-making by the Government is bound (D04).
- "NCERT says six GST rates." Out of date since 22 Sep 2025 (H06).
- "Octroi was not subsumed." The Compensation Act lists octroi under old entry 52 as a subsumed tax; CBIC pages word it differently (F03).
- "Petrol and liquor are under GST." Alcohol is outside by the Constitution's definition; five petroleum goods wait for a Council date; electricity is outside (C08, C11, E07).
- "Compensation ended in 2022, so the cess ended." Compensation ran five years; the cess was extended to 31 March 2026 to repay the loans, and the cess on tobacco and pan masala was withdrawn on 1 Feb 2026. No source read says the loans are fully repaid (G14 to G16).
- "All farm-linked income is tax-free." Only agricultural income as the Act defines it is outside total income (J02, J03); poultry and wool are not named (J05, UNVERIFIED).

**3. Debates (who holds which view).**
- Binding vs persuasive recommendations: the Union argued for binding (D05); the Supreme Court (three judges, one judgment) held persuasive (D02, D03).
- Who must make good the COVID shortfall: some States suggested the Centre borrow and compensate (RS20 reply, 20 Sep 2020); the Centre, on the Attorney General's opinion as the Department of Expenditure reports it, held it is not obliged beyond the Fund and the Council must decide (G09). The Centre in practice borrowed and lent back (G12).
- Where incidence falls: IGNOU makes it depend on elasticities (A03, A04); CBIC states the consumer bears it (A06). Both are on the page; the second is the policy statement, the first the economics.
- Indirect vs direct taxes: IGNOU lists merits of each (A03); NCERT states a preference for direct taxes for deficit reduction (A10, F08).

**4. What changed since the NCERT text (dates).**
- 22 Sep 2025: four slabs to 5% / 18% (and 40%); roti and UHT milk to nil; insurance exempt (H01 to H03).
- 1 Feb 2026: tobacco and pan masala to 40% (bidi 18%), compensation cess nil (H05, G16).
- 1 Apr 2026: Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act (J02).
- 8 Oct 2026: 57th Council on process reform (H07).
- Budget 2026-27 (Feb 2026): no GST compensation cess line; new separate Health Security se National Security Cess (G17); States' share kept at 41% (F10).
- Plan / non-plan wording in NCERT 5.1.3 appears dated (B05).

## PART D. MATERIAL FOR UNDERSTANDING AND FUN
**Everyday examples.**
- The pen: Rs 10 inputs, Rs 1 excise; Rs 20 pen plus Rs 2 excise = Rs 22; VAT on all Rs 22, tax on tax (E12-E03).
- A bill, a price tag: the shop pays GST to the government; the price you pay carries it (A05, A06).
- A family basket and its average GST rate, 9.66% on the old rates, from Class XI Statistics (K02). Rebuild with today's rates only after checking each item's rate at CBIC.
- Roti, soap, toothpaste, an AC: today's rate and yesterday's (H03).
- Goods moving Tamil Nadu to Kerala bear IGST (E09).
**Real examples from India today.** Gross GST Rs 22.27 lakh crore in 2025-26, 1.65 crore taxpayers (F06, E08); Budget 2026-27 numbers (F07 to F09); three subsidies at Rs 4.1 lakh crore (B07); 56th Council rates; tobacco 1 Feb 2026 (H05).
**Surprising true facts.** Budget 'GST' (Rs 10.19 lakh crore, BE 2026-27) is under half of gross GST collection (Rs 22.27 lakh crore, 2025-26; different years, so say 'roughly') (F07). GST subsumed 17 taxes and 13 cesses (F04). Rs 2,69,208 crore of back-to-back loans went to States, repaid out of a cess (G12, G13). A cess created for five years of compensation outlived the compensation (G14, G15). Of each rupee the Union takes in, GST and other taxes supply 15 paise (F09). Midnight launch in Parliament (K01).
**Short real stories.** The midnight rollout (C19, K01); the pandemic summer of 2020: Rs 3 lakh crore shortfall, two options, States choose (G09 to G11); the ocean-freight case that decided the Council's authority (D07, D02).
**Questions people ask.** Why is petrol not in GST (C08, E07)? Why do two taxes (CGST and SGST) appear on one bill (E04)? Can the Centre overrule States in the Council (D01)? Why was my tax-free roti 5% before (H03)? Do farmers pay income tax (J01 to J04)? Is GST a tax on the shop (A05, A06)?
**Guess-first and spot-the-mistake.**
1. "If a shop's cost rises by Rs 10 of tax, does the price you pay rise by Rs 10?" Answer: not necessarily (A04).
2. Spot the mistake: "Indirect taxes are regressive because everyone pays the same" (A10; correct reason: share of income).
3. Spot the slogan: "One Nation, One Tax, One Market" vs the Constitution's "harmonised national market" (K04).
4. Voting: "Can the Centre alone pass a Council decision? Can all States together?" (D01: no, and no).
5. Basket game: 5 items, 5 rates, what is the average rate? (K02).
6. "Does a Council recommendation bind Parliament?" (D02).
7. True or false: "Rural agricultural land is a capital asset" (J04: false). "Poultry income is tax-free" (J05: not supported; no key).
