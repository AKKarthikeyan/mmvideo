## 1. What the topic really is, and the order to meet the ideas

It is one question: **how can a bank rate set by one committee in Mumbai slow the rise in the price of everything, and why does it sometimes not work?**

Order a learner must meet things in:
1. **Inflation**: the *rate* at which the general price level rises (not the level). Measured by a basket index: CPI (retail), WPI (wholesale). India's headline is CPI-Combined, now **base 2024=100** (358 items, 12 divisions, food and beverages 36.75% of the basket). Latest: **5.54% in September 2026** (rural 6.08, urban 4.88, food 7.74), released 12 Oct 2026 (P01-P07).
2. **Why prices rise**: demand above supply (demand-pull) or higher costs (cost-push), including food and fuel supply shocks (S03, P14).
3. **Who is responsible and what the target is**: price stability keeping growth in mind (F01); target **4% with a band of 2 to 6%**, 1 Apr 2026 to 31 Mar 2031 (Gazette S.O. 1580(E), 25 Mar 2026, F04); six-member MPC, one vote each, Governor's casting vote only in a tie (F11-F13).
4. **The tools**: the one rate the MPC sets is the **policy repo rate (5.50%)**; around it a corridor with the **SDF at 5.25% (floor)** and **MSF at 5.75% (ceiling)**; Bank Rate 5.75%; reserve tools **CRR 3.00% and SLR 18.00%**; liquidity tools (variable-rate auctions, OMOs) (I01-I17).
5. **The chain**: repo rate steers the overnight call rate, which pulls money-market rates and bond yields, then bank deposit and lending rates, then borrowing and spending, then prices (T02). RBI staff list five channels: interest rate, credit, asset price, exchange rate, expectations (T01).
6. **The delay and the leaks**: 6 to 18 months in banking by RBI's own citation (T04); credit responds with a peak near month 13 (T05); lending rates only partly follow (T10); and policy acts on **second-round effects**, not on the first hit of a supply shock (T14).
7. **Where we are now**: hike on 7 Oct 2026, stance changed to calibrated tightening (I01, I18); next MPC 2-4 Dec 2026.

## 2. The hard parts and misconceptions (with the correct position)

- **"A repo hike will cut onion prices."** RBI's own resolution says policy mainly works by curtailing second-round effects (inflation expectations, firm pricing) of a supply shock, and that these are hard to tell apart from indirect cost effects (T14, MPC para 12). RBI Box IV.1: temporary supply shocks are often "looked through"; generalised ones are acted on (T15).
- **"RBI sets my loan rate."** It sets the repo rate. Banks set their own rates; loans linked to an external benchmark (68.2% of floating-rate loans) follow repo at a spread; older MCLR loans reset slowly (T08-T09). Pass-through is incomplete: repo +250 bps gave +182 on fresh loans, +115 on outstanding (T10).
- **"Reverse repo is the policy rate/floor."** Old. Since April 2022 the SDF is the floor; fixed reverse repo (3.35% on the panel) is discretionary (X02).
- **"Bank Rate is what RBI charges banks."** NCERT says so; RBI now uses it as a penal reference aligned with the MSF rate (X03).
- **"The Governor decides."** True under the 2015 agreement (clause 3). Since the 2016 Act the MPC decides; the casting vote has never been needed in a decade (F06, F13, F11).
- **Inflation 5% means prices fell/flat?** No: slower rise, not a fall (S01).
- **Target is 4-6%.** It is 4% with a tolerance band of 2 to 6%. "Failure" means three consecutive quarters outside the band (F07).
- **NCERT's CRR of 20% is real.** It is a made-up example; real CRR is 3.00% (N02).

## 3. Debates and who holds which view

- **Headline or core as the target?** RBI's 2025-26 consultation: 66 of 72 respondents wanted headline; RBI's DG states the case for core (volatile food and fuel, limited traction) and why headline was kept (F08, T16). The new basket has less food, so the two may track more closely (P07).
- **Is 4% right; should the band narrow?** Strong support to keep 4%; two-thirds kept the band; 21 respondents wanted narrower, most often +/-1.5 (F08).
- **Main transmission channel?** IGNOU 2024 reports Khundrakpam and Jain (2012) and Kapur and Behera (2012) for the interest-rate channel against Mitra et al. (2021) for the credit channel; RBI staff say interest-rate channel dominates and credit amplifies (S04, T01). Status CONFLICT: a genuine scholarly difference.
- **How hard to answer a mostly supply-side rise (7 Oct 2026 minutes)?** All six voted for +25 bps; two members (Dr Nagesh Kumar, Prof. Ram Singh) preferred to keep the stance neutral, calling inflation largely supply-side; Shri Indranil Bhattacharyya set aside the "cautiousness bias" and urged acting pre-emptively (para 44); Shri Saugata Bhattacharya also voted for the hike and the stance change; Dr Poonam Gupta noted that the projection reaches the 6% upper limit and that policy works with a lag (I18, T04, T17).
