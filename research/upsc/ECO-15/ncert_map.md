## Main chapter

**Introductory Macroeconomics (code 12105), Textbook in Economics for Class XII, Chapter 3 "Money and Banking", printed pp. 36-50.**
- Edition (from the front pages, `leec1.ps.txt`): First edition March 2007; reprinted many times to January 2021; **Revised Edition October 2022**; reprinted March 2024, February 2025, **January 2026** (this is the "Reprint 2026-27").
- What it covers: functions of money (3.1); demand for and supply of money, central bank and commercial banks (3.2); how banks create money, the fictional bank, CRR, money multiplier (3.3); **policy tools to control money supply (3.4, pp. ~42-44)**: CRR, bank rate, open market operations (outright and repo), repo and reverse repo, moral suasion and margin requirement; Box 3.1 (Keynesian demand for money, velocity, liquidity trap); Box 3.2 (demonetisation); summary and exercise Q9 "What are the instruments of monetary policy of RBI?".
- Pages used: pp. ~36-37 (store of value and purchasing power), ~38 (central bank functions), ~40-41 (CRR, SLR, multiplier), ~42-44 (policy tools), ~44-47 (Box 3.1), ~49-50 (summary, key concepts, exercises).
- What NCERT says that is now dated: see "Differences" below.

## Does NCERT cover inflation?

**Not in any chapter of its own.** There is no chapter on inflation in Class IX-XII Economics that I could reach. Inflation appears: as a one-line aside on purchasing power (XII Macro Ch 3), as a price index topic (XII Macro Ch 2, XI Statistics Ch 7), as a stabilisation and deficit question (XII Macro Ch 5), and in the account of the 1991 crisis (XI IED Ch 3). **Demand-pull and cost-push, second-round effects, inflation expectations, the inflation target and the MPC appear nowhere.** For these the episode needs RBI, MoSPI and IGNOU.

## Where NCERT and today differ (the difference, plainly)

1. **Frame.** NCERT: RBI controls the *money supply*. RBI today: price stability against a CPI target, by setting a *policy rate* through a six-member committee (ECO15-X01). Money still matters but is not the target.
2. **"Repo and reverse repo ... main tool."** True in spirit (repo is the policy rate) but **reverse repo is no longer the floor**: since April 2022 the standing deposit facility is (ECO15-X02). The fixed reverse repo is at RBI's discretion.
3. **Bank Rate.** NCERT: the rate at which RBI lends to banks. Now: a penal reference rate, tied to the MSF rate, used on CRR and SLR shortfalls (ECO15-X03). Today 5.75 per cent.
4. **CPI base.** NCERT XI Statistics: base 2012=100. Since 12 Feb 2026 the series is **base 2024=100** (ECO15-X04).
5. **Missing in NCERT:** Monetary Policy Committee, inflation target and band, LAF corridor, MSF, SDF, operating target, transmission and its lags, external benchmark lending, stance, second-round effects, core inflation as policy guide, Monetary Policy Report.
6. **CRR example.** NCERT's 20 per cent CRR is an invented example. Real CRR is 3.00 per cent. SLR is named but no number is given; today 18.00 per cent (single-source, see claims).
7. **Date of the central bank.** NCERT says 1935; RBI says it was established 1 April 1935 under the Act of 1934. Both are right.
8. **IGNOU 2021 note (not NCERT):** MEC-205 Vol 2 Unit 9 still shows the pre-2022 corridor of MSF and reverse repo (ECO15-X05).
