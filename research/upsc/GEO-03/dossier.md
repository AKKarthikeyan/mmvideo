## A1. What the topic is, and the order to meet it
The ground breaks along a fault and waves spread (A1, A3, A4); most breaks happen at plate boundaries (A17, A18). Under the sea, the same breaking can lift or drop the sea floor and make a tsunami (A34, A35). In a viewer's life: India has 170 seismograph stations and a 24-hour tsunami warning centre in Hyderabad (A14, A51); about 59% of the land is in zones III to V (A26); and a phone SMS alert is how a warning reaches an officer or a fisher (A54).
Order: (1) fault, focus, epicentre (A1-A3); (2) P, S, surface waves, shadow zones (A4-A7); (3) magnitude v intensity, Mw (A9-A12, A15); (4) why a belt on the map: plate boundaries, Himalaya, the Peninsula's surprises (A17-A22); (5) India's zones today (A23-A28); (6) NCS (A14, A29); (7) tsunami: cause, mechanism, speed, shape, warning signs (A32-A44); (8) 2004 (A45-A50); (9) warning system and alert levels (A51-A58); (10) Bhuj 2001 and the law (A60-A67).
Other episodes' ground: interior of the Earth (GEO-01), volcanoes (GEO-04), cyclones (GEO-09), currents and tides (GEO-11), floods and droughts (GEO-19), landslides (GEO-38), law of the sea (GED-04). Only links are used here.

## A2. Hard parts and misconceptions (correct position, source)
- **Magnitude is not intensity.** One magnitude per quake; many intensities, by place (A10). NCERT's "0 to 10" is a simplification: no upper limit (A9).
- **Richter is not today's scale.** USGS reports moment magnitude (A12). The USGS explainer pages are bot-blocked (A13), so say "moment magnitude" and show the catalog values.
- **A tsunami is not a tidal wave** (NOAA; NCERT Class 7 uses the wrong name) (A33).
- **A tsunami is fast in deep water, slow near the coast, and grows as it slows.** NCERT Class 11 p.59 says the speed is "more in shallow water": backwards (A37).
- **One wave is a myth:** it is a train; the first need not be the biggest (A39).
- **Not every big undersea quake makes a tsunami** (depth, fault type, size; thresholds differ by source, A36).
- **Prediction:** no precise earthquake prediction exists anywhere (A30). Warning is different: it uses the quake's waves, which outrun the tsunami (A42).
- **Peninsular India is not safe:** Koyna 1967, Latur 1993, Bhuj 2001 (A21).
- **Zone count:** NCERT five, BIS four (A23). A "Zone VI" circulates in coaching pages: not a law now (A24, A25).
- **Numbers that differ by source:** 2004 magnitude 9.0/9.1/9.3 (A45), toll about 2.3 lakh (A47), India 10,749 dead plus 5,640 missing (A48), Bhuj 6.9/7.7/7.9 (A60).
- **NCERT errors/outdated lines:** "Bill 2005" (A63), no early warning in the Indian Ocean and India "volunteered to join" (A51), 150 m record (A40), Indira Point (A49).

## A4. What has changed since the NCERT text, with dates

- NDMA guidelines: earthquakes Apr 2007, tsunamis Aug 2010 (A55, A56).

## D. Material for understanding and fun (every item has a claim id)
**Everyday examples**
- A phone SMS from the state emergency centre in a warning (A54, A51); NCS publishes within minutes the place and magnitude of any M3+ tremor, with a map of felt shaking (A14, A29).
- Standing on the shore vs on a ship: the ship feels nothing, the coast gets a wall of water (A38, A39).
- Why a small earthquake feels worse in an old building than a big one far away: intensity v magnitude (A10).
- Home safety: shelter under a table or desk, away from windows and mirrors (A31).
**India today (dated)**
- 170 stations, detects M3.0+ (1 Apr 2026) (A14). Zone map IS 1893(Part 1):2016 stays (A24). ITEWC alerts 24 hours a day and serves the Indian Ocean rim (A51, A52). Two Odisha villages Tsunami Ready since 2020 (A57). NDMA says about 59% of the land is in zones III-V (A26).
**Surprising true facts**
- A tsunami in deep water moves as fast as a jet, over 500 mph, but is barely a metre tall (A37, A38).
- Seismic waves are about 100 times faster than a tsunami, which is what makes any warning possible (A42).
- Each step in magnitude is 10 times the shaking but about 32 times the energy (A11).
- The 2004 quake was felt as magnitude 9.1; a 9.1 releases about 1,000 times the energy of a 7.1 (A11, A45; our arithmetic).
- The Bhuj quake hit at about 08:46 on Republic Day morning (A60; our conversion from USGS time).
- The sea may pull back before the wave arrives; in 2004 crowds went to the beach to look (A41).
- A 1945 Makran quake sent waves to Mumbai (about 2 m) and the Gulf of Khambhat (about 11 m, estimated) (A44).
**Short real stories**
- Simeulue island: folklore handed down saved many in 2004 (A50, hedged).
- Gujarat created its disaster authority within two weeks of the quake (8 Feb 2001), and built a memorial where nameplates sit inside check-dams that recharge water (A62).
- Odisha's villages Venkatraipur and Noliasahi: drills and maps earned UNESCO recognition in 2020 (A57).
**Common questions and confusions**
- Can scientists predict earthquakes? No (A30). Can they warn of a tsunami? Yes, after the quake, because waves are slower than seismic waves (A42, A51).
- Is Richter still used? USGS reports moment magnitude now (A12); newspapers still say Richter.
- Why does Delhi have tremors from the Hindu Kush? Not covered by a source read; the NCS home page lists Hindu Kush reports, but no explanation was read. Do not answer without a source.
- Is a tsunami the same as a tidal wave? No (A33).
**Guess-first and spot-the-mistake (answers sourced)**
1. "Which is faster, a tsunami in the open ocean or near the beach?" Open ocean, jet speed; near land about car speed (A37).
2. "How much more energy is a magnitude 8 than a 7?" About 32 times (A11).
3. True or false: a tsunami is a tidal wave. False (A33).
4. Spot the mistake: "Tsunami speed is higher in shallow water" (NCERT Class 11 p.59). False; deeper is faster (A37).
5. "Which is bigger, magnitude or intensity?" Not comparable: one is energy, one is effects at a place (A10).
6. "How many seismic zones does BIS use today?" Four, II to V, per the 2016 standard (A23, A24).
7. "The sea suddenly pulls back from the beach. What do you do?" Go to high ground at once (A41).
8. "Which date: Bhuj quake?" 26 Jan 2001, 08:46 IST (A60).
