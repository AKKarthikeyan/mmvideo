## 1. What the topic is, and the order to meet it
A volcano is a vent where magma, gas and ash reach the surface (V001, V003); the planet loses heat that way, about 47 TW in all through the surface (V031). Daily-life reach: volcanic soil feeds farms, including India's black soil on the Deccan basalt (V054, V066); a big eruption can cool the whole planet for a few years (V060, V061); ash grounds aircraft (V063); lahars and tsunamis kill far from the crater (V059, V062). Order for a learner:
1. What comes out: lava, bombs, ash, gases, and why magma is not lava until it erupts (V003-V008).
2. Why some erupt gently and some explode: runny v sticky lava, gas pressure (V009, V010).
3. Shapes it builds: shield, composite, cinder cone, caldera, flood basalt, ridge (V011-V018).
4. What stays underground: batholith, laccolith, lapolith, phacolith, sill, dyke (V019-V023).
5. Where and why: plate boundaries, Ring of Fire, hotspots, ridges (V024-V029); the heat behind it (V030-V032); the layers magma comes from (V034-V038).
6. India's volcanoes and the Deccan story (V043-V055).
7. Effects and benefits (V056-V068).
Interior of the Earth, earthquakes and weathering/soils are other episodes' ground (GEO-01, GEO-03, GEO-05, GEO-14); only links are used.

## 2. Hard parts and misconceptions (each with the correct position)
- **Magma v lava.** Magma is underground; it is lava only after eruption (V003, USGS). NCERT p.23 calls magma "lava" once it starts moving up: looser.
- **"Active" has no single meaning.** NCERT: erupted in the recent past; USGS 1997: erupted in recorded history (V002). The Smithsonian test is BLOCKED.
- **What comes out.** NCERT lists lava, pyroclastic debris, bombs, ash, dust, nitrogen and sulphur compounds, chlorine, hydrogen, argon (V004). USGS says more than 90% of volcanic gas is steam, then CO2, SO2, H2S, H2, fluorine (V005). UPSC 2024 Q3 follows the NCERT list; the answer (d) is our inference, UPSC's key not read (V006).
- **NCERT errors found:** Kola borehole is on land in Russia, not "in Arctic Ocean" (V039); the ridge length is not an NCERT error: sources give three figures, USGS "more than 50,000 km", NOAA about 65,000 km, NCERT "more than 70,000 km" (V017, CONFLICT after the audit, 3 Nov 2026); "Integrated Ocean Drilling Project" is a Programme and has moved on (V041); NCERT's "lacolith" is laccolith; "chlorene" is chlorine; "Pinatoba / El Cion" are Pinatubo / El Chichon (V061). NCERT Ch5 pp.38-39 sends the reader to a "preceding chapter" on igneous rocks that was deleted in 2022 (V032).
- **Lonar is not a volcano.** It is a meteorite crater cut in Deccan basalt (V055).
- **A mud volcano is not a magma volcano** (our inference; UNVERIFIED, V049).
- **Deccan area.** Authors disagree (about 0.5 to 0.8 million km2 now) (V052); Sen's "55,000 years" is a model estimate (V053).
- **Bottle analogy.** USGS's fizzy-drink picture for gas-driven explosions is an analogy; it stops where magma gas comes out of the melt as pressure falls, not by shaking (V009; the limit is ours).

## 4. What has changed since NCERT was written (dated)
- Drilling programmes: Integrated Ocean Drilling Program 2003-2013, International Ocean Discovery Program from 2013, IODP3 from January 2025 (ECORD, 12 Mar 2024) (V041). LIVING.
- Barren Island's activity after the 2021 paper (erupting vigorously from 2018 per Gunda et al.) could not be read (V045, V047, BLOCKED). LIVING.
- Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha'apai erupted 15 Jan 2022 (V064). Tungurahua became a UNESCO Global Geopark in 2025 (V056).
- USGS Fact Sheet 002-97 is marked superseded by FS 2018-3075 (full text not opened) (V005, V063).
- NCERT Class XI Fundamentals of Physical Geography is the Rationalised Edition (Dec 2022): Ch3 lost only the activity "Locating an Epicentre"; the old Ch5 Minerals and Rocks and the Class XI soils chapter were removed (BookletClass11.txt).
- New NCERT books carry volcano material: Grade 9 (June 2026) Ch2, Grade 6 (2025) Ch3 (V024, V025, V049, V050).

## Part D. Material for understanding and fun (all sourced by claim id)
- **Everyday pictures:** fizzy drink with a thumb on the top (V009, USGS's own analogy); the boiling pot with a shaking lid (V069, NCERT Class VII, older book); black cotton soil under the farmland of the Deccan (V054); geothermal power (V066).
