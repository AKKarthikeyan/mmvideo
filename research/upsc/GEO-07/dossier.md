# GEO-07 dossier: The monsoon, how a whole subcontinent breathes

Research only. Read 2 Nov 2026. Claim ids (M001...) are in `claims.csv`; depth items (K001...) in `checklist.csv`.

## 1. What the topic is, and the order to meet it
The monsoon is India's yearly swing of winds and rain: a rainy season of about four months that brings 74.9% of the year's rain (M046), and a second, smaller rainy season in the south-east (Oct-Dec). A learner should meet the ideas in this order:
1. What "monsoon" means: seasonal wind reversal, from *mausim* (M001-M002).
2. The simple picture every school book gives: land heats faster than sea, so a low forms and moist air flows in (M003-M006).
3. Why that picture is not enough (see 2 below) and what current science puts in its place: the rain belt (ITCZ / convergence zone) moves north with the sun, pulling in the south-east trades across the equator (Somali jet, Mascarene High), under the Himalaya's shelter; the heat low and the monsoon trough are its surface marks (M011-M021, M024-M026).
4. The calendar: Andaman Sea in late May, Kerala 1 June, whole country 8 July, withdrawal from the north-west from 17 Sept, exit about 15 Oct; two branches (Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal) (M027-M043).
5. The rain map: Western Ghats and rain shadow, north-east hills, Rajasthan, and why Tamil Nadu gets most rain in Oct-Dec (M056-M063).
6. How the rain is spread in time: active and break spells, depressions (M066-M072).
7. Year-to-year change and what "normal" means: LPA 868.6 mm, five categories, El Nino, IOD, decadal swings, climate change (M044-M085).

## 2. Hard parts and misconceptions
- **"The monsoon is a giant sea breeze."** NCERT Grade 9 (June 2026, p.49) and Class XI both lead with it. Gadgil (J. Earth Syst. Sci. 2018, pp.8-11, 26-27) shows India is hotter in May than July, the hottest north-west gets no monsoon rain, and land temperature is *lower* in good years (r = -0.38, 1950-2014). Geen et al. (Rev. Geophys. 2020, pp.1, 3, 32) report that theory now supports the migrating-convergence-zone picture, but they note mountains and continent shape are still outside it. Position to teach: land-sea heating is the classic first idea and a useful start, but it is not regarded as the full cause. Status CONFLICT (M010). Note the sea breeze itself is real as a daily local wind (M005).
- **Hotter land does not mean more rain.** (M008-M009.)
- **Tibetan Plateau.** IMD (2025 report p.36; RR 3/2020 p.6) calls the plateau an elevated heat source. Boos & Kuang (Nature 2010) find the monsoon survives removal of the plateau if the Himalaya stays, so the mountain wall matters as a barrier to cold dry air. CONFLICT (M024). NCERT Class XI never mentions the plateau.
- **Jet streams.** NCERT says the westerly jet withdrawing and an easterly jet setting in along 15 N "cause" the burst. No source read supports this as cause; IMD's onset test ignores jets (M022, UNVERIFIED). The Tropical Easterly Jet itself is real (M021).
- **Tamil Nadu is not "dry" in the monsoon:** it gets about 35% of its rain then and about 48% in Oct-Dec as IMD prints them (its own mm figures work out to 36% and 47%; use mm or 'about a third / almost half') (M060-M061).
- **"Normal" is a band, not a number.** IMD's present band is 96-104% of the 1971-2020 average (868.6 mm); an older IMD FAQ still says 90-110% and IMD's 2025 report calls 108% "normal" (M048-M050).
- **A break is not just "a dry week."** IMD and Rajeevan et al. define it by the trough position or a core-zone rainfall anomaly (M066-M067).
- **El Nino is not a guarantee of drought** (1997) (M074). NCERT's El Nino box (10 C, "replaced by cold Peruvian current") disagrees with its own sister book (M076).

## 3. Debates and who holds which view
| Question | View A | View B |
|---|---|---|
| Basic cause | Land-sea thermal contrast (Halley 1686; Webster 1987 and others named by Gadgil; most textbooks) | Seasonal migration of the ITCZ/equatorial trough (Blanford 1886 roots; Gadgil 2003, 2018; Geen et al. 2020) |
| Role of Tibet | Elevated heat source (Murakami 1987; Wu et al. 2007, as cited by IMD) | Orographic insulation by the Himalaya and neighbouring ranges (Boos & Kuang 2010) |
| ENSO's grip | IMD: ENSO is a primary driver, IOD and others modify it | Gadgil 2003 p.431 cites Kumar et al. 1999 that the link weakened in the 1990s (second-hand) |
| Whether a heat low helps | IMD FAQ: a deep heat low "suction" is linked to good monsoons | Gadgil 2018: hotter land goes with weaker monsoons (different variable, pressure vs temperature) |

## 4. What changed since NCERT was written (dated)
- Normal dates: whole country 8 July (NCERT: mid-July), NW withdrawal from 17 Sept (NCERT: first week of Sept); proposed in IMD report of 15 May 2020, in use in IMD's 2025 and 2026 reports (M028-M033). IMD's own onset web page still shows 15 July.
- Normal rain: 868.6 mm for 1971-2020 (was 880.6 mm for 1961-2010); annual 1160.1 mm vs NCERT's "125 cm" (M044-M045).
- Forecasting: statistical ensemble plus coupled models since 2021, not "16 indicators" (M079). RONI replaced ONI at NOAA and IMD in 2026 (M077).
- Latest seasons: 2025 108% of LPA; 2026 87% (strong El Nino, neutral IOD), as of 30 Sep 2026 (M054-M055, M080).
- Climate: IMD says June-September rain fell about 6% in 50 years; AR6 attributes the late-20th-century weakening mainly to aerosols and projects a rise, but also says observed changes were 'limited over much of the 20th century' as greenhouse-gas and aerosol effects offset (M083-M084; M084 UNVERIFIED until the published chapter is read).
- Rationalisation: NCERT removed the winter/summer weather mechanism, rain-bearing systems and variability sections of Class XI Ch4 in 2022 (exact survivors UNVERIFIED, M092).
- A new NCERT Grade 9 book (June 2026) now carries the monsoon; it uses the new normal-date maps but teaches only the heating explanation.

## 5. Sensitive points (PLAN.md section 8)
- **Maps:** the monsoon map covers Pakistan, Bangladesh, Myanmar and Sri Lanka. India's boundary must follow the Survey of India map; do not draw the normal-date lines across disputed areas. Rainfall zone maps must show Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh as in the official map.
- **NCERT copyright:** do not reproduce NCERT figures 4.2-4.4 or Grade 9 Figs 3.9-3.11; draw our own maps from IMD data.
- **Agriculture dependence:** NCERT 64% / Class X "two-thirds" / IMD "nearly half": do not state a figure until MoSPI PLFS is read (M085).
- **Distress and drought:** no politics; avoid blaming a state for deficits; 2026 deficit is real and recent, state it as IMD's number.
- **Investing language:** none needed.
