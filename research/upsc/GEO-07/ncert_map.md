# GEO-07 NCERT map: The monsoon

Read 2 Nov 2026. Page numbers are the printed page numbers of each book. All chapters are in `research/upsc/_ncert/` except the two marked "sources/".

## Main chapter
**Class XI, India: Physical Environment, Chapter 4 "Climate" (kegy104), printed pp. 28-41.** Textbook code 11094. First edition March 2006; **Revised Edition February 2022**; reprinted March 2024, January 2025, **March 2026** (front pages, kegy1.ps). Unit III opener (p.27) says the unit covers "Indian monsoons: mechanism, onset and variability".
Covers: unity and diversity of the monsoon climate; factors (latitude, Himalaya, land and water, distance from sea, altitude, relief); ITCZ box; nature of the monsoon, onset, entry dates, break, El Nino box; four seasons; cold, hot, south-west monsoon and retreating seasons; the two branches; Tamil Nadu coast; traditional seasons; rainfall distribution; monsoon and economic life; global warming; exercises.
What it does not teach: Somali jet, Mascarene High, heat low as a named idea, Tibetan Plateau, Indian Ocean Dipole, MJO, the IMD's onset criteria, what "normal rainfall" means, any date for the normal period.

## Where the books differ
1. **Cause of the monsoon.** Grade 9 (p.49) gives one cause, unequal heating of land and sea. Class XI (pp.29-31) gives land-sea heating, then the ITCZ shift, jet streams and El Nino, and admits (p.30) that no single theory explains it. Class XI Fundamentals (p.81) treats the sea breeze as a daily local wind.
2. **Normal dates.** Class XI says the whole country is covered by mid-July and shows the old map (Fig 4.2, p.32). Grade 9 Figs 3.10-3.11 show 8 July, 17 September and 15 October, which match IMD's new normals. Two NCERT books disagree on the same fact.
3. **North-east monsoon.** Class XI calls October-November the "retreating monsoon" (p.37) and lists the Tamil Nadu coast as a winter-rain exception (p.34). Grade 9 (p.52) says the north-east monsoon runs October to February. IMD uses October to December.
4. **Seasons.** Grade 9 (p.47) puts winter at December to early April. IMD's statistical seasons are Jan-Feb, Mar-May, Jun-Sep, Oct-Dec.
5. **El Nino.** Class XI (p.31) says the warm current is "replaced" by the cold Peruvian current; Fundamentals (p.80) says warm water replaces the cool Peruvian current.
