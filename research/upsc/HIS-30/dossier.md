# HIS-30 Plassey and Buxar: how a company got a kingdom (research dossier)

Read 2 Nov 2026. Evidence is in `claims.csv` (83 rows: 60 CONFIRMED, 12 CONFLICT, 1 CORRECTED, 10 UNVERIFIED, 0 BLOCKED rows; blocked sites are in the search log). Claim ids below look like C043.

## 1. What the topic is, and the order to meet it
A trading company with a monopoly and a few forts ended up holding the revenue of three provinces in eight years (1757-65). The ideas have to arrive in this order:
1. **Why a company and a nawab could clash at all** (C023, C025). The Company's goods moved duty-free by an old imperial grant for Rs 3,000 a year; its servants stretched that to private trade; the nawab lost revenue; Calcutta was fortified without his leave.
2. **1756**: Siraj-ud-daula takes power (10 April), takes Kasimbazar and Calcutta (June) (C024-C027). The Black Hole belongs here but is disputed (C028-C035).
3. **Calcutta retaken, treaty of Feb 1757, Chandernagore** (C036-C038): the Company shows it can win, Siraj shows he cannot trust it.
4. **The court faction and the plot**: Mir Jafar, Jagat Seth, Rai Durlabh; Omichund and the second treaty (C039, C042).
5. **Plassey, 23 June 1757** (C043-C055): few men fighting, a commander who stood aside. What it gave: Mir Jafar as nawab and sums of money (C039-C041).
6. **Mir Jafar, then Mir Kasim, then Mir Jafar again** (C057-C063). The internal-trade quarrel is the real cause of the break (C060).
7. **Buxar, Oct 1764** (C064-C068): a larger fight than Plassey, against the emperor and the Nawab of Oudh as well.
8. **Allahabad, Aug 1765 and the diwani** (C069-C075): the new word. Diwani is the office of the revenue; the Company now collects it, pays the emperor 26 lakh and the nawab his allowance, and keeps the rest; the nawab keeps the form of rule.

## 2. Hard parts and misconceptions
- **Diwani is not sovereignty.** The farman gives the office of revenue "from generation to generation"; the nawab keeps the nizamat for an allowance of Rs 53.86 lakh; the emperor gets Rs 26 lakh (C069-C071, C075). The "power without responsibility" problem that follows leads to HIS-33. Whether civil justice was inside diwani is **not settled by the farman text**; leave it for HIS-33.
- **"Treaty of Allahabad" is three or four papers**, not one: the farman (12 Aug), the treaty with Shuja-ud-daula (16 Aug, Rs 50 lakh, Kora and Allahabad to the emperor) and the agreements of 19 and 30 Aug (C069-C072).
- **The older NCERT order is wrong**: Mir Jafar was restored in July 1763, before Buxar, not after (C011, CORRECTED).
- **Plassey as "battle"**: the Company's own report puts its losses at 22 killed and 50 wounded, Hill's tally gives fewer, and the Nawab's side lost, in Clive's estimate, about 500 (C048-C049; Hill repeats Clive, no independent count). The decisive fact was who did not fight (C050).
- **Black Hole figures**: five accounts, five counts (C028-C033). It is the one famous episode that no NCERT book teaches (our own text search of the NCERT files, 2 Nov 2026).
- **Money**: the treaty said Rs 1.77 crore (1 crore + 50 + 20 + 7 lakh); Clive found about Rs 150 lakh in the treasury and took half at once (C039-C040). Clive told Parliament he received about Rs 16 lakh himself; Parliament's 1773 resolution counted at least Rs 20.8 lakh (its third head is '16 lacs or more'; single secondary source, Malcolm) (C041, C076). IGNOU Unit 8 prints Rs 17.5 lakh; that looks like a slip and must not be used (C079).

## 3. Debates, with named views
- **Why the nawabs lost** (IGNOU Unit 8 sec. 8.4, unattributed): (a) personal failings of the nawabs (the unit rejects this as the main cause); (b) the Company's commercial push and servants' private trade; (c) calculations of Indian elites (the Seths, Raj Ballabh, Mir Jafar and others) who wanted a different nawab. H.H. Dodwell (CUP, 1929) writes that English and nawab interests were "irreconcilable" once the nawab claimed independence (C060). Modern university-press views (Marshall, Chaudhury) were **not reachable**.
- **The Black Hole**: J.Z. Holwell (1758, a survivor) gives 146 in and 123 dead; his own letter of July 1756 gives about 165-170 in and about 16 out; Capt. Mills gives 144 in; another account says "near 200" in and fewer than ten out; the Seir's English translator says it was not known in Bengal; J.H. Little (1915-16) doubted Holwell; Dodwell (1929) says the outline stands but no one can fix the number (C028-C034). S.C. Hill (1905) accepts Holwell's 146 and 23.
- **Was the diwani a gift or a price?** The farman's wording is a free gift for services (C069); Dodwell says Clive demanded it "as the price of Allahabad" (C073); the Seir says the emperor and Wazir were "overawed" and granted it reluctantly, and gives the tribute as 24 lakh where the farman says 26 (C074).
- **Wealth and the Industrial Revolution** (current NCERT: Brooks Adams 1895, Utsa Patnaik's 45 trillion dollars): contested thesis, **not examined here**; belongs to ECO-02 (C081).

## 4. What has changed since NCERT, and the current position
- Nothing living: this is fixed history; `docs/upsc/living/HIS-30.csv` is not needed.
- What has changed is the **textbook**: NCERT replaced the older Class 8 account with a much shorter, more evaluative one in 2025 (current Class 8 book, reprint 2026-27). The older book (reprint 2024-25) is still on NCERT's server (last modified May 2024). Both are recorded in `ncert_map.md`. A viewer may hold either. The episode should teach the whole sequence and name the textbook difference only where it matters (older book: sequence to the diwani; newer: framing as "divide and rule").
- The new book's phrase "Mir Jafar remains a synonym for traitor" is a cultural statement; if shown, attribute it to the textbook (C010).

## 5. Sensitive points for the guardrails (PLAN section 8)
- **Contested-history rule**: no villain or hero. Siraj, Mir Jafar, Clive, Holwell and Mir Kasim each acted for stated reasons that are in the record. Say "Mir Jafar did not fight" rather than the word "traitor"; show the Black Hole as "accounts differ".
- **Religion**: the new NCERT line that the British "encouraged tensions between religious communities" is **not tested here** (no source read supports or refutes it); do not state it as fact. Characters are drawn as people, with no religious figures.
- **Violence**: killing of Siraj (2 July 1757), Mir Kasim's killing of English prisoners at Patna (1763) and the Black Hole are real; show them without gore.
- **Maps**: a historical map of Bengal 1757-65 includes places now in Bangladesh (Dacca, Chittagong). Draw it as an old-period map with no modern international boundaries, and keep any modern outline of India to the Survey of India version.
- **Quotations**: use only Clive's Pitt letter (C018) and treaty/farman wording; do not put "We must indeed become nawabs ourselves" or "astonished at my own moderation" on screen until sourced (C013, C017).
- **No implied tie to UPSC** per the plan.

## 6. One video or a series
**Two parts, about 12-15 minutes each.** The checklist has 59 items (35 for Part 1, 24 for Part 2) and three hard ideas (the plot, the numbers, diwani versus nizamat).
- **Part 1 "Siraj to Plassey"** (syllabus HIS2-1): grievances, 1756, the Black Hole dispute, the plot, Plassey, the sums.
- **Part 2 "Mir Jafar to the diwani"** (HIS2-2 plus diwani): Mir Jafar and Mir Kasim, internal trade, Buxar, Allahabad, diwani versus nizamat. It ends where HIS-33 begins.
Squeezing both into one video would drop either the numbers (Part 1) or diwani versus nizamat (Part 2), which are what the exam asks.
