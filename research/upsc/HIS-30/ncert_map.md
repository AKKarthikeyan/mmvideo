**Differences that matter for the script**
1. The sequence Mir Jafar - Mir Qasim - Buxar - diwani exists in full only in the **older** book. A student with the current book will not have met Mir Qasim, Buxar or the word diwani.
2. The older book's order (Mir Qasim, then Buxar, then Mir Jafar "reinstated") is not what the treaties show: Mir Jafar was restored by the treaty of 10 July 1763, before Buxar (claim H30-C011, status CORRECTED).
3. The current book states some figures and phrases I could not confirm at source: the 150 km, the "majority" of the army, the 1770-72 toll and the Patnaik estimate (all UNVERIFIED). The older book's quoted line "We must indeed become nawabs ourselves", the fortune figure and the "astonished at my own moderation" remark are also UNVERIFIED.
4. The older book's boxed Clive letter to Pitt is genuine and checks at source (Malcolm 1836).

## Rationalisation

- **Our Pasts III (hess202) and Class 12 Themes III (lehs301):** each has a "Rationalisation of Content in the Textbooks" page giving only criteria (overlap, difficulty, easily self-learned, irrelevant now) and says the edition is a "reformatted version" after those changes. **Neither page lists chapters or topics deleted.** I could not identify any Plassey, Buxar or diwani item removed from either book. Whether the 2008 edition of Our Pasts III said more on this ground was not checked (UNVERIFIED).
- **Exploring Society Part 1 (hees104):** a new-curriculum book (NCF-SE 2023, per its front pages); no rationalisation page applies.

## What this topic needs that NCERT does not cover

- Siraj's accession, the fortification and dastak quarrel in detail, and the Krishna Das affair.
- The fall of Calcutta, the Black Hole and how its figures differ between accounts.
- The 1757 treaty with Siraj, the retaking of Calcutta, Chandernagore.
- The conspiracy in detail (Jagat Seth, Rai Durlabh, Omichund, the second treaty).
- The treaty articles with Mir Jafar (Rs 1.77 crore) and what was actually paid.
- Numbers on each side at Plassey and Buxar, casualties.
- Mir Kasim's treaty, move to Monghyr, the internal-trade quarrel, the 1763 war, Patna.
- The three instruments of August 1765 (emperor's farman, 19 August agreement, treaty with Shuja-ud-daula), the 26 lakh payment, the nizamat allowance.
- The idea of diwani versus nizamat.
- Historiography: the Black Hole dispute; causes of the 1757-65 "revolution"; Plassey versus Buxar as turning points.
