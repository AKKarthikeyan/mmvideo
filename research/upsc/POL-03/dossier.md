## 1. What the topic is, why it matters, and the order to meet the ideas

**The question for a viewer's day:** what can you do when the State, or someone else, treats you unfairly: a police arrest, a refused seat, a leaked phone record, a closed shop? Part III of the Constitution answers with a short list of promises and, in the same breath, with their limits.

Learners should meet the ideas in this order.
1. **What a right is** (a justified claim; NCERT keps1 5.1-5.5, K01-K05) and why a constitution lists some: the Fundamental Rights are protected by the Constitution itself, not by ordinary law (K06, A04).
2. **Who is bound and what is "law"**: Article 12 (the wide "State") and Article 13 (laws that break Part III are void; "law" includes an Ordinance, a rule, even custom or usage; A01-A07). The tests in UPSC Prelims 2026 Q54 and Prelims 2025 sit here (A05, A09).
3. **Who gets which right**: some for every person (Arts 14, 21 and others), some for citizens only (Arts 15, 16, 19, 29; A10-A12).
4. **Equality** (Arts 14-18) and then **the reservation debate** as the hard test of equality: Art. 16(4), Mandal, Indra Sawhney, the 50% rule, creamy layer, promotions, the 103rd Amendment and Janhit Abhiyan, sub-classification (B01-B13, C01-C25).
5. **Freedom** (Arts 19-22): six freedoms with their limits (D01-D06), the accused (D07), life and liberty: Gopalan to Maneka Gandhi to Puttaswamy (D08-D15), the widening of Art. 21 (education, livelihood, water; D16-D19, D24), arrest and detention (D20-D22), Emergency (D23).
6. **Exploitation** (Arts 23-24; E01-E04), **religion** (Arts 25-28; F01-F09), **cultural and educational rights** (Arts 29-30; G01-G03).
7. **The limits and the remedy**: Arts 31A-31C and the Ninth Schedule, 33-34, and a pointer to Art. 32/226 (H01-H06); the five writs belong to the next episode.
8. **Part IV and Part IVA** (Directive Principles and duties; I01-I08) and how Parts III and IV were reconciled (I06, J01-J03).

## 2. The hard parts and common mistakes (correct position, with source)

- **"Every Indian citizen has these rights."** Wrong in both directions: Arts 14 and 21 are for any person; Art. 19 only for citizens (A10-A12, NCERT hees2 p.147 CORRECTED).
- **"Article 21 means the law can do anything if it follows a procedure."** That was the Gopalan view (1950). In Maneka Gandhi (1978, seven judges) the procedure must be right, just, fair and not arbitrary; Puttaswamy (2017) adds legality, legitimate aim and proportionality (D09-D13). NCERT Grade 9's gloss is the old view (D11).
- **"The Constitution gives a right to privacy."** The word does not appear in the text (zero hits, L05). Nine judges read it into Art. 21 and Part III on 24 Aug 2017; M.P. Sharma and Kharak Singh were overruled to the extent they said otherwise (D12).
- **"An Ordinance is not a law."** For Part III it is (A05, A09).
- **"Fundamental Rights can never be amended."** They can, but not so as to destroy the basic structure (A07, A08; details in POL-06).
- **"The Right to Education came in 2009."** The RTE Act is 2009; Art. 21A is from the 86th Amendment of 2002, in force 1 Apr 2010 (D16, D17 CORRECTED).
- **"Reservation cannot cross 50%."** The 50% limit comes from the lead judgment in Indra Sawhney (1992) for Art. 16(4), with room for "extraordinary situations"; the Maratha quota beyond it was rejected in 2021; the 10% EWS quota sits on top of it by constitutional amendment, upheld 3:2 in 2022 where the majority said the ceiling is not inflexible and applies to the 15(4), 15(5), 16(4) classes (C04, C10, C13). Never state a single "percentage reserved" without date and level (C24, UNVERIFIED).
- **"The Court said X" for reservation cases.** Say who: Indra Sawhney's lead judgment is by Jeevan Reddy J. for four judges; promotion bar: Ahmadi J. did not join (C03-C06). Janhit Abhiyan was 3:2 with four separate opinions (C12). Davinder Singh was 6:1; the creamy-layer passage is Gavai J.'s own opinion, not the Chief Justice's (C15, C16).
- **"Property is a Fundamental Right."** It was, until 20 Jun 1979; now Art. 300A (D04, J01). NCERT's line about the 1973 case is a CONFLICT (J02).
- **"Water is a Fundamental Right in the Constitution."** By judicial reading of Art. 21 (Subhash Kumar, 1991), not by text (D24 CORRECTED).
- **The old NCERT sentence on preventive detention** is right on the text (three months, then an Advisory Board; D21) but the Constitution's own footnote shows the 44th Amendment change is still "date to be notified" (D21b).

## 3. Debates and who holds which view

- **Is reservation an exception to equality or a part of it?** The lead judgment in Indra Sawhney (four judges) says Art. 16(4) is an instance of the classification inside Art. 16(1) (C08). NCERT keps2 says the same in plain words (B09). Bhat J. (dissent, Janhit Abhiyan) holds that economic criteria alone are fine for Art. 15 (access to public goods) but not for Art. 16, and that excluding poor SC/ST/OBC from the EWS quota breaks the basic structure (C14). The majority (Maheshwari, Trivedi, Pardiwala) upheld the amendment (C12, C13).
- **Should the creamy layer apply to SCs and STs?** Gavai J. says the State must evolve a policy (C16). Indra Sawhney applied it to OBCs only (C05). Jarnail Singh (2018) removed the data-on-backwardness requirement for SC/ST promotion (C09). The other 2024 opinions (Vikram Nath, Mithal, Sharma) were not read in full.
- **Who may decide the backward-class list?** The five judges split in 2021: Bhat J.'s reading is that only the President can, after the 102nd Amendment; Parliament restored State lists by the 105th Amendment (C11).
- **Minority rights and secularism.** NCERT Grade 8 says "some people feel" minority protections clash with secularism (F09). The Constitution's answer is Arts 29-30 (G01, G02). Teach the debate, name no side.
- **Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles.** NCERT keps2: courts first held rights were too sacred to be limited for Directive Principles; Minerva Mills (1980, majority) says the balance between Parts III and IV is itself a basic feature (I06).

## 5. Sensitive points (PLAN guardrails)

- **Caste and reservation.** Law and judgments only. Name no community in a way that mocks it; show the Court's split honestly; no party politics (the Mandal period had violent protests: keep to the record). PoA Act examples are graphic; do not dramatise.
- **Religion.** Conversion, "essential religious practices", Art. 44 (uniform civil code), Art. 48 (cattle) and minority institutions are contested. Teach the text, then say the matter is argued. The Sabarimala, hijab and AMU cases were not opened.
- **Sexual orientation.** Puttaswamy mentions it inside privacy (conclusion 3(F)); Navtej Johar and the Transgender Act are not covered.
- **Real private persons.** NCERT names Machal Lalung, Rathnam, a letter writer in Puri: use NCERT's framing and "adapted" wording; second source for Machal Lalung NOT FOUND.
- **Pending matters.** None previewed as results.
- **Maps.** None used.

- **Part 1 (as in the plan):** why rights; Arts 12-13; equality (14-18); the reservation debate (largest block, about 25 claims; promotion, creamy layer, 103rd Amendment, sub-classification); the PYQ on Art. 13 (A05).
- **Part 2:** freedom (19-22), privacy, the widening of Art. 21, arrest, detention, Emergency, exploitation (23-24); the PYQ on privacy (D12).
- **Part 3:** religion, cultural and educational rights, the pointer to remedies (the five writs are POL-04), limits (31A-31C, 33-34), Directive Principles, duties, right to property.
If the series stays at two parts, Part 2 would carry about 70 claims (Arts 19-35, Part IV, property), which the Gold Standard forbids cutting (D1); the overflow would have to move into a third part. The decision on the split is for the lead.
