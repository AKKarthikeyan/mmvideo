# POL-06 NCERT map: "Basic Structure: the case that limited Parliament"

Read on 2 Nov 2026 from the PDFs and text in `research/upsc/_ncert/`. Page numbers are the printed page numbers. Own words only; no NCERT text is reproduced beyond a few words.

## Main chapter

**Indian Constitution at Work (Class XI), Chapter 9, "Constitution as a Living Document" (keps209), pp.196-218.**
- Book: Textbook in Political Science for Class XI, code 11102. First edition March 2006; many reprints; this copy is **Reprint 2026-27** (last listed printing February 2026). Front pages: `keps2.ps.txt`.
- Why it is the main chapter: it is the only NCERT chapter that covers the amending procedure (Art. 368), the reasons for the number of amendments, and the basic structure doctrine in one place.
- Covers: why constitutions change (pp.197-199); how to amend, with the three routes, special majority and State ratification (pp.199-204); why so many amendments and the four kinds (pp.205-210); basic structure and its three effects (pp.211-212); the Venkatachaliah review (p.212); living document, letter and spirit, Minerva Mills (pp.213-216); conclusion and exercises (pp.216-218).
- Dated or doubtful items in this chapter: (a) 106 amendments as of 26 Jan 2024 (p.205), while pp.196-197 still say "last 69 years"; (b) Lok Sabha of 545 and "273 votes" (p.202), see claim POL06-H04; (c) "53 articles" changed by the 42nd Amendment (p.210) not reproduced from the Act, POL06-H11; (d) Venkatachaliah statement not checked, POL06-H02.

## Where NCERT editions differ or disagree

1. **Kesavananda and property.** Class XI ch.6 (p.142) says the Court held the right to property not part of basic structure. Class XI ch.9 (p.211) and Class XII ch.6 (p.97) say nothing like that and only state that some features cannot be amended. The judgment (signed summary) does not hold it either; Minerva Mills attributes the "matter of detail" idea to Khanna J.
2. **Count and years of amendments.** Ch.9 p.205 and p.207 were updated (2024 revision list) to 106 amendments and 74 years; p.196-197 in the same chapter still use the old "69 years". The Class VIII Part 2 book (2026) does not repeat the count.
3. **Depth.** Class VIII (2026) gives one sentence; Class XI gives the doctrine plus two plain-language case accounts; Class XII places it in the Emergency story. No NCERT book gives the case names Shankari Prasad, Sajjan Singh, Golaknath, Waman Rao, Coelho or NJAC. Golaknath is not named anywhere in the chapters read (the conflict is described only as "the third issue: scope of power to amend", Class XI ch.6 p.141).

## Rationalisation and revision notes (read at NCERT)

- **Class XI "Indian Constitution at Work" (11102): "No Changes"** in NCERT's List of Rationalised Content for Class XI (2022), booklet p.56. The ch.9 text in the 2026-27 reprint is the full chapter.
- **Class XII "Politics in India since Independence" (12119), ch.6:** the rationalisation list (2023) shows "Controversies regarding Emergency" (pp.113-117 of the pre-2022 printing) dropped. The Kesavananda paragraph itself stays.
- NCERT's separate "Revised and Updated Content in Social Sciences" (2024) list changes Class XI ch.9 only at pp.205 and 207 (106 amendments; 74 years) and ch.3/ch.4 for women's reservation, EWS and the 104th Amendment. It also records for Class VIII ("Social and Political Life III") that the Lok Sabha's total membership is 543.
- Class VIII Part 2 (hees204): first edition June 2026; `GOLD_STANDARD.md` records that an earlier issue was put on hold; I read only the June 2026 front pages and chapter, and the printed edition line says First Edition June 2026.

## What the topic needs that NCERT does not cover

- The cases and their benches: Shankari Prasad (1951), Sajjan Singh (1964), Golaknath (1967), the 24th, 25th, 29th Amendments, Kesavananda in detail (13 judges, 11 judgments, who held what), Indira Gandhi v Raj Narain (1975), Waman Rao (1980), I.R. Coelho (2007), NJAC (2015), Property Owners Association (2024).
- The actual Article 368 text, Art. 13(4), Art. 31B and the Ninth Schedule, Art. 31C and their status.
- The fact that **no authoritative list** exists and why (Minerva Mills).
- Constituent Assembly's design (Ambedkar, 17 Sep and 25 Nov 1949) beyond one quoted line.
- Judges' reasoning on the democratic argument (Hegde, Ray, Khanna) and the status of the signed summary.
- Anything after 2020: the 106th Amendment's commencement, the 131st Amendment Bill, the 2024 ruling on Art. 31C.
