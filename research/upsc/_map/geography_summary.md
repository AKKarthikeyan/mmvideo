# Geography subtopic map: summary

Built 3 Nov 2026 under `docs/upsc/MAPPING_BRIEF.md`. Mapping only: no scripts, no fact research. Data: `research/upsc/_map/geography_subtopics.csv`.

**Rows:** 842 NCERT headings and subheadings, 158 syllabus phrases, 124 past-paper question ideas.

**How to read the numbers.** NCERT word counts come from the PDF layout and are approximate (about 5 to 10 percent: boxes, captions and exercises are counted, and a heading's words run to the next heading). Several books teach the same idea (for example the monsoon appears in Classes 7, 9, 10 and 11), so summed words over-count. The parts figure is therefore given twice: `high` uses all words summed, `low` uses only the longest single book. Both are estimates at about 15 minutes per 1,500 NCERT words or per 8 unique subtopics, whichever is larger. Editors should pick within the range.

## 7. Where books disagree on structure or emphasis

- Monsoon cause and normal dates: Class 11 (kegy1) gives several causes and the old onset map; Grade 9 (iest1) gives land-sea heating only and IMD's newer normal dates (details in `GEO-07/ncert_map.md`).
- Soils: the Class 11 soils chapter is gone; Class 10 (jess1 ch1) and the Class 8 book (hess4 ch2) now carry soil types, and Grade 9 (iest1 ch2) teaches only soil conservation (bunding, terracing).
- Class 8 and 7 geography exists twice: the old books (hess4, gess2) and the new Exploring Society books (hees1, hees2, gees1, gees2) cover resources, weather and population with different chapter plans; the new books bring in world geography (hees2 ch1) and India's neighbours (gees2 ch2).
- Class 12 teaches transport, trade and population twice, once world-wide (legy1) and once for India (legy2). Class 12 manufacturing survives only in the Class 10 book.
- Population composition now sits only in legy2 ch1 (Population: Distribution, Density, Growth and Composition); legy1 lost its Population Composition chapter and legy2 lost its Migration chapter, so migration (syllabus Paper I and II) has almost no NCERT text left.
- Energy: Class 10 (jess1 ch5) and Class 12 (legy2 ch5) classify energy as conventional and non-conventional; the new Grade 8 book (hees1 ch1) classifies by renewability.

## 8. Past papers and syllabus: coverage and limits

- Only nine UPSC papers are on UPSC's page: Prelims GS I 2024-2026 and Mains GS I-III 2025-2026 (no 2024 Mains, no optional papers). 'Every idea tested' is true only for these nine.
- All nine PDFs are scans. Prelims English pages were re-read by OCR per column; question numbers for 2024 Q1-16 and Q67 were read from page images (pp.3, 5, 7, 29). Other numbers come from OCR sequence and are **unverified** by eye: 2024 Q38, Q79, Q88-90, Q97; most of 2025 and 2026 Prelims; Mains numbers are inferred from question order (2025 and 2026 GS I, GS III) and GS II Q9 (2026). Question text is paraphrased.
- Syllabus rows: Geography optional Paper I and II (PDF pp.65-67) plus the GS lines that name geography (Prelims p.32, Mains GS I p.34, GS III pp.35-36). Page numbers are the PDF page of the notice.
- Past-paper rows are mapped to the nearest topic; many are related rather than direct (marked in the note column). Four past-paper ideas fall in gap G1 (Earth's motions and time).

## 9. Checks run on the extraction

- Every NCERT row was tested against the chapter text: 95.5 percent of headings were found verbatim (the rest are headings split across lines by the layout; 39 noisy fragments were dropped).
- Printed page numbers: 93.6 percent of 763 rows with a readable page number match the page where the heading text is found (the rest are headings that also occur earlier, for example in a figure caption).
- Word conservation: the words under the kept headings are 97 percent (median) of each chapter's words (lowest 82 percent: running heads, boxes and exercises are excluded).
- Practical books (kegy3, legy3) and two chapters (legy103, jess103 sections) had headings that did not extract; they were set by hand from the chapter text (marked in the note column, page numbers there are estimates).
- Not done: no fact checking (brief says map only); NCERT text beyond headings is not copied.
