## 1. Official and court findings on evaluation

UPSC publishes no model answers and no marking scheme (see `UPSC_OFFICIAL.md`). What follows is what UPSC has told courts, the CIC and the public about how scripts are marked.

| # | Source (who says it) | What it says | URL / location |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **UPSC's reply, recited by Delhi High Court**, *Prashant Ramesh Chakkarwar v UPSC*, WP(C) 6586/2010 & connected, 5 Oct 2010 | Descriptive (conventional) papers are "manually evaluated" and "subjected to moderation and not scaling". The paper setter acts as Head Examiner. Each Additional Examiner marks about 250-300 books. Head and additional examiners meet after the exam, discuss "the question paper and the appropriate answers", do a sample valuation, then agree a standard. Head Examiner later re-checks 20 books per examiner (ten top-scoring, ten random) and moderates up or down for leniency or strictness; erratic examiners' scripts are revalued. Scripts carry dummy roll numbers. Across optional subjects, statistical "linear moderation" is applied. | https://indiankanoon.org/doc/14592858/ , the paragraphs numbered 16-25 in the quoted UPSC statement, pp. 12-14 of 43 in the judgment. (Court is reciting UPSC's own account; not the Court's finding on how good answers look.) |
| 2 | **Supreme Court**, *UPSC v Angesh Kumar*, Civil Appeals 6159-6162/2013 with 5924/2013, 20 Feb 2018 (Lalit and Goel JJ.) | Para 8 reproduces the same UPSC counter-affidavit (the problems of showing evaluated answer books, including that "relative merit and not absolute merit" is the criterion). Para 9: Court "approved the method of moderation adopted by the UPSC", citing *Sanjay Singh v UPPSC* (2007) 3 SCC 720 and *UPPSC v Subhash Chandra Dixit* (2003) 12 SCC 701. Para 10: marks need not be furnished mechanically; Court may order it if public interest requires. The case itself concerned Prelims 2010 marks; the quoted affidavit covers descriptive evaluation. | https://indiankanoon.org/doc/153104514/ (paras 8-10). The affidavit extract is para 8.2 per the CIC order in row 3. |
| 3 | **Central Information Commission**, *Nitish Shankar v UPSC*, CIC/UPSCM/A/2022/113179, 13 Oct 2022 | UPSC's CPIO told the applicant "Model Answers" for conventional papers "are not prepared/maintained by the Commission". CIC upheld non-disclosure of evaluated scripts, citing *Angesh Kumar*. | https://indiankanoon.org/doc/32025938/ (the "Point No. 2" reply and the Decision paragraph). |
| 4 | **UPSC Chairman Dr Ajay Kumar**, town hall on DD News, as **reported by Vision IAS** (blog dated 12 Nov 2025; secondary report, not a transcript) | For each subject "a detailed key is prepared": what points to look for. The key goes through 2-3 levels of expert vetting; every evaluator uses it; evaluators' work is reviewed by two levels of supervisors; then cross-subject moderation. | https://www.visionias.in/blog/officers-view/inside-upsc-100-key-takeaways-from-the-upsc-chairmans-historic-town-hall-with-aspirants-part-ii (section "On Mains Evaluation, Interview Variation & Moderation"). Primary DD News video/transcript: NOT FOUND. |
| 5 | **Parliamentary Standing Committee (2016) and UPSC's reply**, as reported by The Hans India, 17 Mar 2016 (news report of an official document) | Committee asked UPSC to consider publishing moderation details. UPSC declined: marking incl. moderation is "an integral component" of its evaluation process, given high confidentiality. | https://thehansindia.com/posts/index/Education-and-Careers/2016-03-17/Parl-panel-for-publicising-CS-exam-marking-process-UPSC-differs/214442 . Committee report on sansad.in: NOT FOUND. |

**Tension worth knowing (my inference from rows 1, 3, 4):** UPSC says no "model answer" exists, yet its own account describes examiner meetings that agree "appropriate answers" and (per the Chairman as reported) a vetted key of points to look for. So marking is against a points key, then moderated. The key is never published.

## 3. Merged directive-word table

Meanings below are **what each institute says**. None is a UPSC definition. UPSC publishes none (see `UPSC_OFFICIAL.md`; NEXT IAS says UPSC "does not explicitly state" its criteria).
Key: **V** Vision IAS keyword sheet (undated PDF); **D** Drishti IAS (2018 page); **VR** Vajiram & Ravi (23 Jun 2026); **I** InsightsIAS (25 Jun 2026); **PW** PW OnlyIAS FAQ; **M** Mrunal (Pavneet Singh 2018; Mrunal 2017). "-" = not defined on a page I read.

| Word | Meaning per institute | Agreed meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Discuss** | V: "written debate": reasoning, evidence for and against, then conclude. D: cover broadly, +/-, causes and consequences, solutions, holistic view. VR: various dimensions, viewpoints, pros, cons, causes, effects, solutions; balanced. I: analytical, about 60-70% analysis. PW: implications, pros and cons, balanced view. M: relevant issue, evidence, pros and cons, positive and futuristic ending; conclusion = summary (M 2017) | Weigh several sides with evidence, covering causes and effects, and end with a closing view. |
| **Examine** | V: close detail; key facts and important issues; why they matter; different readings. D: probe deeper; causes, implications, way forward. VR: investigate deeply, explore aspects for a logical understanding. I: "fact-oriented", facts the larger share. PW: critically analyse and evaluate aspects, balanced conclusion. M: body structured to the word; not same as discuss | Look closely at the facts and issues behind the topic. **Disagreement on whether it includes judgment** (PW yes; I mostly facts; D/V in between). |
| **Critically examine** | V: "critically" adds arguments for and against. D: strengths and weaknesses in separate paragraphs; conclude "without taking sides". VR: examine, then positives and negatives; balanced criticism. PW: positive and negative aspects, balanced judgment. M (2017): conclusion is a finding or stand | Examine plus strengths and weaknesses. **Disagree on the ending:** no side (D), balanced judgment (VR, PW), a stand (M). |
| **Analyse** | V: break issue into parts; each part with arguments and evidence for and against; how they interrelate. D: break into constituent parts, examine each, conclude on central idea. VR: components, causes, effects, linkages. I: analytical orientation (60-70%). M: identify themes and break down; evidence, pros and cons, futuristic conclusion | Split the issue into parts and show causes and links between parts. Most agreed word. |
| **Critically analyse** | V: same sheet as Analyse with "critically" flag. VR: positives and negatives, balanced judgment, own assessment | Analyse, plus a verdict on strengths and weaknesses. |
| **Comment** | V: pick main points, give your opinion with logic and evidence. D: choose your position; arguments with facts; critically = both sides. VR: concise opinion, clear stand, brief. I: analytical (60-70%) | Take and back a position, briefly. (I groups it with analysis, D/V/VR with opinion.) |
| **Elucidate** | D: make clear what was confusing; cite evidence, examples. VR: clearer through detail and examples; explain significance. I: fact-oriented | Explain clearly with examples and evidence. Not defined by V, PW or M in pages read. |
| **Explain** | V: clarify with detailed account of how and why; define key terms. D: clear reasons for what/how/why. VR: what it is, how it works, why it matters; avoid criticism. I: fact-oriented | What, how and why, in plain order, without judgment. |
| **Evaluate** | D: assess both sides; say how worthy or useful. VR: judge effectiveness; criteria, achievements, challenges; final judgment. V (via "critically evaluate"): verdict with evidence for and against | Judge worth against evidence, with a stated verdict. |
| **Critically evaluate** | V: verdict, evidence from a range of sources for and against; justify the choice. D: as evaluate, may lean positive or negative by topic. | As Evaluate with stronger evidence on both sides and an explicit verdict. |
| **Justify** | VR: prove the statement is correct; supporting arguments. (Listed with "Comment/Justify" in PMF IAS search result, not studied.) V, D, I, PW, M: - | Argue for the statement; one-sided by design (VR only; thin evidence). |
| **Enumerate / List** | VR: direct numbered list, minimal explanation. I: fact-oriented. V, D, PW, M: - (M 2007 repost: list points first, then explain) | List the points; little analysis. |
| **Assess** | V: to what extent true; flaws and counter-arguments; state how far you agree. VR: importance, impact, achievements and shortcomings, judgment | Weigh and give a judgment on degree. |
| **Illustrate** | V: like explain, with examples and statistics. VR: concept with examples, diagrams, maps | Explain using examples. |
| **Substantiate** | D: give evidence for the claim. VR: evidence, data, reports, not opinion | Prove with evidence. |
| **Compare / Contrast / Distinguish** | V: compare = similarities and differences; contrast = dissimilarities. VR: tables; compare similarities first, differences next | Table or parallel points. |
| **To what extent / How far** | V: thorough assessment; explore alternative explanations. VR: degree to which true; arguments for and against; balanced judgment | Degree judgment with both sides. |
| **Trace / Account for / Describe** | VR: trace = chronological evolution; account for = reasons; describe = features. V: describe = how and why; account for = why not just what | Trace is timeline; account for is causes. |

**Sources the institutes name for these meanings:** none. Vision's one-page sheet names no source (and carries only a Vision IAS copyright line). Drishti builds its table from its own reading of CSE questions 2013-2017. Vajiram, Insights, PW and Mrunal name none. NEXT IAS (blog, last updated 18 Oct 2024) says the criteria it lists come from analysing toppers' answers and that UPSC "does not explicitly state" them. ## 4. Agreements and disagreements

### Where the institutes agree (at least Vision, Drishti, Vajiram and one more)
1. Intro, body, conclusion; the intro is short. Drishti and Vision cap it at 20% of the limit (Drishti ideal 10%); Mrunal's guest author says 20-30 words; Vajiram says 2-4 lines.
2. Read the directive word first and let it shape the body (all of Vision, Drishti, Vajiram, Insights, PW, Mrunal). Ignoring it is a named error at Vision, Vajiram and Mrunal.
3. Respect the printed word limit (Vision, Drishti, Insights, Vajiram, PW, NEXT).
4. Standard limits and time: 10 marks = 150 words, 15 marks = 250 words; about 7-8 minutes and 11-12 minutes (NEXT, Insights; Vajiram and PW similar).
5. "Analyse" means split into parts; "critically" means weigh both sides.
6. Support with data, examples, reports. Keep language simple (Vision, Drishti).
7. Balanced tone: no extreme bias (NEXT, Vajiram, Vision, Drishti).
8. Headings or bullets for facts; paragraphs for opinion and for intro/conclusion (Drishti, NEXT; Vision with headings used sparingly).

### Where they disagree
| Issue | Positions |
|---|---|
| Is a rigid template good? | Vajiram: I-B-C is "non-negotiable". Insights: a fixed positive-negative-way-forward shape becomes a weakness; "stop forcing" it. |
| Diagrams | Vajiram: one per answer, long menu of types. NEXT: use to fill space fast. Insights: "a diagram wherever possible" is mechanical. Vision: useful, do not overload. |
| Conclusion of "critically examine/evaluate" | Drishti: end without taking sides. Mrunal 2017: give a stand. Vajiram/PW: balanced judgment. |
| Conclusion tone | Drishti: always positive; never end negative or with a question. Mrunal's guest author: positive and futuristic. Vision/Vajiram: balanced or solution-oriented, not necessarily positive. |
| Does "examine" include judgment? | PW: yes (critically analyse and evaluate). Insights: fact-heavy. Vision/Drishti: probe, no verdict. |
| Is "comment" analysis or opinion? | Insights lumps it with analyse; Drishti, Vision, Vajiram: take a position. |
| Intro ceilings | Drishti 10% (max 20%); Vision 15-20%; Insights 10-15%; Mrunal guest 20-30 words; Vajiram 2-4 lines. |
| Time | PW "7 minutes" per question regardless of marks; NEXT/Insights 7-8 and 11-12; Vajiram 7, 10, 13 (and elsewhere 7-8, 10-11, 13-14). |
| Words per marks | Vajiram adds 20-mark = 350 words (GS never has that; Ethics case studies may be near 250-350). Drishti's 2018 page says 200 words for GS; this is outdated against 150/250 now. |
| Page space | PW: 150 words about one page. Insights: extra space, same word limit. Drishti: Essay booklet "very very generous". Official layout: NOT FOUND. |
| Bullets vs paragraphs | NEXT: bullets for facts. Drishti: depends on question type. PW: "no specific rule". Insights: depends on question. |

### What this means for our on-screen model answers (my inference, labelled)
- No institute and no UPSC source requires a particular layout; the only official constraints are those in `UPSC_OFFICIAL.md` (relevant, succinct, exact, economy of words, printed word limits).
- The safest shared shape: short intro (about 10-15%), body keyed to the directive, short conclusion (about 10%), within 150 or 250 words, with mostly bulleted facts and a small number of examples.
- A single glossary cannot be called "UPSC's meaning". If we show directive meanings on screen, label them as common coaching practice, and avoid stating a verdict rule for "critically examine" as official.
