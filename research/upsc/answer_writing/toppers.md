# What a topper's Mains answer looks like (from public copies)

Compiled 10 Nov 2026. Data: `toppers.csv` (32 answers, 8 toppers). Copies saved only in `sources/toppers/` (git-ignored). No answer is reproduced here.

## Read this first: what the evidence is and is not

- **No real Mains script was found.** Every public "topper copy" I could reach is a coaching-institute **mock test** written by a person who later ranked (SPM IAS Academy, Aptiplus, Sarrthi IAS via Dr Shivin, Vajiram & Ravi). UPSC does not publish scripts; the RTI route in 2012-14 blogs concerned candidates' own sheets, not toppers'.
- **Only one source is marked.** Abhijit Ray (AIR 50, 2021) on Aptiplus has evaluator marks and comments (8 answers, plus paper totals 109/250 GS2 and 103/250 GS3). All other copies are blank in the marks boxes: paper score NOT FOUND.
- AIR is as labelled by the institute. The CSE year is NOT FOUND on SPM copies (tests are dated Jul-Aug 2025). Ishitwa Anand (2025) and Vikas Kundu (2025) years come from the host page and are unverified.
- Paper mix: GS2 13, GS3 11, GS1 7, GS4 1. **No geography optional, PSIR, agriculture optional or economics optional copy was found.** Only one GS1 geography answer (Modi, Q18). Priority papers GS1 geography and IR are thin.
- Word counts and point counts are my estimates from pages seen (lines x words per line). Several answers were seen on one of their two or three pages, so "NOT SEEN" intros or conclusions are gaps in my view, not absences.

## Patterns (n = 32 answers unless stated)

1. **Headings: 30 of 32.** Boxed or underlined sub-headings (Factors / Measures, Merits / Demerits, Challenges / Way forward). The two without were one Shakti Dubey ethics answer and one Parmar page not clearly headed. Four toppers answering the same SPM GS2 paper (Neema, Modi, Priyank, Parmar) split the same questions the same way (factors, then measures; merits, then demerits).
2. **Underlining: 32 of 32.** Key terms, article numbers and scheme names underlined or boxed; heavier in SPM copies than in Sarrthi copies.
3. **Numbered or bulleted points, never paragraphs: 32 of 32.** 10-markers carry 8-11 points (typical 8-10); one outlier of 15 (Kundu, a comparison answer). 15-markers carry 6-16 (typical 8-12).
4. **Evidence named per point: 19 of 32 carry a number or article/section in the evidence column; 16 of 32 name an Act, committee, report, scheme or institution.** Typical tokens: Art 279A / 280 / 263, 2nd ARC, Indrajit Gupta committee, Swaminathan C2, PM-AASHA, e-NAM, IPCC, ADR, RBI report. Dropping these is what the evaluator marks down (see 5).
5. **Evaluator marks (Ray only, n = 8):** 10-markers scored 4, 4, 4.5, 4.5 of 10 (40-45%); 15-markers 5.5, 6.5, 6.5, 7.5 of 15 (37-50%). Paper totals 103 and 109 of 250 (41-44%). Comments repeat one theme: add examples, case studies, committee recommendations (named: Budapest Convention, V Kamakoti committee, NITI AI for All, Sendai/GRIHA). The best-marked answer (7.5/15, "Good answer") named two committees and a statutory body. The lowest (5.5/15) was labelled "generic". Mock marks are deliberately low and do not equal Mains marks.
6. **Introduction type (23 identifiable of 32):** data or figure 6, context 6, constitutional 4, definition 3, news or judgement 2, quote 1, concept 1. Nine not seen. Intros are 1-3 lines. SPM polity answers open with a news hook or figure in 5 of 7 cases; Sarrthi economy answers open with a figure (Budget 2024 tax rates) or a definition.
7. **Visuals: 8 of 32.** Flowchart or arrow-box 5, mind-map 3 (counting Modi's two-diagram answers once each). Zero maps, zero tables, zero graphs. Every diagram is a text-node diagram, not a drawing. Ray's evaluator asks "try to include maps and diagrams" and "provide a map" for IOR and disputed regions, so maps are wanted but were not drawn by this topper.
8. **Conclusions (15 seen of 32):** way forward / normative 7 (incl. constitutional ideal and Ambedkar quote), balanced view 3, summary or caution 3, committee citation 1, other 1. A one- or two-line closing sentence tied back to the question appears whenever the last page was seen. No SDG-linked ending beyond one citation of SDG 3 in the body.

## 10-markers versus 15-markers

| | 10 marks, 150 words (n = 23) | 15 marks, 250 words (n = 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Pages | 1.5-2.5 (mostly 2) | 2.5-3 |
| Estimated words | 100-170 | 150-230 |
| Points | 8-11, one outlier | 6-16 |
| Diagram | 5 of 23 | 3 of 8 |
| Structure | one or two heading boxes | dimension headings, more sub-points; chronology bands in history/IR |
| Evaluator marks (Ray) | 4-4.5 of 10 | 5.5-7.5 of 15 |

Writers use the page, not the word limit: real 150-word answers run about 100-150 words, and none exceeds 170 estimated words. In the one marked sample the 15-markers scored about 43% on average (24.0 of 55) against about 42% for 10-markers (17 of 40), so no real difference by length.

## GS versus optional

Not measurable. No optional copy was found. The one Essay copy (Ayushi Sijeria, AIR 914, SPM Essay test) is not in the CSV. GS2 polity answers are the most uniform (headings, numbered, article numbers); GS1 answers use diagrams most (4 of 7); GS2 2 of 13; GS3 least (2 of 11).

## High scorers versus lower scorers

Only two sources compare:

- **Physics Wallah, AIR 50 (Ishitwa Anand, about 796 written) vs AIR 307 (Mantasha, 783):** gap 13 marks. The AIR 50 copy used diagrams and maps regularly, clearer sub-headings, specific points under headings, and schemes, committee reports and statistics; the AIR 307 copy gave longer explanations with fewer empirical examples. Source: https://www.pw.live/upsc/exams/upsc-toppers-copies-air-50-vs-air-307 (year and paper not stated there).
- **VisionIAS on Ayan Jain (AIR 16, 2023, 102 in GS3) and Ritika Verma (AIR 25, 2023, 104 in GS3):** concise intros with a report, survey, scheme or data; headings and bullets; underlined key points; conclusions from committee recommendations, SDGs, conventions and way forward. Source: https://visionias.in/blog/general-studies-3/upsc-topper-ayan-jains-air-16-guide-to-effective-economics-writing-in-gs (strategy article; no scripts shown).

Both agree with the evaluator comments on Ray's copies: evidence and named sources, not length, separate better answers.

## What this suggests for on-screen answers (design inputs, not conclusions)

- Show a one-to-three-line hook, then a boxed heading per dimension, then numbered points each with one named Act, article, report or figure.
- Show underlined keywords (the copies do this in 32 of 32).
- Close with a one-line way-forward or balanced line.
- Use a text-node flowchart or mind-map for 15-markers and comparisons. A drawn map is untested in these copies.

## Gaps

- No actual Mains scripts, no optional-subject copies, one geography answer, no IR answer from an SPM copy; Vision, Forum, Insights, Drishti and NEXT copies were not reachable without login or were not found.

## Update 10 Nov 2026 (second pass): real Mains scripts and optionals

`toppers.csv` now has 41 rows with two new columns: `real_mains` (all `no`) and `subject_type` (GS 32, optional 9).

### 1. Real Mains scripts: none found (real 0, mock 41)

- **No UPSC-issued topper script is public.** Checked: ForumIAS Anudeep Durishetty page (AIR 1, 2017; four files "GS Test 6C/8C/12C" and "Essay 3", scanned, CamScanner stamps; these are test-series copies, not UPSC scripts; saved, not analysed), Lotus Arise, Edukemy, Vajiram, SPM, Aptiplus. Vision, Insights, Drishti and NEXT pages did not expose a topper script.
- **Legal position (secondary reports, not checked at source):** a 2014 CIC order said a candidate may inspect or copy his own evaluated sheet. UPSC v Angesh Kumar (SC, 20 Feb 2018) is cited by UPSC to refuse disclosure of conventional answer books; a 2018 CIC decision went the other way on other candidates' sheets. UPSC told an RTI applicant in 2012 that 2011 Mains books were already weeded out under its retention schedule. A May 2026 report says paper-wise marks of recommended candidates were published until CSE 2017 and only aggregate marks from 2018; a CIC appeal is pending. Sources: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2018/06/13/cic-answer-sheet-of-other-candidates-can-also-be-sought-under-rti/ ; https://news.careers360.com/upsc-mains-marks-disclosure-row-reaches-cic-as-rti-applicant-dopt-raise-coaching-concerns ; https://mrunal.org/2012/11/r-t-i-upsc-destroys-mains-answersheets-gives-prelims-marks-of-prince-dhawan-keeps-a-lid-on-nigvekar-committee-optional-subject-remove.html
- Lotus Arise's page says a UPSC examiner marked its toppers' copies and cites a 327 in geography optional for Pratham Kaushik. That is the site's claim; the PDFs sit in a Drive folder and the ones I opened are Guidance IAS mocks. Treat as unverified.

### 2. Optional copies: geography only (9 answers, 2 toppers, all mocks)

- **Ishitwa Anand (AIR 50)**, Guidance IAS Geography optional modular tests 1-3 via Lotus Arise, **marked**. Modular Test 1 total 111/250 (44%). Parts of 10 marks scored 4-5.5; parts of 15 scored 5-6.5; 20-markers 7.5-8.5 (index table). Test 2 and 3 index tables show the same band (3.5-5 of 10). Four answers recorded.
- **Preksha Agrawal (AIR 30)**, Edukemy Test-4, human geography models and theories, 200 marks, **unmarked**. Five answers recorded.
- **PSIR, Economics, Agriculture: NOT FOUND.** Only question papers, syllabus pages or enrolled-student channels (a PSIR copy page is linked from a Telegram post, not opened; Shankar IAS shares agriculture copies only in its batch channel).

Counts for the 9 optional answers: headings 9 of 9; underlining 9 of 9; any visual 3 of 9 (one theory triangle, one flowchart, one sketch map of the Indus system); **maps 1 of 9, graphs 0 of 9**; thinker or theory names in the evidence column 6 of 9; news hook intro 1 of 9 (Indus Waters Treaty).

What the marked optional answers show (Anand, evaluator): marks sit at 40-55% even for an AIR 50 writer. Comments reward current-affairs links ("good linking"), an apt sketch map (the only map found earned an "apt diagram" comment and 5/10), recent examples and a boxed subheading style, and ask for a world map where hazard locations are discussed. Preksha's unmarked copy is more dash-and-paragraph than numbered and is heavy on named scholars; one short box diagram per answer at most.
