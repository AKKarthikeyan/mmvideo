## Videos

### V1. ForumIAS: Basics of Answer Writing (transcript read)
- Speaker: Shruti Sharma, AIR 1, UPSC CSE 2021 (said she scored 494 in the GS papers). Role: topper.
- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2KS73K7j5EU ; 719,881 views ; 14:53.
- Her five basics (ordered as she gives them):
  - **Decode the question (2:52 to 4:34).** Half the work is answering what is asked. Beginners latch onto one keyword and write all they know. If you lack the content, still reshape what you know to the question and use the question's own words so the examiner sees a flow. She gives the example of a question on a disaster and a body's guidelines: use the heading even if you only know part.
  - **Content (4:34 to 5:55).** Practice does not help without content. Build content from model answers, topper copies and newspapers. Keep separate copies per GS paper and add new examples under the matching topic (cyber attack under security). Prepare 2 to 3 pages of templates for predictable syllabus topics like internal security.
  - **Structure, two kinds (5:55 to 8:40).** Content structure: short intro, body of several points, forward-looking conclusion (can carry a quote, SDG goals or key terms). If the question has two or three parts, give each its own subheading and roughly its own page. Spatial structure: underline only the important points, leave margins, start subheadings and points at set indents, and mark examples with a different symbol (circles for points, triangles for examples). She says this makes weak handwriting read better.
  - **Speed against content (8:40 to 10:22).** Finishing the paper matters more than a beautiful first five answers. Set internal limits (about 7 minutes for early questions; if one overruns, shorten the next). Practise three-question sets under the clock.
  - **Embellishments (10:22 to 12:25).** Only after the first four are sound: diagrams, flowcharts, maps, boxes and arrows, quotes, examples, committee names, a pre-collected list of keywords for stock phrases and a list of about 10 to 15 award winners to cite across papers. Revise this list because there is no time to think in the hall.
- Review habit (12:47 to 13:49): after every paper, note a better intro or an unused example from her notes in the margin; she treated rewriting as her revision.
- If you do not know the question (13:49 to 14:50): write something relevant with confidence, since most candidates will not know it either. She gives a blue-LED question as the example.
- Not covered: word limit, handwriting beyond layout, sources for data.

### V11. Vajiram and Ravi: Answer writing strategy session (transcript read)
- Speaker: Shruti Sharma, AIR 1 (same person as V1). The long 1:18:10 session. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jMQnizlUCkw ; 623,827 views.
- Repeats V1's five basics (0:05 to 12:50) and adds paper-wise detail. I read the first 30 minutes in full and searched the rest by keyword; the last 45 minutes (polity, economy, ethics) were not read line by line.
- Added points:
  - Write in points or paragraphs, her view is that this is a needless debate; coherence is what matters; she personally used points because they let her cover more dimensions (2:06 to 3:31).
  - Cover more than one dimension: more dimensions earn more marks, in her view (6:59).
  - Put the best point first, since you may run out of time (11:32).
  - Set a target after each review, e.g. at least seven points in a 10-marker (12:14).
  - History intro: begin with dates, key figures and a definition, so you need not think hard (14:58 to 16:40). History conclusion: link forward to the next period, to India, or to the Constitution (22:12).
  - Break a two-part question into two parts and give them equal weight (17:00 to 20:26). Draw a small map to show something quickly, with a few place names plotted (17:41).
  - Give a reason or example for each point (19:06).
  - Geography: use definitions or one official data point in the intro, a map with numbered regions, and add solutions even if only causes are asked, backed by named committees (24:38 to 27:44).
  - Polity and governance: use articles and committees, and in the way forward add a recommendation of a named committee or report where you can (33:58 to 34:59). Use SDG goals as a conclusion (35:21). Use headings like problems, legal, constitutional and institutional to brainstorm (36:03). Box any diagrams (37:25).
  - Ready templates for intros and conclusions on recurrent topics such as secularism or population (29:48 to 30:50).
  - Ethics case study: write a short paragraph (a quote, an anecdote or the list of dilemmas) before answering the sub-parts; do not reuse one quote more than once or twice (68:51 to 71:13). Conclusion: forward-looking, with key ethical terms (74:40 to 75:22).

### V3. Manuj Jindal: Real-time live answer writing practice (transcript read)
- Speaker: Manuj Jindal, IAS, AIR 53 (as titled by his channel on other videos). Role: former candidate and teacher.
- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P6mgLtNDnRA ; 77,871 views ; 15:59.
- Works one question live: the 2023 GS1 question on the socioeconomic effects of railways (150 words, 10 marks).
- Advice:
  - **Break the question into parts first (1:03 to 2:06):** overall coverage, then key terms (socio-economic means both social and economic), then examples for different countries.
  - **Do not explain, provide (2:27 to 3:07):** get straight to points because 10 marks gives you about 7 minutes at most.
  - **Time over words (3:07 to 4:08):** watch the clock rather than word count; if you manage the time the length follows, and 150 to 200 words in 7 minutes is realistic with practice.
  - **Handwriting (4:08):** legible and clear is enough; it need not be beautiful.
  - **Introduction as lead sentence (5:10 to 6:15):** when content is plentiful, turn the intro into one lead sentence that leads straight into the points.
  - **Headings in capitals and underlining (6:15, 9:50):** put the question's key terms (economic, social) as visible headings; underline countries, since the question asks about different countries.
  - **Points with a short example (11:36 to 14:28):** social points can be bare pointers (mixing of castes, women's mobility, labour class, political movements, vernacular press) and still earn decent marks, in his view.
  - **Use whatever you have (14:49):** if you lack foreign examples, use Indian ones. Such questions are derived knowledge that no coaching teaches directly (15:09).
- Not covered: conclusion, diagrams, data sources (he ends without a conclusion in this clip).

### V4. Vajiram and Ravi: How to write an answer in 7.5 minutes (transcript read)
- Speaker: a Vajiram and Ravi presenter; the auto-caption garbles the name, so it is not given here. Role: teacher and programme lead (his own description at 0:00). https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kzGFo8lMQag ; 66,266 views ; 14:38.
- Seven ways to raise speed:
  1. **Brainstorming templates (1:04 to 4:55):** e.g. for a scheme, think planning, targeting, delivery, uses (PTDU in his shorthand); he applies it to a DBT question: data accuracy and state involvement; inclusion and exclusion errors; leakage, biometric failure, last-mile; awareness, access for SC/ST/women, weak social audit. Claims it yields 7 to 8 points in 15 to 20 seconds.
  2. **Ready intros, conclusions and data (4:55 to 6:59):** the thinking time goes into the intro and conclusion, not the body, in his view. He has seen toppers with 300+ ready intros/conclusions for recurrent topics. Example: a topper (AIR 155) who mapped each directive principle to a sector so a labour question ends with Article 43.
  3. **Fixed time slots (6:59 to 10:06):** plan per 30-minute block (4 ten-markers, or 2.5 to 3 fifteen-markers); 7.5 minutes for a 10-marker, 10.5 for a 15-marker, 13 to 14 for an ethics case study. If you overrun one, catch up in the next. If an answer is unfinished, write the way forward and conclude quickly. In the 5 minutes of reading time he picked the 3 or 4 questions he knew least and did them last in each slot.
  4. **Pen (10:06 to 10:46):** a fast pen; names two pens.
  5. **One-page notes (10:46 to 11:49):** a one-page note per topic (e.g. cyber security) means more revision and less recall time.
  6. **Diagrams (11:49 to 12:51):** diagrams cut writing for non-core or factual parts and for the way forward, e.g. flowchart, table, circular map.
  7. **Practice (12:51 to 13:53):** first answers take 30 to 45 minutes even for the eventual rank one (his statement), and speed improves.

Most agreed (three of three): time budgeting, multi-point structure with intro, body and conclusion, practice with review, and examples from named sources.

## Disagreements and tensions

- **Explain or just list.** MJ tells students to give points and not explain (2:47, 12:18) when time is short. SS recommends a reason or example under each point (19:06). VR's DBT template is points only. Reading: SS for depth, MJ for speed; they are answering different time pressures.
- **Word limit.** MJ says to ignore word count and watch the clock (3:07). SS and VR give time budgets and do not mention words. No speaker read here gives a word count rule, and none says what UPSC does with overshoot.
- **What to do with a question you don't know.** VR says to push the least-known questions to the end of each 30-minute slot (9:24), spending less time on them. SS says to write something confident and relevant (13:49). Different ideas, not necessarily opposed.
- **Introduction length.** MJ turns the intro into one lead sentence when content is plentiful (5:55). SS wants a 2 to 3 line intro and allows skipping it when time is short (6:16 to 6:58). VR argues the intro and conclusion are what takes thinking time, so pre-write them (5:17).
- **Diagrams.** SS uses maps and charts as extra embellishment after the basics are sound (10:10). VR treats diagrams as a time saver for non-core parts (12:11). Same tool, different aim.
- **Paragraph or points.** SS calls it a needless debate and prefers points (2:06). Not contested by the others read.
