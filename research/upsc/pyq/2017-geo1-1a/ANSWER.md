[Diagram: Fig. 1: A rootless cone] Cross-section: lava flow over wet ground; steam blasts build a small cone; no pipe down to a magma chamber. _(demands D2; claims 2017-GEO1-1A-03)_

- _Rootless cones_: lava crosses wet ground; steam explosions build craters with no magma chamber below (Mývatn, Iceland; NASA).
- _Lonar_, Maharashtra: a _meteorite impact_ crater in Deccan basalt, 1.8 km wide, 150 m deep (Chandran et al.).
- _Mud volcano_: NCERT says gases and pressure push mud up at Baratang, Andaman, "India's only" one. _(demands D2; claims 2017-GEO1-1A-03, 2017-GEO1-1A-07, 2017-GEO1-1A-09)_

**2. Borderline features (our reading)** _(demands D3; claims 2017-GEO1-1A-04)_

- Some exam-prep notes list _geysers_ and _fumaroles_ here; no publisher we read does. USGS ties them to heat around a magma reservoir in young volcanic regions, "sometimes called dying volcanoes".
- _Fumarole_: a vent releasing volcanic gas even when not erupting. _Geyser_: Old Faithful erupts about every 65 minutes on average; the mechanism is "not completely understood" (USGS). _(demands D3; claims 2017-GEO1-1A-04, 2017-GEO1-1A-05, 2017-GEO1-1A-06)_
