# PYQ-2017-GS2-11: The 101st Amendment: Features, and Did It End Cascading? (UPSC GS II 2017)

**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2017, General Studies Paper II, Q11, 15 marks):
> Explain the salient features of the Constitution (One Hundred and First Amendment) Act, 2016. Do you think it is efficacious enough “to remove cascading effect of taxes and provide for common national market for goods and services”?

## Demands

- **D1** The salient features of the 101st Amendment Act, 2016
- **D2** Whether it is efficacious to remove cascading and give a common national market

## The answer sheet

The Amendment Act (assent 8 September 2016) lets the Union and States tax goods and services together, guided by a Council. _(demands D1; claims 2017-GS2-11-01, 2017-GS2-11-02, 2017-GS2-11-05)_

**1. Salient features** _(demands D1; claims 2017-GS2-11-02)_

- _Art 246A_: Parliament and State Legislatures both make GST law; Parliament alone on inter-State supply.
- _Art 269A_: the Union levies and collects IGST and apportions it.
- _GST Council_ (Art 279A): the Union Finance Minister chairs, each State has a Minister; decisions need three-fourths of weighted votes; Centre one-third.
- Service tax, entry tax and advertisement taxes left the Lists; 'supply' replaces 'sale' (Art 286).
- Council recommends on rates, exemptions, special provision for 11 States (279A(4)); it must set up a dispute mechanism (279A(11)).
- Outside, in the 2016 text: alcohol; five petroleum goods until the Council recommends a date.
- Compensation for five years (s.18). _(demands D1; claims 2017-GS2-11-02, 2017-GS2-11-03, 2017-GS2-11-04, 2017-GS2-11-05, 2017-GS2-11-10, 2017-GS2-11-01, 2017-GS2-11-12, 2017-GS2-11-13, 2017-GS2-11-06, 2017-GS2-11-11, 2017-GS2-11-07, 2017-GS2-11-08, 2017-GS2-11-14)_

**2. Is it efficacious?** _(demands D2; claims 2017-GS2-11-16)_

- Aim, as the Court recites the Bill: remove the _cascading effect_, give a common national market.
- We found no audit measuring cascading removed; we argue from design.
- _For_: 8 central and 9 State taxes merged; the Council is guided by a harmonised national market (Art 279A(6)); entry tax left the Lists (our reading: fewer breaks in the chain).
- _Against_: petroleum and alcohol outside, tobacco excise stays; in September 2025 the 56th Council recommended two rates plus 40%, and listed inverted-duty correction (textiles, fertilizers).
- Supreme Court (Mohit Minerals): recommendations are persuasive, not binding. _(demands D2; claims 2017-GS2-11-16, 2017-GS2-11-19, 2017-GS2-11-09, 2017-GS2-11-12, 2017-GS2-11-13, 2017-GS2-11-07, 2017-GS2-11-24, 2017-GS2-11-21)_

Efficacious in design, unfinished in practice: one GST and one Council, but, our reading, exclusions and slab corrections suggest cascading is not fully gone. _(demands D2; claims 2017-GS2-11-24, 2017-GS2-11-07)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- GST removed cascading completely. → **we found no audit measuring it; petroleum and alcohol are outside, and in 2025 the Council was still correcting inverted duty**
- The Council's recommendations bind the Union and States. → **the Court held them persuasive, not binding**
- GST covers all goods and services. → **alcohol for human consumption is outside by the Constitution's own definition**
- The Centre alone decides in the Council. → **decisions need three-fourths of weighted votes; the Centre holds one-third**
