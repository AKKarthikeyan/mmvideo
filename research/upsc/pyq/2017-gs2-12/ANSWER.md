**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2017, General Studies Paper II, Q12, 15 marks):
> Examine the scope of Fundamental Rights in the light of the latest judgement of the Supreme Court on Right to Privacy.

**Working budget:** 200-250 words, about 11 minutes in the hall (UPSC's printed limit is 250 words; 11 minutes is our arithmetic from 3 hours for 250 marks). Sheet: 207 words.

## Demands

- **D1** The latest judgment: what it decided (Puttaswamy, 24 Aug 2017)
- **D2** How it widens the scope of Fundamental Rights (nature and new content)
- **D3** Who is bound by the right, and its limits
- **D4** Examine: how far the scope now reaches

## The answer sheet

On 24 Aug 2017 nine judges held in _Puttaswamy_ that privacy is protected under Article 21 and Part III, overruling M.P. Sharma and Kharak Singh. _(demands D1; claims 2017-GS2-12-01, POL03-D12)_

**1. Scope widened** _(demands D2; claims 2017-GS2-12-03)_

- _Recognised, not granted_: life and liberty are 'inalienable' and inhere in each individual.
- _Overlap_: Gopalan's 'silos' gave way to overlapping rights (Cooper, Maneka); ADM Jabalpur was overruled.
- _Evolving_: the Constitution 'must evolve with the felt necessities of time'.
- New content: dignity, family life, procreation, sexual orientation, informational privacy and self-identity (NALSA).
- Koushal's 'so-called' rights view fell; _Navtej Johar_ (2018) followed. _(demands D2; claims 2017-GS2-12-03, POL03-D09, POL03-D14, 2017-GS2-12-06, 2017-GS2-12-05, 2017-GS2-12-08, 2017-GS2-12-12, 2017-GS2-12-10, 2017-GS2-12-15)_

**2. Who is bound, and the limits** _(demands D3; claims 2017-GS2-12-11)_

- Privacy runs against the State and _non-State actors_ (Kaul J.); the Court commended a data-protection regime, now the DPDP Act, 2023.
- Not absolute: _legality, need, proportionality_, with Article 21 procedure fair, just and reasonable.
- The Aadhaar judgment (2018) applied a four-part proportionality test. _(demands D3; claims 2017-GS2-12-11, 2017-GS2-12-08, POL03-X06, 2017-GS2-12-07, 2017-GS2-12-13, 2017-GS2-12-18)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: From silos to privacy] A timeline: Gopalan's silos, then Maneka's overlap, then Puttaswamy, then 2018's cases. _(demands D2; claims POL03-D09, POL03-D10, POL03-D12, 2017-GS2-12-15, 2017-GS2-12-18)_

**3. Weighing it** _(demands D4; claims 2017-GS2-12-04)_

- The Court was 'not amending the Constitution' but recognising a right already there.
- The Court did not list every entitlement, so each new facet is settled case by case.
- Gain: a base for later dignity cases, such as _Navtej Johar_ and Aadhaar; cost: an open-ended list. _(demands D4; claims 2017-GS2-12-04, 2017-GS2-12-06, 2017-GS2-12-15, 2017-GS2-12-16, 2017-GS2-12-18)_

So Part III is wider and still growing, but never absolute: each new facet must pass legality, need and proportionality. _(demands D2, D3, D4; claims 2017-GS2-12-07)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- The Court created privacy by amending Article 21. → **it recognised a right already inside Article 21, not an amendment**
- Privacy is an absolute right. → **it must pass legality, need and proportionality**
- Only the State must respect privacy. → **Kaul J.: privacy is protected against State and non-State actors**
- Fundamental Rights are separate silos. → **Gopalan's silos were replaced by overlapping rights**
- (no mention of dignity or the 2018 cases) → **dignity is 'the constitutional core' and the 2018 cases applied the right**
