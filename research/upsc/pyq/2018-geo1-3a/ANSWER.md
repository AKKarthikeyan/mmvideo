**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2018, Geography optional Paper I, Q3(a), 20 marks):
> Explain the origin, progress and retreat of the Indian monsoon and discuss its impact on the Indian economy.

## Demands

- **D1** Explain the origin (the mechanism: why it forms)
- **D2** Explain the progress (onset, advance, branches, spells)
- **D3** Explain the retreat (withdrawal and what follows)
- **D4** Discuss the impact on the Indian economy (more than one effect, with figures)

## The answer sheet

The Indian monsoon is a _seasonal reversal of winds_ (Arabic mausim, 'season'). June-September brings about 75% of India's annual rain. _(demands D1; claims M001, M002, M085)_

**1. Origin: no single theory** _(demands D1; claims M004, M122)_

- _Thermal concept_ (Halley, 1686): land heats faster than sea; a heat low over north-west India and a monsoon trough draw in moist sea air.
- _Dynamic concept_ (Flohn, 1951): the ITCZ shifts north with the sun, to about 20-25°N in July; today's view sees the monsoon as this rain belt's seasonal migration.
- Cross-equatorial flow: trades from the Mascarene High cross the equator at 40-60°E, turn south-west and ride the _Somali jet_.
- _Tibetan heating_ (Koteswaram, 1958): the heated plateau builds the _Tropical Easterly Jet_ aloft.
- _El Niño_ tends to weaken it. _(demands D1; claims M007, M015, M016, M119, M013, M095, M011, M020, M026, M018, M120, M121, M021, M073)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: How the monsoon arrives] India outline; Mascarene High south of the equator; arrows crossing the equator and bending north-east (Somali jet); Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal branches; heat low and monsoon trough; dashed upper-air easterly jet; ITCZ line at 20-25°N in July. _(demands D1, D2; claims M020, M018, M042, M015, M016, M021, M013)_

**2. Progress** _(demands D2; claims )_

- Onset: Andaman Sea about 22 May, Kerala about 1 June, often with a 'burst'.
- Two branches: the Arabian Sea branch strikes the Western Ghats; the Bay branch turns up the Ganga and Brahmaputra valleys.
- All India by about _8 July_ (IMD's new normal), in active spells and breaks as the trough shifts. _(demands D2; claims M031, M027, M043, M042, M029, M017, M066)_

**3. Retreat** _(demands D3; claims )_

- Withdrawal starts in north-west India about 17 September and clears most of the country by 15 October.
- The winds reverse: the _north-east monsoon_ gives Tamil Nadu about 48% of its yearly rain. _(demands D3; claims M033, M034, M060)_

**4. Impact on the economy** _(demands D4; claims )_

- Farms: agriculture employs nearly half of Indians; nearly half of farming is _rain-fed_.
- Food: kharif gave 46.7% of foodgrain output in 2025-26.
- Drought: an all-India deficit above 10% with 20-40% of the area in drought; 2002, 2009 and 2015 were El Niño years.
- Water: the above-normal 2025 monsoon raised reservoir levels (Economic Survey 2025-26).
- Prices: RBI (October 2026) names the deficient monsoon as a risk to rural demand and a food-price pressure. _(demands D4; claims M085, M086, M116, M093, M073, M117, M114, M115)_

No single theory explains it; the newer view sees a migrating rain belt shaped by land, ocean and jets. With June-September rain down about 6% in 50 years and wet spells more intense, better forecasts (Mission Mausam) and stored water are India's insurance. _(demands D1; claims M004, M011, M083, M088)_
