## Demands

## The answer sheet

A _phreatic eruption_ is a _steam explosion_. Water meets hot rock, and only old rock is blasted out, _not new magma_. _(demands D1; claims 2019-GEO1-1A-01, 2019-GEO1-1A-02)_

**1. How they work** _(demands D1; claims 2019-GEO1-1A-02, 2019-GEO1-1A-04)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: A steam blast] Cross-section: cold water or crater lake above hot rock; a mineral seal; steam pressure building; blast throwing out old rock; a dashed magma body below, not erupted. _(demands D1; claims 2019-GEO1-1A-02, 2019-GEO1-1A-04)_

- USGS: steam expands where water meets hot rock; no new magma erupts.
- GeoNet: gas and steam build up behind a _mineral seal_ until it breaks.
- Often weak, but can be violent: Taal 1965, La Soufriere 1975-76. _(demands D1; claims 2019-GEO1-1A-02, 2019-GEO1-1A-03, 2019-GEO1-1A-04)_

**2. Consequences** _(demands D2; claims 2019-GEO1-1A-08, 2019-GEO1-1A-11)_

- _Little warning_: GeoNet: Whakaari's explosions typically give little to no warning, ejecting hot rocks, ash and surges. 9 December 2019 (our reading: phreatic): 1-2 minutes; 22 died (WorkSafe NZ).
- _Hazards_: Ontake, 27 September 2014 (Cabinet Office: 'difficult-to-predict phreatic'): ash 7,000 m, projectiles about 1 km, flows 2.5 km (JMA); 63 dead or missing, among climbers.
- _Response_: entry limits stayed even at Level 1; shelters, radio followed. _(demands D2; claims 2019-GEO1-1A-06, 2019-GEO1-1A-07, 2019-GEO1-1A-08, 2019-GEO1-1A-10, 2019-GEO1-1A-11, 2019-GEO1-1A-12, 2019-GEO1-1A-13)_

In our reading, effects stay local (a few km) but deadly; with little warning, visitor limits matter most. _(demands D2; claims 2019-GEO1-1A-07, 2019-GEO1-1A-11, 2019-GEO1-1A-13)_
