## The answer sheet

_High-powered money_ (H) is currency held by the public plus bank reserves. The theory says the _money supply_ (M) is a multiple of it: M = m × H. _(demands D1; claims ECO16-N07, 2021-ECO1-4B-02)_

**1. The theory** _(demands D1; claims )_

With currency C, demand deposits D and bank reserves R: M = C + D and H = C + R. So _m = (cdr + 1)/(cdr + rr)_, where cdr = C/D and rr = R/D. With no cash held (cdr = 0), m = 1/rr: at a 20% reserve ratio, m = 5. _(demands D1; claims 2021-ECO1-4B-01, ECO16-T07, ECO16-N16)_

- RBI is the only issuer of currency and lender of last resort. It changes H by _open market operations_; a CRR change alters rr, and so m.
- RBI's _reserve money_ (M0) is currency in circulation plus bankers' deposits with RBI plus 'other' deposits.
- India: RBI's multiplier is M3 / base money: 5.57 (2019-20), 5.20 (2021-22) (RBI Bulletin, June 2022). _(demands D1; claims ECO16-N17, ECO16-N18, ECO16-N19, 2021-ECO1-4B-03, 2021-ECO1-4B-04)_

**2. Assumptions (our reading of the derivation; no source we read lists them)** _(demands D2; claims )_

- The public holds a stable currency-deposit ratio, cdr = C/D. Fails in a crisis: cash holding rises and m falls (RBI Bulletin).
- Banks hold required reserves plus desired excess reserves, a fixed fraction of deposits, inside rr. Fails when excess reserves jump: IGNOU: US money fell about 35% from March 1930 to March 1933 while the base rose about 20%, as cdr and excess reserves both rose.
- RBI sets H. RBI authors say money is determined endogenously and, in shocks, the link from H breaks.
- Money is only cash plus demand deposits (M = C + D). _(demands D2; claims 2021-ECO1-4B-01, 2024-ECO1-4A-04, ECO16-T08, 2024-ECO1-4A-06, ECO16-T09, 2021-ECO1-4B-05)_

In our view, M = m × H holds while cdr and rr are stable; in shocks m moves and, RBI authors say, the link from H breaks. _(demands D1, D2; claims ECO16-T07, 2021-ECO1-4B-05)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- H is the money printed by RBI, and M is a multiple of it. → **no formula: C, R, cdr and rr are never named**
- Multiplier = 1/CRR. → **only true with no cash held; the full formula has the currency-deposit ratio**
- Assumption: banks keep CRR. → **leaves out stable cdr, demand deposits only, desired excess reserves and RBI control of H**
- (no India figure) → **RBI's multiplier of 5.20 for 2021-22 is never used to test the theory**
- (no failure case) → **never says what happens to the assumptions in a crisis**
