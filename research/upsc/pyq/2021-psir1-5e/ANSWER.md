## The answer sheet

Act of 18 June 1951. Its Statement of Objects: court rulings raised difficulties on _fundamental rights_. _(demands D1; claims 2021-PSIR1-5E-01, 2021-PSIR1-5E-02)_

**1. What it changed** _(demands D1; claims 2021-PSIR1-5E-04, 2021-PSIR1-5E-07, 2021-PSIR1-5E-08)_

- _Article 19(2)_: public order added (old clause narrower), with retrospective effect.
- _Article 15(4)_: backward classes.
- Articles 31A, 31B and the _Ninth Schedule_ (13 entries) shielded land reform. _(demands D1; claims 2021-PSIR1-5E-04, 2021-PSIR1-5E-07, 2021-PSIR1-5E-08)_

**2. Why it mattered** _(demands D2; claims 2021-PSIR1-5E-05, 2021-PSIR1-5E-12, 2021-PSIR1-5E-13, 2021-PSIR1-5E-14)_

- _Romesh Thappar_ (1950): a public-order law fell outside Art 19(2); the new ground, a likely reply (our inference).
- _Champakam Dorairajan_ (9 Apr 1951): communal seat order void under Art 29(2); Art 15(4), overriding 29(2), reads as the reply (our inference; SOR names no case).
- _Shankari Prasad_ (1951): Act upheld; Provisional Parliament could amend under Art 368.
- _Coelho_ (2007, nine judges): Ninth Schedule laws from 24 April 1973 open to basic-structure challenge. _(demands D2; claims 2021-PSIR1-5E-05, 2021-PSIR1-5E-07, 2021-PSIR1-5E-11, 2021-PSIR1-5E-12, 2021-PSIR1-5E-13, 2021-PSIR1-5E-14)_

Our reading: it began the contest over amending power; Coelho reaches laws added from 24 April 1973, not the 1951 entries. _(demands D2; claims 2021-PSIR1-5E-13, 2021-PSIR1-5E-14)_
