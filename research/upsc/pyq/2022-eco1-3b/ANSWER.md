## The answer sheet

Both show how far reserves stretch. We found _deposit multiplier_ in neither NCERT nor IGNOU: it is our label for _1/rr_. The _money multiplier_ is _(cdr + 1)/(cdr + rr)_ (IGNOU). _(demands D1; claims ECO16-T07)_

**1. Comparison** _(demands D1; claims )_

- _Deposit multiplier_: deposits per rupee of reserves when no cash is held. At a 20% reserve ratio, ₹100 of reserves supports ₹500: m = 5.
- _Money multiplier_: M/H, which counts the cash the public holds (cdr). Cash leaks from the lending chain, so it is smaller.
- At today's CRR of 3.00%: 1/rr is 33.3, while the money multiplier is 6.40 with cdr 0.1496 (toy figures, 31 Jul 2026).
- RBI's actual multiplier fell from 5.57 in 2019-20 to 5.20 in 2021-22 (RBI Bulletin, June 2022). _(demands D1; claims ECO16-N16, ECO16-A04, ECO16-T07, ECO16-A14, 2024-ECO1-4A-07)_

**2. Impact of cards** _(demands D2; claims )_

- Reasoning (ours): a card payment moves a deposit between accounts. If it replaces cash, cdr falls and the money multiplier rises. Credit cards are deferred-payment loans, not cash (ours).
- An RBI staff paper (authors' views) finds digital payments (all modes, not cards alone) inversely linked to currency use, but income outweighs it. Currency peaked at 14.4% of GDP in 2020-21 while digital payments grew 27% a year by value (2016-17 to 2022-23).
- Cards: ₹2,36,660 crore in June 2026, 0.75% of digital payment value (our arithmetic from RBI). Debit-card purchases were ₹35,299 crore against ₹2,16,359 crore of ATM cash. _(demands D2; claims ECO16-T07, ECO16-N16, 2022-ECO1-3B-08, 2022-ECO1-3B-01, 2022-ECO1-3B-02, 2022-ECO1-3B-03, 2022-ECO1-3B-05, 2022-ECO1-3B-07, 2022-ECO1-3B-06)_

**3. Justification** _(demands D3; claims )_

Yes in direction (our reasoning), but small on today's data (our inference). Less cash raises the money multiplier, but cards are a sliver of payments and income dominates. We found no measured fall in cdr caused by cards. _(demands D2, D3; claims 2022-ECO1-3B-01, 2022-ECO1-3B-07)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- Both multipliers are the same: 1/CRR. → **the money multiplier also counts the cash the public holds**
- At today's CRR the money multiplier is 33.3. → **that is the cash-free figure; with today's cash ratio the toy value is 6.40**
- Cards have no effect, as they are only a payment method. → **a card payment can replace cash, which moves cdr and so the multiplier**
- Cards will raise the multiplier a lot. → **no size: cards are 0.75% of digital value and debit cards mostly draw cash**
- (no label for the two terms) → **never says that 'deposit multiplier' was not found as a defined term in NCERT or IGNOU**
