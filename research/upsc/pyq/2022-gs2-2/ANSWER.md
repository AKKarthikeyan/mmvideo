# PYQ-2022-GS2-2: Free to Move, But Not Absolute (UPSC GS II 2022)

**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2022, General Studies Paper II, Q2, 10 marks):
> “Right of movement and residence throughout the territory of India are freely available to the Indian citizens, but these rights are not absolute.” Comment.

Checked against the PDF: research/upsc/_pyq/mains/2022_gs2.pdf page 2.

**Working budget:** 120-150 words, about 7 minutes in the hall (UPSC's printed limit is 150 words; 7 minutes is our arithmetic from 3 hours for 250 marks). Sheet: 132 words.

## Demands

- **D1** The rights are free, and only for citizens
- **D2** Why they are not absolute: the limits the Constitution itself sets
- **D3** Comment: what the courts held, and the verdict

## The answer sheet

Article 19(1)(d) and (e) give citizens, not foreigners, free movement and residence in India. _(demands D1; claims 2022-GS2-2-01, POL03-A11)_

**1. The Constitution's limits** _(demands D2; claims 2022-GS2-2-02)_

- _Article 19(5)_: reasonable restrictions in the general public interest or to protect any Scheduled Tribe.
- _Emergency_: Article 358 suspends Article 19 during a war or external-aggression Emergency.
- Residence: Article 16(3) lets Parliament require it for some posts. _(demands D2; claims 2022-GS2-2-02, 2022-GS2-2-03, 2022-GS2-2-04)_

**2. What the courts held** _(demands D3; claims 2022-GS2-2-05)_

- _Gurbachan Singh_ (1952): externment upheld as a reasonable restriction.
- _Ebrahim Vazir Mavat_ (1954): removing a citizen violated Article 19(1)(e).
- _Anuradha Bhasin_ (2020): section 144 orders must be necessary and reasoned.
- _Maneka Gandhi_ (1978): these freedoms 'are not and cannot be absolute'.
- _Amit Sahni_ (2020): a protest cannot block public ways indefinitely. _(demands D3; claims 2022-GS2-2-05, 2022-GS2-2-07, 2022-GS2-2-11, 2022-GS2-2-10, 2022-GS2-2-12)_

So the rights are fundamental yet conditional: a restriction must be by law and reasonable. _(demands D3; claims 2022-GS2-2-02, 2022-GS2-2-05)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- Article 19 rights belong to every person in India, including foreigners. → **Article 19 says 'All citizens'**
- Article 19(5) lets the police stop anyone's movement at will. → **a restriction must be by law, reasonable and on two named grounds**
- Externment was struck down in Gurbachan Singh. → **the Court upheld it as reasonable**
- (no case named) → **no named source for 'not absolute'**
