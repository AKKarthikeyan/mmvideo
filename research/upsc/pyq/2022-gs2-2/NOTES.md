# 2022 GS2 Q2 (10 marks, 150 words): "Right of movement and residence ... freely available to the Indian citizens, but these rights are not absolute." Comment.

Directive: "Comment": agree and show how, in a balanced way. Claims: `claims.csv` (ids 2022-GS2-2-NN). Ledger ids: POL03-xx.

## The question's demands
1. **The rights exist and are free (for citizens)**: Art 19(1)(d), (e) (-01; ledger D01, A10, A11, L10). Citizens only: Art 19 "All citizens" (ledger A11, L10); -16 is UNVERIFIED as a case but the Articles are CONFIRMED in the ledger.
2. **Not absolute: constitutional limits**: Art 19(5) (-02; ledger D03): general public and Scheduled Tribe interests; Art 358, Emergency (-03); residence in public employment, Art 16(2)-(3) (-04).
3. **Not absolute: the leading cases** (what the courts held):
   - Externment and public order upheld as reasonable restrictions: Gurbachan Singh 1952 (-05), Khare 1950 (-06).
   - Restrictions struck down: Ebrahim Vazir Mavat 1954 (-07: removal of a citizen violated Art 19(1)(e)); Kharak Singh 1962 (-08: night visits violated Art 21).
   - Movement abroad: Satwant Singh 1967 (-09) and Maneka Gandhi 1978 (-10; ledger B03, D10, L04): travel abroad is Art 21, so a fair, just, reasonable procedure is needed.
4. **Contemporary restrictions** (give 1-2 examples): s.144 Cr.P.C. orders and Anuradha Bhasin 2020 (-11; now s.163 BNSS, ledger X05), protests blocking roads (-12, -13 for the other side), the 2020 lockdown (-14).
5. **View**: the rights are fundamental but conditional: each restriction must be by a law, in the general public or tribal interest, and "reasonable" (courts test proportionality/necessity: -05 to -11); the Constitution itself names two grounds in Art 19(5).
