**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2023, General Studies Paper II, Q12, 15 marks):
> Explain the constitutional perspectives of Gender Justice with the help of relevant Constitutional Provisions and case laws.

## Demands

- **D1** The constitutional provisions on gender justice
- **D2** The case laws that gave them meaning

## The answer sheet

Gender justice means equality in law and in fact. The Constitution bars discrimination on sex in _Article 15(1)_ yet permits special provision for women in Article 15(3). _(demands D1; claims POL03-B04, 2023-GS2-12-01)_

**1. Constitutional provisions** _(demands D1; claims POL03-B01)_

- _Equality_: Articles 14, 15(1) and 16(1)-(2).
- _Dignity_: Article 21; Article 23(1) bars traffic in human beings.
- _Directive Principles_: 39(d) equal pay for equal work; Article 42, just and humane conditions of work.
- Duty: 51A(e).
- Politics: one-third of seats in Panchayats and Municipalities (243D, 243T); the 106th Amendment adds one-third of Lok Sabha and Assembly seats after delimitation. _(demands D1; claims POL03-B01, POL03-B04, POL03-B08, POL03-D08, POL03-E01, 2023-GS2-12-02, 2023-GS2-12-03, 2023-GS2-12-04, 2023-GS2-12-05)_

**2. Case laws** _(demands D2; claims 2023-GS2-12-06)_

- _Vishaka_ (1997): workplace guidelines until a law was made.
- _Anuj Garg_ (2007): a ban on women in bars failed Articles 14, 15 and 19(1)(g).
- _Joseph Shine_ (2018): section 497 IPC, adultery, struck down.
- _Babita Puniya_ (2020): permanent commission for women officers; _Nitisha_ (2021): indirect discrimination recognised. _(demands D2; claims 2023-GS2-12-06, 2023-GS2-12-10, 2023-GS2-12-07, 2023-GS2-12-11, 2023-GS2-12-12)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: From protection to equal treatment] A timeline of the five cases. _(demands D2; claims 2023-GS2-12-06, 2023-GS2-12-10, 2023-GS2-12-07, 2023-GS2-12-11, 2023-GS2-12-12)_

**3. Laws and figures** _(demands D3; claims 2023-GS2-12-22)_

- The POSH Act, 2013: every employer must set up an Internal Committee led by a senior woman.
- Maternity leave rose from 12 to 26 weeks (2017).
- Female labour force participation rose from 23.3 per cent in 2017-18 to 41.7 per cent in 2023-24. _(demands D3; claims 2023-GS2-12-22, 2023-GS2-12-16, 2023-GS2-12-17)_

**4. Weighing it** _(demands D4; claims 2023-GS2-12-05)_

So law has moved from protecting women to securing their autonomy and equal outcomes. Yet reserved seats in legislatures await delimitation: text and practice still differ. _(demands D4; claims 2023-GS2-12-05, 2023-GS2-12-12)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- Article 15(3) means women cannot be treated equally. → **15(3) permits special provision; 15(1) still bars discrimination**
- Mary Roy v State of Kerala was decided under Article 14. → **it is a statutory ruling on the Succession Act, 1925**
- The Vishaka guidelines are still the only law on workplace harassment. → **the 2013 POSH Act now sets the law**
- Women's reservation in Parliament is already in force. → **it takes effect only after delimitation**
- (no cases named) → **the question asks for case laws**
