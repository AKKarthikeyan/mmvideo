> Explain the significance of the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act. To what extent does it reflect the accommodative spirit of federalism ?

## Demands

- **D1** The significance of the 101st Amendment Act

## The answer sheet

The 101st Amendment (2016) gave the Union and States one shared GST power and a Council where States hold two-thirds of the weighted vote. _(demands D1; claims 2023-GS2-15-01, 2023-GS2-15-03)_

**1. Significance** _(demands D1; claims 2023-GS2-15-13)_

- _One tax, one market_: the Council is guided by a harmonised national market (Art 279A(6)).
- _Art 246A_: Parliament and State Legislatures both legislate on GST.
- Both gave up heads: States their sales and entry taxes, the Union service tax.
- Compensation for five years (s.18) was the price of the deal (our reading). _(demands D1; claims 2023-GS2-15-13, 2023-GS2-15-01, 2023-GS2-15-11, 2023-GS2-15-12, 2023-GS2-15-09)_

**2. How accommodative?** _(demands D2; claims 2023-GS2-15-05)_

- _Accommodative_: States share two-thirds of the vote equally; the Centre has one-third. Special provision for 11 named States (Art 279A(4)(g), 2016 text).
- _Mohit Minerals_ (2022): Council recommendations are not binding; the Court calls federalism a dialogue of cooperative and uncooperative federalism, 'collaboration to contestation'.
- The 2011 draft dispute authority and consensus rule were dropped. _(demands D2; claims 2023-GS2-15-03, 2023-GS2-15-02, 2023-GS2-15-10, 2023-GS2-15-05, 2023-GS2-15-06, 2023-GS2-15-07)_

- _Limits_: a three-fourths majority gives the Centre's one-third a veto (our inference from Art 279A(9)).
- The Union collects and apportions IGST (Art 269A); Parliament alone legislates on inter-State supply.
- The s.18 duty ran five years (to June 2022); the Council extended the cess (Oct 2020) and in Sept 2025 recommended the tobacco cess stay until loans are repaid.
- We found no record that the Art 279A(11) dispute mechanism was set up.
- _Tested_: at the 42nd Council (Oct 2020) Punjab, Chhattisgarh, Kerala and West Bengal dissented on the borrowing options; Punjab cited Art 279A(11). _(demands D2; claims 2023-GS2-15-03, 2023-GS2-15-08, 2023-GS2-15-01, 2023-GS2-15-16, 2023-GS2-15-15, 2023-GS2-15-04, 2020-GS3-12-15, 2020-GS3-12-22)_

Largely accommodative in design; the borrowing dispute tested it. 'Accommodative' is the question's word. _(demands D1, D2; claims 2023-GS2-15-04, 2020-GS3-12-15)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- The Council's recommendations bind States. → **the Court held them persuasive, not binding**
- The Centre alone decides GST rates. → **the Centre holds one-third of the vote; States together hold two-thirds**
- The Council always decides by consensus. → **no tally of consensus against division was found; a division by secret ballot is allowed**
- The dispute authority under Art 279A(11) is working. → **the Article requires it; we saw no record that it was set up**
