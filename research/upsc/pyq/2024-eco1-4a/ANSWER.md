## The answer sheet

_Credit creation_: when a bank lends, a new deposit opens in the borrower's name and money supply rises (NCERT 3.3). Multiplier = money (credit) multiplier. _(demands D1; claims ECO16-N11)_

**1. Mechanism of credit creation** _(demands D1; claims )_

- Banks lend part of deposits, as depositors do not withdraw together; the _spread_ is profit.
- Bank of England staff (2014): most money is created by bank loans, as deposits.
- NCERT's example assumes the loan returns to the same bank. At its made-up 20% _reserve ratio_, ₹100 gives ₹500 of deposits.
- RBI sets the CRR by law: 3.00%; SLR 18.00% (11 Nov 2026). _(demands D1; claims ECO16-N08, ECO16-N10, ECO16-T01, 2024-ECO1-4A-01, ECO16-N14, ECO16-N15, ECO16-R02, 2024-ECO1-4A-02)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: How ₹100 of reserves becomes ₹500 of deposits] Lending rounds at a 20% reserve ratio: ₹100, ₹80, ₹64 … adding up to ₹500 of deposits. _(demands D1; claims ECO16-N15, ECO16-A04)_

**2. Implications for the multiplier** _(demands D2; claims )_

_m = 1/rr_ (IGNOU's formula with cdr = 0); with cash held, _m = (cdr + 1)/(cdr + rr)_, where rr is the reserve-deposit ratio and cdr the currency-deposit ratio. So RBI can move deposits by a multiple: at a 25% ratio ₹100 of reserves supports ₹400, not ₹500 (NCERT; our arithmetic). CRR and open market operations change these. RBI's multiplier (M3 / base money) was 5.57, 5.48, 5.20 in 2019-20 to 2021-22, the latest we could read (RBI Bulletin, 2022), as cdr rose 0.16 to 0.18. Our arithmetic: 1/rr at today's CRR of 3.00% is 33.3, a toy ceiling. _(demands D2; claims ECO16-N16, ECO16-A04, ECO16-T07, ECO16-A05, ECO16-N18, ECO16-N19, 2024-ECO1-4A-07, ECO16-A06, 2024-ECO1-4A-02)_

**3. Limitations** _(demands D3; claims )_

- _Cash drain_: a higher cdr cuts the multiplier; crisis cash holding can be offset by a lower CRR (RBI Bulletin).
- _Excess reserves_: IGNOU: US money fell about 35% (March 1930 to March 1933) while the base rose about 20%, as cdr and excess reserves rose.
- _Leakage_: loans paid out as cash or to other banks break NCERT's same-bank chain (our reading).
- _Loan demand_: the process continues only if there is sufficient demand for loans (IGNOU); profit and regulation limit it too (Bundesbank, 2017).
- _Lending share_: banks lent 81.9% of deposits on 31 Jul 2026 (our arithmetic from RBI).
- The multiplier is a reduced form, not a causal rule (Bundesbank). Bank of England staff (2014): in normal times money depends on interest-rate policy, not a multiplier of reserves. _(demands D3; claims 2024-ECO1-4A-06, 2024-ECO1-4A-04, ECO16-T09, 2024-ECO1-4A-01, 2024-ECO1-4A-03, ECO16-T05, ECO16-A09, 2024-ECO1-4A-05, ECO16-T01, ECO16-T04)_

Our reading: the multiplier is a ceiling in a toy model: cash habits, spare reserves and loan demand keep real money below it; it is not a causal rule. _(demands D2, D3; claims ECO16-N16, ECO16-T05)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- Banks create money by lending deposits. → **no mechanism: the loan opening a new deposit is never said**
- Multiplier = 1/CRR, so about 33.3 today. → **a toy ceiling at today's CRR; it ignores cash held and real limits**
- Limitation: banks keep CRR and SLR. → **leaves out cash drain, idle reserves and weak loan demand**
- (no worked example) → **a 20-mark answer that never shows the ₹100 to ₹500 rounds**
