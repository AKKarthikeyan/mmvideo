# PYQ-2024-GS1-18: Why Reservation Benefits Still Fall Short (UPSC GS I 2024)

**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2024, General Studies Paper I, Q18, 15 marks):
> Despite comprehensive policies for equity and social justice, underprivileged sections are not yet getting the full benefits of affirmative action envisaged by the Constitution. Comment.

Checked against the PDF: research/upsc/_pyq/mains/2024_gs1.pdf page 4.

**Working budget:** 200-250 words, about 11 minutes in the hall (UPSC's printed limit is 250 words; 11 minutes is our arithmetic from 3 hours for 250 marks). Sheet: 208 words.

## Demands

- **D1** The frame: the policies and constitutional provisions for equity and social justice
- **D2** Why the benefits are not yet fully reaching the underprivileged (the reasons, each with a named source)
- **D3** Comment: weigh the policies against the gaps and say what the Court's answer is

## The answer sheet

The Constitution backs affirmative action through _Articles 15(4), 16(4)_ and 46, and posts carry quotas of 15, 7.5 and 27 per cent. Official records still show gaps. _(demands D1; claims POL03-B05, POL03-B09, POL03-C18, 2024-GS1-18-21)_

**1. Where the benefit stalls** _(demands D2; claims 2024-GS1-18-20)_

- _Backlog_: unfilled reserved posts in ten Ministries did not fall between 2016 and 2019 (SC: 8,223 to 14,366); DoPT keeps no consolidated vacancy count.
- Rank: in Group A, SC hold 14.20, ST 6.54 and OBC 19.14 per cent (1 Jan 2024), against the quotas (our comparison).
- _Creamy layer_: OBC limit Rs 8 lakh since 2017; none for SC and ST in policy, and the judges differ.
- Spread within groups: Punjab's 2006 Act gave half the SC quota first to two castes.
- Education: ST gross enrolment is 22.8 against 30 overall (2023-24). _(demands D2; claims 2024-GS1-18-03, 2024-GS1-18-23, 2024-GS1-18-20, 2024-GS1-18-21, 2024-GS1-18-05, 2024-GS1-18-06, 2024-GS1-18-13, 2024-GS1-18-12, 2024-GS1-18-15)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: Group A representation, 1 Jan 2024] Bars: Group A shares against the quotas. _(demands D2; claims 2024-GS1-18-20, 2024-GS1-18-21)_

**2. The Court's answer** _(demands D3; claims )_

- _Davinder Singh_ (2024, 6:1) allowed _sub-classification_ of SC, but only on data showing inadequate representation.
- Article 335 is no bar.
- Backlog drives have filled over 4.80 lakh posts since 2016.
- The Rohini Commission, appointed in 2017 to study uneven OBC benefits, reported on 31 Jul 2023. _(demands D3; claims POL03-C15, 2024-GS1-18-09, 2024-GS1-18-10, 2024-GS1-18-22, 2024-GS1-18-07)_

So the policy exists, but backlogs, uneven spread within groups and gaps in education show the benefit has not reached all. The way forward is data on representation, as the Court asked. _(demands D1, D3; claims 2024-GS1-18-04, 2024-GS1-18-09)_
