# PYQ-2024-GS2-12: Privacy, Article 21 and the DNA Paternity Test (UPSC GS II 2024)

**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2024, General Studies Paper II, Q12, 15 marks):
> Right to privacy is intrinsic to life and personal liberty and is inherently protected under Article 21 of the constitution. Explain. In this reference discuss the law relating to D.N.A. testing of child in the womb to establish its paternity.

Checked against the PDF: research/upsc/_pyq/mains/2024_gs2.pdf page 3.

**Working budget:** 200-250 words, about 11 minutes in the hall (UPSC's printed limit is 250 words; 11 minutes is our arithmetic from 3 hours for 250 marks). Sheet: 234 words.

## Demands

- **D1** Explain why privacy is intrinsic to life and liberty under Article 21
- **D2** The law on DNA testing to establish paternity
- **D3** The child, and the child in the womb: privacy and the pre-natal limit (a part with no case law yet)

## The answer sheet

_Puttaswamy_ (2017) held privacy an intrinsic part of life and liberty under Article 21; a paternity test tests that right against a court's search for truth. _(demands D1; claims POL03-D12, 2024-GS2-12-01)_

**1. Privacy within Article 21** _(demands D1; claims 2024-GS2-12-01)_

- Nine judges (2017): privacy is protected under Article 21 and Part III, though the word is not in the Constitution.
- _Dignity_: 'the constitutional core of human dignity'; it covers family life and procreation.
- Not absolute: _legality, need, proportionality_. _(demands D1; claims POL03-D12, POL03-L05, 2024-GS2-12-02, 2024-GS2-12-03, 2024-GS2-12-04)_

**2. DNA test to prove paternity** _(demands D2; claims 2024-GS2-12-06)_

- Section 112, Evidence Act (now s.116, BSA 2023): birth in marriage is _conclusive proof_ of legitimacy unless non-access.
- Courts allow a test only on 'eminent need' and a strong prima facie case (_Bhabani Prasad Jena_, _Goutam Kundu_); never exploratory.
- _Sharda v Dharmpal_: a test is not per se against Article 21, but not mechanical.
- _Dipanwita Roy_: allowed on a specific non-access plea; refusal may draw an adverse inference, which is discretionary. _(demands D2; claims 2024-GS2-12-06, 2024-GS2-12-15, 2024-GS2-12-07, 2024-GS2-12-08, 2024-GS2-12-10, 2024-GS2-12-09, 2024-GS2-12-11, 2024-GS2-12-14, POL03-D08)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: When a court orders a paternity test] A flow: presumption, then a strong prima facie case, then eminent need weighed against the child's privacy, then an order, or a refusal. _(demands D2; claims 2024-GS2-12-06, 2024-GS2-12-08, 2024-GS2-12-10)_

**3. The child and the womb** _(demands D3; claims 2024-GS2-12-12)_

- _Firodia_ (2023) refused a test of the child: legitimacy is 'an essential attribute of the right to privacy'; 'the child cannot be used as a pawn'.
- PC-PNDT Act, s.4(2): pre-natal tests only for listed conditions; paternity is not one.
- We found no Supreme Court ruling on a foetal paternity test; these rules apply by analogy. _(demands D3; claims 2024-GS2-12-05, 2024-GS2-12-12, 2024-GS2-12-13, 2024-GS2-12-16)_

So privacy shields the child from casual tests; a court orders one only on eminent need, and the womb limb has no ruling we could find. _(demands D1, D2, D3; claims 2024-GS2-12-08)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- A husband can get a DNA test of the child whenever he doubts paternity. → **courts refuse a test on a mere asking; eminent need and a prima facie case are needed**
- The section 112 presumption falls once a DNA test is sought. → **birth in marriage is conclusive proof unless non-access is shown**
- Pre-natal DNA tests are allowed to settle paternity. → **PC-PNDT s.4(2) lists the permitted purposes; paternity is not one**
- Privacy is an absolute right under Article 21. → **it must pass legality, need and proportionality**
- Privacy is written in Article 21. → **the word 'privacy' appears nowhere in the Constitution**
