## Demands

## The answer sheet

The _money multiplier_ (m) links the money supply (M) to _high-powered money_ (H): M = m × H. H, also called reserve money, is the currency issued by the central bank, held by the public or by banks. _(demands D1; claims ECO16-T07, ECO16-N07)_

**1. Definition and formula** _(demands D1; claims )_

With currency C, deposits D and bank reserves R: M = C + D and H = C + R, so _m = (cdr + 1)/(cdr + rr)_, where cdr = C/D and rr = R/D. If no cash is held, m = 1/rr: at rr = 20%, ₹100 of reserves supports ₹500 of deposits (m = 5). _(demands D1; claims ECO16-T07, ECO16-N15, ECO16-N16, ECO16-A04)_

[Diagram: Fig. 1: How ₹100 of reserves becomes ₹500 of deposits] Lending rounds at a 20% reserve ratio: ₹100, ₹80, ₹64 … adding up to ₹500 of deposits. _(demands D1; claims ECO16-N15, ECO16-A04)_

**2. Determinants** _(demands D2; claims )_

- _Reserve ratio_ (rr): set partly by RBI's CRR (3.00% of NDTL since November 2025) and SLR (18.00%); a higher rr lowers m.
- _Currency-deposit ratio_ (cdr): the public's taste for cash, about 15% in July 2026; a higher cdr lowers m.
- Banks' willingness to lend and the demand for loans: profit, regulation and borrowers limit lending (Bundesbank, 2017).
- Shocks: in 1930-1933 US money fell about 35% while the base rose about 20%, as people pulled cash from banks. _(demands D2; claims ECO16-R03, ECO16-R05, ECO16-R07, ECO16-A09, ECO16-T05, ECO16-T09)_

**3. How the banking system controls money supply** _(demands D3; claims )_

- RBI changes H: buying bonds in _open market operations_ adds reserves; selling them drains reserves.
- RBI changes rr: a higher CRR makes banks cut lending, so m and M fall.
- The _policy rate_ (repo 5.50%) is today's main lever, working through banks' lending decisions.
- Banks create deposits when they lend; the multiplier is the outer limit, not a constant. _(demands D3; claims ECO16-N19, ECO16-N18, ECO16-I05, ECO16-T06, ECO16-T03, ECO16-A06)_

So M = m × H: RBI steers H, the reserve ratio and the policy rate, while people's cash habits and banks' lending decide how far the multiplier really works. _(demands D2, D3; claims ECO16-T07, ECO16-T06)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- Money multiplier = 1/CRR. → **only true with no cash held; the full formula has the currency-deposit ratio**
- With today's CRR the multiplier is about 33.3. → **a toy ceiling; real lending is capped by more than the CRR**
- Determinants: CRR and SLR. → **leaves out the public's cash habits and banks' wish to lend**
- RBI controls money supply through the multiplier. → **no mechanism: H, rr and the policy rate are never named**
- (no worked example) → **a definition question that never shows the ₹100 → ₹500 rounds**
