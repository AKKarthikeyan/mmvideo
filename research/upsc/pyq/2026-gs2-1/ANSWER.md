**Question** (UPSC CSE (Main) 2026, General Studies Paper II, Q1, 10 marks):
> Right to privacy relating to self-identity is very dear to every human being and well protected under Article 21 of the Constitution. In this context, examine the effect of the amendment in 2026, to the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019.

## Demands

- **D1** The premise: privacy and self-identity under Article 21
- **D2** What the 2026 amendment changed
- **D3** Examine its effect on that right, tested against Puttaswamy

## The answer sheet

_NALSA_ (2014) and _Puttaswamy_ (2017) place privacy and self-identity within Article 21. _(demands D1; claims POL03-D08, POL03-D12, 2026-GS2-1-16, 2026-GS2-1-17)_

**1. What the Act changed** _(demands D2; claims 2026-GS2-1-05)_

- Act 3 of 2026, in force 25 May 2026, omits s.4(2): the statutory right to _self-perceived gender identity_.
- A closed definition; its proviso excludes 'self-perceived sexual identities'.
- A _medical board_ must recommend before the District Magistrate issues a certificate.
- New penalties for forcing a transgender identity. _(demands D2; claims 2026-GS2-1-01, 2026-GS2-1-03, 2026-GS2-1-04, 2026-GS2-1-05, 2026-GS2-1-08, 2026-GS2-1-10, 2026-GS2-1-14)_

**2. Tested against Puttaswamy** _(demands D3; claims 2026-GS2-1-18)_

- The test: _legality, need, proportionality_ (Chandrachud J.).
- NALSA called self-identity a fundamental value; a board's recommendation replaces self-perception.
- Puttaswamy called LGBT rights 'real rights'; the proviso is where that reasoning would be tested.
- No court has yet ruled on the Act. _(demands D3; claims 2026-GS2-1-18, 2026-GS2-1-17, 2026-GS2-1-20, 2026-GS2-1-10)_

So the Act narrows self-identity in statute; whether that passes the _proportionality_ test awaits the courts. _(demands D1, D3; claims 2026-GS2-1-18)_

## The weaker answer (ours, for comparison)

- The 2026 Act keeps the right to self-perceived gender identity in section 4(2). → **section 3 of the 2026 Act omits s.4(2)**
- The Act took effect on 30 March 2026, the day of assent. → **s.1(2) waits for a notification; it began on 25 May 2026**
- The Supreme Court has struck the amendment down. → **no court ruling on its validity is in the record we read**
- Article 21 does not cover self-identity. → **NALSA, quoted in Puttaswamy, ties self-identity to privacy and autonomy**
- (no mention of the three-fold test) → **the question asks for the effect on a right; Puttaswamy's test is the yardstick**
