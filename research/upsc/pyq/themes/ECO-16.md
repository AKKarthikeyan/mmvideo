## The one frame

**Money supply M = money multiplier m x high-powered money H, with m = (cdr + 1) / (cdr + rr).**
H is what the central bank issues (currency with the public plus bank reserves). m is set by two habits: how much cash the public
keeps per rupee of deposits (cdr) and how much banks keep back per rupee of deposits (rr: required plus excess reserves).
Every one of the 11 questions asks one of four things about this line: (1) how H is made and measured, (2) how a loan becomes
deposits round by round (the 1/rr case, cdr = 0), (3) what moves cdr and rr (cards, hoarding, excess reserves, policy,
crises), (4) whether the central bank really controls M (exogenous v endogenous money). Frame rows: ECO16-T07 (formula),
ECO16-N15/N16 (Rs 100 to 500, multiplier 5), ECO16-A14 (toy numbers), 2024-ECO1-4A-07 (RBI's actual 5.57 to 5.20), ECO16-T08 (exogenous v endogenous).

## What the ten years say

- Asked every one or two years (8 of 9 exam years, 2023 absent). The 15-20 mark questions are almost all about cdr and rr
  (2017, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025); the exogenous v endogenous debate returns in 2017 and 2020.
- One frame answers all: write M = mH, derive m from H = C + R and M = C + D, then change one habit at a time.
- Gaps no ledger can fill from a primary source yet: today's reserve money and M3/M0 (BLOCKED), post-Keynesian
  horizontalist/structuralist writers, treasury-bill mechanics.
