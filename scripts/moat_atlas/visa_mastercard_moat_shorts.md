# Visa and Mastercard: five Moat Atlas shorts, under 30 s each

The two networks are close to twins, so each script works for both (swap the name and the number). Three are moats, two are the cracks.
Pace about 2.8 words a second. Jokes are opinion, not claims. Anything tagged CHECK needs the primary source before recording.
Not investment advice · not SEBI-registered.

## 1. The club: nobody carries a card nobody accepts (network effect)
VO: "Nobody carries a card that nobody accepts. Okay, library cards. Every shop that takes Visa makes the card more useful, and every
card makes the shop more eager to take it. That loop is the moat. Visa booked $45.9 billion of net revenue last fiscal year, up 15%,
just by sitting between the buyer and the seller. A rival doesn't need a better idea. It needs a few billion strangers to agree. Who's brave enough to ask?"
Visual: two crowds (shoppers, shops) with a loop of arrows; counter to $45.9B.
Sources: Visa fiscal Q4 and full-year 2026 release, 27 Oct 2026 (net revenue $45.9B, +15%), via SEC 8-K, CHECK against the filing.

## 2. The toll without the pothole (asset-light economics)
VO: "When you don't repay your credit card, the bank takes the loss. Not Visa. Not Mastercard. They run the road, the bank owns the potholes.
Result: Mastercard kept about 60 cents of every revenue dollar as operating profit last quarter, 59.8%. Visa's works out near 59%.
Most businesses would frame that margin. So why can't someone just build a cheaper road?"
Visual: road with a toll gate, banks fixing potholes; margin bar 59.8%.
Sources: Mastercard Q3 2026 release, 29 Oct 2026 (GAAP operating margin 59.8%, net revenue $9.8B). Visa: our arithmetic, (net revenue $12.1B less GAAP operating expenses $4.9B) / $12.1B = about 59%, CHECK against Visa's operating income line.

## 3. The sticker on the door (brand, trust, acceptance)
VO: "That little sticker on the shop door is a promise: your card works here, even in a town you can't pronounce. Mastercard's gross dollar volume
was about three trillion dollars in one quarter. Trust at that scale is boring, and boring is a moat. Nobody wakes up wanting to try a payment network they've
never heard of. The sticker is the product. So what can beat a sticker? Hint: a QR code."
Visual: shop door with a sticker; trillion counter; a QR code slides in at the end.
Sources: Mastercard Q3 2026 release (gross dollar volume about $3.0T, +7% local currency), CHECK.

## 4. The referee (courts and regulation)
VO: "Every moat has a lawyer waiting at the gate. In the US, the Justice Department alleges that more than 60% of debit transactions run on Visa's network.
And a judge has signalled approval for a settlement with merchants that trims swipe fees. Critics call the cut tiny. Visa's most expensive hobby is court.
If the toll gets capped, does the moat shrink, or just the margin?"
Visual: toll gate with a judge's gavel; fee bar with a small slice trimmed.
Sources: DOJ complaint against Visa (debit), the 60% is the DOJ's allegation, not a finding; case status in 2026 not confirmed, CHECK the docket. Swipe-fee settlement: preliminary approval by Judge Cogan, June 2026; final approval pending; critics: Merchants Payments Coalition (PYMNTS, Payments Dive), CHECK final-approval date.

## 5. The footpath next to the toll road (UPI, India)
VO: "In India, something odd happened: people stopped asking for cards. UPI handled about 24 billion transactions in September 2026, all from a phone,
no card in sight. Visa and Mastercard built the toll road. UPI built the footpath next to it. The best competitor to a card is not having to find your wallet.
Can a toll road survive a footpath that everyone prefers?"
Visual: toll road with a thin footpath beside it filling up with phones; counter 24B.
Sources: NPCI via DD India: UPI 24.07 billion transactions, September 2026, +23% year on year, CHECK against NPCI's monthly release. No 2026 card-volume or share figure found, so the script claims no decline in cards.

## Series note
Order for an Indian audience: 5 as the hook is tempting, but 1 explains what a network is first. Suggested order: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, each ending on a question. Compare with Apple: a moat is also a margin, and both lists end on the crack.
