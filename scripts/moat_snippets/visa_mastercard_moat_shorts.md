# Moat Snippets: Visa and Mastercard, five snippets (a Moat Atlas series), under 30 s each

The two networks are close to twins, so each script works for both (swap the name and the number). Three snippets are moats, two are the cracks.
Shape of every snippet: HOOK → ANALOGY → FACT (sourced) → DARK JOKE → CLOSE. Pace about 2.8 spoken words a second, 80 words at most.
Jokes and analogies are opinion, not claims; the dark humour is aimed at companies and situations, never at people. Anything tagged CHECK needs the
primary source before recording. Not investment advice · not SEBI-registered.

## 1. The club: the Esperanto of money (network effect)
Hook: Why can't anyone replace Visa? Analogy: a payment network is a language. Dark joke: build a better one and be right and completely alone.
VO: "Why can't anyone replace Visa? Because a payment network is a language: useful only because everyone speaks it. Build a better one and you'll be the Esperanto
of money, right and completely alone. Every shop that takes Visa makes the card more useful, and every card makes shops more eager. That loop earned Visa $45.9 billion
last fiscal year, up 15%. To beat it, you need billions of strangers to switch at once. Who's going to organise that group chat?"
Visual: two crowds with a loop of arrows; a lone speaker of a made-up language; counter to $45.9B.
Sources: Visa fiscal Q4 and full-year 2026 release, 27 Oct 2026 (net revenue $45.9B, +15%), via SEC 8-K, CHECK against the filing.

## 2. The toll without the pothole (asset-light economics)
Hook: How does a company earn operating margins near 60%? Analogy: running the road, not fixing the potholes. Dark joke: the bank holds the funeral, the network collects its fee at the door.
VO: "How does a company earn operating margins near 60%? By running the road, not fixing the potholes. When you don't repay your card, the bank takes the loss.
The network just collected its fee at the door and sent no flowers. Mastercard kept 59.8% of revenue as operating profit last quarter. Visa's works out near 59%.
Most businesses would frame that. So why can't someone build a cheaper road?"
Visual: a road with a toll gate, banks fixing potholes; margin bar 59.8%.
Sources: Mastercard Q3 2026 release, 29 Oct 2026 (GAAP operating margin 59.8%, net revenue $9.8B). Visa: our arithmetic, (net revenue $12.1B less GAAP operating expenses $4.9B) / $12.1B = about 59%, CHECK against Visa's operating income line.

## 3. The sticker on the door (brand, trust, acceptance)
Hook: Why do shops put a tiny sticker on the door? Analogy: the A-grade in a restaurant window. Dark joke: you only think about it after the food poisoning.
VO: "Why do shops put a tiny sticker on the door? It's the A-grade in a restaurant window: nobody reads the rules, everybody trusts the letter. Mastercard's gross dollar
volume was about $3 trillion in one quarter, and trust at that scale is boring, which is the point. Nobody thinks about it until the food poisoning. So what could beat
a letter in a window? Hint: a QR code."
Visual: a shop door with a sticker graded A; trillion counter; a QR code slides in at the end.
Sources: Mastercard Q3 2026 release (gross dollar volume about $3.0T, +7% local currency), CHECK.

## 4. The sheriff (courts and regulation)
Hook: Can a company this profitable ever be told no? Analogy: a toll road with a determined sheriff. Dark joke: a diet where you lose one pound and keep the cake.
VO: "Can a company this profitable ever be told no? The sheriff is trying. In the US, the Justice Department alleges more than 60% of debit transactions run on Visa. A judge
has signalled approval for a settlement with merchants that trims swipe fees. Critics call the cut tiny, a diet where you lose a pound and keep the cake. Visa's most
expensive hobby is court. If the toll gets capped, does the moat shrink, or just the margin?"
Visual: toll gate with a sheriff and a gavel; fee bar with a thin slice trimmed off, a cake on the side.
Sources: DOJ complaint against Visa (debit), the 60% is the DOJ's allegation, not a finding; 2026 case status not confirmed, CHECK the docket. Swipe-fee settlement: preliminary approval by Judge Cogan, June 2026; final approval pending; critics: Merchants Payments Coalition (PYMNTS, Payments Dive), CHECK final-approval date.

## 5. The footpath beside the toll road (UPI, India)
Hook: What happens when a footpath appears beside your toll road? Analogy: the footpath. Dark joke: the card isn't dead, it's just not invited to everything.
VO: "What happens when a footpath appears beside your toll road? In India, UPI handled about 24 billion transactions in September 2026, from a phone, no card in sight.
Visa and Mastercard built the toll road. UPI built the footpath, and the footpath has no gift shop. The card isn't dead. It's just not invited to everything.
Can a toll road survive a footpath that busy?"
Visual: a toll road with a thin footpath beside it filling up with phones; counter 24B.
Sources: NPCI via DD India: UPI 24.07 billion transactions, September 2026, +23% year on year, CHECK against NPCI's monthly release. No 2026 card-volume or share figure found, so the script claims no decline in cards; the joke is opinion.
