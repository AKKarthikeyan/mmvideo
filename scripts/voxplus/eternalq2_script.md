## The story in one breath
Why does ninety paise matter? That is Blinkit's margin, on Eternal's own adjusted EBITDA measure, in the September quarter: ₹184 crore on ₹20,940 crore of order value (after discounts), against a ₹156 crore loss a year earlier. The strange part: the longest answer in Eternal's shareholder letter is about AI agents that shop for you. Our question: when a computer can do your shopping, what part of Blinkit can it not skip? First, the money: food delivery made ₹627 crore of the group's ₹666 crore (94%, our arithmetic, after losses elsewhere are netted off), and the measure adds back ₹262 crore of staff share awards. On the standard accounts, tax (₹198 crore) was bigger than the ₹125 crore profit, because the parent's old losses run out this year while the subsidiaries' wait for their own profits. Revenue jumped 76% on the adjusted measure, partly because Blinkit now owns more of its stock (58% like for like). Why ninety paise matters: six quarters of improvement (the letter's count), mature cities above 5%, density lowers the cost of each order, so Eternal is building fast (308 net new stores in the quarter, 2,751 in all, expects to cross 3,500 by March), mostly in smaller cities that the letter says ramp like metros. Then the agent question: browsing is fun (65% of Zomato opens exploratory), agents can only skip a platform when the product exists without it (a table, a non-exclusive ticket, not a meal in minutes), and "An agent can place an order, but it cannot own the outcome." Its verdict: Blinkit and Zomato low risk, District medium to high. But... it is the company's own argument and the letter admits the incumbent's bias; the filings give no cost per new store, payback figure or market share; the letter's first answer is its own AI assistant, dark.ai, with no price or spend given; cash fell ₹1,813 crore; the letter itself says agents could make some categories "winner takes all". Back to ninety paise: our reading, the bet is that the physical part is what a computer cannot skip, and the company says it will cross 3,500 stores by March, with later letters showing whether the margin kept improving.
