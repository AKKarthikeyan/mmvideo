## The story in one breath
What does a nine rupee store cost? Swiggy's letter lists the "INR 9 Store" among the price offers it ran on Instamart last quarter and gives no price for it. It does give Instamart's loss on the company's own adjusted EBITDA measure: ₹895 crore, up from ₹778 crore the quarter before, while food delivery made a record ₹341 crore on the same measure, and the letter says Instamart's spending was a choice. Our question: what is that loss buying, and what does Swiggy say it needs before the losses stop? Food first: ₹10,054 crore of orders (+17.7%), about ₹3.40 of every ₹100 as adjusted EBITDA (₹2.80 a year earlier, the letter's chart), lifted by advertising (take rate 26.1%) and delivery efficiency (Bolt and 99-Store, more than a quarter of volumes); but the group lost ₹922 crore on the standard accounts. What the loss bought: order value after discounts +13.4% in three months (after 4% and 2.5%), users +1.3 million to 14.8 million, month-one retention over 60% (not defined), 69 net new dark stores, busier stores (1,192 orders a day from 1,089). What it cost: Swiggy's cut of each order fell 1.4 points; contribution, at break-even in May, was –1.0% of gross order value for the quarter, inside the guided range; of the 0.8-point quarterly fall, 1 point "Targeted investments", 0.4 mix, 0.6 clawed back; the loss rose ₹118 crore but barely moved as a share of gross order value (–9.8% to –9.7%). Break-even: the letter believes losses "peaked out", expects the price offers to start reversing, about 1 point of margin improvement in the December quarter, and needs about 2.3 times today's size (₹60,000 crore a year from ₹26,400 crore) and ₹30 of contribution per order (today about –₹7, our arithmetic), roughly two-thirds from earning more per order, one-third from lower costs; the move to owning inventory could add about 0.8 points. But... it is the company's forecast, with no break-even date in this letter; it says competition will not ease soon; it expects contribution of zero to –1% in the December quarter, still short of ₹30 a order; Eternal's Blinkit is about three times the size and in profit (Eternal letter, 21 Oct); group cash fell about ₹700 crore; Platform Innovations (where Toing sits) lost more and the letter does not split Toing out. Back to the nine rupee store: about –₹7 per order today (our arithmetic), +₹30 needed; the December quarter is the first checkpoint.
