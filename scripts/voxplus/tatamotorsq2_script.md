## The story in one breath
Why would a truck maker raise its offer by thirty cents a share? On 9 October Tata Motors raised its offer for Iveco Group from €14.10 to €14.40 a share, its slide says "in consideration of the slight delay in the completion of the Offer"; the results put the maximum at about ₹42,561 crore (€3.91 billion). The same filing shows the truck and bus business at home: wholesales +42%, revenue +42%, yet less kept per rupee. Which Tata Motors? The former TML Commercial Vehicles, which took the name after the demerger (effective 1 Oct 2025). Volume: 135.1K, every segment up double digits, international +177% (an Indonesia order), domestic +30%; revenue ₹23,930 crore; market share 36.2% (+110 bps on FY26), heavy trucks 55.7%; but mid-size share fell 170 bps and the biggest gain was buses and vans (+500 bps). Margin: EBITDA +31% vs revenue +42%; margin 11.7% vs 12.6%; the CFO blames "significant commodity cost pressures, particularly in steel and rubber" and says margins held against June; the profit bridge: volume/mix +1,159, realisation +1,017, variable costs −1,416; the slide says "largely offset", the release "helped partially mitigate"; neither numbers steel and rubber. Profit before exceptional items and tax still +51% to ₹2,660 crore. Cash: free cash flow ₹4,606 crore (₹2,210 a year earlier), of which ₹2,679 crore from working capital (58%, our arithmetic); net cash ₹11.3K crore (company), ₹15.9K crore (group, counting the Tata Capital stake at market value). Iveco: all-cash offer agreed 30 July 2025, excluding the defence business, about ₹41,363 crore at the old price, up to ₹42,561 crore now; approvals in, Iveco EGM approved 16 Oct, 35% tendered by 22 Oct, closes 26 Oct, settlement 30 Oct "subject to Offer conditions being met"; consolidation "from next quarter". But... the filings do not say how it is paid (offer ≈ 3.8× the company's net cash, our arithmetic), what acceptance is needed or what the conditions are, or what Iveco does to margin or debt; the CEO warns growth will "moderate" and commodity costs "remain a headwind"; our reading: more than half the quarter's free cash came from working capital, which can swing back. Back to €14.40: settlement on 30 October, if conditions are met; the next filing is the one to read.
