## Spoken-aloud check
Read it once without the pictures:

> Antimatter vanishes the moment it touches anything. Yet in October twenty twenty-six, physicists reported holding one anti-electron, a positron, for months. So they built a container with no walls. Magnetic and electric fields hold it in the middle, touching nothing. One ancestor was a vacuum gauge. In nineteen thirty-six, Frans Penning used a magnet to measure almost-empty air. Decades later, Dehmelt wrote that he wondered how to hold one electron still, then built a trap from Penning's gauge, and named it after him. But holding one for months is not a first. Dehmelt's lab announced a positron named Priscilla, held in their trap for three months. So what is new? Gabrielse's team cooled theirs almost to absolute zero, and can nudge it up one tiny energy step at a time. The paper is published. The big test, matching it against an electron, is not done. One particle, caged for months. Priscilla had a name. What would you call this one?
