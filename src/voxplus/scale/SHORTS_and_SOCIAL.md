## Short 1: Low cost — "Is Jio the cheapest network to run?" (~38 s)

**SAY:**
> Does Jio have a low-cost moat? Much of a tower's cost is paid before the next phone connects. So more traffic spreads that cost thinner. Jio's network carried two hundred and forty one billion gigabytes last year, more than twice Airtel's hundred and one. But Jio's figure includes home broadband. Airtel's is mobile only. So the filings can't tell us how big the cost edge really is. Verdict: a plausible cost advantage, not a proven one. The full Jio review is on our channel.

## Short 2: A name people trust — "What happened when Jio raised prices?" (~40 s)

**SAY:**
> A trusted name lets a company raise prices and keep its customers. So what happened when Jio raised prices? In the second quarter of financial year twenty five, Jio and other operators increased tariffs. Jio's revenue per user rose, from about one hundred and eighty two rupees to two hundred and six by the end of that year. But churn rose too, and net additions fell from four point two five crore to sixty four lakh. Jio says so in its own prospectus. Growth recovered the next year. Verdict: mixed. The full Jio review is on our channel.

## Short 3: Being hard to leave — "How many customers leave Jio each month?" (~40 s)

**SAY:**
> Kind three: being hard to leave. How many customers walk away each month? In the last quarter of financial year twenty six, Jio reported monthly churn of one point six seven percent. Airtel's India mobile business, two point four three. That's about seventeen out of every thousand customers, against twenty four. And Jio was lower in each of the last three years. But two cautions. Jio's base includes home broadband customers; Airtel's figure is mobile only. And after the price rise, Jio's churn went up while Airtel's went down. Low churn shows customers stay, not that they can't leave. Verdict: encouraging, not proven. The full Jio review is on our channel.

## Short 4: A crowd — "Does more customers make Jio better?" (~40 s)

**SAY:**
> Kind four: a crowd. Some businesses get better for everyone as more people join, like a marketplace. Jio has India's largest crowd: fifty one point seven six crore wireless subscribers in June twenty twenty six. But a phone network doesn't work like a marketplace. A Jio customer can call anyone, on any network. And on our estimates, Airtel keeps about thirty five rupees more EBITDA from every customer, every month. A bigger crowd hasn't meant more earned per customer. Verdict: scale, but not a crowd moat. The full Jio review is on our channel.

**SCREEN:**

3. Two phones on different networks joined by a call line (illustration).

5. Verdict stamp: SCALE, NOT A CROWD MOAT.

**Sources:** TRAI QPIR Table 1.11; RHP p.34, p.131; AAR p.180.

## Short 5: A licence — "Is Jio's spectrum really unique?" (~42 s)

**SAY:**
> Kind five: a licence, the rule-maker's permission to operate. Jio holds the most spectrum: twenty six thousand eight hundred megahertz. But eighty two percent of it is one band, twenty six gigahertz, where Airtel also holds eighteen thousand megahertz. What's distinctive is smaller: Jio holds seven hundred megahertz in all twenty two circles, valid until twenty forty two. And spectrum costs money: Jio still owes about one point zero five lakh crore rupees for spectrum it has already bought. Verdict: a partial licence moat. The full Jio review is on our channel.
